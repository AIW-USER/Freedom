### Overview / Key Concepts
- First lecture of a neurosurgery series for the exam: general brain anatomy, brain development and its malformations, circumventricular organs, cortical surface anatomy and Brodmann areas, craniometric points and the homunculus.
- Coming lectures (announced): skull foramina, arterial territories and venous anatomy, coma and brain death (criteria and Indian law), congenital malformations and hydrocephalus, infections, tumours, vascular anomalies, neurotrauma, functional neurosurgery (e.g. deep brain stimulation for Parkinson disease).
- Neurons cannot multiply, but glial cells can; so gliomas (from glial cells) are among the commonest brain tumours, and lost neurons after head injury are not replaced.

General
- Brain weighs about 1300–1400 g; each neuron is surrounded by about 10 glial cells.
- Covered, like the cord, by dura, arachnoid and pia mater.
- Parts: cerebrum (two hemispheres), cerebellum, brainstem (midbrain, pons, medulla) and diencephalon (thalamus, hypothalamus, epithalamus with pineal gland).
- Each hemisphere controls the opposite side of the body because the motor fibres cross (decussate) in the lower brainstem (medulla).

Ventricles and CSF pathway
- Four ventricles: two lateral ventricles (one per hemisphere), the third ventricle (between the thalami, above the hypothalamus) and the fourth ventricle (between the pons/medulla and the cerebellum).
- Lateral ventricle, interventricular foramen (of Monro), third ventricle, cerebral aqueduct (of Sylvius), fourth ventricle, then out through the foramina of Luschka and Magendie to the subarachnoid space and the spinal central canal; absorbed by the arachnoid villi and granulations.
- Obstruction at any point causes hydrocephalus (later lecture).

Cerebrum
- Largest part: thought, judgment, memory, problem solving, language, imagination.
- Cerebral cortex = superficial grey matter (neurons); white matter lies deep. The grey matter grows faster than the white matter in development, producing folds: gyri (convolutions), sulci (shallow grooves) and fissures (deep grooves).
- The longitudinal fissure separates the hemispheres; the corpus callosum is the main connection carrying information between them.

| Lobe | Position | Main functions |
|---|---|---|
| Frontal (largest) | Anterior; separated from the parietal lobe by the central sulcus | Motor cortex (in front of the central sulcus), personality, motor speech (Broca), reasoning, planning, emotion, problem solving |
| Parietal | Top of the head | Sensory cortex: touch, pressure, temperature, pain; recognising shapes drawn on the skin; language interpretation |
| Occipital | Back of the head | Visual cortex |
| Temporal (right and left) | Sides, above the ears | Auditory cortex, hearing and smell; posterior part shares in speech comprehension |

Cerebellum
- Second largest part, in the posterior cranial fossa below the occipital lobes, separated from the cerebrum by the transverse fissure; about 10% of brain weight but about 50% of its neurons.
- Two hemispheres joined by the vermis (posture, sitting upright). Anterior and posterior lobes (subconscious control of movement) and the flocculonodular lobe on the inferior surface (equilibrium and balance).
- Cortex with leaf-like folia; inner white matter is the "arbor vitae" (tree-like); deep nuclei.
- Functions: coordination of voluntary movement, posture and balance; some role in cognition and language. Cerebellar lesions give staccato ("scanning") speech.

- Brainstem lesions cause drowsiness or coma (arousal centres).

Diencephalon
- Posterior part of the forebrain, joining the midbrain to the cerebral hemispheres; encloses the third ventricle.
- Thalamus ("inner room"): about 80% of the diencephalon; the gateway to the cortex: relays nearly all sensory input (pain, temperature, touch, pressure) to the cortex.
- Hypothalamus: below the thalamus, next to the pituitary: autonomic control, emotion, food intake, temperature, water balance, sleep-wake (circadian) rhythm, endocrine control through the hypothalamo-pituitary axis, heart rate and BP (with the medulla).
- Epithalamus and pineal gland: small region above and behind the thalamus; the pea-sized pineal gland secretes melatonin in darkness, promoting sleep (melatonin is used for disturbed sleep after head injury).

Circumventricular Organs ("windows of the brain")
- Specialised structures around the ventricles WITHOUT a normal blood-brain barrier; sensory and secretory, letting the brain monitor and exchange substances with blood and CSF.
- Mnemonic "Medimix SOAP": Median eminence of the hypothalamus, Subfornical organ, Subcommissural organ, Organum vasculosum of the lamina terminalis (OVLT), Area postrema, Pineal gland, Posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis).

### Cortical Surface Anatomy and Brodmann Areas
- The central sulcus joins the Sylvian (lateral) fissure in only about 2% (separated by the subcentral gyrus in 98%).
- Parietal lobe: superior and inferior parietal lobules separated by the intraparietal sulcus; the inferior lobule contains the supramarginal gyrus (area 40, where the Sylvian fissure ends) and the angular gyrus (area 39, where the superior temporal sulcus ends).
- Pars opercularis, triangularis and orbitalis belong to the inferior FRONTAL gyrus.

### Craniometric Points
- Surface landmarks for surgical access and for forensic work.
- Pterion: where the frontal, parietal, temporal and greater wing of sphenoid meet; about two fingerbreadths above the zygomatic arch and a thumb's breadth behind the frontal process of the zygoma. The thinnest part of the skull: a fracture here tears the middle meningeal vessels and causes an extradural haematoma. Overlies the Sylvian fissure.
- Others: asterion, lambda, bregma (coronal + sagittal sutures), stephanion, glabella, opisthion, inion.
- Sagittal suture: often assumed to overlie the superior sagittal sinus, but the sinus usually lies slightly to the right of it.

### Somatotopic Organisation (Homunculus)
- Body parts are mapped on the motor and sensory cortex; hand (thumb), face, lips, tongue and eyes have large areas, the trunk and legs small ones.
- Leg and genitals are medial (on the interhemispheric surface); face and hand are lateral. This explains the deficits of strokes in different arterial territories (next lecture).

### Exam & Viva Pearls
- CSF: lateral ventricle, foramen of Monro, third ventricle, aqueduct, fourth ventricle, Luschka/Magendie.
- Cerebellar peduncles: superior-midbrain, middle-pons, inferior-medulla.
- Medial geniculate = hearing; lateral geniculate = vision.
- Substantia nigra lesion = Parkinsonism; pineal = melatonin.
- Metencephalon = pons + cerebellum; myelencephalon = medulla.
- Circumventricular organs: "Medimix SOAP".
- Broca 44/45 (non-fluent), Wernicke 22/39/40 (fluent, no comprehension), motor 4, sensory 3- 1-2, auditory 41/42.
- Central sulcus joins the Sylvian fissure in only 2%.
- Pterion fracture: middle meningeal artery: extradural haematoma.

### Take-Home Summary
- Know the parts of the brain, their functions and connections, the CSF pathway, development stages with their malformations, and the key cortical areas: the basis for all later neurosurgery topics.
