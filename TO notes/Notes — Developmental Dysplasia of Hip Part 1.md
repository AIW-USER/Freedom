Overview / Key Concepts
Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip (DDH) is a preventable cause of early hip
arthritis if diagnosed and treated in infancy. Infantile DDH differs fundamentally from
walking-age DDH in terms of clinical signs, imaging modality, and management
strategies. Early detection relies heavily on clinical examination and ultrasound
rather than radiographs.

Definition
DDH is a spectrum of abnormal hip development ranging from: - Acetabular
dysplasia - Subluxation - Reducible dislocation - Irreducible dislocation
The term developmental is preferred because hips may be normal at birth and become
dysplastic later.

Epidemiology
- Incidence at birth: ~1 per 100
- Incidence at 6 weeks: ~1 per 1000
- Female predominance
- Left hip affected more commonly
- Bilateral involvement less frequent

### Risk Factors
- Frank breech presentation
- Female sex
- First-born child
- Positive family history
- Oligohydramnios
- Postnatal swaddling with hips in extension

Clinical Examination in Infants
Most Sensitive Clinical Sign (>3 months)
- Limited hip abduction in flexion
### Neonatal Tests
- Barlow test: Provocative dislocation
- Ortolani test: Reduction of dislocated hip
Click is benign. Clunk is pathological.
### Important Clinical Points
- Asymmetric thigh or gluteal folds are not reliable
- Galeazzi sign becomes more evident with age
- Limited abduction persists into walking age DDH

Imaging Principles in Infantile DDH
Ultrasound is the Modality of Choice
- Best before femoral head ossification (<4–6 months)
- Allows dynamic assessment
- Evaluates cartilaginous structures

### Ideal Timing of Ultrasound Screening
- 6 weeks after birth
- Not recommended immediately at birth due to high false positives

### Ultrasound Classifications

| Classification | Modality | Best Use |
|---|---|---|
| Graf | Ultrasound | Neonatal screening |
| Ferretti | MRI | Preoperative planning |
| Tönnis | X-ray | After ossification (>6 months) |

Graf Ultrasound Technique
### Requirements for Standard Graf Plane
- Horizontal iliac wing
- Visible labrum
- Os ilium and ischium seen
- Correct probe orientation
### Key Measurements
- Alpha angle (bony roof)
  - Normal: >60°
  - Severe dysplasia: <43°
- Beta angle (cartilaginous roof)
  - Increased in dysplasia

### Interpretation of Ultrasound
- Dysplastic hip: Reduced alpha angle
- Dislocated hip: Femoral head uncovered even at rest
- Stress ultrasound helps assess instability

Management Principles in Infantile DDH
Goals
- Achieve concentric reduction
- Maintain reduction safely
- Allow normal acetabular development

First-Line Treatment
### Pavlik Harness
- Dynamic flexion–abduction orthosis
- Best before 6 months of age
- Allows movement within safe cone
Incorrect application increases risk of AVN
### Monitoring During Pavlik Treatment
- Regular clinical examination
- Serial ultrasound

<!-- page 4 -->

- Stress ultrasound to assess stability
### Failure of Pavlik Harness
- No improvement on serial ultrasound
- Persistent dislocation after 3–4 weeks

### Second-Line Options
- Fixed abduction brace (HAB)
- Short trial only
If unsuccessful → proceed to closed reduction

### Closed Reduction (6–12 Months)
- Performed under anesthesia
- Often combined with:
  - Adductor longus tenotomy
  - Iliopsoas release (if required)
- Hip spica application

### Role of Arthrogram
- Essential when ossific nucleus absent
- Assesses:
  - Empty acetabulum
  - Pulvinar
  - Ligamentum teres
  - Inverted labrum

Arthrogram Interpretation
### Key Prognostic Indicators
- Medial dye pooling
- Femoral head coverage >50% is favorable
- <30% coverage predicts poor outcome
### Overlap Assessment
- Type A: Good overlap
- Type B: Borderline
- Type C: Poor overlap, worse prognosis

<!-- page 5 -->

Dynamic Stability Assessment
### Ramsey Zone of Safety
- Wide zone = stable hip
- Narrow zone = poor prognosis
### Improving Zone of Safety
- Adductor longus tenotomy
- Iliopsoas release
Abduction >35–40° increases AVN risk
Complications
- Proximal femoral growth arrest (preferred term over AVN)
- Redislocation
- Persistent acetabular dysplasia
### Kalamchi–MacEwen Classification
- Type I: Normal
- Type II: Lateral physeal arrest
- Type III: Complete physeal arrest
- Type IV: Epiphyseal involvement

Faculty Quote
“In infantile DDH, ultrasound is your eyes and limited abduction is your warning sign.” –
Dr. Shalin Shah

### Exam Pearls
- Most sensitive sign after 3 months is limited hip abduction
- Ultrasound at 6 weeks is ideal screening tool
- Alpha angle >60° is normal
- Pavlik harness is first-line before 6 months
- Arthrogram is critical when epiphysis is absent
- Femoral head coverage >50% predicts good outcome
- Excessive abduction increases AVN risk
