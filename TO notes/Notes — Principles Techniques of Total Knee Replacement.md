OVERVIEW
This lecture explains the principles, biomechanics, implant evolution, and surgical
techniques of Total Knee Replacement (TKR). It integrates essential details from both
surgical practice and exam perspective, particularly for DNB/FNB candidates.
Quote: “TKR is easier done than understood.” – Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### KNEE BIOMECHANICS & MOVEMENTS
- Flexion–Extension Motion: Primary movement in TKR.
- Screw-Home Mechanism: External rotation of tibia in terminal extension and internal rotation during flexion.
- Posterior Femoral Rollback: Posterior translation of femoral condyles during flexion; improves flexion and prevents impingement.
- Posterior Condylar Offset: Distance between posterior cortex of femur and posterior condyle → higher offset = greater flexion.
- Posterior Tibial Slope: Normally 7°; promotes rollback. Key Biomechanical Goal: Equal and symmetrical flexion–extension gaps with proper rollback.

### Modern Classification:
- Unconstrained (CR): Collaterals & PCL intact.
- Semi-Constrained (CS, PS, UC): Collaterals intact, PCL removed.
- Constrained: Collaterals deficient. Ultra-Congruent (Deep Dish): Substitutes PCL without cam–post mechanism.

### TIBIAL COMPONENT DESIGN
- Symmetric: Easier rotational alignment; less anatomic.
- Asymmetric: Matches anatomy; better coverage.
- Posterior Tibial Slope: 5–7° ideal (3–5° bone cut + 2° from insert). > Excess (>10°) → risk of posterior fracture.

### POLYETHYLENE SPACER (INSERT)
- Cross-linked UHMWPE (6th gen) → best wear profile.
- Anterior bevel prevents patellar impingement.
- Size Matching Rule: Femoral & tibial sizes differ by ±1 only.

### HIGH-FLEX KNEE DESIGN PRINCIPLES
- High posterior condylar offset.
- Posterior tibial slope ~7°.
- Deep trochlear groove.
- Beveled anterior spacer.
- Balanced flexion–extension gaps. Goal: Allow ≥130° flexion without instability.

### ALIGNMENT & AXIS
- Mechanical Axis: Center of femoral head → center of ankle.
- Anatomical Axis (Femur): Piriform fossa → femoral condyle center.
- Angle between anatomical & mechanical axis = 6°.

### REFERENCING SYSTEMS

| System | Fixed Cut | Variable Cut | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Posterior | Posterior | Anterior cut | Upsize to avoid |
| Referencing | cut fixed | variable | notching |
| Anterior | Anterior cut | Posterior cut | Downsize to avoid |
| Referencing | fixed | variable | overstuffing |

Posterior referencing: standard in varus knees.
Anterior referencing: preferred in valgus/RA knees.
Femoral Component External Rotation: +3° to posterior condyles → makes flexion
gap rectangular.

### GAP BALANCING

| Problem | Correction |
|---|---|
| Both gaps tight | Re-cut tibia |
| Extension gap tight | Add +2 mm distal femoral cut |
| Flexion gap tight | Increase posterior femoral cut |
| Unequal gap (ML) | Release tight collateral |

Measured Resection Principle: Bone removed = implant thickness (~20 mm total).

LIGAMENT BALANCING
### Varus Knee (Medial Tight)
1. Release capsule from tibia.
2. Strip superficial MCL.
3. Remove pes anserinus.
4. Strip periosteum 4–5 cm distally.
### Valgus Knee (Lateral Tight)
1. Sacrifice PCL.

<!-- page 5 -->

2. Release IT band at joint line.
  3. Strip LCL & Popliteus from LFC.
  4. Detach lateral gastroc head & posterolateral capsule. If valgus >20°, use constrained prosthesis.
### Flexion Deformity

| Degree | Management |
|---|---|
| <15° | Posterior capsule release |
| 15–30° | Add +2 mm distal femoral cut |
| >30° | Two-stage or constrained prosthesis |

### PATELLAR MANAGEMENT
  - Do NOT resurface patella in OA routinely.
  - Indications for resurfacing:
  - Rheumatoid / Crystalline arthropathy (Gout).
  - Severely damaged cartilage.
  - Ensure central tracking and avoid lateral tilt.

SPECIAL SCENARIOS
### Bone Defects – AORI Classification

| Type | Description | Management |
|---|---|---|
| I | <5 mm, contained | Cement or graft |
| II | 5–15 mm, uncontained | Structural graft / augments |
| III | >15 mm, uncontained | Sleeves / cones / hinged prosthesis |

### Extra-Articular Deformity
  - Correction possible only if angulation <20°.
  - 20° → pre-correct deformity before TKR.
Ankylosis
  - ROM <10–95° = Ankylosis.
  - Requires good extensor mechanism; may need mesh/allograft reconstruction.
  - Flexion >90° difficult to achieve; complication rate high.

### KEY EXAM PEARLS
- Posterior rollback: PCL / cam-post / deep dish mechanism.
- Posterior tibial slope: 7° ideal.
- Posterior condylar offset ↑ = ↑ flexion.
- Trochlear groove deep = better tracking.
- Posterior referencing → upsize; Anterior referencing → downsize.
- Tibial cut: 8–10 mm, perpendicular to mechanical axis.
- Flexion tight → posterior cut; Extension tight → distal femur cut.
- Valgus deformity distal femoral cut: 3° valgus (not 6°).
- Ankylosis = poor prognosis; requires extensor integrity.
