Pediatric Hip Deformities – DDH, Coxa Vara, PFFD
– Dr. Sanjay Arora
Overview / Key Concepts
Pediatric hip deformities represent a spectrum of developmental and congenital
disorders affecting hip stability, alignment, and limb length. This lecture focuses on
three high-yield entities: - Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip (DDH) - Congenital Coxa
Vara - Proximal Femoral Focal Deficiency (PFFD)
A sound understanding of pathoanatomy, age-based management, and long-term
planning is essential, as treatment goals extend beyond reduction to growth
modulation and hip preservation.

Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip (DDH)
Definition
DDH is a dynamic spectrum of hip development disorders ranging from acetabular
dysplasia to subluxation and complete dislocation.
- Dysplasia: Deficient acetabular development
- Subluxation: Partial loss of femoral head–acetabulum contact
- Dislocation: Complete loss of contact
Term “Developmental” is preferred as the condition may evolve after birth.
### Etiology and Risk Factors (DDH)
- Ligamentous laxity (often inherited)
- Breech presentation (especially footling)
- Postnatal swaddling with hips in extension
- Oligohydramnios (packaging disorders)
### Associated Conditions
- Torticollis (15–20%)
- Metatarsus adductus (1.5–10%)

### Pathoanatomy of DDH
- Shallow acetabulum
- Everted or inverted labrum

- Hypertrophied ligamentum teres
- Pulvinar tissue within acetabulum
- Hourglass constriction of capsule
- Iliopsoas tendon obstruction
These factors impede stable reduction, especially in older children.

Clinical Examination in DDH
Neonate
- Barlow test: Provocative dislocation
- Ortolani test: Reduction of dislocated hip
### Infant and Child
- Limitation of abduction
- Galeazzi sign (apparent limb shortening)
- Klisic test (line from ASIS to GT passing below umbilicus)
- Asymmetrical skin folds
- Limp and Trendelenburg gait in walkers

Imaging in DDH
Ultrasound (Infants)
- Alpha angle >60° normal
- Beta angle <55° normal
- Superior for monitoring Pavlik harness treatment
### X-ray (Older Infants)
- Hilgenreiner’s line
- Perkin’s line
- Shenton’s line continuity
- Acetabular index
- Medial joint space widening

### Principles of DDH Management
- First goal: Achieve reduction
- Second goal: Maintain reduction
- Third goal: Allow normal acetabular and femoral head development

Age-Based Management of DDH
### Birth to 6 Months
- Pavlik harness (dynamic flexion-abduction)
- Concept of Safe Cone (3D safe zone of stability)
- No role for triple diapers or abduction diapers
Complications: Femoral nerve palsy, AVN with excessive abduction
### 6 to 18 Months
- Closed reduction under anesthesia
- Arthrogram to assess reduction quality
- Hip spica application
- CT or MRI to confirm concentric reduction

>18 Months
- Open reduction
- Medial approach for non-walkers
- Anterior approach for walkers
- May require:
  - Femoral shortening
  - Derotation osteotomy
  - Pelvic osteotomy

### Avascular Necrosis (AVN) in DDH
- Incidence: 4–10%
- More common after closed reduction in infants
- Caused by excessive abduction or flexion
Classifications
- Bucholz and Ogden
- Kalamchi and MacEwen (Types I–IV)

Congenital Coxa Vara
Definition
A developmental abnormality characterized by: - Decreased femoral neck–shaft angle -
Short femoral neck - Relative overgrowth of greater trochanter - Limb shortening

<!-- page 4 -->

Primary pathology is a cartilaginous defect in femoral neck.

### Etiology and Associations (Coxa Vara)
- Skeletal dysplasias:
  - Cleidocranial dysostosis
  - Metaphyseal dysplasia
  - Spondylometaphyseal dysplasia
- Often autosomal dominant

### Clinical Features (Coxa Vara)
- Painless limp
- Limb shortening
- Difficulty in cross-leg sitting
- Reduced abduction and internal rotation
- Trendelenburg gait

Radiological Assessment (Coxa Vara)
### Hilgenreiner–Epiphyseal (H-E) Angle
- <45°: Observe
- 45–59°: Gray zone
- ≥60°: Surgical correction indicated
Neck–shaft angle is less reliable than H-E angle.

### Management of Congenital Coxa Vara
- Observation if H-E angle <45°
- Valgus intertrochanteric osteotomy if ≥60°
- Pauwels osteotomy commonly used
Aim is to reduce H-E angle to ~25–30° to restore biomechanics and allow
remodeling.

Proximal Femoral Focal Deficiency (PFFD)
Definition
A spectrum of congenital femoral deficiencies characterized by: - Short femur -
Discontinuity between femoral head, neck, and shaft - Associated limb deformities

### Associated Anomalies (PFFD)
- Fibular hemimelia (≈45%)
- Clubfoot
- Congenital heart disease
- Spinal and facial dysplasia

### Clinical Features (PFFD)
- Markedly shortened thigh
- Hip flexion, abduction, external rotation deformity
- Knee flexion contracture
- Foot often at level of contralateral knee
- Skin dimple over greater trochanter

Classification of PFFD
### Aitken Classification
- Type A: Femoral head present, short femur
- Type B: Pseudoarthrosis of femoral neck
- Type C: Absence of femoral head
- Type D: Absence of head and acetabulum
### Paley Grouping
- Group A: Congenital short femur
- Group B–D: Increasing severity and discontinuity

### Treatment Goals in PFFD
- Family education and counseling
- Achieve stable gait
- Optimize use of existing limb structures
- Correct deformities of hip and knee
- Address limb length discrepancy

<!-- page 6 -->

### Management Options in PFFD
- Orthotic support
- Limb lengthening (selected Group A cases)
- Valgus osteotomy for coxa vara component
- Knee fusion with extension prosthesis
- Rotationplasty
- Amputation (selected severe cases)
Limb lengthening is challenging due to hip and knee instability.
Faculty Quote
“In pediatric hip deformities, the objective is not just reduction or correction. It is to plan
the child’s hip for adulthood.” – Dr. Sanjay Arora

### Exam Pearls
- DDH is a dynamic disorder, not always present at birth
- Pavlik harness works best before 6 months
- Safe cone concept is preferred over safe zone
- Excessive abduction increases AVN risk
- H-E angle guides treatment in congenital coxa vara
- Valgus osteotomy is treatment of choice for coxa vara
- PFFD management requires long-term life planning, not isolated surgery
