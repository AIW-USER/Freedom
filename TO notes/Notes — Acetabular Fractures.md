Acetabular Fractures – Dr. Rahul Upadhyay
Overview
Acetabular fractures are complex intra articular injuries that demand meticulous
radiographic assessment and a deep understanding of acetabular anatomy.
Management is guided by fracture pattern, stability, associated injuries, and soft tissue
status.
Faculty Remark: “Every acetabular fracture must first be understood on an AP and
Judet radiograph. CT only refines your understanding.”

Anatomy Essentials
Columns
- Anterior Column (Ilio pubic)
  - Iliac crest → anterior acetabulum → pubic symphysis.
- Posterior Column (Ilio ischial)
  - Greater sciatic notch → posterior acetabulum → ischial tuberosity.
Walls
- Anterior wall: anterior rim.
- Posterior wall: posterior rim.
### Key Structures
- Quadrilateral plate.
- Iliac wing.
- Obturator foramen.
- Corona Mortis: vascular variant to avoid during anterior approaches.
- Superior gluteal nerve and artery: at risk in posterior approaches.

Radiographic Assessment
### AP Pelvis – Key Lines (6 Radiographic Lines)
1. Iliopectineal line – anterior column.
2. Ilioischial line – posterior column.
3. Teardrop (Radiologic U) – quadrilateral plate and obturator canal.
4. Sourcil – acetabular roof, weight bearing dome.
5. Anterior rim.
6. Posterior rim.

### Judet Views

| View | Shows |
|---|---|
| Obturator (internal) oblique | Anterior column + posterior wall |
| Iliac (external) oblique | Posterior column + anterior wall |

### CT Scan
- Essential for fracture mapping.
- Identifies:
  - Marginal impaction.
  - Intra articular fragments.
  - Roof involvement.
- 3D reconstruction helps visualization but not a substitute for axial CT.
Roof Arc Measurement (Matta)
Used to define weight bearing dome integrity.
- Medial >30°, Anterior >40°, Posterior >50°.
- Revised biomechanical values are higher.

### Mechanism of Injury
- Femoral head driven into acetabulum.
- Depends on hip position and direction of force.
- Dashboard impact, fall from height, side impact.

## Classification – Judet and Letournel
### Elementary Fractures
1. Posterior Wall (most common)
- Associated with posterior dislocation.
- Marginal impaction common.
- Capsule disruption determines fragment viability.
Radiology:
- Obturator oblique: wall fragment.
- Iliac oblique: posterior border intact.

2. Posterior Column
- Entire posterior column detaches.

<!-- page 3 -->

- Femoral head migrates medially and posteriorly.
- SGN (superior gluteal neurovascular bundle) may be entrapped.
Radiology:
- Medial displacement of ilioischial line.
- Iliac oblique defines fracture exit.

3. Anterior Wall
- Rare.
- Fragment from anterior acetabulum and quadrilateral plate.
Radiology:
- Obturator oblique: trapezoid fragment.
- Iliac oblique: intact posterior column.

4. Anterior Column
- From iliac crest/ASIS → pelvic brim → quadrilateral plate.
- Low, intermediate, high subtypes.
Radiology:
- Obturator view: anterior column displacement.
- Iliac view: iliac wing extension.

5. Transverse Fracture
- Cuts both columns.
- Subtypes: infratectal, juxtatectal, transtectal.
Radiology:
- Both iliopectineal + ilioischial lines disrupted.
- Posterior displacement more than anterior.

## Associated Fracture Patterns
1. Posterior Column + Posterior Wall
- Combination injury.
- Wall fragment moves with femoral head.

<!-- page 4 -->

2. Transverse + Posterior Wall
- Common (20 percent).
- Combines transverse line with wall fragment.
- Frequently associated with dislocation.
3. T Type
- Transverse fracture + vertical stem entering obturator foramen.
- May resemble both column but retains attachment to ilium.
4. Anterior Column + Posterior Hemitransverse
- Anterior primary fracture with posterior extension.
- Femoral head follows anterior segment.
5. Both Column Fracture (most common associated)
- Complete separation of acetabulum from axial skeleton.
- “Spur sign” on obturator oblique is pathognomonic.
- Secondary congruence possible.

### Key Signs and Concepts
- Gull Sign: impacted medial roof fragment (not same as posterior column gull sign).
- Marginal impaction: impacted articular surface requiring elevation.
- Secondary congruence: both column fracture maintaining congruent relationship with head.
- Corona mortis: dangerous vascular anastomosis during anterior approach.

## Stability Assessment
### Examination Under Anaesthesia (EUA)
- Axial loading at 90° flexion under fluoroscopy.
- Loss of joint parallelism indicates instability.
- Determines whether posterior wall fractures need fixation.

## Management Principles
### Non Operative
- Indications:
  - Minimally displaced.

<!-- page 5 -->

- Roof arcs preserved.

### Operative Indications
  - Hip stable on EUA.

- Incongruent hip.
- Dome involvement.
- Marginal impaction.
- Intra articular fragments.
- Instability on EUA.

Surgical Approaches
### Posterior (Kocher Langenbeck)
- Posterior wall and column.
- Risk to SGN.
Anterior (Ilioinguinal)
- Anterior column.
- Beware corona mortis.
### Extended Approaches
- Trochanteric flip osteotomy (Ganz).
- Stoppa approach.

## Complications
- Sciatic nerve palsy.
- AVN of femoral head.
- Heterotopic ossification.
- Post traumatic arthritis.
- Infection.
- Malreduction.

### Exam Pearls
- Most common: posterior wall fracture.
- “Spur sign” → associated both column fracture.
- Obturator oblique = anterior column + posterior wall.
- Iliac oblique = posterior column + anterior wall.
- Roof arc concept guides stability.

<!-- page 6 -->

- CT mandatory for all acetabular fractures.

Final Faculty Insight: “Master the anatomy, master the radiographs, and the
classification will unfold naturally. Treat the hip, not the image.”
