Clinical Assessment of a Scoliosis Patient – Dr. C S
Vishnu Prasath (Sep 2026)
### Overview / Key Concepts
- Definition (Scoliosis Research Society): lateral curvature of the spine greater than 10 degrees measured by the Cobb method on a standing X-ray of the thoracolumbar spine.
- Assessment = clinical history, clinical examination (morphometry, general/systemic, spine and curve, neurological) and radiological assessment (next lecture).
- Idiopathic scoliosis = no neurological or structural cause found. Scoliosis is normally PAINLESS; pain, weakness, tenderness or bladder/bowel symptoms are red flags for another cause.

History
Age

| Type | Age |
|---|---|
| Infantile idiopathic | 0 to 3 years |
| Juvenile idiopathic | 4 to 9 years |
| Adolescent idiopathic (commonest) | 10 to 20 years |
| Congenital | From birth (vertebral anomalies) |

- Lungs: alveoli multiply until about 4 years and lung growth is complete by about 8 years (golden period before 5). A significant curve in this period limits lung growth: restrictive lung disease, disabling dyspnoea or cardiorespiratory failure.
- Pubertal growth spurt: girls 10 to 14 years, boys 12 to 16 years. Peak growth velocity (about a year before and after puberty) is when curves progress fastest.
- Fusion for AIS is usually delayed until around/after puberty: early posterior fusion stops trunk growth and the anterior column keeps growing (crankshaft phenomenon, recurrence).

Birth, development and family history
- Term or preterm, birth asphyxia (delayed cry), deformity noticed at birth, milestones, family/sibling history (strong genetic predisposition in both idiopathic and congenital scoliosis), menarche in girls.
- Clues: birth asphyxia + delayed milestones = cerebral palsy; delayed or REGRESSING motor milestones (walked, then stopped) = neuromuscular (degenerative) disorder; multiple congenital anomalies (hand, foot, knee) = syndromic scoliosis.

Symptoms needing special attention
- PAIN with deformity (rare in idiopathic): osteoid osteoma (painful scoliosis), nerve root compression, degeneration in older patients.
- Weakness of limbs or bowel/bladder disturbance: cord compression or stretching, neuromuscular disorder.

### Morphometric Measurements

| Measurement | Key points |
|---|---|
| Standing height | Supine up to 5 years, standing after. Standing height = subischial height (lower limb) + sitting height (trunk). Lower limbs have an earlier spurt than the trunk. At 5 years = 60% and at puberty = 86% of adult height. Growth of more than 0.5 cm (5 mm) per month = entering the pubertal growth spurt. Before surgery compare with parents' height (operate safely once about 90% reached). |
| Sitting height | Lying under 2 years, sitting after. Best reflects spinal growth. Onset of puberty: about 75 cm in girls, 78 cm in boys. |
| Arm span | Arms horizontal, tip to tip of middle fingers. Standing height = 97% of arm span. Used to estimate height in wheelchair-bound or severely deformed patients. Increased in Marfan syndrome. |
| Weight | Doubles between 10 and 17 years. More than 10% overweight: brace may not work. Underweight girls: menarche (and the growth spurt) may be delayed. Obesity in Prader-Willi syndrome may mask scoliosis. |

Systemic examination
- Cardiovascular: 7% of congenital scoliosis patients have congenital heart disease (septal, valvular); aortic root aneurysm in connective tissue disorders. Echocardiography before surgery.
- Respiratory: thoracic curves impair lung function; over 100 degrees vital capacity falls below 70% of predicted; over 120 degrees respiratory failure and cor pulmonale. Rapid shallow breathing; oxygen cost of breathing 3 to 5 times normal (worse with surgery, pneumonia, sepsis).
- Bedside lung check: breath-holding (20 to 30 s is acceptable), breathlessness while speaking or walking, unable to play with peers. Optimise before surgery: nebulisation, breathing exercises, and in severe cases halo-gravity traction (continuous for about 6 weeks while standing, sitting, walking and lying) to open the chest.
- Abdomen: hepatosplenomegaly (storage disorders), umbilical and inguinal hernias.
- Genitourinary: anomalies in 6 to 12% of congenital scoliosis: ectopic kidney (care in anterior approach), renal agenesis, PUJ obstruction with hydronephrosis. Ultrasound abdomen.

### Spine Examination and Curve Assessment
- Expose fully so head, shoulders, trunk and pelvis are seen.

| Step | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Paraspinal muscles | SPASM: nerve root irritation (disc prolapse with sciatic list = functional scoliosis), infection; deep median furrow. ATROPHY: neuromuscular scoliosis (e.g. post-polio). |
| Plane of deformity | Coronal (scoliosis), sagittal (kyphosis) or both (kyphoscoliosis). |
| Shoulder level | Normally the shoulder on the side of the major (convex) curve is HIGHER. If the OPPOSITE shoulder is higher, a structural proximal thoracic curve is present; matters when choosing the upper level of fusion. |
| Coronal balance | Head centred over the pelvis; C7 plumb line through the natal cleft. Truncal shift or whole- body tilt = coronal imbalance; an imbalanced patient is more likely to need surgery. |
| Sagittal balance | Lateral C7 plumb line should pass through the posterosuperior corner of S1. Falling in front = positive sagittal imbalance (stooped; typical of degenerative scoliosis; causes fatigue pain of paraspinal muscles). |
| Palpation | Spinal TENDERNESS is a red flag: investigate for infection or disc pathology. |
| ASIS level | Asymmetry = leg length discrepancy (compensatory scoliosis that corrects when legs are levelled; take X-ray with the legs equalised) or pelvic obliquity. |
| Pelvic obliquity (neuromuscular) | Corrects with manoeuvre = pelvifemoral contracture; does not correct = FIXED pelvic |

Adams forward bending test and scoliometer
- Forward bending increases vertebral rotation, so the RIB HUMP (razor-back) increases in structural scoliosis; a functional curve (e.g. sciatic list) disappears on bending forward. Used as a mass screening test.

<!-- page 6 -->

- Scoliometer: run from caudal to cranial with the patient in the Adams position; measures TRUNK ROTATION, not the Cobb angle. Trunk rotation of 7 degrees corresponds to a Cobb angle of about 20 degrees.
- Flexibility: clinical side bending (or lifting a small child gently by the head) shows how much the curve corrects. Rigid curves need surgical releases; flexible curves are easier to correct.

### Neurological Assessment

| Component | Findings and meaning |
|---|---|
| Higher mental functions | Consciousness, alertness, orientation, speech: altered in cerebral palsy, mucopolysaccharidosis, syndromic children. |
| Gait | Balance (shoulder level, waist line); need for support; myelopathic gait (cord compression or stretching by the curve: urgent MRI); spastic gait (cerebral palsy, UMN causes); inability to walk (neuromuscular). |
| Muscle bulk | Asymmetrical calf wasting = tethered cord; atrophy in poliomyelitis, spinal muscular atrophy; pseudohypertrophy in Duchenne dystrophy. |
| Tone | Hypotonia (LMN): poliomyelitis, spinal muscular atrophy, myelomeningocele. Hypertonia (UMN): cerebral palsy, Friedreich ataxia, Charcot-Marie- Tooth, mucopolysaccharidosis with craniovertebral junction stenosis. |
| Power | Normal in idiopathic scoliosis; reduced in neuromuscular scoliosis and spinal cord anomalies. |
| Sensation | Light and deep touch, temperature, pain. Syringomyelia: pain and temperature lost, crude touch and position sense preserved. |

### Exam & Viva Pearls
- Scoliosis = over 10 degrees Cobb on a standing film.
- Infantile idiopathic scoliosis is the only type commoner in boys.
- Growth over 0.5 cm per month = pubertal growth spurt; sitting height 75 cm (girls)/78 cm (boys) at puberty onset.
- Arm span: height = 97% of arm span; increased in Marfan.
- Curves over 100 degrees: vital capacity under 70%; over 120 degrees: cor pulmonale.
- Opposite shoulder higher = structural proximal thoracic curve.
- Scoliometer 7 degrees = Cobb about 20 degrees.
- Asymmetric abdominal reflex = MRI (syrinx, Chiari, tethered cord, diastematomyelia).
- Painful scoliosis: think osteoid osteoma, infection, disc.

### Take-Home Summary
- Use age, sex, birth/developmental history and red-flag symptoms to find the cause; measure growth (height, sitting height, arm span, weight) to judge the growth spurt; examine skin, heart, lungs and kidneys for associated anomalies; assess the curve (shoulders, balance, Adams test, flexibility) and always do a full neurological examination including abdominal reflexes before planning treatment.
