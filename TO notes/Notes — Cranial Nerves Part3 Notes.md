Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Cranial Nerves
(Part 3)
Faculty: Dr. Himanshu Champaneri
Topic: Vestibulocochlear, Glossopharyngeal, Vagus, and Accessory Nerves

OVERVIEW
This lecture covers the Vestibulocochlear (VIII), Glossopharyngeal (IX), Vagus (X),
and Accessory (XI) nerves — their nuclei, functional anatomy, and clinical correlations.
Dr. Himanshu’s Quote:
“Hearing and balance are the symphony of the brainstem, while the lower
cranial nerves carry the orchestra of speech, swallowing, and expression.”

VESTIBULOCOCHLEAR NERVE (CN VIII)
Type: Purely Sensory (Special Somatic Afferent)
Divisions: 1⃣ Cochlear (hearing) 2⃣ Vestibular (balance)

COCHLEAR COMPONENT (HEARING)
### Receptor: Organ of Corti (in Cochlea)

| Structure | Description |
|---|---|
| Hair cells | Primary sensory receptors (inner & outer) |
| Supporting cells | Deiters’, Claudius’, and Hensen’s cells |
| Rods of Corti | Form tunnel within cochlea |
| Tectorial membrane | Gelatinous roof embedding cilia |

### Mechanism of Hearing
1. Sound waves → Tympanic membrane → Ossicles (malleus, incus, stapes).

2. Vibrations → Oval window → Perilymph of scala vestibuli.

3. Pressure wave → Endolymph of cochlear duct → Basilar membrane movement.

<!-- page 2 -->

4. Hair cells bend → Nerve impulse → Cochlear nerve.
Note: Inner hair cells (2 rows) and outer hair cells (3–4 rows) act as transducers
converting mechanical energy into neural impulses.

### Supporting Structures
  - Basilar membrane: Foundation for hair cells.

  - Reticular lamina: Supports hair cell tops.

  - Tectorial membrane: Contact with cilia → triggers depolarization.

  - Spiral ganglion: 1st order neuron of auditory pathway.

### AUDITORY PATHWAY

| Order | Structure | Function / Relay |
|---|---|---|
| 1⃣ | Spiral Ganglion | First-order neurons within cochlea |
| 2⃣ | Cochlear Nuclei (dorsal & ventral, upper medulla) | Relay station for auditory input |
| 3⃣ | Superior Olivary Nucleus (pons, bilateral) | Sound localization; binaural hearing |
| 4⃣ | Trapezoid Body | Crossed fibers connecting both sides |
| 5⃣ | Lateral Lemniscus | Ascending auditory tract to midbrain |
| 6⃣ | Inferior Colliculus | Midbrain relay for auditory reflexes |
| 7⃣ | Medial Geniculate Body (Thalamus) | Final thalamic relay |
| 8⃣ | Primary Auditory Cortex (Area 41, 42 – Heschl’s Gyrus) | Sound perception in temporal lobe |

Mnemonic: “Some Cats Sleep Till Late In Midday” → Spiral, Cochlear, Superior
Olivary, Trapezoid, Lemniscus, Inferior Colliculus, Medial Geniculate.

GLOSSOPHARYNGEAL NERVE (CN IX)
Type: Mixed (Motor, Sensory, Parasympathetic)

### Nuclei & Components

| Component | Nucleus | Function |
|---|---|---|
| SVE | Nucleus Ambiguus | Motor to stylopharyngeus (3rd branchial arch) |
| GVE | Inferior Salivatory | Parasympathetic to parotid gland (via otic ganglion) |
| GVA | Dorsal Nucleus of Vagus | Sensory from pharynx, carotid body/sinus |
| SVA | Nucleus Tractus Solitarius | Taste from posterior 1/3 of tongue |

### Course & Branches
- Arises from medulla between olive & pyramid.

- Exits via jugular foramen → two ganglia (superior & inferior).

- Related to stylopharyngeus muscle.

| Branch | Function |
|---|---|
| Tympanic (Jacobson’s) | Middle ear plexus → lesser petrosal → otic ganglion → parotid gland |
| Carotid | To carotid body & sinus |
| Pharyngeal | Sensory to pharynx |
| Stylopharyngeal | Motor to stylopharyngeus |
| Tonsillar & Lingual | Taste & sensation (posterior 1/3 tongue) |

### Lesion Effects
- Loss of gag reflex (afferent limb)

- Loss of taste and sensation in posterior 1/3 tongue

- Loss of sensation in pharynx, tonsils, and fauces

VAGUS NERVE (CN X)
Type: Mixed – Sensory, Motor, and Parasympathetic

### Nuclei & Components

| Component | Nucleus | Function |
|---|---|---|
| SVE | Nucleus Ambiguus | Motor to pharynx, larynx, palate |
| GVE | Dorsal Nucleus of Vagus | Parasympathetic to heart, lungs, GIT |
| GVA | Dorsal Nucleus of Vagus | Sensory from thoracic/abdominal viscera |
| SVA | Nucleus Tractus Solitarius | Taste from epiglottis & pharynx |
| GSA | Spinal Nucleus of Trigeminal | Sensory from external ear |

### Course & Branches
- Origin: Medulla, exits via jugular foramen → 2 ganglia (superior, inferior).

- Descends within carotid sheath between carotid artery and jugular vein.
Major Branches: - Meningeal & Auricular (Arnold’s nerve) – Sensory to dura & ear.
- Pharyngeal branch – Motor to pharyngeal muscles.
- Superior laryngeal nerve → divides into:
- External branch → cricothyroid muscle.
- Internal branch → sensory to larynx above vocal cords.
- Recurrent laryngeal nerve → sensory below vocal cords; motor to all intrinsic
laryngeal muscles except cricothyroid.

ACCESSORY NERVE (CN XI)
Type: Motor (SVE + GSE)

### Roots & Nuclei

| Root | Nucleus | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Cranial root | Nucleus Ambiguus | Joins vagus → supplies pharynx, larynx, palate |
| Spinal root | C1–C5 segments (spinal nucleus) | Supplies sternocleidomastoid & trapezius |

Course: - Spinal root ascends via foramen magnum → joins cranial root → exits
jugular foramen.
- Cranial part merges with vagus; spinal part descends to SCM and trapezius.

### Clinical Significance

| Lesion | Effect |
|---|---|
| Cranial part | Dysphonia, dysphagia (via vagus) |
| Spinal part | Weakness in head rotation (SCM) & shoulder shrug (trapezius) |

Anatomical note: Spinal accessory rootlets lie posterior to the denticulate
ligament, between dorsal & ventral roots up to C5.

### VIVA & EXAM PEARLS
- VIII Nerve: Cochlear = hearing; Vestibular = balance; Lateral Lemniscus = auditory tract.

- Utricle/Saccule: Linear acceleration; Semicircular canals: Angular acceleration.

- IX Nerve: Stylopharyngeus only muscle supplied; lesion → loss of gag reflex (afferent).

- X Nerve: Uvula deviates to opposite side in lesion; recurrent laryngeal nerve – hoarseness.

- XI Nerve: SCM + Trapezius; lesion → shoulder droop & weak head rotation.

<!-- page 7 -->

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- VIII: Hearing & equilibrium (Cochlear & Vestibular divisions).

- IX: Taste (posterior 1/3 tongue), parotid secretion, stylopharyngeus.

- X: Voice, swallowing, autonomic control to viscera.

- XI: Palate, pharynx, larynx (cranial part) + SCM & trapezius (spinal part). Dr. Himanshu’s Closing Remark: “The lower cranial nerves control how we hear, speak, and stay upright — mastering them means mastering human interaction itself.”
