Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes –
Spondylolisthesis & Spinopelvic Alignment
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Spondylolisthesis refers to the slipping of one vertebra over another, most commonly
L5 over S1. It may be developmental or acquired, symptomatic or asymptomatic, and
can range from low-grade stable slips to high-grade progressive deformities.
Epidemiology:
- Seen in ~5% of population.
- Rare below 5 years of age.
- ~6% show progression.
- Never seen in paraplegics (requires erect spine mechanics).
Why It Matters:
Understanding spinopelvic parameters and sagittal balance is critical for surgical
planning—especially in high-grade spondylolisthesis, guiding whether to reduce or fix
in situ.

SAGITTAL BALANCE
### What Is Sagittal Balance?
- Alignment of C7 plumb line with the posterior superior corner of sacrum on standing lateral radiograph.

- Normal: C7 plumb line passes within ±1–2 cm of posterior superior sacral corner.

- Positive imbalance: Plumb line falls anterior to sacrum (forward stooping).

- Negative imbalance: Plumb line falls posterior to sacrum (excessive lordosis).
Compensatory Mechanisms
When sagittal balance is lost (forward stooping): 1. ↑ Lumbar lordosis
2. Posterior pelvic tilt (retroversion)
3. Hip extension & knee flexion

These help maintain horizontal gaze and upright posture, but at the cost of increased
muscular effort and back pain.

### Muscle Correlation

| Pelvic Motion | Active Muscles |
|---|---|
| Anterior tilt | Iliopsoas, Rectus femoris |
| Posterior tilt | Hamstrings, Rectus abdominis |

Retroverted Pelvis:
Seen in high-grade listhesis; pelvis rotates backward to restore sagittal balance. Causes
hamstring tightness from chronic activation.

CLASSIFICATION
### 1⃣ Wiltse–Newman–Macnab (1976) – Anatomic Classification

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Dysplastic | Congenital facet abnormality; intact pars interarticularis |
| Isthmic (Lytic) | Defect in pars interarticularis → slip of L5 over S1 |
| Degenerative | Due to long-standing instability & facet remodeling |
| Traumatic | Acute fracture elsewhere in posterior elements |
| Pathological | Secondary to bone disease or metastasis |

### 2⃣ Marchetti–Bartolozzi (1994) – Developmental vs Acquired

| Developmental | Acquired |
|---|---|
| High Dysplastic | Traumatic (acute/stress fracture) |
| Low Dysplastic | Post-surgery (iatrogenic) Degenerative / Pathological |

High vs Low Dysplastic:
- High dysplastic: Vertical sacrum, high slip angle, rapid progression.
- Low dysplastic: Horizontal sacrum, parallel endplates, slower progression.

RADIOLOGICAL EVALUATION
### Grading (Meyerding’s Classification):

| Grade | % of Slip |
|---|---|
| I | 0–25% |
| II | 25–50% |
| III | 50–75% |
| IV | 75–100% |
| V | >100% (Spondyloptosis) |

- Low-grade: ≤ Grade II

- High-grade: ≥ Grade III
### Slip Angle
- Angle between L5 inferior endplate and S1 superior endplate.

- >55° → High risk of progression.

<!-- page 4 -->

### Other Imaging Notes:
- Standing lateral X-ray: Always use for grading.

- Flexion–extension views: Detect instability.

- MRI: Evaluate canal, nerve root compression.

- CT/SPECT: Detect pars defects or metabolic activity (fresh vs chronic lysis).

### CLINICAL FEATURES

| Symptom Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Asymptomatic | Often incidental finding |
| Mechanical pain | Pain with activity; due to instability |
| Instability catch | Painful catch while straightening from flexion |
| Radiculopathy | Nerve root compression → leg pain |
| Claudication | Seen in degenerative type |
| Cauda equina syndrome | Rare, severe cases |

Root Involvement:
- Degenerative: Compresses traversing root.
- Isthmic: Compresses exiting root.
- Common Levels:
- Degenerative – L4–L5
- Lytic/Dysplastic – L5–S1

MANAGEMENT
Conservative (Majority)
- NSAIDs (short course).

- Weight reduction.

- Lumbosacral brace.

- Physiotherapy: Flexion-based back strengthening.

<!-- page 5 -->

- Steroid/local anesthetic injection: Only in acute phase.

- Avoid extension exercises initially.
Most patients improve without surgery.

SURGICAL MANAGEMENT
Principles:
- Decompression for neural elements.

- Stabilization using instrumented fusion.

### Posterolateral Fusion (PLF)
- Bone graft placed between transverse processes after decortication.

- Simpler but limited foraminal decompression.
### Interbody Fusion

| Type | Approach | Key Point |
|---|---|---|
| PLIF | Midline posterior; dura retracted | High nerve traction risk |
| TLIF | Via foramen after facetectomy | Preferred; safer root handling |

XLIF / OLIF / Lateral or anterior Less muscle trauma, better

### MIS-TLIF (Minimally Invasive TLIF)
- Reduces muscle retraction injury.

- Maintains dorsolumbar fascia integrity.

- Comparable fusion rates with faster recovery.

HIGH-GRADE SPONDYLOLISTHESIS
Key Surgical Options: 1. In-situ fusion – no reduction; for balanced pelvis.
2. Partial reduction + fusion – for retroverted pelvis with balance.
3. Complete reduction + fusion – for unbalanced spine.

Spinopelvic Relationship in High-Grade Listhesis
Recommended

| Pelvic Status | Spine Status | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Normal pelvis | Balanced spine | In-situ fixation |
| Retroverted pelvis | Balanced spine | Partial reduction + fusion |
| Retroverted pelvis | Imbalanced spine | Complete reduction + fusion |

### Delta Fixation
- S1 screw angled into L5 body + additional L5 pedicle screws → triangular construct for stability.
### Bohlmann’s Technique
- Fibular strut graft from sacrum to L5 across defect under dura.

- Enhances stability and fusion in high-grade cases.
### Reduction Technique
- Insert screws at sacrum and proximal vertebra.

- Distraction maneuver reduces slip gradually.

- Follow with interbody fusion for stability.

### PARS DEFECT (SPONDYLOLYSIS)
- Seen in adolescents, often athletes (gymnasts, cricketers, swimmers).

- Caused by repetitive hyperextension → stress fracture at pars interarticularis.

- SPECT scan: Hot spot = acute; cold = chronic.

- MRI: T2 hyperintensity = edema = active lesion.
Management
- Acute: Rest + NSAIDs + gradual physiotherapy.

- Chronic: Core strengthening and flexibility training.

<!-- page 7 -->

- Persistent pain / nonunion: Pars repair.
### Pars Repair Techniques

| Technique | Description |
|---|---|
| Kimura (1968) | Cancellous bone graft + cast |
| Buck (1970) | Screw fixation + graft |
| Nicol & Scott (1986) | Tension band wiring |
| Morscher (1984) | Hook screw construct |
| Gillet (1999) | V-shaped rod-screw system |

### VIVA & EXAM PEARLS
- Commonest level: L5–S1 (lytic) > L4–L5 (degenerative).

- Degenerative: Traversing root compression.

- Lytic: Exiting root compression.

- Slip angle >55°: Predicts progression.

- Pelvic incidence: Constant; high PI → high risk of listhesis.

- Equation: PI = PT + SS.

- Balanced vs Retroverted pelvis: Guides reduction need.

- Instrumented PLF remains the gold standard.

- MIS-TLIF offers lower pain and muscle trauma.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Spondylolisthesis = vertebral slip; most due to lytic or degenerative causes.

- Sagittal and spinopelvic balance determine symptoms and surgical planning.

- PI = PT + SS is key to understanding posture and deformity.

- Low-grade: Decompression + instrumented fusion (PLF/TLIF).

- High-grade: Assess pelvic balance → choose reduction or in-situ fusion.

<!-- page 8 -->

- Pars repair indicated in young with persistent pain.

- Goal: Decompression, stability, and alignment restoration for long-term function.
