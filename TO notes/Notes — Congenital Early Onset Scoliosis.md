Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Congenital &
Early Onset Scoliosis
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition:
- Early Onset Scoliosis (EOS): Deformity developing before 10 years of age.
- Congenital Scoliosis: Results from defects of vertebral formation or
segmentation during embryogenesis (4–6 weeks gestation).
Dr. Vishnu’s Quote:
“Late-onset scoliosis changes appearance; early-onset scoliosis changes
physiology.”
Key Difference:
- Late-onset scoliosis → cosmetic deformity.
- Early-onset scoliosis → disease affecting lung development, cardiac function,
and growth.
Imaging for Assessment: - X-ray: Essential for diagnosis and follow-up.
- MRI: If neurological deficit or cutaneous markers present.
- CT: For bony anatomy and surgical planning in congenital scoliosis.

RADIOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT IN SCOLIOSIS
Essential Views: - Standing AP & lateral long cassette whole spine X-rays.
- Include occipitocervical junction to pelvis with both shoulders.
Special Views: - Supine bending views – assess flexibility.
- Traction / Fulcrum bending – for stiff or >100° curves.
Why Standing?
True curve magnitude and sagittal/coronal balance appear only in standing position due
to gravity.

Definition of Scoliosis:
Lateral spinal curvature >10° (Cobb’s angle).

MEASUREMENT TECHNIQUES
Cobb’s Angle
Angle between superior endplate of upper end vertebra and inferior endplate of lower
end vertebra.

### Curve Types:

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| Structural Curve | Side-bending Cobb’s >25° → rigid |
| Non-Structural | Side-bending Cobb’s <25° → flexible / |
| Curve | compensatory |

### Rotation & Tilt:
- Max tilt → at end vertebra.

- Max rotation → at apical vertebra.

- Critical period: Birth–8 years (max thoracic & alveolar growth).

MANAGEMENT OVERVIEW
Observation (<25°)
- For mild deformity and post-peak skeletal growth.

- Serial X-rays every 6 months.

- Indicated if: Incarcerated or non-segmented hemivertebra with no documented progression.
Bracing (25°–45°)
- For flexible curves in skeletally immature patients (Risser 0–2).

- Goal: Stop progression (not correct deformity).

- Duration: 16–23 hrs/day until skeletal maturity.

- Weaning: Over 6 months post-Risser 4–5.

### SURGICAL INDICATIONS (AIS/EOS)

| Indication | Approach |
|---|---|
| Curve >45° | Posterior spinal fusion (PSF) – most common |
| Thoracolumbar/Lumbar | Anterior fusion (ASF) – saves segments but ↑ morbidity |
| Rigid/Severe deformity | Combined (A+P) fusion |

### Correction Techniques

| Technique | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Posterior Release (Ponte’s | Improves flexibility by removing |
| Osteotomy) | facet joints |
| Cantilever Technique | Applies lever arm correction using rods |
| Rod Rotation Method | Converts coronal to sagittal alignment for 3D correction |

### Fusion Levels
- Upper Instrumented Vertebra (UIV): Based on shoulder level:
  - Right shoulder high → T4

  - Equal shoulders → T3

  - Left shoulder high → T2

- Lower Instrumented Vertebra (LIV): Stable vertebra bisected by CSVL.

| Type | Monitored Pathway | Key Note |
|---|---|---|
| SSEP | Sensory tracts | Continuous monitoring; drop >50% amplitude = warning |
| MEP | Motor tracts | Detects motor deficits intraoperatively; no muscle relaxant used |

If event occurs:
- Check BP, Hb, anesthesia depth.
- Reverse correction.
- Perform wake-up test.
- Remove implant if unstable.

### COMPLICATIONS
- Neurologic injury

- Pseudoarthrosis (1–2%)

- Infection (1–2%)

- Flat back syndrome

- Crankshaft phenomenon (in immature spines)

- Superior Mesenteric Artery (SMA) syndrome

- Hardware failure

- Junctional kyphosis

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Scoliosis = Cobb’s >10°.

- Primary vs secondary curve → compensation for balance.

- Structural if bending Cobb >25°, Non-structural if <25°.

- EOS affects lung & thoracic growth (restrictive pattern).

- Risser 0–2 = active growth → close follow-up.

- Observation → Bracing → Surgery = progressive treatment ladder.

- Posterior approach = mainstay of surgical correction.

- Always use neuromonitoring for safety.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- EOS <10 yrs → impacts thoracic & lung growth.

- Congenital scoliosis → vertebral formation/segmentation defects.

- Measure and classify using Cobb’s, apical, end, neutral, and stable vertebrae.

- Observe mild, brace moderate, operate progressive curves.

- Posterior fusion with pedicle screws = gold standard for idiopathic deformities.

- In congenital cases, early detection prevents irreversible thoracic insufficiency.
