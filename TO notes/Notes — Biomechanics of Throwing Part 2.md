Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Biomechanics
of Throwing (Part 2)
Faculty: Dr. Shikha Dhaundiyal

OVERVIEW
Topic: Detailed analysis of the phases of throwing – from preparation to
follow-through, including biomechanics, muscle activation, and injury relevance.
Dr. Shikha’s Quote: “Every throw is a kinetic story — from the ground to
the fingertips, every link matters.”

### ⏱ DURATION & PHASE DISTRIBUTION
- Total Throwing Cycle: ~2 seconds.
- Wind-Up & Acceleration: ~75% of the total duration (≈1.5 s).
- Six Phases:
  1. Wind-Up / Preparation
  2. Stride
  3. Cocking (Early & Late)
  4. Acceleration
  5. Deceleration
  6. Follow-Through

1⃣ WIND-UP PHASE
Definition: Initial movement → maximum knee lift of the stride leg.

Purpose:
1. Establish rhythm & timing.

2. Conceal the ball to distract the batter.

3. Position the body for propulsion.
Biomechanics:
- Weight shifts to stance leg (pivot leg) → COG stays back.

- Stability by hip abductors, adductors, extensors (stance leg).

- Generates base for kinetic chain & momentum buildup.

♂ 2⃣ STRIDE PHASE
Definition: From maximum knee height → stride foot contact.
- Stride angle: ≈15° outward from midline.

- Stride foot distance: ~10 cm from midline.
Force Implication:
- +3.0 N anterior force for every 1 cm increase in stride distance.
- +2.1 N anterior force per degree increase in foot angle.

Role:
- Linear + angular momentum generation.

- Hip & knee extensors (stance leg) initiate pelvic rotation (400–700°/s).

- Abdominal obliques prevent lumbar hyperextension.
Stride Leg: External rotation & abduction to align toward target.

3⃣ ARM COCKING PHASE
Begins: Lead foot contact.
Ends: Maximum External Rotation (MER) of shoulder.

Kinetics:
- ~80% of total kinetic energy transferred from lower limbs & trunk.

<!-- page 3 -->

- Two sub-phases:
  1. Early Cocking (Stride Phase)
  2. Late Cocking (MER Phase)

EARLY COCKING PHASE
Key Movements: - Shoulder at 90° abduction and 50° external rotation (semi-cocked
position).
- Scapula: Protracted & upwardly rotated by serratus anterior & upper trapezius.

LATE COCKING PHASE
Definition: From foot contact → MER (Maximum External Rotation).
Movements: - Scapula retracts & tilts upward (rhomboids, levator scapulae, trapezius).
- Humerus: Abduction 90–95°, External Rotation 165–175°. - Subtle internal rotation
(10–20°) via subscapularis (eccentric control).

4⃣ ACCELERATION PHASE
Definition: From MER → Ball Release.
Duration: ~42–58 milliseconds.
Movements: - Shoulder: Violent internal rotation (7000–9000°/s). - Elbow: 120°
flexion → ~25° extension at ball release.
- Scapula: Protraction & anterior tilt (serratus anterior).
- Trunk: Rapid forward flexion (32–55°) aiding kinetic transfer.
Muscle Activation: | Muscle | Function | |———|———–| | Subscapularis | Max activity
– humeral internal rotation | | Pectoralis major & Latissimus dorsi | Powerful concentric
IR | | Serratus anterior | Max activity for scapular protraction | | Triceps | Assists
extension (with centrifugal torso force) | | Rectus abdominis, Obliques, Lumbar
paraspinals | Accentuate trunk rotation & tilt |
Injury Risk: - UCL injury & Valgus Hyperextension Overload Syndrome – due to
valgus-varus torque mismatch.
- Medial elbow pain, chondromalacia, posterior impingement.
“Acceleration is the body’s explosion — milliseconds of power, but high
injury cost.”

5⃣ DECELERATION PHASE
Definition: From ball release → maximum internal rotation.
Forces: - Posterior shear: ~400 N.
- Inferior shear: ~300 N.
- Compressive: >1000 N.

6⃣ FOLLOW-THROUGH PHASE
Definition: From completion of internal rotation → cessation of motion.
- Body continues forward, arm slows gradually.

- Horizontal adduction: ↑ up to 60°.

- Joint load & muscle activity ↓ progressively.
Muscle Activation: | Muscle | Function | |———|———–| | Trunk extensors |
Concentric action → bring body upright | | Hip flexors (pivot leg) | Move leg forward,
prepare for fielding position |
“Follow-through saves the shoulder — it’s the body’s way of slowing
chaos.”

### KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Throwing involves 6 integrated phases, each contributing to energy transfer.
- Wind-up → Cocking: Energy buildup.

- Acceleration: Explosive release.

- Deceleration → Follow-through: Energy dissipation.

- Common injury sites: UCL, posterior capsule, RC tendons.
- Prevention = Strength + Control + Sequencing. “Efficiency is not just about speed — it’s about preserving the chain.”
