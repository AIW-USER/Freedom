Management
Faculty: Dr. C S Vishnu Prasath

### OVERVIEW
- Subaxial Cervical Spine includes C3–C7.
- Motion:
  - Majority of flexion–extension occurs between C3–C7.
  - Lateral bending and ~50% of rotation occur at these levels.
- Three-Step Approach:
  1. Identify if there’s a cervical injury (clinical + imaging).
  2. Differentiate stable vs. unstable.
  3. If unstable → plan surgery (anterior/posterior/combined).
“Never accept a cervical X-ray that doesn’t show C7–T1 junction.” – Dr. C S
Vishnu Prasath

RADIOGRAPHIC EVALUATION
### Basic Principles
- Always visualize C1 to C7–T1 junction. If not → order CT scan.
- Four Alignment Lines:
  1. Anterior vertebral line
  2. Posterior vertebral line
  3. Spinolaminar line
  4. Posterior spinous process line → Interruption = subluxation/dislocation.
- Prevertebral Soft Tissue:
  - C2: ≤ 7 mm
  - C6–C7: ≤ 20 mm
  - Thickening → suspect hematoma or ligamentous injury.
### CT & MRI Evaluation
- CT: Sequential review (right parasagittal → midsagittal → left parasagittal) to avoid missing unifacetal dislocations.
- MRI: For ligamentous or cord injury; start from parasagittal slices.

CLASSIFICATION SYSTEMS
### Allen & Ferguson (1982)
- Based on mechanism: compression, flexion, extension, distraction.
- Theoretical value only – does not guide treatment.
SLIC – Subaxial Cervical Injury Classification (Vaccaro et al., 2007)
- Mechanism-based systems replaced by morphology + neurology + DLC integrity.
- 3 Components:
  1. Injury Morphology
  2. Discoligamentous Complex (DLC) Integrity
  3. Neurological Status

### SURGICAL PRINCIPLES
- Stable 1-column injury → Conservative (collar/halo).
- 2-column (anterior + posterior) injury → Unstable → Surgery.
- Approach:
  - Anterior (ACDF/Corpectomy): For burst/compression with intact facets.
  - Posterior fixation: For posterior element failure.
  - Combined A+P: If both columns fail.
Tip: Facets intact → Anterior only; facets broken → Add posterior fixation.

### UNILATERAL FACET DISLOCATION
- Mechanism: Flexion–rotation.
- Features: Painful neck, 70% radiculopathy, 10% SCI.
- Radiological Sign: “Bow-tie sign” – both facets visible, not overlapping.
- Treatment:
  - Reduction (flex–rotate).
  - If reducible → Anterior discectomy + plating.
  - If irreducible → Posterior reduction + fixation.
  - Non-op: Halo/Cervicothoracic brace × 12 weeks.

PHARMACOLOGICAL MANAGEMENT
### Steroids (Methylprednisolone – NASCIS Trials)

| Trial | Key Finding | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| NASCIS I (1984) | High vs low dose → no difference | Not beneficial |
| NASCIS II (1990) | <3 hr benefit (post-hoc only) | Controversial |
| NASCIS III (1997) | No definitive outcome | High complication risk |

### Current Consensus:
- Not standard of care.
- Consider optional use within 8 hrs (Dr. Fehlings: “use as an option, not mandate”).
- Risks: infection, steroid myopathy, pneumonia.
Other studied drugs: Riluzole, Erythropoietin, Nimodipine, Minocycline, GM-1
ganglioside, etc. (experimental).

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- C2: 7 mm, C7: 20 mm prevertebral thickness rule.
- Four alignment lines = must check.
- Translation >3.5 mm = unstable.
- SLIC <4 → Conservative, >4 → Surgery.
- Central cord = commonest incomplete injury.
- Anterior cord = worst prognosis.
- Steroids: Not standard; optional within 8 hrs.
- Never hyperextend neck in intubation (cord buckling risk in myelopathy).
