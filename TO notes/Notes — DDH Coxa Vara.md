Developmental Dysplasia of Hip (DDH)
Definition
- DDH is a spectrum of abnormal hip development, ranging from acetabular dysplasia to subluxation and frank dislocation.
- Preferred term is Developmental Dysplasia (not congenital), as pathology may evolve after birth.

### Spectrum of DDH
- Simple acetabular dysplasia
- Subluxation
- Frank dislocation
- Teratological dysplasia (associated with syndromes like arthrogryposis, resistant to conservative treatment)

### Etiology – “Faulty 5 Fs”
- Female child (5–9× more common)
- First born
- Fair complexion
- Fetal malpresentation (especially extended breech)
- Family history DDH is idiopathic, likely multifactorial, with genetic and packaging disorder theories.

Epidemiology
- Incidence: ~1 per 1000 live births
- Side involved: Left > Bilateral > Right

### Associated Conditions
- Congenital muscular torticollis (≈20%)
- Metatarsus adductus
- CTEV
- Congenital dislocation of knee

### Pathological Changes
- Small femoral head with delayed ossification
- Shallow, flat acetabulum

- Coxa valga
- Increased femoral anteversion
- Hypertrophied ligamentum teres and transverse acetabular ligament
- Limbus (inverted hypertrophied labrum)
- Neolimbus (hypertrophied posterolateral acetabular cartilage)
- Pulvinar (fatty tissue filling acetabulum)

Clinical Features
Non-walking child
- Asymmetrical groin folds (not sensitive)
- Limb length discrepancy
Walking child
- Painless limp
- Trendelenburg lurch
### Examination Findings
- Wide perineum
- Galeazzi (Allis) sign
- Supratrochanteric shortening
- Restricted abduction and internal rotation (most sensitive sign)
- Telescopy
- Exaggerated lumbar lordosis
- Vascular sign of Narath (absent femoral pulse)

Klisic Sign (Most reliable in bilateral DDH)
- Line joining ASIS to greater trochanter passes below umbilicus

Radiological Evaluation
X-ray (after 6 months)
- Von Rosen view (hips abducted and internally rotated)
- Hilgenreiner’s line
- Perkin’s line
- Dislocated head lies in upper outer quadrant
- Acetabular Index
  - Normal < 20°
  - 30° suggests dysplasia
- Center Edge Angle of Wiberg (decreases with subluxation) If femoral head ossification center not visible, use proximal femoral metaphyseal beak.

<!-- page 3 -->

Ultrasonography (Below 6 months)
- Graf classification
- Alpha angle: bony roof
- Beta angle: cartilaginous roof
- ↓ Alpha or ↑ Beta = poor prognosis

### MRI Classification
- Kashiwagi classification (based on acetabular morphology)

### Screening for DDH
- Clinical examination is the gold standard
- Screening tests (0–3 months):
  - Barlow test (dislocation)
  - Ortolani test (reduction)
Reason for clunk: Neolimbus, not labrum.

Management of DDH (Age-based)
< 6 months
- Pavlik harness / Abduction orthosis
- Continue 6 weeks after hip becomes stable
- If difficult reduction: higher abduction + serial imaging

6–18 months
- Trial of Closed Reduction under anesthesia
- Hip spica in human position
- Assess Ramsey Safe Zone
  - Narrow zone (<20°): Add adductor tenotomy
- CT scan for confirmation

18 months – 3 years
- Closed reduction contraindicated
- Open reduction
- Add femoral shortening (>2 years) to prevent AVN
- Assess stability intra-operatively

<!-- page 4 -->

- Flexion instability → Acetabular osteotomy
  - Abduction + IR instability → VDRO

3–10 years
- Open reduction + bony procedure mandatory
- < 4 years: VDRO preferred
- 4 years: Acetabular osteotomy preferred

> 10 years
- Limited role of reconstructive surgery
- Natural history often better
- Painful DDH: Tectoplasty (salvage)

### Summary Table

| Age | Treatment |
|---|---|
| < 6 months | Abduction orthosis |
| 6–18 months | Closed reduction + spica |
| 18 months–3 years | Open reduction ± femoral shortening |
| 3–10 years | Open reduction + osteotomy |
| > 10 years | Observation / Salvage |

Coxa Vara
Definition
- Decrease in neck shaft angle due to developmental growth plate defect

### Developmental Coxa Vara
- Characterized by Fairbank’s triangle (osteoid defect)
- Neck shaft angle is unreliable due to physeal slip

### Hilgenreiner Epiphyseal (HE) Angle
- Angle between Hilgenreiner’s line and femoral physeal line
- Normal: ~45°
- > 60° indicates surgery

<!-- page 5 -->

Pathophysiology
- Progressive vertical orientation of femoral physis
- Leads to increasing varus with growth

### Clinical Features
- Painless limp
- Limb length discrepancy
- Trendelenburg gait

Treatment Principles (3 goals)
1. Correct HE angle
2. Distalize greater trochanter
3. Prevent recurrence via epiphysiodesis

### Powell’s Osteotomy (for Coxa Vara)
- High valgus osteotomy
- Fixation crosses trochanteric physis
- Converts vertical physis to horizontal
- Leads to compression and fusion
Wedge angle = HE angle – 16°

### Other Osteotomies
- Mosher’s osteotomy (multiple cuts)

### Important Differentials of DDH
- Developmental Coxa Vara
- Proximal Femoral Focal Deficiency (PFFD)

### Exam Pearls
- DDH is acetabular dysplasia primarily
- Best screening test: Clinical examination
- Clunk in Ortolani/Barlow due to Neolimbus
- Acetabular index quantifies dysplasia
- HE angle, not neck shaft angle, defines Coxa Vara
- Surgery for Coxa Vara when HE angle > 60°
