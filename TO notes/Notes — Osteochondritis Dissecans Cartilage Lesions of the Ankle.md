Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Osteochondritis Dissecans & Cartilage
Lesions of the Ankle (Osteochondral Lesions of the Talus)
Faculty: Dr. Rahul Upadhyay

### OVERVIEW
- Osteochondral Lesions of the Talus (OLT) involve erosion, fracture, or separation of the articular cartilage of the talus with or without subchondral bone involvement.
- Represent ankle counterparts of Osteochondritis Dissecans (OCD) seen in the knee.
- Common in young, active individuals; frequently post-traumatic or related to chronic microinstability.
- Koenig (1888): Described OCD of the knee.
- Kappis (1922): Applied term to ankle.
- Berndt & Harty (1959): Introduced term Transchondral Fractures and proposed radiographic classification. “When you miss a talar OCD, you miss the chance to stop an ankle from aging too soon.” – Dr. Rahul Upadhyay

### ETIOLOGY / CAUSES

| Cause | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Acute Trauma | Direct impact → osteochondral fracture (85% of cases). |
| Chronic Microtrauma | Repeated inversion or instability (athletes). |

### Zonal Distribution (Berndt & Harty)
- Zone 4 (centromedial) – Most common (65–70%).
- Zone 6 (centrolateral) – Second most common. Exam Tip: If Zone 4 not an option → answer Zone 6 as next most common.

### PATHOGENESIS
- Initial trauma → subchondral ischemia/necrosis → softening → separation → loose fragment.
- Acute trauma → distinct fracture; chronic instability → progressive cartilage softening & cystic change.
- Malalignment or instability exacerbates chronic lesions and kissing/bipolar defects.

### CLINICAL FEATURES
- Chronic ankle pain, instability, or locking/clicking.
- Persistent swelling, recurrent effusion, or delayed recovery post-“sprain.”
- High index of suspicion essential:
  - 4% of all OCDs are missed.
  - 50% of acute ankle sprains have an associated OLT.
Mnemonic: Pain + Effusion + Locking = Think OLT.

### Operative Indications
- Symptomatic displaced lesions.
- Stage 3–4 acute lesions → Early surgery.
- Stage 2–3 nondisplaced in children → Immobilization.
- Large or cystic lesions (>1.5 cm²) → Surgery. Brace of choice: PTB brace – avoids axial loading on talus.

SURGICAL OPTIONS
### 1⃣ Arthroscopic Debridement / Microfracture
- For small, stable lesions (<1.5 cm²).
- Stimulates fibrocartilage repair.
### 2⃣ Retrograde Drilling / Bone Grafting
- For intact cartilage cap with subchondral cyst.
- Minimally invasive, avoids malleolar osteotomy.
### 3⃣ Percutaneous Arthroscopic Drilling
- Indications: Early lesions, mild osteosclerosis, intact cartilage, stable fragment.
- Advantage: Avoids medial malleolus osteotomy.
### 4⃣ Osteochondral Autograft Transfer (OATS)
- For defects >5 mm; single bone plug harvested (usually from femoral condyle).
- Restores hyaline cartilage surface.
### 5⃣ Mosaicplasty
- Multiple small osteochondral plugs for irregular large lesions.
### 6⃣ Mega-OATS / Allograft Reconstruction
- >12 mm defects, use of fresh talar allograft within 7 days of harvest.
- Technically demanding, costly.
### 7⃣ ACI (Autologous Chondrocyte Implantation)
- Two-stage procedure: harvest → in-vitro culture (2–5 weeks) → reimplantation under periosteal flap.
- Indications: Stage 3–4, large contained lesions, failed prior surgery.
- Contraindications: Malalignment, arthritis, instability, kissing lesions.
- Ideal age: 15–55 years.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Most common site: Medial talar dome (Zone 4).
- Mechanism: Inversion + plantarflexion (medial); inversion + dorsiflexion (lateral).
- Shape: Medial – deep/cup; Lateral – shallow/wafer.
- CT cut thickness: 2 mm.
- Stage 3–4 → Surgery.
- Best brace: PTB brace.
- Early lesion: Percutaneous drilling.
- >5 mm defect: OATS.
- >12 mm defect: Mega-OATS / Allograft.
- Large contained lesion: ACI.
- Preferred approach: Arthroscopic (minimal morbidity).
- Future: MACI, Hyalograft, stem cells.
