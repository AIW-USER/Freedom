Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – OA Knee: Conservative Options,
Viscosupplementation & Orthobiologics
Faculty: Dr. Rahul Upadhyay

Introduction
This lecture focuses on non-operative management of osteoarthritis (OA) knee,
emphasizing practical, exam-relevant aspects for DNB/FNB candidates. Conservative
options include physiotherapy, intra-articular injections, and emerging orthobiologic
therapies such as PRP, BMAC, and adipose-derived stem cells.

### Conservative Management Overview
1. Physiotherapy and Lifestyle Modification
- Quadriceps strengthening (static & dynamic)
- Hamstring conditioning exercises
- Avoid activities that overload the knee (e.g., squatting, climbing stairs)
- Weight reduction – proven to reduce joint load and symptoms.
2. Intra-articular Corticosteroids
- Still considered the gold standard for early pain relief.
- Mechanism: reduces inflammation but does not regenerate or degrade cartilage.
- Effect duration: short-term (4–6 weeks) relief of pain & swelling.
- Frequency: Up to 3 injections in 6 months (early OA, up to Kellgren–Lawrence Grade 3A).
- AAOS Guidelines: (2013) – inconclusive recommendation for or against; short-term efficacy only. Key Point: Steroids are best reserved as adjuncts in early OA for pain control – not as disease-modifying therapy.

Viscosupplementation (Hyaluronic Acid Therapy)
Definition: Intra-articular injection of hyaluronic acid (HA) to restore synovial fluid
viscosity and elasticity, providing lubrication and possible anti-inflammatory benefits.
### Characteristics of Hyaluronic Acid (HA)
- Anionic, non-sulfated glycosaminoglycan found in connective, epithelial, and neural tissue.
- Synthesized by plasma membrane enzymes.

- Major component of extracellular matrix, supporting cell proliferation and migration.
- Provides viscoelastic properties essential for joint lubrication.

### Mechanism of Action
- Viscoinduction: Stimulates chondrocytes & synoviocytes to produce native HA.
- Chondroprotection: Prevents cartilage breakdown.
- Viscosupplementation: Provides lubrication & mechanical cushioning.
- Molecular Interaction: HA binds to CD44 receptors, inhibiting IL-1β & MMP production → ↓ catabolic activity → protects cartilage.
### Clinical Use & Recommendations
- 2013 AAOS: Strong recommendation against use in OA knee (due to low-quality evidence).
- 2015 Campbell et al.: Found HA to be most effective among NSAIDs, corticosteroids, PRP, and placebo for early OA – benefits lasting 26–32 weeks.
- Indicated in:
  - Early–moderate OA (KL grade II–III)
  - Patients unresponsive to other therapies or contraindicated for surgery
### Adverse Effects & Safety
- No major adverse effects; minor arthralgia or effusion (0–14% cases)
- Repeat injections safe with no increase in adverse reactions

Orthobiologics in OA Knee
Definition (AAOS): Biologic substances naturally found in the body that enhance repair
or regeneration of bone, cartilage, tendon, or ligament.
Examples:
- Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP)
- Autologous Conditioned Serum (ACS)
- Bone Marrow Aspirate Concentrate (BMAC)
- Adipose-derived stem cells
- Mesenchymal-derived & amniotic concentrates
- Cord blood concentrates
- Prolotherapy, Ozone therapy Key Principle: All aim to reduce inflammation, stimulate cartilage regeneration, or delay progression.

Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP)
### Mechanism of Action:
- Supra-physiological platelet concentration → releases cytokines & growth factors.
- Enhances chemotaxis of chondrocytes & MSCs → promotes proteoglycan synthesis.
- Modifies gene expression → inhibits MMP-13 & NF-κB → reduces inflammation.
### Processing Steps
1. Draw venous blood.
2. Differential centrifugation → separates into:
  - Top layer: Platelet-rich plasma (+ few WBCs)
  - Middle (buffy coat): WBC-rich
  - Bottom: RBCs
3. Platelet concentration: 200–1000 × 10³/µL (no fixed ideal range).

### Types of PRP
- Leukocyte-poor (LP-PRP): Yellow; better functional outcomes & fewer immunogenic reactions.
- Leukocyte-rich (LR-PRP): Red; higher inflammation & side effects.
### Clinical Evidence
- Meta-analyses: PRP improves function more than HA or placebo, especially in early OA.
- Chang et al. (2014): PRP > HA (pain & function improvement).
- Laudy et al. (2015): PRP superior to HA/placebo in mild OA.
- Mayock et al. (2016): PRP better at 12 months but not at 6 months.
- AAOS (2013): Recommendation inconclusive – insufficient evidence for or against PRP use. Summary: PRP may delay surgery in early OA but is not disease-modifying. LP-PRP preferred.

Bone Marrow Aspirate Concentrate (BMAC)
Definition: Concentrated autologous bone marrow aspirate rich in progenitor & stem
cells.
- Harvest Sites: Iliac crest (best), distal femur, proximal tibia, humerus, calcaneus.
- Processing: Centrifugation → isolate marrow concentrate (similar to PRP preparation).
### Clinical Role:
- Early trials: pain & function improved with minimal adverse effects.
- Shapiro et al.: No statistically significant difference between BMAC & saline at 12 months.
- Consensus: Promising, safe, but requires randomized controlled trials.

Adipose-Derived Mesenchymal Stem Cells (ADMSCs)
Rationale: Minimally manipulated stem cells; abundant progenitor population.
### Harvesting & Isolation:
- Via lipoaspiration (abdomen/flank/buttock) or arthroscopically from infrapatellar fat pad.
- Isolation of stromal vascular fraction (SVF) – rich in progenitor cells.
### Processing Techniques:
- Microfragmentation & cleansing

- Vibrational energy extraction
- Enzymatic digestion → differential centrifugation → SVF isolation
### Clinical Role:
- Phase I trials → excellent safety, mild local adverse effects.
- No FDA approval yet; no randomized placebo-controlled trials.

### Key Takeaways for Exams
- HA: Anionic non-sulfated glycosaminoglycan; improves viscoelasticity.
- PRP: LP-PRP superior; acts via cytokines & MMP inhibition.
- BMAC: Iliac crest → best harvest site.
- Adipose cells: High progenitor yield; safe but unapproved.
- Steroids: Max 3 injections in 6 months; early OA only.
- Duration of HA effect: 24–26 weeks.
- Main HA mechanism: Viscoinduction, Chondroprotection, Visco-supplementation.

Conclusion
- The role of orthobiologics and viscosupplementation in OA knee is evolving but still debated.
- They are safe and effective for early-to-moderate OA, especially when standard conservative measures fail.
- Long-term disease-modifying benefits remain unproven.
- For exams: focus on mechanisms, indications, contraindications, and latest AAOS recommendations.
