Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Total Ankle Arthroplasty (TAR)
Faculty: Dr. Rahul Upadhyay

OVERVIEW
Total Ankle Arthroplasty (TAR) involves replacing the ankle joint with an artificial
implant to treat end-stage ankle arthritis, serving as an alternative to ankle
arthrodesis (fusion). The goal is to relieve pain, restore alignment, and preserve
motion, especially dorsiflexion–plantarflexion range, thus maintaining near-normal gait.
Quote: “TAR aims to achieve what arthrodesis cannot – motion with stability.”
– Dr. Rahul Upadhyay

INDICATIONS FOR TAR
### Primary Indications
- Degenerative arthritis (primary OA)
- Post-traumatic arthritis (most common in India)
- Rheumatoid arthritis with stable, well-aligned hindfoot

### Favorable Patient Factors
- Age: >50 years (middle-aged to elderly)
- Activity level: Low demand (e.g., walking, golf, cycling)
- Mobility: Reasonably mobile
- BMI: Normal or low (<25 kg/m²)
- Hindfoot alignment: Well-aligned & stable
- Soft tissue envelope: Good skin & vascularity; no prior major scars
- Bone stock: Adequate with no osteopenic risk
- ROM: Preserved (>10°–15°)

### Minor Criteria
- Soft tissues: Poor → Arthrodesis; Good → TAR
- BMI: >25 → Arthrodesis; <25 → TAR
- Diabetes: Uncontrolled → Arthrodesis; Controlled → TAR
- Compliance: Low → Arthrodesis; High → TAR
- AVN or infection: Avoid TAR If all parameters are borderline → Arthrodesis is safer.

CLASSIFICATION & DEVELOPMENT OF TAR
### Classification Factors
- Fixation: Cemented vs. Uncemented
- Number of Components: 2 vs. 3
- Constraint: Constrained / Semi-constrained / Unconstrained
- Congruency: Congruent vs. Incongruent
- Component Shape: Anatomic vs. Non-anatomic
- Bearing Type: Fixed vs. Mobile bearing

### PREOPERATIVE PLANNING
- Radiographs: Weight-bearing AP & lateral of ankle.
- CT Scan: Evaluate adjacent joint arthritis.
- MRI: Assess osteonecrosis & ligament integrity.
- Neurovascular work-up: If neuropathy or vascular compromise suspected. Use Iowa ROM device or precise goniometric measurement to record pre-op motion (<10° → arthrodesis candidate).

Ensure neutral alignment; confirm cuts via fluoroscopy.

### TECHNICAL GOALS
- Restore mechanical alignment of tibia–talus axis.
- Achieve balanced ligamentous tension (especially deltoid).
- Create a stable, neutrally aligned, plantigrade, weight-bearing ankle.
- May require:
  - Ligament reconstruction (for instability)
  - Osteotomies (for malalignment)

- Tendon transfers / Achilles lengthening
Failure to achieve balance → high risk of early loosening or instability.

### CLINICAL OUTCOMES
- Review (1105 cases): 90% survivorship at 5 years (range 68–100%).
- No clear superiority between implant designs.
- 3-component systems show better kinematics but similar clinical results.
- Revision rate: Higher than THR/TKR due to smaller bone stock & thin soft-tissue envelope. Clinical success = Pain-free, mobile, plantigrade ankle with ≥15° ROM.

### EXAM PEARLS & TAKEAWAYS
- TAR = motion-preserving alternative to arthrodesis for select elderly patients.
- Ideal candidate: >50 yrs, low-demand, BMI <25, stable hindfoot, good soft tissue.
- Cutoff: <10° ROM → Arthrodesis; >15° → TAR.
- Rheumatoid arthritis with stable hindfoot → best indication.

<!-- page 5 -->

- Post-traumatic arthritis in young → Arthrodesis preferred.
- Most common complication: Wound problems & loosening.
- Implant choice: 3-component uncemented, mobile-bearing systems.
- If in doubt: Choose arthrodesis.
