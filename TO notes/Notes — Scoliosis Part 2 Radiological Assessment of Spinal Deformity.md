2: Radiological Assessment of Spinal Deformity
(2025)
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition (SRS):
Scoliosis is defined as a lateral curvature of the spine >10° (Cobb’s angle) on a
standing radiograph.
Dr. Vishnu’s Quote:
“A good scoliosis surgeon must first be a good radiologist — because X-rays
tell the true story of every curve.”
Imaging Modalities: 1. X-ray (AP, Lateral, Stress views) 2. CT Scan 3. MRI
Purpose:
To identify the type, magnitude, flexibility, balance, and etiology of the spinal
deformity before planning management.

RADIOGRAPHIC EVALUATION
1⃣ Whole Spine X-Ray
Views Required: - Standing AP view (Coronal plane) - Standing Lateral view
(Sagittal plane) - Stress views: - Supine bending views (right & left) - Traction view -
Fulcrum bending view
Why Standing?
Only in standing position is the true nature of the curve (severity, side, and balance)
appreciated. Supine views often underestimate deformity magnitude.

### BASIC RADIOLOGICAL TERMS

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Primary Curve | First curve to develop — rigid, larger magnitude. |
| Secondary Curve | Compensatory curve above/below primary; usually flexible. |

Curve Description Format:
e.g. Right thoracic curve with apex at T11 extending from T7–L2.

COBB’S ANGLE MEASUREMENT
Steps: 1. Identify upper and lower end vertebrae.
2. Draw tangents to superior and inferior endplates.
3. Measure the angle between the perpendiculars of these tangents.

| Range | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| <10° | Not scoliosis |
| 10°–25° | Mild (Observe) |
| 25°–45° | Moderate (Brace) |
| >45° | Severe (Surgery) |

SRS Definition: Lateral curvature >10°.

BALANCE ASSESSMENT
### Coronal Balance
- C7 plumb line – drawn vertically from C7 body center.

- CSVL (Central Sacral Vertical Line) – drawn perpendicular to PSIS line.

- Imbalance: >2 cm deviation between these two lines.
### Sagittal Balance
- Measured in lateral view.

- C7 plumb line should pass through posterosuperior corner of S1.

- Positive sagittal imbalance: Plumb line falls anterior to S1 (>2 cm).

- Negative imbalance: Plumb line falls posteriorly.

STAGNARA VIEW
Definition: Oblique X-ray view taken to assess vertebral rotation and 3D deformity,
especially when CT/MRI is unavailable.
Use: Historical; replaced by modern cross-sectional imaging, but helpful in severe
deformities (>100°) where AP view distorts curve appearance.

### 2⃣ Congenital Kyphosis
- Failure of anterior formation or segmentation.

- Produces sharp angular kyphotic deformity, often thoracolumbar.

- Progresses rapidly → requires close monitoring or surgical correction.
### 3⃣ Scheuermann’s Kyphosis
- Anterior wedging >5° in ≥3 consecutive vertebrae.

<!-- page 5 -->

- Common in adolescents.

- Managed conservatively unless >75° or symptomatic.

CROSS-SECTIONAL IMAGING
CT Scan
Indications: - Complex congenital anomalies (e.g., hemivertebra, bar).
- Pedicle morphology assessment (for fixation).
- Bony septum in diastematomyelia (split cord).
- Preoperative planning.
Advantages: Excellent 3D visualization of bone; defines fusion levels.

MRI
Indications: - Neurological deficit or absent reflexes.
- Neurocutaneous markers (e.g., hairy patch, lipoma).
- Rapid curve progression or severe pain.
- Preoperative screening for cord anomalies.
Common Findings: - Tethered cord (cord ending below L2).
- Arnold–Chiari Malformation (ACM) with syrinx.
- Neurofibromatosis: Dystrophic pedicles, neural ectasia, paraspinal masses.

### KEY RADIOLOGICAL FINDINGS SUMMARY
- Always take standing whole-spine AP & lateral X-rays.

- Assess apex, end, neutral, stable vertebrae.

<!-- page 6 -->

- Determine Cobb’s angle and curve flexibility.

- Use Risser grading and triradiate cartilage to assess growth potential.

- MRI mandatory before surgery in painful, progressive, or atypical curves.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Scoliosis = lateral curvature >10° (SRS).

- Structural curve ≥25° on bending.

- Lenke classification = 6 types based on curve pattern.

- Coronal imbalance = C7–CSVL >2 cm.

- Sagittal imbalance = C7 plumb line anterior to S1.

- CT → bony detail; MRI → cord & soft tissue.

- Unilateral bar + contralateral hemivertebra = worst prognosis.

- Scheuermann’s disease: anterior wedging of ≥3 vertebrae.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- X-ray is the foundation of scoliosis diagnosis; always full-length standing views.

- Understand and identify apex, end, neutral, and stable vertebrae for curve description.

- Use Cobb’s angle and bending films to assess flexibility.

- Evaluate balance (C7 plumb line vs CSVL).

- Assess growth markers (Risser, triradiate cartilage) to predict progression.

- CT and MRI are essential for complex, congenital, or neurological scoliosis.
