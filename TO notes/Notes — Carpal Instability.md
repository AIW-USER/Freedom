Carpal Instability – Dr. Amit Mittal
Overview
Carpal instability represents abnormal kinematics between carpal bones, resulting
from ligament injury, altered biomechanics, or fracture dislocations. Early recognition is
crucial because delayed treatment leads to chronic dysfunction and degenerative
arthritis.
Faculty Remark: “You may miss instability even on X-ray, but I can diagnose it
clinically. You must learn to see it.” turn37file0

Wrist Ligament Anatomy
Extrinsic Ligaments
Connect forearm bones (radius, ulna) to carpus. - Stiffer and lower yield strength. -
Usually rupture through midsubstance. - Mostly on volar side. No dorsal ulnocarpal
ligaments. turn37file1
Key Extrinsic Ligaments: - Radial side (volar): Radioscaphoid, Radioscaphocapitate,
Long radiolunate. - Medial radial: Short radiolunate. - Ulnar/TFCC side: Ulnolunate,
Ulnotriquetral. - Ulnar styloid: Ulnocapitate. turn37file1

Intrinsic Ligaments
Connect carpal bones to each other. - Usually avulse, not rupture. Examples: SL
(scapholunate), LT (lunotriquetral). turn37file1
Important Intrinsic Ligaments: - SL ligament – dorsal strongest. - LT ligament –
palmar strongest. - Scaphocapitate, triquetrocapitate (cross midcarpal joint).
turn37file1

Ring Concept (Page 14)
Shows interdependent stability between proximal and distal rows. Failure at one point
disrupts the ring → instability patterns like VISI or DISI.

Mechanism of Instability
Radiocarpal Instability
Failure of extrinsic volar ligaments → proximal row translates volarly.

<!-- page 2 -->

Midcarpal Instability
Failure of midcarpal stabilizers → capitate translation causing VISI.

Proximal Row Instability
SL or LT ligament injury → dissociative instability.

Distal Row Instability
Occurs only as axial/longitudinal instability, because distal row acts as a fixed unit.
turn37file0

### Radiology: Key Assessment
1. Standard Views
- PA/AP in neutral.
- Lateral view.
- Ensure correct neutral position by clear 3rd CMC joint space. turn37file1
2. Special Views
- Clenched fist view – stress load reveals SL gap (Terry Thomas sign).
- PA with 10° angulation from ulna – measures mid-SL interval.
- Radial/ulnar deviation views – assesses scaphoid flexion/extension.
- Flexion/extension lateral – lunate tilt evaluation.
- Stress views – volar/dorsal drawer to detect midcarpal instability. turn37file1
3. Lunate Shapes
- DISI → piece-of-pie sign on PA.
- VISI → C-shaped / moon-like lunate.
- Mild palmar tilt → isosceles appearance. turn37file1
4. Lesser vs Greater Arc Injuries
- Lesser arc → pure ligamentous.
- Greater arc → fractures of scaphoid, capitate, radial styloid. turn37file1

### Six Features of Carpal Instability (Core Concept)
1. Chronicity: Acute (0–1 week), Subacute (1–6 weeks), Chronic (>6 weeks).
2. Etiology: Traumatic or inflammatory.
3. Location: Radiocarpal, Proximal row, Midcarpal, Distal intercarpal.

<!-- page 3 -->

4. Severity:
  - Predynamic – pain only, no malalignment.
  - Dynamic – malalignment on stress views.
  - Static – malalignment on plain X-rays.
5. Direction: DISI, VISI, dorsal, volar, radial or ulnar translation.
6. Pattern:
  - Dissociative → SL/LT injuries.
  - Non dissociative → midcarpal or radiocarpal instabilities.
  - Complex → both dissociation + dislocation (perilunate). turn37file0

### Clinical Patterns
1. Carpal Instability Dissociative (CID)
Injury within same row. - SLD is classic. - LTD, scaphoid fractures, Kienbock.

2. Carpal Instability Non-dissociative (CIND)
Failure between proximal and distal rows. - Radiocarpal instability. - Midcarpal
instability.

3. Carpal Instability Complex (CIC)
Combination of dissociation + dislocation. - Perilunate dislocation (lesser or greater arc).
- Axial dislocations. turn37file1

Case-Based Learning
Case 1 – Subacute Lesser Arc Injury
Findings: - Overlapping carpal bones. - Scaphoid ring sign, SL gap. - Lunate rotated
90°. - Capitate dorsal to lunate. turn37file1
Plan: - ORIF + ligament reconstruction preferred. - Dorsal approach for SL ligament
visualization. - Postop: Thumb spica 2 weeks, K-wire removal at 8 weeks.

Case 2 – Trans-scaphoid Trans-styloid Dorsal Perilunate Dislocation
Findings: - Scaphoid waist fracture. - Ulnar styloid fracture. - Capitate dorsal
displacement. turn37file1
Plan: - ORIF of scaphoid + fixation of perilunate. - Consider volar approach if palmar
comminution present.

Case 3 – Dorsal Perilunate Dislocation without Fracture
Findings: - Lunate moon-shaped. - Dorsal translation. - No bony fracture.

Plan: - Open reduction (not reducible closed). - Repair avulsed SL ligament with
anchor.

Case 4 – Combined Trans-styloid, SL Gap, DISI
Plan: - ORIF + ligament reconstruction. - Fix SL, LT, and scaphoid-capitate relationships
with K-wires. turn37file1

Treatment Principles
### Scapholunate Dissociation – Staging System
- Stage I (Occult) – Conservative.
- Stage II (Repairable) – SL repair + dorsal capsulodesis.
- Stage III (Dynamic DISI) – Three-ligament tenodesis.
- Stage IV (Reducible Static) – Tenodesis.
- Stage V (Irreducible) – Radiocarpal fusion + distal scaphoid excision.
- Stage VI (SLAC wrist) – Scaphoid excision + midcarpal fusion. turn37file1
### Modified Brunelli Technique (Key Steps)
- Harvest distally based 8 cm split FCR.
- Drill SL from dorsal to palmar.
- Pass tendon into scaphoid.
- Create transverse trough in lunate.
- Fix using suture anchors.
- Tension using RT ligament as pulley. turn37file1

Surgical Approaches
### Dorsal Approach
- Lazy-S incision centered on Lister’s tubercle.
- Expose compartments 2 and 4.
- Optional posterior interosseous nerve neurectomy.
- Best visualization of SL ligament.
### Volar Approach
- For palmar comminution or volar perilunate dislocations. turn37file1

<!-- page 5 -->

### Postoperative Protocol
- Thumb spica for 2 weeks.
- K-wire removal at 8 weeks.
- Detachable splint + ROM exercises after 8 weeks.
- Physiotherapy critical for outcome. turn37file1

### Exam Pearls
- Extrinsic ligaments rupture; intrinsic ligaments avulse.
- SL ligament: dorsal strongest. LT ligament: palmar strongest.
- DISI = SL injury; VISI = LT injury.
- Clenched fist view reveals SL instability.
- Lesser arc = ligamentous. Greater arc = fracture associated.
- Dorsal approach preferred for SL repair.
- Modified Brunelli = standard reconstruction for static SL instability.
- SL gap > 3 mm = pathologic.

Final Faculty Insight: “Diagnosis depends on understanding ligaments and motion.
X-rays help but your eyes and hands diagnose first.”
