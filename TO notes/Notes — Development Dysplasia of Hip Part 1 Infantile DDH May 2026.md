Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip Part 1: Infantile
DDH – Dr. Shalin Shah (May 2026)
### Overview / Key Concepts
- DDH is taught in two parts: non-walking (infantile) DDH here, walking-age DDH in Part 2. Presentation, treatment and goals differ.
- DDH = abnormal bony shape, so an unstable joint, so premature arthritis. It is preventable and fully treatable if detected in infancy.
- Incidence: about 1 in 100 at birth (newborn laxity, maternal relaxin) but about 1 in 1000 at 6 weeks, and it stays so afterwards. This is why screening ultrasound is done at 6 weeks, not at birth.
- A very obvious dislocated hip in a syndromic newborn (with clubfeet, knee contracture) is usually a TERATOLOGIC dislocation, not routine DDH.

### Indian DDH Screening Pathway
- Recommended jointly by the Paediatric Orthopaedic Society of India, Indian Orthopaedic Association and Indian Academy of Pediatrics (guidelines, not a funded government programme). Asked as a long question.
- Step 1: routine clinical examination at birth and at well-baby/vaccination visits. Positive Ortolani, positive Galeazzi/limb length difference, limited hip abduction, limp, or an uncertain examination = suspected DDH.
- Step 2 by age: under 6 weeks: schedule an ultrasound at 6 weeks; 6–14 weeks: ultrasound; over 14 weeks (or no quality ultrasound): AP pelvis X-ray.
- Abnormal imaging: refer to a (paediatric) orthopaedic surgeon for treatment.

### Clinical Examination

| Age | Key signs |
|---|---|
| Newborn | Ortolani and Barlow tests (gentle); telescoping |
| Over 3 months | LIMITED HIP ABDUCTION (most sensitive sign), apparent shortening, Galeazzi sign |
| Walking child | Limp; waddling gait if bilateral |

Barlow positive vs Ortolani positive
- Barlow ("bahar lo"): pushes a reduced hip OUT. Ortolani: puts a dislocated hip back IN.
- The difference is where the hip lies AT REST: a hip that rests inside and can be pushed out is Barlow positive; a hip that rests outside and can be reduced is Ortolani positive.
- Ortolani-positive hips are more resistant to treatment than Barlow-positive hips.
- Click vs clunk: a click is tendon snapping, common and harmless; a clunk is the hip reducing or dislocating.

### Risk Factors
- Breech (FRANK breech carries the highest risk: the hyperflexed hip pushes the head posteriorly so the acetabulum does not develop).
- Female, first born, family history, oligohydramnios.
- Swaddling with the legs straight: swaddle so the baby can kick freely.

Imaging

| Classification | Modality | Best use |
|---|---|---|
| Graf | Ultrasound | Neonatal screening, before about 6 months |
| Tönnis | X-ray | After femoral head ossification |
| Ferretti | MRI | Pre-operative planning, non- ossified hips (needs sedation; rarely used) |

- Ultrasound is the imaging of choice up to about 4 months (visualises cartilage); X-ray from about 3–4 months onwards.

Hip ultrasound (Graf)
- Linear probe over the greater trochanter, coronal plane perpendicular to the acetabulum. Small tilts of the probe change the angles a lot, so the standard plane is essential.
- Standard Graf plane: straight (horizontal) iliac wing, lower limb of the os ilium, mid- section of the bony acetabular roof, and the labrum all visible. Focus on the acetabulum, not the femoral head.

<!-- page 3 -->

- Alpha angle: between the iliac wing line and the bony roof line. Normal over 60°; severe dysplasia under 43°.
- Also look at femoral head coverage and do a STRESS ultrasound before and at the end of treatment.
- Transverse (posterior) views can be used with the Pavlik harness on; less common, but may be shown in exams.

### Treatment by Age

| Age | Best test | Standard treatment | Pitfall |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–6 weeks | Clinical examination | Observation (if mild); Pavlik harness if dislocated | Ultrasound too early: high false-positive rate |
| 6 weeks–3 months | Ultrasound | Pavlik harness | "Pavlik disease": leaving the harness on when the hip will not reduce |
| 3–18 months | X-ray | Hip abduction orthosis (3–6 months); closed reduction + spica cast | Forcing extreme abduction in the cast: avascular necrosis |
| Over 18 months | X-ray | Open reduction + spica cast | Delaying surgery: bones too deformed for closed reduction |

Pavlik harness
- First choice from 6 weeks to about 3 months. Size by chest circumference at nipple level; apply correctly (the International Hip Dysplasia Institute has a video guide).
- Weekly ultrasound while in the harness; improvement is gradual. Continue for up to 3 weeks; if the hip is still out, switch to a rigid hip abduction orthosis (femoral abduction brace).

<!-- page 4 -->

- Femoral nerve palsy: from hip flexion over about 110°; the baby stops moving the leg. Remove the harness for a week, then reapply with less flexion, or change to a rigid abduction orthosis.
- Rigid hip abduction orthoses are the standard in some European countries (e.g. Germany).

### Closed Reduction, Arthrogram and the Safe Zone
- X-ray landmarks: Hilgenreiner line (horizontal through the triradiate cartilages), Perkin line (perpendicular from the lateral edge of the TRUE acetabulum). The head should lie in the inner lower quadrant; in DDH it may be in quadrant II–IV.
- Before the femoral head ossifies, use the H point (centre of the proximal femoral metaphysis) to locate it.
- Arthrogram: the head should point to the triradiate cartilage; look for capsular hourglass constriction and the cartilaginous acetabular edge.
- Ramsey safe zone: the arc between the abduction needed to keep the hip reduced and the abduction at which it redislocates (maximum abduction limited to avoid AVN). The more abduction a hip needs to stay in, the higher the AVN risk.
- Post-spica CT confirms reduction (beware off-plane images).

### AVN in DDH
- In children AVN shows as proximal femoral growth disturbance rather than adult-type collapse and arthritis: coxa valga or vara, short neck (coxa breva), coxa magna, torsional deformity, eccentric epiphysis.

### Exam & Viva Pearls
- Incidence 1/100 at birth, 1/1000 at 6 weeks.
- Alpha angle: normal over 60°, severe under 43°.
- Pavlik: 6 weeks to 3 months; weekly ultrasound; stop after 3 weeks if not reduced; watch for femoral nerve palsy.
- Over 6 months: closed reduction + spica; over 18 months: open reduction.
- Know the Indian screening pathway (POSI, IOA, IAP).

### Take-Home Summary
- Screen clinically, image at the right age (ultrasound at 6 weeks, X-ray after about 3–4 months), and treat by age: harness, then brace, then closed and finally open reduction, always avoiding forced abduction to protect the femoral head.
