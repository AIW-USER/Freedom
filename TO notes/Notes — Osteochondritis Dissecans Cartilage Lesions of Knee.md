OVERVIEW
This lecture covers Osteochondritis Dissecans (OCD), Spontaneous Osteonecrosis
of the Knee (SPONK), and Transient Osteoporosis, forming a continuum of
osteochondral lesions affecting subchondral bone and cartilage.
Quote: “Identify early whether it’s necrosis, insufficiency fracture, or separation
— treatment depends on the stage of bone failure.” – Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### TRAUMATIC OSTEOCHONDRAL LESIONS
- History of injury present.
- May present as:
  - Bone bruise: trabecular microfracture + edema (T2 bright).
  - Osteochondral fracture: visible fracture line through cartilage.
- Differentiation: no meniscal extrusion; edema localized.

SPONTANEOUS OSTEONECROSIS OF KNEE (SPONK)
Definition
Subchondral insufficiency fracture occurring spontaneously, mostly in medial femoral
condyle of osteopenic patients, progressing to articular collapse and OA.

Etiology
- Osteopenic bone + meniscal root tears → loss of load distribution.
- Medial meniscus extrusion = hallmark finding.
- Previously thought to be AVN, now proven to be non‑vascular insufficiency fracture.
### MRI Findings
- Subchondral fracture line with extensive bone marrow edema.
- Medial meniscus extrusion in >50% cases.
- Early diagnosis possible within 48 hours of pain onset.
### Radiological Progression

| Stage (Aglietti) | X‑ray Feature |
|---|---|
| I | Normal X‑ray |
| II | Subtle flattening of condyle |
| III | Subchondral lucency without collapse |
| IV | Subchondral collapse visible |

### Chondylar Ratio
- Lesion width ÷ condylar width × 100.
- 40% → poor prognosis; rapid OA progression.
Management

| Stage / Ratio | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Stage I–II / | Restricted weight bearing (6–12 wks) + |
| <40% | Bisphosphonates |
| Stage III–IV / | Surgical – Subchondroplasty, OATS, HTO, or UKA |

>40%

Subchondroplasty
- Image‑guided injection of Calcium phosphate (AccuFill®) into lesion.
- Acts as internal bone graft substitute and scaffold for healing.
### Unicondylar Knee Arthroplasty (UKA)
- Reserved for Stage IV with isolated medial involvement.

- Indicated only if:
  - Opposite compartment intact.
  - No flexion‑varus deformity.
  - Secondary causes ruled out.
- Bisphosphonate course (3 months) before surgery.

TRANSIENT OSTEOPOROSIS / BONE MARROW EDEMA
SYNDROME
Overview
- Self‑limiting, non‑progressive bone marrow edema condition.
- Common in middle‑aged men or 3rd trimester pregnancy.
- Most frequent in hip, rarely knee.
### MRI Findings
- Diffuse marrow edema, no subchondral fracture line.
- Resolves within 2–3 months.
Etiology (Proposed)
- Reflex sympathetic dystrophy variant.
- Transient ischemia or inflammatory response.
Management
- Non‑weight‑bearing 6–8 weeks.
- Bisphosphonates ± Calcitonin.
- Usually resolves spontaneously. In pregnancy → only rest; bisphosphonates contraindicated.

OSTEOCHONDRITIS DISSECANS (OCD)
Definition
Separation of an osteochondral fragment from subchondral bone due to vascular insult
or repetitive microtrauma.
Dissecans = “to dissect/separate” – separation of fragment, not
degeneration.

### Common Site
- Posterolateral aspect of medial femoral condyle (70%).
- Others: lateral femoral condyle, patella (lower pole).
Etiopathogenesis
- Repetitive microtrauma, ligament laxity, sports.
- Genetic & endocrine factors (PTH, ossification center anomalies).
- Associated with discoid meniscus (esp. lateral OCD).

### CLINICAL FEATURES
- Dull pain, swelling, recurrent effusions.
- Mechanical symptoms: locking/catching (loose body).
- Wilson’s Test:
  - Patient seated → knee flexed 90° → internal rotation → active extension.
  - Pain at 30° flexion = positive (impingement of tibial spine on lesion).
Commonest cause of loose body in knee in adolescents.

RADIOLOGY & MRI
### Kühnel & Berg (Kälin–Berg) Classification
- Based on location: posterolateral medial femoral condyle (2C zone).
- Tunnel view X‑ray best for visualization.
### MRI (De Smet Criteria) for Unstable Lesion

|---|---|
| High‑signal line >5 mm beneath lesion | Indicates fluid between bone & fragment |
| Fluid‑filled cyst >5 mm | Suggests instability |

MANAGEMENT
### Juvenile (Skeletally Immature)
- Stage I–II (Stable) → Conservative:
  - Immobilization 6 weeks + non‑weight bearing.
  - Gradual mobilization to full WB by 3 months.
- Stage III–IV (Unstable/Non‑healing) → Surgical (after 3 months of failed conservative care).
### Adult (Skeletally Mature)
- Poor spontaneous healing → surgery indicated for symptomatic or >1 cm lesion.

### EXAM PEARLS
- SPONK: Subchondral insufficiency fracture, medial condyle, meniscus extrusion.
- Transient Osteoporosis: Bone marrow edema syndrome, no fracture, self‑limiting.
- OCD: Posterolateral medial condyle lesion, separation of osteochondral fragment.
- Wilson’s Test: Diagnostic for OCD.
- De Smet MRI criteria: >5 mm high‑signal line = unstable.
- Juvenile OCD: Good prognosis, heals with rest.
- Adult OCD: Needs surgery; retrograde drilling or fixation.
- OATS/ACI: Used for non‑union or loose fragment stages.
- Subchondroplasty: SPONK management; inject Ca‑phosphate filler.
- Bisphosphonates: Core conservative treatment for SPONK/Transient Osteoporosis.
