Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Dural
Arteriovenous Fistula (Dural AVF)
Faculty: Dr. Himanshu Champaneri

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Dural Arteriovenous Fistulas (DAVFs) are abnormal arteriovenous shunts located
within the layers of the dura mater, where arteries drain directly into dural venous
sinuses or cortical veins without an intervening capillary bed.
Dr. Himanshu’s Quote:
“Every dural AVF is a reminder that nature sometimes forgets to build a
capillary bed.”
Nature:
- Acquired lesion, unlike congenital AVMs.
- Supplied by meningeal branches of the ICA, ECA, or vertebral arteries.
- Shunt confined to dural leaflets (within venous sinus wall).

### EPIDEMIOLOGY & ETIOLOGY

| Parameter | Findings |
|---|---|
| Incidence | 10–15% of all intracranial AVMs |
| Age | 40–59 years (rare in children) |
| Sex | Female predominance (≈60%) |
| Etiology | Acquired / Idiopathic |
| Predisposing factor | Venous sinus thrombosis |
| Proposed Mechanisms | - Activation of dormant embryonic AV channels - De novo angiogenesis - Post-thrombotic recanalization |

### PATHOGENESIS
- Venous sinus thrombosis → elevated venous pressure → recruitment of meningeal feeders → arteriovenous shunt formation.

- Results in cortical venous hypertension and impaired CSF absorption, leading to raised intracranial pressure (ICP).

- In severe cases → global cerebral edema or communicating hydrocephalus.

### NATURAL HISTORY

| Type | Prognosis |
|---|---|
| Benign | 98% remain stable over 3 years |
| Aggressive | 8% hemorrhage, 7% neurological deficit, 10% mortality (4 years) |

Aggressiveness depends on:
- Cortical venous reflux
- Deep drainage
- High-flow shunt
- Tentorial or anterior fossa location

MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES
### 1⃣ Conservative / Manual Carotid Compression
- Useful in low-flow, benign lesions.

- Self-compression of carotid artery → promotes thrombosis (22% success).

- Opposite hand technique to prevent ipsilateral ischemia.

- Begin with 10 min/day → gradually increase.
2⃣ Endovascular Embolization
First-line treatment for most DAVFs.

### 3⃣ Microsurgical / Combined Approach
- Reserved for failed / inaccessible endovascular cases.

- Pre-op embolization reduces blood flow before craniotomy.

- Risk: torrential bleeding upon dural opening → plan craniotomy away from sinus.

- Occasionally surgical-assisted embolization (sinus cannulation intraoperatively).
### 4⃣ Stereotactic Radiosurgery (SRS)
- Used for residual feeders post-embolization.

- Obliteration rate: 58–70%.

- Not primary modality due to latency period (2–3 yrs).

CAVERNOUS SINUS VARIANT – Carotid Cavernous Fistula
(CCF)
Type of Dural AVF at the cavernous sinus.
- Barrow Classification:
- Type A: Direct ICA–cavernous sinus (traumatic; high flow)
- Type B: Meningeal branches of ICA
- Type C: Meningeal branches of ECA
- Type D: Both ICA + ECA meningeal branches
- Symptoms: Pulsatile proptosis, chemosis, diplopia, cranial nerve palsies (III, IV, VI),
raised IOP.
- Treatment:
- Low-flow: Conservative / carotid compression
- High-flow: Transvenous balloon or coil embolization
- Surgery: Only if endovascular fails
- SRS: Not preferred (risk to optic apparatus)
Most common cause of pulsatile proptosis = Carotid–Cavernous Fistula.

### COMPLICATIONS
- Venous rupture → subdural hematoma

- Neurological deficits (from cortical venous hypertension)

- Cranial nerve palsies (esp. III, IV, VI in CCF)

- Stroke (due to balloon/stent migration)

- Post-embolization recurrence if venous outflow not occluded

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- DAVF = acquired dural AV shunt (within dural leaflets).

- Most common site: Transverse–Sigmoid junction (63%).

- Most common symptom: Pulsatile tinnitus.

- Subdural hemorrhage = hallmark bleed type.

- Gold standard diagnosis: DSA (6-vessel study).

- Cortical venous reflux = marker of aggressiveness.

- Manual carotid compression: 22% spontaneous cure.

- First-line therapy: Endovascular embolization (trans-venous).

- SRS: Only adjunct for residual feeders.

- CCF: Type A – traumatic, direct ICA–sinus; presents with pulsatile proptosis.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Dural AVF = acquired, dural-based AV shunt usually following sinus thrombosis.

- Venous hypertension → raised ICP, subdural bleed, hydrocephalus.

- Classification (Borden/Cognard) based on venous drainage & reflux.

<!-- page 7 -->

- Treatment priority: Eliminate cortical venous reflux.

- Endovascular approach (transvenous) = mainstay.

- Surgery reserved for failures or inaccessible sinuses.

- Goal: Prevent hemorrhage, relieve symptoms, and restore normal venous drainage.
