Hallux Valgus and Varus – Dr. Rahul Upadhyay
Overview
Hallux valgus is a static subluxation of the first MTP joint with lateral deviation of the
great toe and medial deviation of the first metatarsal. Hallux varus is the medial
deviation of the great toe, most commonly a complication of hallux valgus surgery.
Faculty Remark: “Understand the anatomy of the sesamoids and the MTC joint. If
those are not derotated during surgery, recurrence or varus is inevitable.”

Anatomy Essentials
### Key Structures
- Plantar plate
- Sesamoid complex (medial and lateral sesamoids)
- Collateral ligaments
- Flexor hallucis brevis (FHB) – two heads with sesamoids
- Adductor hallucis (transverse and oblique heads)
- Abductor hallucis
- Extensor hallucis longus & brevis attached via hood ligament
Biomechanics
- No muscle inserts directly on the metatarsal head, making it vulnerable to deforming forces from narrow footwear.
- As deformity progresses:
  - Sesamoids sublux medially
  - Crista becomes eroded
  - Flexor and extensor tendons shift laterally
  - Abductor hallucis loses mechanical advantage

Causes of Hallux Valgus
Extrinsic
- Footwear, occupation, trauma
Intrinsic
- Heredity
- Pes planus
- Hypermobility of TMT joint

- Ligamentous laxity
- Gastrocnemius tightness
Miscellaneous
- Obesity
- Plantar fasciitis
- Degenerative arthritis
- Second toe amputation
- Space occupying lesions in first web space

### Clinical Presentation
- Medial eminence pain (bunion)
- Lateral deviation of great toe
- Second toe deviation/crossover deformity
- Callosities beneath 2nd metatarsal
- Capsulitis, instability at lesser MTP joints
- Lateral forefoot overload

### Physical Examination
- Gait assessment
- Standing and sitting alignment evaluation
- Assess pronation of hallux
- Measure first MTC joint mobility
- Silfverskiöld test for gastrocnemius tightness
- Check tenderness along medial eminence
- Neurovascular assessment
### Juvenile Hallux Valgus Features
- Onset in adolescence
- Hypermobile first MTC joint
- Higher IMA, lower HVA
- Less pronation
- Bursal thickening uncommon
- Open physis present

Radiology
### Recommended Views
- Weight bearing AP, lateral, oblique foot views

<!-- page 3 -->

### Key Radiological Angles
- Hallux Valgus Angle (HVA)
  - Normal <15°
  - Mild: 15–20°
  - Moderate: 20–40°
  - Severe: >40°
- Intermetatarsal Angle (IMA)
  - Normal <9°
  - Mild: 9–11°
  - Moderate: 11–16°
  - Severe: >16°
- Hallux Valgus Interphalangeus Angle (HVIA) – increased in distal deformity
- Distal Metatarsal Articular Angle (DMAA)
  - Normal <6°
### Joint Congruency
- Congruent joint: stable
- Incongruent joint: progressive deformity
### Metatarsus Primus Varus
- Orientation of MTC joint determines degree of varus
- Medial inclination up to 8° is normal

### Pathophysiology of Hallux Valgus
- Tight footwear → lateral drift of great toe
- Sesamoids sublux medially
- Flexor/extensor tendons shift laterally → pronation of hallux
- Crista erosion → instability
- Progressive IM angle widening
### Functional Consequences
- Abnormal weight distribution
- Callosities under 2nd MT head
- Plantar capsulitis
- Instability of 2nd MTP joint

### Conservative Management
- Wide toe box footwear

<!-- page 4 -->

- Bunion pads
- Toe spacers
- Orthotics for pes planus
- NSAIDs for symptomatic relief
- Avoid cosmetic-only requests in asymptomatic patients

Surgical Decision Making
### Factors Considered
- Chief complaint
- Activity level and occupation
- Physical findings
- Radiological parameters (HVA, IMA, DMAA)
- Joint congruency
- Presence of arthritis
- Hallux pronation
- First MTC joint hypermobility
- Patient expectations
### Algorithm Summary
- Congruent joint + mild deformity → distal osteotomy (Chevron)
- Moderate deformity → Scarf osteotomy ± distal soft tissue procedure
- Severe deformity → Proximal metatarsal osteotomy
- Hypermobile MTC joint → MTC fusion (Lapidus procedure)
- Degenerative MTP joint → MTP fusion

### Complications of Hallux Valgus Surgery
- Infection
- Delayed wound healing
- Skin sloughing
- Paresthesia
- AVN of metatarsal head
- Shortening of first ray
- Non union (in proximal osteotomies)
- Recurrence
- Hallux varus

HALLUX VARUS
Overview
Hallux varus is medial deviation of the great toe. Most commonly, it is an iatrogenic
complication of hallux valgus surgery.

Causes
- Over-plication of medial capsule
- Excessive medial eminence resection
- Overcorrection in post op dressing
- Medial displacement of tibial sesamoid
- Overpull of abductor hallucis
### Clinical Features
- Pain over medial side
- Cosmetic deformity
- Difficulty in toe off
- Narrow shoe intolerance
Management
- Early flexible deformity → taping, splinting
- Late or rigid deformity → soft tissue balancing or bony reconstruction
- Severe, rigid deformity → osteotomy or MTP fusion

### Exam Pearls
- HVA, IMA, DMAA define deformity magnitude
- Congruent joint → distal osteotomy; incongruent → proximal correction
- Sesamoid position is the key indicator for deformity severity
- Hypermobile MTC joint → Lapidus fusion indicated
- Hallux varus is most often due to overcorrection during surgery

Final Faculty Insight: “Hallux valgus is a multiplanar deformity. If you correct only
one plane, the deformity will return. Respect the sesamoids, the capsule, and the MTC
joint.”
