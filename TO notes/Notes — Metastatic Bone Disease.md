Metastatic Bone Disease – Dr. Rajat Gupta
Overview
Metastatic Bone Disease (MBD) is the third most common site of metastasis, after
lung and liver. The spine is the most frequently involved site, followed by pelvis, ribs,
and proximal appendicular skeleton. Early recognition and proper oncologic workflow
are crucial for diagnosis, stabilization, and improving quality of life.
Faculty Remark: “Never assume metastasis. Prove it. A wrong assumption can cost
the limb or even life.”

## Key Concepts
Common Primaries Causing Bone Metastasis
“Kosher Pickle, Bacon Lettuce Tomato” mnemonic: - Kidney - Prostate - Breast
(most common in women) - Lung (most common in men) - Thyroid
Other primaries: GI cancers, bladder, melanoma.

### Sites of Metastasis
- Spine (most common)
- Proximal femur, pelvis, humerus
- Lytic lesions more common than sclerotic
- Acral metastasis is strongly suggestive of lung or kidney carcinoma
### Pathological Fractures
- Increased risk in: lung, kidney, myeloma
- Healing potential varies:
  - Myeloma – 67 percent
  - RCC – 44 percent
  - Breast – 27 percent
  - Lung – 0 percent

## Approach to a Suspected Metastatic Lesion
### Step 1: Clinical Assessment
- Tobacco use
- Urinary symptoms
- Non healing ulcer

- Weight loss or anorexia
- Swelling or lump
- Bleeding from natural orifices
### Step 2: Physical Examination
- Lymph node evaluation
- Breast examination (female)
- Thyroid swelling
- Per rectal exam (male)

## Radiological Evaluation
### X Rays
- Lytic lesions → myeloma, RCC, thyroid, lung
- Sclerotic lesions → prostate, bladder, carcinoid, medullary thyroid carcinoma
### MRI
- Soft tissue extension
- Neurovascular bundle relation
- Skip lesions
### Bone Scan
- Screens entire skeleton
- Best for osteoblastic metastasis
- False negatives: RCC, myeloma
- False positives: trauma, infection
### PET CT
- Whole body screening
- Better for lytic lesions
- Helps in tumor response assessment

## Biopsy Principles
- Do not assume metastasis in elderly without proof.
- Biopsy only after complete systemic evaluation.
- Ideal: Core needle biopsy.
- Avoid contaminated compartments.
“After a bad biopsy, even the best surgeon cannot salvage the oncologic plan.”
### Reaming Biopsy
- Not reliable for diagnosis (only 60 percent diagnostic yield).
- Tissue crushing leads to missed diagnosis.

## Prognostic Scoring
Katagiri Score
Considers: - Primary tumor growth rate - Visceral metastasis - ECOG performance
status - Prior chemotherapy - Multiple bone metastases
Higher score → poorer survival.

Tomita Score
Used for spinal metastasis to guide surgical strategy: - General condition - Number of
bone metastases - Primary tumor growth rate - Visceral metastasis

Modified Bauer Score
Considers: - Visceral metastasis - Primary site - Palsy status - Bone only or multiple
sites

## Treatment Principles
Goals
- Improve quality of life
- Achieve pain free mobility
- Allow early weight bearing
- Implant should outlive the patient

<!-- page 4 -->

Modalities
1. Surgery
  - Indications:
    - Pathological fractures
    - Impending fractures
    - Solitary metastasis (resection possible)
  - Intramedullary nailing commonly used
  - Cement augmentation for stability
2. Bone Strengthening Agents
  - Denosumab (120 mg SC monthly)
  - Zoledronic acid (4 mg IV monthly)
3. Radiation Therapy
  - Pain relief
  - Tumor control
  - Protocols: 8 Gy single fraction or 30 Gy in 10 fractions
4. Systemic Therapy
  - Hormonal therapy
  - Chemotherapy
  - Immunotherapy

## Spine Metastasis Protocol
### Indications for Biopsy
- Life expectancy more than 3 months
- Neurological deficits
- Uncertain diagnosis
### Management Based on Tumor Type
- Radioresistant tumor → Decompression and stabilization
- Radiosensitive tumor → Radiotherapy first, then stabilization
Other options: - Vertebroplasty or kyphoplasty for pain due to instability

### Exam Pearls
- Spine is the most common site for bone metastasis.
- Lytic metastasis: RCC, thyroid, lung.
- Sclerotic metastasis: prostate.
- PET CT best for lytic lesions, bone scan best for osteoblastic lesions.
- Never biopsy before systemic evaluation.
- Healing potential varies according to primary tumor.
- Cement augmentation often needed in metastatic fractures.
- Aim is function restoration, not oncologic cure.

Final Faculty Insight: “Treat the patient, not just the lesion. The goal is a pain free,
functional life for whatever time remains.”
