Recent ATLS Guidelines – Dr. Daivik T Shetty

### Key Concepts / Overview
- ATLS (10th ed.) refines trauma care with stronger emphasis on physiology‑driven resuscitation, early hemorrhage control, and minimizing the “second hit.”
- ABCDE with C‑spine control: Airway (with cervical immobilization) → Breathing → Circulation (hemorrhage control) → Disability → Exposure.
- System readiness: pre‑hospital notification, organized trauma team, warmed fluids, and rapid access to blood products.
- Multiple vs Mass casualty: treat life‑threatening/multisystem first when capacity is adequate; in mass casualty, prioritize greatest chance of survival with least resource use.

### Pathophysiology / Principles
- First hit/Second hit: initial trauma → SIRS; extensive early surgery/over‑resuscitation can precipitate ARDS/MODS.
- Trauma triad of death: coagulopathy, hypothermia, acidosis—prevent via active warming, balanced transfusion, and bleeding control.

Protocol Highlights & What’s New in ATLS‑10
### Airway (A) + Cervical Spine Control
- Approach from head end, inline C‑spine immobilization (collar + blocks + tape), suction, jaw thrust/chin lift; OPA/NPA as indicated.
- Definitive airway: cuffed orotracheal intubation; nasotracheal may be advantageous with suspected C‑spine injury.
- New/clarified: in suspected tracheobronchial injury (hemoptysis + subcutaneous emphysema) use fiber‑optic intubation and bronchoscopy to guide/confirm—avoid blind attempts.
- Severe maxillofacial/laryngeal/neck trauma: emergency airway = needle cricothyroidotomy (contraindicated <12 yrs due to subglottic stenosis); definitive = tracheostomy.
- Burns/inhalational injury: consider pre‑emptive intubation even with initially normal airway.
Breathing (B)
- Airway patency ≠ adequate ventilation; assess chest rise, rate, depth.
- Tension pneumothorax decompression (updated):

- Adults: needle at 4th/5th intercostal space, anterior to mid‑axillary line, above the rib.
  - Children: mid‑clavicular approach as per pediatric guidance.

### Circulation (C) with Hemorrhage Control
- Shock in trauma is hemorrhage until proven otherwise; look for blood on the floor + four (chest, abdomen, pelvis, long bones).
- Initial fluids (adults): 1 L warm isotonic crystalloid (reduced from 2 L). If ongoing shock → early blood products rather than more crystalloids.
- Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP): >10 units pRBC/24 h or >4 units pRBC/1 h; target 1:1:1 (pRBC:FFP:platelets). Use TEG/ROTEM to guide.
- Tranexamic acid (CRASH‑2): 1 g IV over 10 min, then 1 g over 8 h, within 3 hours of injury.
- Best perfusion marker: urine output (adults ≥0.5 mL/kg/h). Best guide to replacement: CVP/trends per local policy.
Disability (D)
- GCS (E4 V5 M6); document best motor response. “NT” items score 0 (not 1) in totals.
- GCS‑P: GCS minus pupil reactivity (both non‑reactive = 2; range 1–15). GCS‑PACT adds age and CT.
- C‑spine imaging rules:
  - NEXUS: if no midline tenderness, no intoxication, normal alertness, no focal deficits, no distracting injury → no imaging.
  - Canadian C‑Spine Rule: high‑risk (age ≥65, dangerous mechanism) → image; low‑risk with 45° active rotation → no imaging.
### Exposure (E) & Environment
- Fully expose; prevent hypothermia (warmed fluids, forced‑air warming), log‑roll (ideal 5‑person team; minimum 4) to examine posterior and perineum.

### Radiology / Imaging
- Adjuncts to primary survey: CXR, pelvic X‑ray, FAST as indicated.
- Pregnancy: perform indicated imaging; benefits outweigh fetal risks. Position 15–30° left lateral tilt to relieve IVC compression; monitor fetal heart (Doppler >10 weeks; continuous >20–24 weeks).

### Musculoskeletal & Pelvis
- Pelvic “open‑book” injuries: apply pelvic binder/sheet early → tamponade venous bleeding; then FAST/CT → preperitoneal packing/angio as needed; external fixation for stability.

<!-- page 3 -->

- Significant limb vascular injury: stepwise control—direct pressure → pressure dressing/elastics → proximal pressure → tourniquet.
  - Pneumatic tourniquet pressures: upper limb ~250 mmHg, lower limb ~400 mmHg.
- Urethral injury suspicion (perineal/scrotal ecchymosis, blood at meatus): Retrograde urethrogram before Foley (prostate exam no longer relied upon).

### Pediatric Pearls
- Airway: orotracheal intubation (uncuffed ETT common in infants); avoid cricothyroidotomy <12 yrs.
- Breathing: for tension pneumothorax, pediatric guidance favors mid‑clavicular needle site (size 14–18G as appropriate).
- Circulation: initial 20 mL/kg crystalloid once, then 10–20 mL/kg pRBC + plasma + platelets (often via pediatric MTP).
- Access: if IV difficult → intraosseous (prox tibia/distal femur).
- Urine output goals: infants 1–2 mL/kg/h; children 1–1.5 mL/kg/h; teens 0.5 mL/kg/h.

### Faculty Quotes or Key Takeaways
- “Replace blood with blood—after the first liter of crystalloid, search for bleeding and start transfusion early.”
- “Airway patency does not equal ventilation—always re‑assess breathing.”

### Exam / Viva Pearls
- List adult vs pediatric needle decompression sites and indications.
- Define MTP thresholds and the 1:1:1 ratio.
- Outline TXA dosing and the 3‑hour window (CRASH‑2).
- Interpret NEXUS and Canadian C‑Spine rules for imaging decisions.
- State adult urine output target and pediatric variations.
- Describe pelvic binder indications and first steps in open‑book injuries.
- Explain GCS updates (best motor; NT scoring) and GCS‑P.
