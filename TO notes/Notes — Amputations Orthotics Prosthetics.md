Amputations, Orthotics and Prosthetics – Dr. Mukul
Mohindra
Overview
This lecture covers principles of amputation surgery, prosthetic choices, and
orthotic devices, focusing on practical exam oriented concepts such as stump
handling, flap selection, prosthesis components, gait biomechanics, and correct orthosis
prescription.
Faculty Remark: “If you open an amputation, you must know how to close it.
Technique decides function more than the level.”

## Indications for Amputation
### Main Causes
- Dead limb: crush injury, gangrene.
- Deadly disease in limb: gas gangrene, malignancy.
- Dis useful limb: congenital deficiencies.
- Advanced PVD cases.
MESS Score
Used in mangled extremity to decide salvage vs amputation. Considers: - Skeletal and
soft tissue injury. - Limb ischemia. - Age. - Blood pressure.

## Types of Amputations
### Closed Amputation
- Stump covered with skin.
### Open Guillotine Amputation
- Stump left open initially, converted later to closed.

## Closure Principles
Muscle Closure
Myodesis
- Muscles sutured to bone through drill holes.
- Preferred method in most cases.
Myoplasty
- Opposing muscle groups sutured together.
- Preferred in PVD, where bone vascularity is poor.

Skin Flaps
### Skewed (Long Posterior) Flap
- Most preferred.
- Posterior flap becomes weight bearing surface.
### Scandinavian (Equal) Flaps
- Used in PVD due to better vascularity.

## Handling Neurovascular Bundle
- Vessels: double ligation.
- Nerves: stretch and cut sharply to avoid neuroma.
- Large nerves (sciatic): ligate first, then cut (vasa nervorum bleeding risk).
### Tourniquet Use
- Generally preferred.
- In infection or malignancy: no exsanguination.

## Nomenclature of Amputations
### Upper Limb
- Shoulder disarticulation
- Mid arm amputation
- Elbow disarticulation
- Forearm amputation
- Krukenberg amputation: forearm bones split as a functional pincer.
- Ray amputation: complete finger removal.

<!-- page 3 -->

### Lower Limb
- Hindquarter amputation
- Hip disarticulation
- Above knee amputation
- Knee disarticulation
- Below knee (transtibial) amputation
### Foot and Ankle
- Syme’s: through ankle (talus removed, malleoli retained).
- Chopart: intertarsal.
- Lisfranc: tarsometatarsal.

## Variations of Syme’s Amputation
- Sarmiento: 1.3 cm above malleoli.
- Boyd: talectomy, calcaneum shifted forward and fused to tibia.
- Pirogoff: vertical section of calcaneum rotated and fused to tibia.
### Gritti Stokes Amputation
- Through distal femur.
- Prepatellar tissues used for closure.

## Surgical Principles
### Prefer Disarticulation When Possible
- Gives end bearing stump.
### Preserve Maximum Length
- Better prosthetic fitting.
- Lower energy expenditure.
### Energy Expenditure After Amputation
- Long BKA: minimal, around 10 percent.
- Medium BKA: ~25 percent.
- Short BKA: ~40 percent.
- Above knee: ~65 percent.
- Hip disarticulation: ~100 percent.
### Recommended Stump Lengths
- BK: 15 cm below knee.
- AK: 20 cm above knee.

<!-- page 4 -->

- BE: 15 cm below olecranon.
- AE: 18–20 cm above elbow.

## Complications
- Infection.
- Bleeding.
- Skin flap necrosis.
- Phantom sensations (telescope effect).
- Post amputation neuroma.
Best treatment for neuroma: excision.
Best conservative option: TENS.

## Amputations in Children – Krajbich Principles
- Preserve growth plates.
- Prefer disarticulation.
- Prevent terminal overgrowth using epiphyseal caps.

## Prosthetics
Types
- Body powered prosthesis.
- Battery powered (myoelectric) prosthesis.
### Important Prostheses
- Above knee: quadrilateral socket prosthesis.
- Below knee: PTB prosthesis.
- Syme’s: Canadian prosthesis.
- Partial foot/hand: cosmetic fillers.

## Components of a Prosthesis
- Suspension: connects stump to socket.
- Socket: contacts stump.
- Prosthetic extension: substitutes long bones and joints.
- Terminal device: foot or hand.

<!-- page 5 -->

## Terminal Devices (Foot)
SACH Foot
Solid Ankle Cushioned Heel - Rigid keel, cushioned heel for pseudo plantar flexion.

### Jaipur Foot
- Flexible keel.
- Allows squatting, cross leg sitting, barefoot walking.
### SAFE Foot
- Stationary Attachment Flexible Endoskeletal.
### Energy Storing Feet
- Elastic heel stores and releases energy.

## Pylon Prosthesis
- Temporary endoskeletal prosthesis.
- Used immediately post amputation.
- Ideal time for final prosthesis: around 3 months.

## Orthotics

Static vs Dynamic
Orthosis = device that assists function.

- Static: no joint movement (e.g., finger splint).
- Dynamic: controlled movement allowed (e.g., wrist drop splint).

## Systematic Nomenclature
Name orthosis by joints spanned, proximal to distal + “O”.
Example:
Foot drop splint = AFO (ankle foot orthosis).

## Upper Limb Orthoses
- Knuckle bender: ulnar nerve palsy (claw hand).
- Opponens splint: median nerve palsy.
- Wrist drop splint: radial nerve palsy.

<!-- page 6 -->

- Finger splint: mallet finger.

## Nerve Injury – When to Splint
- Observe closed injuries for 3–6 weeks.
- Splint during observation phase.
- No splint for transected nerve (repair needed).

## Turnbuckle Splint (VIC)
- Used in mild Volkmann ischemic contracture.
- Progressive stretching via screw mechanism.

## Aeroplane Splint
Used in brachial plexus palsy while awaiting recovery (up to 3 months).
Indications: - No avulsion. - Toronto score > 3.5.

## Tennis Elbow Strap
- Works on counter force bracing.
- Strap applied 5 cm distal to lateral epicondyle.

## Cervical Orthosis
- Soft collar: muscle strain.
- Rigid collar: spondylosis.
- Philadelphia collar: suspected cervical spine injury.
- SOMI brace: confirmed fracture.
- Halo vest: maximal stabilization.

<!-- page 7 -->

## Lumbar Orthotics
### Lumbosacral Belt
- Works via hydraulic principle.

- Used only during standing work.
### Taylor’s Brace
- Dorsolumbar support (T10–L3).
### ASH Brace
- Used in compression fractures, kyphosis control.

## Scoliosis Bracing
- Milwaukee brace: high profile, lateral pad on apical vertebra.
- Boston brace: low profile, most used.
- Worn 20 hours/day.
When to brace: - Cobb angle < 45°. - Risser grade 3–5.

## Pediatric Orthotics
- Pavlik harness: DDH, 23 hours/day.
- Dennis Browne splint: CTEV, non walking stage.
- CTEV shoes: walking age.

## Foot Orthotics
- Hallux valgus: bunion splint.
- Flat foot: Thomas heel.
- Plantar fasciitis: silicone heel pad.
- Metatarsalgia: metatarsal rocker bar.

## Floor Reaction Orthosis (FRO)
Used in:
- Polio

<!-- page 8 -->

- CP
- Spina bifida
Mechanism: - Foot touches in equinus → knee flexes. - Heel moves down → shell
pushes knee into extension.
Assists weak quadriceps and plantar flexors.

## Ambulatory Aids
- Cane, crutch, walker.
- Increase area of support to stabilize CoG.
### Correct Sizing
- Cane: held in opposite hand of painful hip.
- Crutch: 15 cm ahead, axillary pad 5 cm below fold.
- Walker: elbows and shoulders at 30° flexion.

### Exam Pearls
- Myodesis preferred, except in PVD.
- Long posterior flap preferred except in PVD.
- Syme’s modifies into Sarmiento, Boyd, Pirogoff.
- Gritti Stokes uses prepatellar tissues for closure.
- Energy expenditure lowest in long BKA.
- Jaipur foot allows barefoot walking and squatting.
- AFO = ankle foot orthosis.
- Pavlik harness worn 23 hours/day.
- Thomas heel for flat foot correction.
- Cane always held in opposite hand.

Final Faculty Insight: “Rehabilitation is not only prosthesis. It needs correct stump,
correct device, and correct prescription. Technique is everything.”
