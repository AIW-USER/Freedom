Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Cervical Disc Prolapse
Faculty: Dr. C S Vishnu Prasath

### OVERVIEW
- Cervical disc prolapse (CDP) is one of the most common causes of neck pain and radiculopathy.
- Common levels: C5–C6 and C6–C7 (most mobile and stress-bearing segments).
- Results from disc degeneration → herniation of nucleus pulposus → nerve root compression and inflammation. “80% of cervical disc prolapse cases resolve conservatively within 3–6 weeks.” – Dr. Vishnu Prasath

### ANATOMY & BIOMECHANICS
- Disc composition:
  - Central nucleus pulposus (gelatinous)
  - Peripheral annulus fibrosus (fibrocartilaginous)
- Function: Shock absorption & controlled mobility.
- Cervical spine is highly mobile → predisposed to wear-and-tear and disc degeneration.
- Cervical roots (8 roots, 7 vertebrae):
  - C1–C7 roots exit above their respective vertebrae.
  - C8 exits below C7.
Root involvement rule:
Disc level C5–C6 → C6 root affected; C6–C7 → C7 root affected.

### PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
- Degeneration of annulus fibrosus → protrusion/extrusion of nucleus pulposus.
- Leads to radiculopathy (root compression) and/or myelopathy (cord compression).
- Contributing factors:
  - Age-related changes.
  - Repetitive neck strain.
  - Facet & uncovertebral joint osteophytes → foraminal narrowing.
Compression mechanisms:
1. Herniated nucleus pulposus (anterior compression).

2. Facet joint arthropathy (posterior compression).
3. Uncovertebral osteophytes (anterolateral compression).

- Motor weakness – myotomal pattern.
- Upper cervical radiculopathy: Occipital & scapular pain.
- Exaggerated reflexes → think myelopathy.

### CLINICAL TESTS
- Spurling’s Test:
  - Neck extension + rotation to same side → ↑ radicular pain.
  - Indicates foraminal narrowing.
- Axial Compression Test: Downward pressure on head → reproduces symptoms.
- Cervical Distraction Test: Gentle upward traction → pain relief.
- Davidson’s (Shoulder Abduction) Sign: Arm abduction relieves pain (nerve tension relieved). Tension-increasing tests: Spurling’s, Axial compression. Tension-relieving tests: Cervical distraction, Shoulder abduction.

IMAGING
### X-RAY CERVICAL SPINE
- Loss of lordosis (muscle spasm).
- Decreased disc height, osteophytes.
- May reveal OPLL (Ossified Posterior Longitudinal Ligament) → may cause myelopathy.
MRI – Investigation of Choice
Indications:
- Persistent symptoms despite rest/medications.
- Neurological deficit or gait disturbance.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Constitutional symptoms (fever, malignancy).
- Suspected infection or myelopathy.
Findings:
- Central / paracentral / foraminal disc protrusion.
- Cord signal changes (T2 hyperintensity → edema/myelomalacia).
- Helps differentiate OPLL vs disc collapse:
  - OPLL: Dense ossified line compressing cord above/below disc.
  - Disc collapse: Focal bulge at intervertebral level.

### CONSERVATIVE MANAGEMENT
- Rest: 3–7 days, soft collar, large pillow → neck flexion relieves tension.
- Medications:
  - NSAIDs, pregabalin, short steroid taper (e.g., Deflazacort 24 mg → taper).
  - Sleep aid (Zolpidem) for restless arm.
- Physiotherapy: Ice/heat therapy, neck exercises after 2 weeks.
- Avoid traction in acute soft-disc cases (extension may worsen pain).
  - If used, always in flexion position.
- Invasive options: Cervical epidural steroid or selective nerve root block (for inflammation-dominant pain). “Inflammation plays a bigger role than compression in radicular pain.” – Dr. Vishnu Prasath

SURGICAL MANAGEMENT
Indications
- Failure of conservative treatment >6 weeks.
- Progressive motor weakness or severe neurological deficit.
- Cervical myelopathy (cord compression).

### Surgical Steps (ACDF)
1. Supine position; roll under scapula to extend neck.
2. Transverse incision → dissect between SCM (laterally) & strap muscles (medially).
3. Retract trachea & esophagus medially, carotid sheath laterally.
4. Confirm level → incise annulus → remove disc material using punch & curette.
5. Excise posterior longitudinal ligament to clear fragment.
6. Insert bone graft/cage ± plate fixation.
### Approach Preference:
- Left-sided for singers/teachers – reduces risk of recurrent laryngeal nerve injury.

### COMPLICATIONS
- Dysphagia (esophageal retraction edema).
- Hoarseness (recurrent laryngeal nerve injury, esp. right side).
- Graft-site pain / pseudoarthrosis (rare).
- Esophageal perforation (rare but fatal).
- Persistent radiculopathy (inadequate decompression).

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Most common level: C6–C7.
- Most affected root: C7.
- Spurling’s & Davidson’s signs: hallmark tests.
- OPLL: causes myelopathy, not radiculopathy.
- MRI T2 hyperintensity: myelomalacia.
- Preferred surgery: ACDF (not discectomy alone).
- Voice professionals: Left-sided approach.
