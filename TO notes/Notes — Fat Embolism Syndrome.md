Fat Embolism Syndrome – Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### Key Concepts / Overview
- Fat Embolism (FE): Presence of fat globules in circulation without symptoms.
- Fat Embolism Syndrome (FES): Clinical manifestation of fat embolism – a potentially fatal complication following trauma, particularly long bone and pelvic fractures.
- Incidence:
  - Fat embolism: 65–90% in solitary long bone or pelvic fractures.
  - FES: 2–4% in isolated femur fractures; up to 10% in multiple long bone/pelvic injuries.
- Mortality: 5–20% (average ~10%).
Dr. Mukul’s Key Remark:
“Fat embolism may be common, but fat embolism syndrome kills. Early suspicion and
prevention are what save lives.”

Etiology & Risk Factors
### Common Causes
- Traumatic (90%) – Long bone (femur, tibia), pelvic fractures, orthopedic surgery (esp. THR/TKR).
- Non-Traumatic – Pancreatitis, burns, liposuction, diabetes (ketoacidosis), cardiopulmonary bypass, parenteral lipid infusion, decompression sickness, corticosteroid administration.
### Risk Factors for Severe FES
- High-velocity trauma.
- Surgical delay >10–12 hours.
- Fixation of multiple fractures in a single sitting.
- Associated lung contusion.

Clinical Features
### Latent Period
- Asymptomatic interval: 12–72 hours post-injury (never immediate).

3. Lindeque’s Criteria (Respiratory-based)
- Based purely on respiratory derangement:
  - PaO₂ < 60 mmHg
  - PaCO₂ > 55 mmHg
  - Respiratory rate > 35/min
  - Dyspnea in a sedated patient

### Surgical Considerations

| Principle | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Early stabilization (<12 hrs) | Reduces FES risk. |
| Avoid reaming | Use unreamed intramedullary nailing to limit fat embolization. |
| If unstable / borderline patient | Apply external fixator first (DCO principle). |

Precautions during IM
nailing
- Bone venting (4.5 mm drill hole in distal cortex).
- Multiple cortical drill holes → reduce intramedullary pressure.
- Lavage canal before fixation.
- Use tourniquet (where feasible) to prevent embolization.
Ineffective Technique | Corticosteroid administration before nailing – no role. |

Dr. Mukul’s Closing Insight:
“Early fixation within 12 hours, unreamed nails, and vigilant observation are your best
defenses. Prevention remains the only real cure for fat embolism syndrome.”

### Exam / Viva Pearls
- Define FES and distinguish it from fat embolism.
- Describe biochemical vs. mechanical theory.
- Gurd–Wilson criteria – 1 Major + 4 Minor + 1 Lab.
- Earliest finding: Fat in urine/sputum; earliest confirmatory: BAL or retinal lesions.
- Most significant ABG feature: ↓PaO₂ / widened A–a gradient.
- Snowstorm on X-ray, Crazy paving on HRCT.
- Avoid reaming, use unreamed nailing + venting holes.
- Prophylaxis: Early fixation, gentle handling, and albumin hydration.
