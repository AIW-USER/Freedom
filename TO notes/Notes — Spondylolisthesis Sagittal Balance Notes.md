Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes –
Spondylolisthesis & Sagittal Spinal Balance
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Spondylolisthesis = slipping of one vertebra over another (usually L5 over S1).
Closely related to sagittal spinal balance—the harmony between spinal curves and
pelvic orientation that maintains upright posture.
Epidemiology:
- Affects ~5% of population.
- Rare below 5 years of age.
- ~6% progress to higher grades.
- Never seen in paraplegics — requires erect posture mechanics.

SAGITTAL BALANCE
### What Is Sagittal Balance?
- Alignment of C7 plumb line with posterior superior corner of S1 in upright lateral radiograph.

- Normal: C7 plumb line within ±2 cm of posterior superior sacrum.

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Positive Sagittal | Plumb line falls anterior to sacrum → stooped |
| Imbalance | posture |
| Negative Sagittal | Plumb line falls posterior → excessive lordosis |
| Imbalance | (rare) |

Key Concept:
Head should remain centered over pelvis — like holding a glass close to your body
(less strain) vs. extended arm (fatigue).

COMPENSATORY MECHANISMS
When forward stooping occurs (positive imbalance), the body tries to restore balance
by: 1. ↑ Lumbar lordosis

2. Pelvic retroversion (pelvis tilts backward)
3. Hip extension
4. Knee flexion
These work together to bring the center of gravity back over the feet.

### PI = PT + SS
- High PI → high compensatory capacity, but predisposition to listhesis.

- Low PI → compact alignment, limited compensation.

- PI – fixed (morphological).
- PT & SS – posture‑dependent (measured in standing lateral X‑ray only).

CLASSIFICATION
### 1⃣ Wiltse–Newman–Macnab (1976) – Anatomic

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Dysplastic | Congenital abnormality of facets (no pars defect) |
| Isthmic | Defect in pars interarticularis → slip of L5 on S1 |
| Degenerative | From chronic instability & facet remodeling |
| Traumatic | Fracture of posterior elements other than pars |
| Pathological | Bone disease, tumor, or infection related |

### 2⃣ Marchetti–Bartolozzi (1994) – Developmental vs Acquired

| Developmental | Acquired |
|---|---|
| High/Low Dysplastic | Traumatic (acute/stress) |
| With Lysis / Elongation | Post‑surgery / Degenerative / Pathologic |

RADIOLOGY
Grading (Meyerding)

| Grade | Slip (%) |
|---|---|
| I | 0–25 |
| II | 25–50 |
| III | 50–75 |
| IV | 75–100 |
| V | >100 (Spondyloptosis) |

### Slip Angle
- Angle between inferior endplate of L5 & superior endplate of S1.

- > 55° → High risk of progression.
### Imaging Protocol
- Standing lateral X‑ray: mandatory for grading.

- Flexion–extension views: detect instability.

<!-- page 4 -->

- MRI: canal/nerve root compression.

- CT/SPECT: pars defect or metabolic activity.

### SPONDYLOLYSIS
- Stress fracture at pars interarticularis (common in adolescent athletes).

- Often due to repetitive hyperextension (gymnastics, cricket, swimming).
### Stages on CT:
1. Stress reaction.

2. Micro‑fracture.

3. Macro‑fracture.

4. Non‑union / Pseudoarthrosis.
### Imaging Notes:
- SPECT: Most sensitive; hot = acute, cold = chronic.

- MRI: T2 hyperintense & T1 hypointense → edema = active lesion.
Management: - Rest, NSAIDs, physiotherapy.
- Flexion‑based rehab.
- Persistent pain → pars repair (Buck / Kimura / Morscher / Gillet techniques).

### CLINICAL FEATURES

| Type | Presentation |
|---|---|
| Asymptomatic | Incidental finding |
| Mechanical pain | Back pain on motion (instability catch) |
| Radiculopathy | Nerve root pain (traversing = degenerative; exiting = lytic) |
| Claudication | Seen in degenerative listhesis |
| Cauda equina | Rare, severe cases |

Characteristic Signs: - Step‑off deformity.
- Hamstring tightness → flexed hip/knee (crouched gait).
- Short trunk, protruding abdomen in high‑grade slips.

MANAGEMENT OVERVIEW
### Conservative Treatment
- NSAIDs (short course).

- Weight reduction.

- Bracing for support.

- Physiotherapy: Flexion exercises (open canal/foramen).

- Epidural or root block injections (for acute phase). Majority are asymptomatic; trial of non‑operative management first.
SURGICAL MANAGEMENT
### Low‑Grade Listhesis

| Step | Procedure |
|---|---|
| Decompression | Laminectomy / Flavectomy |
| Fixation | Pedicle screws |
| Fusion | Posterolateral or Interbody fusion |

### Posterolateral Fusion (PLF)
- Bone graft between transverse processes after decortication.

- Often combined with pedicle screw fixation.
### Interbody Fusion Options

| Type | Approach | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| PLIF | Midline posterior, dura retraction | Risk of root traction |
| TLIF | Via foramen after facetectomy | Preferred; safer |

HIGH‑GRADE SPONDYLOLISTHESIS
### Treatment Options

| Pelvic/Spine Type | Approach |
|---|---|
| Balanced pelvis & spine | In‑situ fixation + fusion |
| Retroverted pelvis, balanced spine | Partial reduction + fusion |
| Retroverted pelvis, unbalanced spine | Complete reduction + fusion |

Delta Fixation:
Triangular construct using L5–S1 pedicle screws + sacral screws for stability.
Bohlmann’s Technique:
Fibular strut graft from sacrum → L5 across defect; used in high‑grade slips.
Reduction Screw Technique:
Long‑headed screws gradually pull L5 posteriorly during tightening to restore alignment.

### ROLE OF SAGITTAL PARAMETERS IN SURGERY
- Surgical goal = decompression + stabilization + sagittal realignment.

- Slip angle correction > creation of lumbosacral lordosis.

- Lordosis restoration > full anatomic reduction.

- High PI → favors partial or full reduction.

- Low PI → limited correction; in‑situ fusion sufficient.

### VIVA & EXAM PEARLS
- PI = PT + SS.

- High PI → ↑ risk of listhesis but better compensation.

- Slip angle > 55° = progression risk.

- Degenerative = traversing root; Lytic = exiting root.

- Most common levels:
  - L5–S1 (Lytic/Dysplastic).

<!-- page 7 -->

- L4–L5 (Degenerative).

- Posterolateral Instrumented Fusion = Gold standard.

- MIS‑TLIF = definitive advantage in low‑grade listhesis.

### TAKE‑HOME SUMMARY
- Sagittal balance = C7 aligned over S1.

- PI = PT + SS → foundation of spinopelvic harmony.

- High PI = greater risk but better compensation.

- Listhesis types: Dysplastic / Isthmic / Degenerative / Traumatic / Pathologic.

- Imaging: Standing lateral X‑ray mandatory.

- Surgery → decompression + fixation + fusion ± reduction.

- Balanced vs. retroverted pelvis determines surgical plan.

- Goal: Restore stability, alignment, and function.
