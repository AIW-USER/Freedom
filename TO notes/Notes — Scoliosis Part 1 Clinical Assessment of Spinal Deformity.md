OVERVIEW
Definition (SRS):
Scoliosis is defined as a lateral curvature of the spine >10° (Cobb’s angle) on a
standing thoracolumbar X-ray.
Dr. Vishnu’s Quote:
“In scoliosis, we don’t just assess a curve — we assess growth, lungs, and the
story the spine is trying to tell.”

- Girls: 10–14 years
- Boys: 12–16 years

Lung Development:
- Alveoli increase 10× till 4 years.
- Early scoliosis (<5 yrs) → limits lung growth → restrictive lung disease / dyspnea.

### Birth and Developmental History
- Full-term or premature birth.

- Birth asphyxia → possible Cerebral Palsy → Neuromuscular Scoliosis.

- Delayed or regressed motor milestones → neuromuscular disorder.

- Multiple congenital anomalies → syndromic scoliosis.

- Family history → higher idiopathic risk.

- Menarche status (for growth potential).
Prenatal Detection:
Single hemivertebra → continue pregnancy.
Multiple hemivertebrae with thoracic anomalies → poor prognosis, may need
termination.

2⃣ General & Systemic Examination
Inspect for: - Neurocutaneous markers: - Hairy patches, dimples, lipomas → Spinal
dysraphism.
- Café-au-lait spots, axillary freckles → Neurofibromatosis. - Ligamentous laxity:
Marfan’s syndrome → long arm span, hyperextensible joints. - Syndromic features:
Arthrogryposis, Mucopolysaccharidoses, Epiphyseal dysplasia.
Systemic Review: - Cardiac: 7% congenital scoliosis → septal/valvular defects;
evaluate aortic root.
- Respiratory: Thoracic curves >100° → ↓VC (<70%). >120° → respiratory failure.
- Abdomen: Hepatosplenomegaly (storage disorders), hernias.
- Genitourinary: 6–12% have anomalies (ectopic kidney, renal agenesis, PUJ
obstruction).

3⃣ Spine Examination & Curve Assessment
Inspection:
- Paraspinal spasm → infection / painful scoliosis.
- Paraspinal atrophy → neuromuscular scoliosis.
- Plane of deformity: scoliosis / kyphoscoliosis.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Scoliosis = Cobb’s >10° (SRS).

- Early scoliosis → restricts lung growth (<5 yrs critical).

- Red flag: Pain, weakness, bowel/bladder disturbance.

- Check for neurocutaneous markers before surgery (MRI mandatory).

- ASIS asymmetry → limb length or pelvic obliquity.

- Rib hump = structural curve.

- Neurological exam crucial to rule out tethered cord, syrinx, or HGPPS.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Clinical assessment is the foundation before imaging.

- Identify early scoliosis and evaluate systemic involvement.

- Assess balance in coronal & sagittal planes.

- Pain → red flag; MRI before planning correction.

- Always correlate growth, neurology, and systemic health before management.
