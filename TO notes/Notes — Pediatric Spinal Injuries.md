Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Pediatric Spinal Injuries
Faculty: Dr. C S Vishnu Prasath

### OVERVIEW
- Incidence: 5–10% of all spinal injuries, 5% of all cord injuries.
- High mortality & missed diagnosis (≈50%) due to high-velocity trauma and associated injuries.
- Unique challenges: Anatomical immaturity, ligamentous laxity, and flexible spinal column. “Pediatric spine can tolerate high deforming forces without bony disruption — but the cord still suffers.” – Dr. C S Vishnu Prasath

EPIDEMIOLOGY & AGE-WISE DISTRIBUTION
Common

| Age Group | Common Cause | Region |
|---|---|---|
| Newborn | Birth injury (breech, difficult delivery) | Cervical (C1–C2) |
| Infants | Child abuse | Cervical |
| Older children | Sports injuries | Thoracolumb ar |
| <8 years | Ligamentous, upper cervical | C1–C3 |
| 8–10 years | Subaxial injuries | C3–C7 |
| >10 years | Thoracolumbar | T12–L1 |

### ANATOMICAL & BIOMECHANICAL DIFFERENCES
- Underdeveloped neck musculature → less stability.
- Ligamentous laxity → higher flexibility.
- Large head-to-body ratio → shifts flexion fulcrum upwards.
- Smaller vertebral bodies & larger disc–body ratio → cartilaginous elements dominate.
- Facet orientation:
  - 30° at birth → 60–70° by 10 years.
  - Shallow, horizontal facets → prone to dislocation.
- Fulcrum of motion: C2–C3 in children → descends to C5–C6 with age.
- Spinal column flexibility: Up to 5 cm mobility vs 5 mm cord movement → explains SCIWORA.

### EVALUATION & IMAGING
- ATLS protocol – prioritize airway, breathing, circulation.
- X-ray: Lateral with visualization of C7–T1.
- CT scan: Essential for cervicothoracic junction.
- MRI:
  - Neurological deficit.
  - Suspected SCIWORA (Spinal Cord Injury Without Radiographic Abnormality).
  - Evaluate disc–ligamentous continuity and posterior soft tissue complex.
### Trauma Mimics (Normal Variants)

| Mimic | Feature | Management |
|---|---|---|
| Pseudosubluxation | ≤2 mm | Observe; >2 mm = suspect |
| (C2–C3) | displacement | real subluxation |
| Synchondrosis line | Physiologic growth plate | MRI to confirm (no edema) |
| Physiological wedging | Mild vertebral wedging | Normal due to high disc height |

Rule: <8 mm → close reduction + halo; >8–10 mm → stabilization.

### COMMON PEDIATRIC INJURY PATTERNS
1. Atlanto-axial dislocation (AAD)
  - Rare but fatal; high suspicion with asymmetric occipital condyles.
  - Avoid collars – can worsen distraction.
  - Treatment: Occipito-cervical fusion.
2. Dens/Synchondrosis Fracture
  - Common in <7 yrs; through odontoid synchondrosis.

<!-- page 3 -->

- Treatment: Halo immobilization for 6–8 weeks.
3. SCIWORA (Spinal Cord Injury Without Radiological Abnormality)
  - Hyperextension + ligamentous laxity → momentary dislocation → spontaneous reduction.
  - Common <9 yrs (80%).
  - 6% of pediatric spinal trauma.
  - Presents within 30 min–96 hrs post-injury.
  - Treatment: Brace for 12 weeks + close monitoring.
4. Apophyseal Ring Injury
  - Adolescent; mimics disc prolapse.
  - Larger soft tissue component than adult herniation.
  - CT/MRI: Avulsion of endplate fragment.
  - Treatment: 6 weeks rest → decompression if persistent symptoms.
5. Axial Compression Injuries
  - Compression fractures: Pain → conservative care.
  - Burst fractures: Surgery if kyphosis >30°, >50% body height loss, or canal compromise.
6. Flexion–Distraction (Lap Belt) Injuries
  - Posterior ligament complex disruption.
  - Unstable – surgical stabilization required.

MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES
### Non-operative Management
- Immobilization: Pediatric-specific spine board or modified mattress (for large head).
- Bracing: Cervical collar / Halo / TLSO depending on level.
- Controlled flexion-extension X-rays before discontinuing brace.
### Surgical Indications
- Progressive neurological deficit.
- 17° kyphosis or >30° (burst).
- 50% vertebral body height loss or >50% canal compromise.
- 3-column injury with rotation (most unstable).
### Surgical Considerations
- Halo placement: More pins, less torque.
- Pedicle screws: Feasible >4 years (3.5–4 mm).

<!-- page 4 -->

- Wires/Fibro-optic intubation: For younger or unstable cervical cases.
- Avoid long-segment fusion → risk of crankshaft phenomenon. Crankshaft phenomenon: Continued anterior growth with posterior fusion → progressive rotation deformity.

COMPLICATIONS
Acute
- Pneumonia, sepsis, autonomic dysreflexia.
Chronic
- Myelomeningocele
- Syringomyelia
- Kyphoscoliosis (up to 100%) post-paralysis.

### PROGNOSIS
- Better recovery than adults due to flexible column and better neural plasticity.
- Recovery correlates with initial neurological status.
- Cord edema only: 75% recover; minor hemorrhage: 40%; cord transection: poor.
- Early decompression prevents post-traumatic syringomyelia and kyphoscoliosis. “Decompress what is compressed, stabilize what is unstable, and preserve growth.” – Dr. C S Vishnu Prasath

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Pediatric spine more flexible → SCIWORA common.
- <8 yrs → C2–C3 fulcrum; >8 yrs → C5–C6.
- Always differentiate synchondrosis from fracture.
- MRI: essential for ligament injury & cord edema.
- Avoid collar in AAD or AS spine.
- Crankshaft phenomenon = growth after posterior fusion.
- Prognosis best with early decompression + halo immobilization.
