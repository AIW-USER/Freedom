Principles of Ilizarov – Part 2 – Dr. Daivik T Shetty
Overview
Part 2 focuses on frame assembly, ring positioning, wire insertion techniques,
stability principles, and a detailed understanding of corticotomy. The goal is to create
a stable Ilizarov construct capable of precise distraction, compression, and deformity
correction.
Faculty Remark: “A stable frame is the heart of Ilizarov surgery. One loose wire or
poorly placed ring can ruin the entire construct.”

## Frame Assembly Principles
### Why Assembly Matters
- Determines stability of fixation.
- Prevents bone fragment motion during distraction or compression.
- Allows controlled manipulation: straightening, bending, rotation, distraction,

Methods of Assembly
compression.

1. Pre assembled Frame
- Saves surgical time.
- Allows component variation and planning.
- Requires adjustments during surgery because exact anatomy cannot be fully predicted.
2. Intra operative Assembly
- More accurate alignment.
- Surgeon adapts to real time deformity mechanics.
- Increases operative time.

## Ring Types and Functions
### Main Proximal Frame Supporting Ring
- Stationary, bears full construct weight.
- Placed on the strongest and widest bone area.
- Usually 3–5 cm distal to joint line.
- Examples:

<!-- page 2 -->

- Humerus: Omega ring.

### Stabilizing Frame Supporting Ring
  - Femur: Italian arch.

- Distal ring.
- Can be stationary or mobile.

### Pusher Puller Ring
- Balances the main supporting ring.

- Movable ring for distraction and compression.

### Reference Ring
- Kept 2–5 cm from the osteotomy / fracture site.

- Placed at the apex of deformity.
- No wires inserted here.
- Guides translation forces.

## Ring Level and Positioning
- Main supporting ring supports primary stabilization forces.
- Must be placed at the strongest, widest metaphyseal region.
- Stabilizing ring provides balance.

Important Muscle Based Corrections
Tibia
- Lateral muscles pull distal fragment laterally (valgus).
- Posterior muscles pull fragment posteriorly (antecurvatum).
- Correction: Tilt proximal ring 5° in both planes.
Femur
- Adductors → medial pull (varus).
- Biceps femoris → posterior pull.
- Correction: 7–10° abduction + 5–7° anterior elevation of arch.
Humerus
- Coracobrachialis and triceps pull medially → varus.
- Correction: 5–7° abduction of Omega ring.

### Ideal Ring Inclination
- 90 degrees to bone axis.
- Not aligned to skin but to bony center.

<!-- page 3 -->

### Skin to Ring Distance
- Minimum 3 cm gap.
- Practically confirmed using two finger breadth rule.

## Wire Techniques and Safe Insertion
Why K wires?
- Minimal bone damage.
- Strong tensioning capacity.
- Elastic micromotion promotes rapid callus formation.

### Wire Insertion Steps
- Small hole reduces contamination.

1. Determine safe entrance and exit points.
2. Mark arteries and avoid neurovascular bundles by 1.5–2 cm.
3. Align wire according to ring.
4. Insert at low speed (<40 rpm) with start stop technique.
5. Support skin during penetration.
6. Keep muscles in maximal functional stretch to prevent contractures.
7. Follow One Wire One Hole rule.

Incorrect Technique (Exam Question)
High speed drilling → burns soft tissue, risks neurovascular injury.
### Wire Angulation
- <30° between wires → unstable, side to side displacement.
- 30–45° → risk of ring shearing.
- >45° ideal, 90° most stable.

### Offset Wires
- Used to increase ring stability.
- Do not bend wire down to ring. Instead use washers, posts, hinges.

### Wires Near Joints
- Must avoid penetrating joint capsule.
- Know capsule limits:
  - Shoulder: capsule reaches below surgical neck.
  - Knee: suprapatellar pouch extends 4–5 cm above patella.
  - Elbow: capsule extends above coronoid/olecranon fossae.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Ankle: above tibial tendon grooves.

### Olive Wires – Indications
- Interfragmentary compression.
- Fragment displacement correction.
- Internal transport.
- Osteoporotic bone fixation.

Tensioning (General Range 50–130 kg)
- Half rings: 50–70 kg.
- Offset wires: 50–80 kg.
- 2–3 wires per ring:
  - Young: 110 kg.
  - Adult: 120–130 kg.
- Olive wires: 100–110 kg.
- For interfragmentary decompression: 50 kg.

## Stability Principles
### To Increase Frame Stability
- Increase pin diameter.
- Increase number of pins.
- Increase pin spread.
- Increase number of planes.
- Increase rod diameter or number of rods.
- Decrease bone to rod distance.
- Decrease pin to fracture distance.
Increasing pin to fracture distance reduces stability.
## Pin Site Infection (Checketts–Otterburn)
### Grade 1
- Mild erythema.
- Improve pin care.
### Grade 2
- Erythema, discharge, pain.
- Topical or oral antibiotics.

<!-- page 5 -->

### Grade 3
- No improvement.
- Remove pin + change antibiotics.
### Grade 4
- Soft tissue infection involving several pins.
- No bone changes.
- Remove loose pins + antibiotics.
### Grade 5
- Radiological bone involvement.
- Remove entire fixator + curettage.
### Grade 6
- Infection after fixator removal.
- Debridement + systemic antibiotics.

## Corticotomy – Core Concepts
Goals
- Low energy osteotomy cutting only cortex.
- Preserve periosteal and endosteal blood supply.
- Stimulate regenerate formation.

Steps
1. Small 0.5–1 cm incision at area where bone is close to skin.
2. Use 0.5 cm osteotome to avoid deep slip.
3. Avoid periosteal stripping.
4. Fan shaped tapping until cortex cut.
5. Stop when marrow blood appears (dark, fatty).
6. Complete cut by rotating osteotome until crackling sound.

### Confirming Completion
- Perform AP + lateral radiographs after 5 mm distraction.

### Errors and Complications
- Posterior tibial oblique fracture (incomplete cut).
- Butterfly fragment.
- Comminution if wires placed too close.

<!-- page 6 -->

### Rotational Movement After Proximal Tibia Corticotomy
- Must perform external rotation after fibular osteotomy.
- Prevents common peroneal nerve stretch.

## Level of Corticotomy
### Anatomic Factors
- Avoid nutrient artery zone (mid diaphysis).
- Prefer metaphyseal areas.
### Biomechanical Factors
- Must allow joint motion.
- Keep 6–7 cm away from joint line.
### Physiologic Factors
- Avoid areas with:
  - Scars
  - Infection
  - Sclerosis
  - Bone cysts
### Most Suitable
- Metaphyseal segment – optimal vascularity and biology.

## Monofocal vs Bifocal Corticotomy
Monofocal
- Lengthening up to 5 cm.
- Transport 5–7 cm.
- Pseudoarthrosis and nonunion stimulation.
- Gradual deformity correction.

Bifocal
- Lengthening 10–12 cm.
- Transport 10–16 cm.
- Simultaneous lengthening + deformity correction.
- Metabolic bone disorders: Paget, OI, Ollier.

<!-- page 7 -->

### Exam Pearls
- Ideal ring inclination is 90° to bone axis.
- Skin to ring distance must be ≥3 cm.
- Olive wires for interfragmentary compression and transport.
- Start-step low speed drilling prevents neurovascular injury.
- One wire one hole rule prevents bone weakening.
- Wire angulation must be >45° for stability.
- Pusher puller ring must be 2–5 cm from corticotomy.
- Confirm corticotomy with 5 mm distraction + radiographs.
- After tibial corticotomy, perform external rotation.

Final Faculty Insight: “Ilizarov looks complex only until you understand the logic of
each component. Build the frame with respect for biology and mechanics, and the bone
will respond beautifully.”
