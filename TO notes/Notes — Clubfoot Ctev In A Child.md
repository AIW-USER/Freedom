Clubfoot (CTEV) in a Child – Dr. Shalin Shah
Overview / Key Concepts
Congenital Talipes Equino Varus (CTEV) is one of the most common pediatric
orthopedic deformities and a high-yield exam topic. It is a three dimensional deformity
consisting of cavus, adduction, varus and equinus. Understanding pathoanatomy and
biomechanics is essential to mastering Ponseti technique and managing relapses and
complex clubfeet.

### Definition and Epidemiology
- Incidence: 1 in 1000 live births.
- Polygenic multifactorial inheritance.
- Male predominance.
- Bilateral involvement in about 50 percent.
### Components of Deformity (CAVE)
- Cavus: High medial arch.
- Adduction: Forefoot adduction.
- Varus: Hindfoot inversion.
- Equinus: Ankle plantar flexion.

Etiopathogenesis
Multiple theories explain idiopathic CTEV: - Mechanical theory (intrauterine constraint). -
Neuromuscular theory. - Vascular theory (hypoplastic dorsalis pedis, medial
compartment ischemia). - Germ plasm defect. - Genetic theory, CASP10 gene
implicated.
Antenatal ultrasound clues: - First trimester detection suggests syndromic etiology. -
Second trimester usually idiopathic. - Third trimester often positional.

Pathoanatomy
### Bony Changes
- Talus: Short neck, medially deviated, broad superior surface.
- Calcaneus: Small, medially displaced, in varus.
- Navicular: Medially displaced, hypoplastic.

### Soft Tissue Contractures
- Capsules: Ankle, subtalar, talonavicular joints.
- Ligaments: Spring ligament, deltoid, calcaneofibular, subtalar ligaments.
### Muscle Imbalance

| Deformity | Responsible Structures |
|---|---|
| Cavus | Intrinsics, FHL, FDL |
| Adduction | Tibialis posterior |
| Varus | Tendo Achilles, tibialis posterior |
| Equinus | Tendo Achilles |

### Biomechanics and Acetabulum Pedis Concept
- Foot behaves like a rung towel.
- Forefoot pronation maintains hindfoot varus.
- Calcaneal eversion occurs only after forefoot supination.
- Talonavicular joint acts as acetabulum pedis.
Key Principle - Correction must be done in supination followed by abduction.

Clinical Examination
### General Examination
- Head to toe evaluation.
- Examine spine and neurological status.
- Look for neurocutaneous markers (sacral dimple, sinus, hairy patch).
- Examine hips and knees.
- Assess upper limbs for syndromic clues.
Differentiation
- Positional foot vs metatarsus adductus vs true CTEV.
- Dorsiflexion or toe to shin test helps rule out positional deformity.
### Syndromic Associations
- Arthrogryposis
- Larsen syndrome
- Freeman Sheldon syndrome
- Diastrophic dysplasia
- Myelomeningocele
- Amniotic band syndrome

Scoring Systems
Pirani Score (0–6)
Midfoot - Medial crease - Curved lateral border - Talar head coverage
Hindfoot - Posterior crease - Rigid equinus - Empty heel
End point of casting is easy reduction of talonavicular joint, not degree of abduction.

### Dimeglio Score
- Used in older children and neglected cases.

Radiology
Radiographs not routine but useful to identify: - Hemimelias - Tarsal coalition - Flat top
talus
Important angles: - AP talocalcaneal angle <20° (Kite). - Lateral talocalcaneal angle
<35°. - Talus–first metatarsal angle >15°.

Ponseti Method (Gold Standard)
Principles
- Weekly long leg casts.
- Correct cavus first by elevating first metatarsal.
- Abduct foot in supination with counter pressure on talar head.
- Never pronate.
### Tendo Achilles Tenotomy
- Indicated after talonavicular reduction.
- Done under local anesthesia.
- Entry medial to lateral.
- Signs of completion: sudden dorsiflexion, palpable gap, audible pop.
### Bracing Protocol
- 23 hours per day initially.
- Night time and nap time till at least 4 years.
- Steenbeek, Dennis Browne or John Mitchell brace.

Atypical (Complex) Clubfoot
Features
- Short, stubby foot.
- Severe cavus with transverse plantar crease.
- Rigid equinus.
- Hyperextended short first toe.
- Fibrotic tendo Achilles.
### Modified Ponseti Technique
- Two thumb technique.
- Heel pull down maneuver.
- Maximum abduction only 20°.
- Cast in 100–110° knee flexion.

Surgical Management
### Tibialis Anterior Tendon Transfer
- Indication: Dynamic supination.
- Performed after 4 years.
- Transferred to lateral cuneiform.
### Soft Tissue Procedures
- Postero medial soft tissue release (PMSTR).
- Tendon Z lengthening.
- Capsulotomies.
### Bony Procedures
- Dwyer calcaneal osteotomy.
- Lateral column shortening (Dilwyn Evans).
- Lichtblau procedure.
- Joshi external fixator for neglected cases.

<!-- page 5 -->

Complications
- Flat top talus.
- Dorsal bunion.
- Rocker bottom foot.
- Residual deformity.
- Early arthritis.

Faculty Quote
“The end point of clubfoot correction is not abduction angle. It is easy reduction of the
talonavicular joint.” – Dr. Shalin Shah

### Exam Pearls
- Ponseti method is first line even in older children if foot is supple.
- End point of casting is talonavicular reduction.
- Bracing compliance is the most important factor preventing relapse.
- Atypical clubfoot requires modified Ponseti technique.
- Intervene early, ideally within first 2 weeks of life.
- CASP10 gene is associated with idiopathic clubfoot.
- Radiographs are for outliers, not routine diagnosis.
