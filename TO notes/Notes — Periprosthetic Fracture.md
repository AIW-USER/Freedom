Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Periprosthetic Fractures
Faculty: Dr. Anurag Tiwari

### Definition & Overview
- Definition: Fracture occurring around or adjacent to a joint prosthesis (THR, TKR, shoulder, elbow, ankle, etc.).
- Includes:
  - Prosthetic fracture – break in the implant itself.
  - Periprosthetic fracture – fracture in bone around implant.
  - Interprosthetic fracture – fracture between two prostheses (e.g., ipsilateral THR and TKR).
Key concept: Even if the fracture is distal to the stem but in the same bone
containing the prosthesis → it is still called a periprosthetic fracture.

### Epidemiology & Etiopathogenesis
- Common after: THR and TKR; rarer around shoulder, elbow, or ankle.
- Mechanism: Mostly low-energy trauma or trivial fall in elderly post-arthroplasty patients.

### Tips & Surgical Pearls for THR Fractures
- Always prepare for revision even in Vancouver B1 cases.
- Keep long-stem prosthesis and plates ready on the table.
- Pass cables/cerclage wires through muscle (not subperiosteal) to preserve biology.
- Avoid excessive periosteal stripping.
- Prosthesis stem should extend >2 cortical diameters beyond fracture site.

### Acetabular Periprosthetic Fractures (THR)
- Usually intraoperative rather than postoperative.
- Classification: Peterson (Type 1 & Type 2)
  - Type 1: Stable, undisplaced – minimal pain.
  - Type 2: Displaced or unstable – significant pain, instability.
- Management:
  - Stable prosthesis → Retain cup ± trans-acetabular screws.
  - Loose cup → Revision with plating (column plating ± anti-protrusio cage).

### TKR Periprosthetic Fractures
1. Femoral Component
- Commonest intraoperative site: Medial femoral condyle.
- Risk factors: PS design (box cut), anterior femoral notching.
Classifications
- Lewis & Rorabeck:
  - Type I: Stable, undisplaced.
  - Type II: Stable, displaced.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Type III: Unstable prosthesis.
- Su Classification (location-based):
  - Type I: Fracture proximal to component.
  - Type II: Originates at component, extends proximally.
  - Type III: Distal to component → often needs revision stem.
Treatment
- Stable prosthesis + proximal fracture → ORIF (locking plate or nail).
- Stable prosthesis + distal fracture → Long-stem femoral component ± plate.
- Loose prosthesis → Revision arthroplasty (stemmed component / megaprosthesis). Retrograde nailing possible only if open box PS design allows notch entry and adequate intercondylar width.

### Interprosthetic Fractures
- Occur between THR and TKR implants on same limb.
- For THR: classified as Vancouver type C. For TKR: considered Su type I.
- Management: ORIF with long bridging plate ± bone graft.

<!-- page 5 -->

### Prevention Summary
- Preoperative templating & implant planning.
- Adequate exposure during arthroplasty.
- Avoid notching & oversizing implants.
- Handle uncemented components carefully.
- Intraoperative vigilance for early fracture detection.

### Key MCQ & Exam Pearls
- Most accepted classification: Vancouver (THR), Felix (Tibia), Su (Femur-TKR).
- THR: Uncemented > Cemented for fracture risk.
- Common intraop fracture site in TKR: Medial femoral condyle.
- PS > CR design → higher fracture risk.
- Pistol grip / Notching: act as stress risers.
- Retrograde nail only possible if open-box femoral component.
- Ogden construct = Plate + Cable + Screw combination.
- Interprosthetic fracture: Between THR & TKR – managed by long-bridging plate.
