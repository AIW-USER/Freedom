Fracture Neck Femur – Dr. Rahul Upadhyay
Overview
Femoral neck fractures are among the most commonly encountered orthopedic
emergencies. They carry high morbidity, mortality, and healthcare burden. Management
differs significantly between young adults and geriatric patients.
Faculty Remark: “Reduction quality decides the fate of the femoral neck. If
reduction is imperfect, nothing else will save it.” – Dr. Rahul Upadhyay

Epidemiology
- Over 300,000 hip fractures annually in the US.
- Expected to exceed 6 million worldwide by 2050.
- Bimodal distribution:
  - Elderly: trivial trauma, osteoporosis.
  - Young: high energy trauma.
- Significant mortality and functional decline.

Applied Anatomy
### Key Angles and Structures
- Neck shaft angle: 130 ± 7 degrees.
- Anteversion: 10 ± 7 degrees.
- Calcar femorale: posteromedial dense bone plate crucial for stability.
- Blood supply:
  - Medial circumflex femoral artery via retinacular vessels.
  - Greater displacement increases retinacular disruption.
  - Tamponade effect in intact capsule theoretically increases AVN risk.

Diagnosis
### Plain Radiographs
- AP pelvis and cross table lateral.
- Consider traction internal rotation view.
### CT Scan
- Useful for assessing comminution and displacement.

<!-- page 2 -->

### MRI
- Most sensitive for occult fractures.
- Must be used in elderly unable to weight bear.
- 100 percent sensitivity and specificity.

Classification
### Garden Classification
- Type 1: valgus impacted, incomplete.
- Type 2: complete, non displaced.
- Type 3: complete, partially displaced.
- Type 4: complete, fully displaced.
Modified Grouping: - Non displaced: Garden 1, 2. - Displaced: Garden 3, 4.

Pauwels Classification
Based on verticality of fracture line. - Type 1: less than 30 degrees, stable. - Type 2: 30
to 50 degrees. - Type 3: more than 50 degrees, highest shear, most unstable.

### Clinical Evaluation
- Elderly: trivial trauma, inability to bear weight.
- Young: high energy injuries, multisystem trauma.
- Observe limb position:
  - Less external rotation suggests intracapsular.
  - Marked external rotation suggests extracapsular.

Management Principles
Treatment Goals
### Geriatric Patients
- Early mobilization and weight bearing as tolerated.
- Safest and shortest procedure.
- Minimize reoperation.
### Young Adults
- Preserve the head.
- Achieve anatomic reduction.
- Avoid deformity, minimize AVN and non union.
- Fix within 24 hours ideally.

Treatment Options
### Non Operative
- Very limited role.
- Reserved for:
  - High operative risk patients.
  - Valgus impacted fractures in non ambulators.
### Reduction and Fixation
- Closed or open reduction followed by fixation.
- Closed reduction must always be gentle.
- Avoid more than 3 to 4 attempts.
Arthroplasty
- Hemi or total hip.
- Elderly with displaced fractures: arthroplasty preferred.
- Active elderly: consider THA.

Pre-operative Considerations
Traction
- Offers no benefit in reduction or pain control.
- Increases risk of sores.
### Timing of Surgery
- Best within 12 to 24 hours.
- Delay beyond 4 days increases mortality 2.25 times.
Anesthesia
- Regional anesthesia reduces DVT, PE, pneumonia, transfusion.
Capsulotomy
- Reduces pressure but no proven reduction in AVN.

Reduction Techniques
### Whitman Technique
- Flexion, slight adduction, traction.
- Internal rotation to 45 degrees.
- Full extension followed by slight abduction.

<!-- page 4 -->

### Key Concepts
- Avoid varus. Even 1 degree varus is unacceptable.
- Varus malreduction increases non union risk.

### Garden Alignment Index
- AP: 160 degrees.
- Lateral: 180 degrees.
- Landmarks:
  - Long axis of medial cortex.
  - Medial trabecular pattern.

Open Reduction Approaches
### Smith Peterson
- Between TFL and Sartorius.
- Direct visualization.
- Needs second approach for screws.
### Heuter Modification
- Incision over TFL to reduce LFCN injury.
### Watson Jones
- Between TFL and gluteus medius.
- Same approach for reduction and fixation.
- Best for basicervical fractures.

Fixation Constructs
### Three Screw Construct
- Gold standard.
- Good spread essential.
- Inferior screw within 3 mm of cortex.
- Posterior screw within 3 mm of cortex.
### Four Screw Construct
- Reserved for calcar comminution.
### DHS
- Ideal for basicervical fractures.
- Add derotation screw.

<!-- page 5 -->

### Fixed Angle Devices
- Better union rates in some studies compared to screws.

### Evidence Highlights
- Cannulated screws have higher short term failure.
- Poor reduction predicts failure more than implant choice.
- Shortening correlates with functional decline.
- Conversion to THA:
  - 10 percent at 2 years.
  - 20 percent at 12 years.
  - 65 percent at 14 years.

Stress Fractures of Femoral Neck
Types
- Compression side.
- Tension side.
Treatment
- Compression less than 50 percent: conservative.
- Compression more than 50 percent: fixation.
- Tension side: always fixation.
Complications
- Varus malunion.
- Displacement.
- AVN up to 42 percent.

Non Union
Causes
- Posterior comminution.
- Initial displacement.
- Imperfect reduction.
- Non compressive fixation.
Treatment
- Elderly: arthroplasty.
- Young: valgus osteotomy or revision fixation.

<!-- page 6 -->

### Osteotomy Options
- McMurray displacement.
- Schanz angulation.
- Pauwel Muller reorientation (best accepted).
Templating
- Always template on normal side.
- Determine wedge angle, generally around 25 degrees.

AVN
Risk
- 5 to 8 percent in non displaced.
- 20 to 45 percent in displaced.
Causes
- Inadequate reduction.
- Delayed reduction.
- Initial displacement.
- Associated hip dislocation.
Management
- Elderly: arthroplasty.
- Young: proximal femoral osteotomy or arthrodesis.

### Exam Pearls
- Reduction quality is the most important determinant of outcome.
- Garden 1 and 2: treat with fixation.
- Garden 3 and 4 in elderly: treat with arthroplasty.
- Watch for screws below lesser trochanter, may cause subtrochanteric fracture.
- MRI is the best tool for occult fracture diagnosis.
- Do not start screws below lesser trochanter to avoid stress riser.
- Posterior and inferior screws are key for stability.

Final Faculty Insight
“Fix early, fix accurately, and avoid varus. These three principles alone prevent most
complications in neck femur fracture surgery.” – Dr. Rahul Upadhyay
