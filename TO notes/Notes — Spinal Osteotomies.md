Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Spinal
Osteotomies (2025)
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Spinal osteotomies are surgical bone resections performed to correct fixed spinal
deformities such as kyphosis and scoliosis, especially when soft-tissue releases and
instrumentation alone are insufficient.
Dr. Vishnu’s Quote:
“When deformity refuses to yield to soft tissue release, it’s time to bring in the
osteotome.”
Indication:
Used for rigid sagittal or coronal plane deformities uncorrectable by instrumentation
or ligamentous releases alone.
Hierarchy (increasing complexity):
1. Smith-Petersen / Ponte Osteotomy (SPO)
2. Pedicle Subtraction Osteotomy (PSO)
3. Bone–Disc–Bone Osteotomy (BDBO)
4. Vertebral Column Resection (VCR)

TYPES OF SPINAL OSTEOTOMIES
1⃣ Smith-Petersen / Ponte Osteotomy (SPO)
Type: Posterior column osteotomy.
Prerequisite: Mobile anterior disc.

| Step | Key Action |
|---|---|
| Exposure | Remove supraspinous, interspinous ligaments, and spinous process |
| Facet resection | Excise bilateral facets and ligamentum flavum |
| Correction | Posterior compression opens anterior disc space |

Correction: ~10° per level (≈1° per mm of bone removed).
Indication:

<!-- page 2 -->

- Scheuermann’s kyphosis
- Flexible sagittal and coronal deformities
Advantages:
- Less extensive
- Suitable for early deformity correction
- Can be combined across multiple levels for cumulative correction
Complications:
- Epidural bleeding
- Neural impingement (foraminal narrowing)
- Pseudoarthrosis if bone graft inadequate

2⃣ Pedicle Subtraction Osteotomy (PSO)
Type: Three-column posterior wedge osteotomy.
Prerequisite: Can be done even in fused spines (e.g., Ankylosing Spondylitis).
Technique: - Posterior elements, pedicles, and wedge of vertebral body removed.
- Anterior cortex acts as hinge.
- Closure through posterior compression.
Correction: 30°–40° per level.
Typical Level: L3 or L4 (safe zone; avoids thoracic cord).
Main Indications:
- Fixed sagittal imbalance
- Ankylosing spondylitis kyphosis
- Post-laminectomy or post-traumatic kyphosis with fusion
Advantages:
- Single-stage correction
- No need for mobile disc space
- Restores horizontal gaze
Pitfalls:
- Blood loss up to 2 L
- Neurological deficit (radiculopathy, cauda equina)
- Pseudoarthrosis (especially TL/L-S junction)
- Infection 4–7%
Technical Tips:
- Position patient with kyphotic table alignment.
- Fix ≥3 levels above and below osteotomy.
- Close osteotomy by straightening table gradually.

3⃣ Bone–Disc–Bone Osteotomy (BDBO)
Definition: Osteotomy through disc and adjacent endplates above and below —
includes the disc in the resection.

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| Correction | 35°–60° |
| Site | At the apex (CORA – Center of Rotation of Angulation) |
| Indication | Post-traumatic kyphosis, moderate rigid deformities |
| Limitation | Not possible when anterior disc is calcified (e.g., Ankylosing Spondylitis) |

Advantages:
- Correction at true apex of deformity.
- More correction than PSO.
Technique:
- Wide laminectomy + discectomy above and below.
- Removal of posterior elements + partial endplates.
- Insert cage/bone graft anteriorly → compress posteriorly.
Pitfalls:
- Vascular injury risk
- Technically demanding (steep learning curve)

4⃣ Vertebral Column Resection (VCR / PVCR)
Definition: Complete circumferential excision of one or more vertebral segments,
allowing translation and correction in all planes.
Types: - Anterior + Posterior (classic VCR)
- Posterior-only (PVCR) – currently preferred.
Indications: - Rigid multiplanar deformities
- Sharp angular deformities
- Hemivertebra excision
- Severe post-traumatic kyphosis
- Spinal tumors or infections with collapse
Technique: 1. Insert pedicle screws above and below.
2. Temporary rods applied to prevent translation.
3. Remove discs above & below → vertebral body resection.
4. Place anterior structural graft/cage → compress posteriorly for correction.

<!-- page 4 -->

Correction Achieved:
- 60° coronal / 70° sagittal plane.
Pitfalls:
- Blood loss up to 5 L
- High neurological risk (especially myelopathic patients)
- Translation injury due to inadequate control
- Delayed deficit from subluxation
- Longer operative time, infection risk
Technical Pearls:
- Always use temporary rods to prevent sudden translation.
- Start resection from convex side in scoliosis.
- Remove posterior wall last to minimize bleeding.

| Type I-B | Ankylosing Spondylitis | PSO |

| Type III-A/B | Severe combined loss | BDBO / VCR |
| Type III-C | Buckling collapse | PVCR + Stabilization |

### GENERAL COMPLICATIONS
- Massive blood loss (esp. PSO, VCR).

- Neurological deficit (stretch or translation injury).

- Infection and pseudoarthrosis.

- Implant failure.

- Dural tear.

- Vascular injury (especially in BDBO and VCR).

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Pontes/SPO = Posterior column osteotomy, needs mobile disc.

- PSO = 3-column wedge osteotomy, hinge = anterior cortex.

- BDBO = Osteotomy through disc + endplates, apex correction.

- VCR = Complete vertebral resection, no hinge → max correction.

- Correction per level: SPO – 10°, PSO – 30–40°, BDBO – 35–60°, VCR – 70°.

- Ankylosing spondylitis → PSO at L3/L4.

- Always use temporary rods in PVCR to avoid translation.

- Blood loss: SPO < PSO < BDBO < VCR.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Osteotomies are essential for rigid sagittal/coronal deformities uncorrectable by soft-tissue release.

- Choose technique based on column deficiency and disc mobility.

- Always plan levels, hinge, and correction angle preoperatively.

- PSO and VCR demand high surgical skill and neuro-monitoring.

- Goal: Stable, balanced spine with restored sagittal alignment and horizontal gaze.
