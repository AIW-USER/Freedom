Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Early Onset
Scoliosis: Diagnosis & Management
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Early Onset Scoliosis (EOS) = spinal deformity before 10 years of age.
Earlier definition was <5 years, but as treatment principles for <5 and 5–10 years are
similar, scoliosis <10 years is now classified as EOS.
Dr. Vishnu’s Quote:
“Adolescent scoliosis changes appearance – early onset scoliosis changes
physiology.”
Why Important:
Deformity occurs before full thoracic and lung development → risk of restrictive lung
disease and cardiorespiratory failure if untreated.
### Etiological Types: 1. Idiopathic
2. Congenital
3. Neuromuscular
4. Syndromic

### IDIOPATHIC SCOLIOSIS – SUBTYPES

| Type | Age Range |
|---|---|
| Infantile | 0–3 years |
| Juvenile | 4–9 years |
| Adolescent | 10–20 years |

Criteria for Idiopathic: - No vertebral structural anomaly.
- No spinal cord abnormality.
Deformity:
Three-dimensional — lateral curve + vertebral rotation + rib deformity.
Rotation Mechanics: - Vertebra rotates toward convex side.
- Convex ribs → prominent (rib hump).
- Concave ribs → crowded → restrictive lung disease.

CONGENITAL SCOLIOSIS – TYPES & PROGNOSIS
Two Major Mechanisms: 1. Defect of Formation
- Fully segmented hemivertebra – high progression risk.
- Semi-segmented – moderate risk.
- Incarcerated / Non-segmented – minimal risk. 2. Defect of Segmentation
- Unilateral bar → progressive scoliosis (arrested growth one side).
- Bilateral segmentation failure → block vertebra (stable).
Worst Prognosis:
- Fully segmented hemivertebra + contralateral bar.
- Rapid progression; requires early surgical correction.
Exam Pearl:
MCQ favorite – Worst prognosis in congenital scoliosis = hemivertebra with
contralateral bar.

OTHER ETIOLOGIES
Syndromic:
- Neurofibromatosis – Café-au-lait spots, axillary freckles, skin tags.
- Arthrogryposis multiplex congenita – multiple joint contractures.
Neuromuscular:
- Seen in Cerebral Palsy, SMA, etc. → rapid progression due to muscle imbalance.

### THORACIC & LUNG DEVELOPMENT

| Age | % Thoracic Growth | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Birth | 6% | — |
| 5 years | 30% | Rapid phase |
| 10 years | 50% | Half achieved |
| 10–Maturity | Doubles | Volume doubling |

Golden Period: Birth–8 years = critical for lung and thoracic cage development.
Alveolar Development:
- 10× increase till 4 years of age.
- Deformity <5 years → irreversible restrictive lung pattern.

RADIOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT
### 1⃣ Cobb’s Angle
- <20° → Observation.

- 25° → Intervention threshold.
### 2⃣ Rib Phase (Mehta’s Classification)

| Phase | Description | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Convex rib does not overlap vertebral body | Stable – Observe |
| Phase 2 | Convex rib overlaps vertebral body | Progressive – Intervene |

### 3⃣ Rib–Vertebral Angle Difference (RVAD)
- >20° → High chance of progression.
### 4⃣ Risser’s Grading (Iliac Apophysis)

| Grade | Description | Growth Stage |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | None visible | Immature |
| 1–2 | 25–50% ossification | Rapid growth phase |
| 3–4 | 75–100% | Slowing growth |
| 5 | Fully fused | Skeletally mature |

Other Growth Indicators:
- Triradiate cartilage closure – peak growth onset.
- Fusion of greater trochanteric epiphysis – near maturity.

MANAGEMENT STRATEGY
### Observation (Mild / Non-progressive)

| Criteria | Details |
|---|---|
| Cobb’s angle | <20° |
| RVAD | <20° |
| Rib phase | Phase 1 |
| Follow-up | Every 6 months X-ray |

### When to Intervene:
- Cobb’s >25°.

<!-- page 4 -->

- RVAD >20°.

- Rib phase 2.

- Curve progression >10° in 6 months or >20°/year.

CASTING (25°–40° CURVES)
Technique: Mehta’s EDF (Elongation–Derotation–Flexion) Cast. - Applied under GA
with traction.
- Molded from axilla to pelvis.
- Reapplied every 6 months.
- Works best for mid-thoracic & thoracolumbar curves.
Mechanism: Pressure over rib hump → derotation → correction.
Limitations: Ineffective for proximal thoracic (T1–T5) curves.
Casting Age Window: 6 months – 5 years (instrumentation difficult before 5 years).

### BRACING (Post-Casting or Mild Curves)

| Type | Best For | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Milwaukee (CTLSO) | Apex ≥T5 | Includes neck ring |
| Boston (TLSO) | Apex <T5 | Underarm type |

Protocol:
- Used after successful casting correction.
- Maintain until skeletal maturity.
- Transition to observation after Risser 4–5.

FUSIONLESS SURGERIES (>40° CURVES)
### Types of Systems

| Category | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Distraction-based | Growth rods (dual) | Periodic lengthening every 6 months |
| Compression-based | Arrest convex growth | Vertebral body stapling / tethering |
| Guided-growth | Sliding construct allows longitudinal growth | Shilla system |

<!-- page 5 -->

Key Principle:
Preserve growth → correct deformity without spinal fusion.

### Growth Rod Technique:
- Two anchors (proximal & distal), rods submuscularly tunneled.

- Every 6 months → 1–1.5 cm distraction.

- Dual rods preferred → stability, reduced breakage.

- Now advanced to Magnetic Growth Rods (MCGR) — external, non-invasive lengthening.
### Compression-Based Systems (Stapling/Tethering):
- Thoracoscopic insertion of staples along convex side → growth arrest → gradual correction.
Guided-Growth (Shilla):
- Screws at apex fused; upper and lower screws slide along rods → longitudinal spinal growth.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- EOS = deformity <10 yrs (definition updated).

- Etiology: Idiopathic / Congenital / Neuromuscular / Syndromic.

<!-- page 6 -->

- RVAD >20° or Rib Phase 2 → intervene.

- Casting = 25–40° (best <5 yrs).

- Fusionless surgery = >40° or progressive.

- Avoid posterior fusion <10 yrs → prevents thoracic growth.

- VEPTR → for rib fusion / thoracic insufficiency.

- Thoracic & lung growth 6% → 30% → 50% → 100%.

- Golden period: Birth–8 years.

- Untreated EOS → early restrictive lung disease & cardiac failure.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- EOS = deformity before 10 years → threatens lung growth.

- Identify early using RVAD & Rib Phase.

- Observation for mild, casting for moderate, fusionless surgery for severe.

- Congenital scoliosis with hemivertebra + bar = intervene early.

- Goal: Preserve spinal growth, thoracic volume, and respiratory function.
