Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Femoroacetabular Impingement (FAI)
Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### Definition & Synonyms
- Synonyms: Cervicoacetabular Impingement, Acetabular Rim Syndrome.
- Definition: Abnormal contact between the femoral head-neck junction and the acetabular rim during hip motion, particularly flexion and internal rotation, leading to labral tears and chondral damage.

### Etiology & Pathomechanics
- Occurs in patients with:
  - Abnormal hip morphology (structural deformity of head/neck or acetabulum)
  - Normal morphology but excessive range of motion (e.g., athletes, dancers)
- Repetitive impingement leads to:
  1. Labral damage
  2. Cartilage delamination
  3. Early osteoarthritis
Common in activities requiring deep hip flexion – e.g., squatting, Indian toilets,
gymnastics.

Clinical Features
Symptoms
- Age: 25–50 years (healthy, active adults)
- Pain: Groin pain worsens with hip flexion; difficulty sitting for long periods.
- Clicking/popping sensations → labral tear indicator.
- May have gluteal or trochanteric pain due to abnormal gait mechanics.
### Examination Findings
- Limited flexion (<90°) and internal rotation (<5°).
- Positive FADIR (Impingement Test):
  - Pain reproduced in Flexion, ADduction, Internal Rotation.
  - Mnemonic: FADIR → Flexion, Adduction, Internal Rotation.
- C-sign / Grip sign (Byrd sign): Patient grips lateral hip with hand in ‘C’ shape → suggests labral tear.

### Radiological Evaluation
1. X-ray Views
- AP Pelvis (hip internally rotated 15°)
- Dunn/Frog-leg Lateral (shows head-neck junction)
2. CAM-type Features
- Pistol Grip Deformity – loss of head sphericity on AP view.
- ↑ Alpha Angle (>50–55°) on frog-leg lateral view.
  - Measured between line along head–neck axis and line from head center to point where contour exits the circle.
- ↓ Head–Neck Offset Ratio (<0.17) – indicates bony bump at anterolateral junction.
3. Pincer-type Features
- Coxa Profunda: Acetabular floor touches Kohler’s (ilioischial) line.
- Protusio Acetabuli: Head lies medial to Kohler’s line.

<!-- page 3 -->

- Crossover Sign: Anterior acetabular wall crosses lateral to posterior wall → acetabular retroversion.
- Posterior Wall Sign: Head center lies lateral to posterior wall → also indicates retroversion.
- Posteroinferior Joint Space Narrowing: Secondary to counter-coping mechanism. MCQ Tip:
- First radiological sign in CAM → Anterolateral migration of femoral head.
- In PINCER → Posteroinferior joint space narrowing.
4. MRI / MRA
- Best for labral and cartilage lesions.
- Confirms head-neck morphology and calculates alpha angle.
- MRI arthrogram may show labral tears, cartilage delamination, or acetabular retroversion.

Management
Aim: Preserve hip, relieve pain, and prevent progression to arthritis.
1. Conservative Management
- Activity modification: Avoid deep flexion (Indian toilets, squatting, cross-legged sitting).
- NSAIDs & Physiotherapy: Core strengthening, ROM maintenance.
- Short course Oral Steroids: For acute inflammatory phase (symptom relief only).
2. Surgical Management
### A. Open Surgical Dislocation (Hip Preservation)
- Approaches:
  - Ganz (Trochanteric Flip) Approach: Posterior approach with trochanteric osteotomy → anterior dislocation preserving medial circumflex femoral artery.
  - Smith-Petersen (Anterior) Approach: Alternative; avoids main vascular injury.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Procedures:
  - Osteoplasty / Cheilectomy: Resection of bony bump (CAM) – do not resect >30% of neck width (risk of fracture).
  - Acetabular Rim Trimming / Reorientation Osteotomy: For pincer lesions (retroversion correction).
### B. Arthroscopic Management (Preferred Modern Technique)
- Indications: Symptomatic early-stage FAI with labral tear and preserved joint space.
- Setup:
  - Patient supine on fracture table; traction applied (10–12 mm joint distraction).
  - Portals:
    - Anterolateral (1 cm above & anterior to GT) → Viewing portal.

    - Posterolateral (1 cm above & posterior to GT) → Shuttling portal.

    - Mid-anterior portal (intersection of ASIS & GT lines) → Working portal.
- Precautions: Avoid injury to lateral femoral cutaneous, superior gluteal, and sciatic nerves.
- Procedures:
  - Osteoplasty of CAM lesion.
  - Rim trimming (pincer correction).
  - Labral repair or reattachment (if torn).
Key Steps in Arthroscopy: Traction → Visualization → Resection → Labral
repair → ROM check.

Prognosis
- If untreated → Early hip arthritis due to repetitive cartilage-labral trauma.
- Early identification allows hip preservation and delay of arthroplasty.
- Mixed type is most common (≈80%).

### Key MCQ & Exam Pearls
- Commonest Type: Mixed > CAM > Pincer.
- CAM: Young males, bony bump at head-neck junction, cartilage first → labrum later.
- PINCER: Middle-aged females, retroverted acetabulum, labrum first → cartilage later.

<!-- page 5 -->

- Impingement Test Position: Flexion, Adduction, Internal Rotation (FADIR).
- Alpha Angle >55° → CAM deformity.
- Head-Neck Offset Ratio <0.17 → CAM deformity.
- Coxa profunda, crossover & posterior wall sign → Pincer.
- Safe surgical dislocation: Preserves medial circumflex femoral artery.
- Arthroscopic portals: Anterolateral, Posterolateral, Mid-anterior.
- Do not resect >30% of anterolateral neck during osteoplasty.
