Median Nerve Palsy ("Labourer's Nerve") and
Management – Dr. Priyanka Sharma (Nov 2025)
### Overview / Key Concepts
- Anatomy and branches, clinical signs, compression sites (carpal tunnel, pronator syndrome, anterior interosseous syndrome), nerve repair and transfers, high vs low palsy, and tendon transfers (flexion restoration and opponensplasty).
- Continues the series after radial nerve palsy; the principles of nerve repair and tendon transfer are the same for every nerve.

Anatomy
- Origin: lateral cord (C5, C6, C7) + medial cord (C8, T1).
- Arm: no branches; runs with the brachial artery, crossing it from lateral to medial in front of it.
- Cubital fossa: medial to the brachial artery, deep to the bicipital aponeurosis (lacertus fibrosus), in front of brachialis.
- Forearm: passes between the humeral and ulnar heads of pronator teres, under the fibrous arch of FDS, runs on FDP; about 5 cm above the flexor retinaculum it lies between FCR and palmaris longus.
- Hand: through the carpal tunnel deep to the flexor retinaculum.

| Level | Branches |
|---|---|
| Cubital fossa / proximal forearm | Superficial flexors: pronator teres, FCR, palmaris longus, FDS |
| Anterior interosseous nerve (about 4 cm below the elbow) | Deep flexors: FPL, FDP to index and middle, pronator quadratus |
| Palmar cutaneous branch (just above the flexor retinaculum) | Passes SUPERFICIAL to the retinaculum (not through the tunnel): sensation to the thenar and mid-palm |
| Recurrent (motor) branch in the hand | Thenar muscles: APB, FPB (superficial head), opponens pollicis |
| Digital branches | Lumbricals 1 and 2; sensation to the radial 3.5 digits (palmar side) and their dorsal tips (distal and middle phalanges) |

Carpal tunnel and Kaplan cardinal line
- Carpal tunnel contents: 9 flexor tendons (4 FDS, 4 FDP, FPL) + the median nerve.
- Kaplan cardinal line: hand flat, thumb slightly abducted; line from the apex of the first web space (ulnar border of the thumb) towards the hook of hamate/hypothenar area, parallel to the

### Clinical Signs and Tests

| Sign / test | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Hand of benediction | High lesion: index and middle cannot flex (FDS + FDP loss) on trying to make a fist |
| Pointing index (clasping) test | Index stays straight when both hands are clasped |
| O sign (Kiloh-Nevin) | AIN palsy: cannot make an "O" with thumb and index tip (FPL and index FDP weak): pinch with pulp flat |
| Ape thumb | Long-standing palsy: thenar wasting, thumb adducted and extended, first metacarpal in the plane of the others |
| Pen test | APB: hand flat, patient abducts the thumb up to touch a pen held above it |
| Phalen / reverse Phalen | Carpal tunnel compression |
| Sensory loss | Radial 3.5 digits; dry, cool, atrophic skin |

### Compression Sites

| Syndrome | Site / cause | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Carpal tunnel syndrome (most common; low palsy) | Under the transverse carpal ligament; ageing, pregnancy, hypothyroidism, rheumatoid arthritis | Numbness of thumb and index at night and early morning; APB weakness; Phalen positive |
| Pronator syndrome | Between the heads of pronator teres, arch of FDS, lacertus fibrosus; repetitive pronation (sport, work) | No morning symptoms; worse with activity; no Tinel at the wrist; paraesthesia in the palmar triangle (palmar cutaneous area); pain over pronator teres |
| Anterior interosseous syndrome | Pronator teres, FDS, aberrant structures | Forearm pain, weak fine pinch (FPL, index FDP); no sensory loss |

- For all compression neuropathies: 4–8 weeks of observation first (activity change, splints that do not cause disuse, treat pregnancy/hypothyroid oedema), then decompression if it fails.

Nerve transfers
- Motor: brachialis branch of the musculocutaneous nerve to the median nerve (fascicles in the arm: lateral sensory, medial motor); ECRB branch of the radial nerve to the AIN.
- Sensory: fascicles from the dorsal branch of the ulnar nerve to the median digital territory; autografts to reconstruct the donor.

### Tendon Transfers
- Indications: leprosy, nerve not amenable to repair, old age, comorbidities.
- Principles: good skin cover, protective sensation, full passive ROM, expendable donor, synergistic action (tenodesis), donor power at least grade 4/5 (expect a loss of one grade), straight line of pull, one pulley, one tendon for one function.
- Junctions are woven with the Pulvertaft technique; tension adjusted by the tenodesis effect.

Low palsy: opponensplasty
- Ideal donor tension and length close to APB; avoid tendon grafts; use a pulley (pisiform/FCU region) for the line of pull. Set tension with the wrist neutral and thumb in opposition; tenodesis check: thumb abducts with wrist extension.

| Procedure | Donor | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Burkhalter | Extensor indicis (EIP; PIN) | Divided at insertion (repair the extensor expansion to avoid extensor lag); routed around the ULNAR border of the wrist subcutaneously to APB/EPL/MP capsule; POP in opposition 4 weeks, then protective splint 3–4 weeks |
| Bunnell | FDS of ring finger | Divided between A1 and A2, delivered in the forearm, looped through an FCU pulley, tunnelled to APB. STRONGEST transfer (flexor to flexor). Not in combined ulnar palsy; risk of swan neck of the ring finger |
| Camitz | Palmaris longus with a strip of palmar fascia | To APB; for elderly and carpal tunnel cases (done with release); gives abduction more than true opposition; PL may be absent |
| Miller | ECU | Rerouted around the ulnar border; APB divided at origin and turned as a distally based tendon to meet it |
| Huber | Abductor digiti minimi (islanded on its neurovascular bundle) | Children (congenital thumb hypoplasia, Blauth 3); turned 180° like a page of a book, adds thenar bulk |

- Insertion techniques into the thumb: Brand, Littler, Riordan, Royle-Thompson (ulnar border of palmar aponeurosis as pulley).

### Viva Questions to Prepare
- Incisions to harvest and insert, choice of pulley, tension setting, splintage position, mobilisation/hand therapy, and management of combined median and ulnar palsy.

### Exam & Viva Pearls
- Median nerve = lateral + medial cords (C5–T1); no branches in the arm.
- AIN: FPL, index/middle FDP, PQ; O sign; no sensory loss.
- Palmar cutaneous branch spares sensation in carpal tunnel syndrome.
- Pronator syndrome: palmar triangle numbness, no night symptoms.
- Graft for gaps over 2.5 cm; sural nerve most used.
- Bunnell ring FDS = strongest opponensplasty; Huber ADM for children; Camitz PL for the elderly.

### Take-Home Summary
- Localise the lesion (high, AIN, low) from the motor and sensory map, repair or transfer nerves early when possible, and use tendon transfers following the classic principles to restore flexion and opposition when nerve recovery is not expected.
