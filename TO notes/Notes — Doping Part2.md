### Doping Control Procedure
1. Notification of Athlete:
- The process begins with notifying the athlete for doping control.
- Can occur in-competition or out-of-competition.
- Once notified, the athlete must report to the Doping Control Station (DCS) within 60
minutes.
2. Reporting to Doping Control Station:
- Athlete reports to DCS within 60 minutes of notification.
- Allowed to complete press conference, training, or hydration before reporting but must
be under constant observation by the chaperone.
3. Sample Collection:
- Samples can be urine or blood.
- Urine samples must be collected under direct supervision of a chaperone of the
same gender.
- Undressing: from nipple to mid-thigh to ensure no tampering.
4. Selection of Collection Vessel:
- Athlete selects the vessel and Doping Control Kit themselves.
- Ensures no tampering and maintains chain of custody.
5. Provision of Sample:
- Minimum urine required: 90 ml (range 90–110 ml).
- If specific gravity is low, athlete may be advised to hydrate gradually (max 1.5 L fluids
allowed).
- Blood sample collection performed by Blood Collection Officer (BCO) — minimum
20 ml (10 ml each tube).
6. Sample Division and Sealing:
- Athlete divides sample into A and B bottles and seals them personally.
- Sealing done in tamper-evident kits.
7. Specific Gravity Measurement:
- Measured by refractometer or lab sticks by the Doping Control Officer (DCO).
- Minimum accepted specific gravity:
- 1.010 (lab stick) or 1.003 (refractometer).
8. Documentation:
- Athlete fills Doping Control Form (DCF): includes personal info, medications,

supplements, and observations.
- Athlete, DCO, and representative sign the form.
- Athlete retains a copy; lab copy carries only code number, not name.

Roles and Responsibilities
Doping Control Officer (DCO):
- Sets up and manages DCS.
- Ensures supplies and forms are available.
- Monitors athlete notification, collection, and sealing.
- Samples must never be left unattended.
Blood Control Officer (BCO):
- Draws blood samples, ensures aftercare (athlete rests 10–20 min).
- Minimum 20 ml sample (10 ml each tube).
Chaperone:
- Notifies athlete, accompanies to DCS, supervises sample collection.
- Must be same gender.
- Assists with minors or disabled athletes.

Special Scenarios
Minor or Disabled Athlete:
- May have a representative assist in mobility, collection, or signing forms.
- Visually impaired athletes: representative reads and signs DCF.
Unable to Locate Athlete:
- If athlete not found within 60 min — Whereabouts Failure.
- 3 such failures in 12 months = Anti-Doping Rule Violation (ADRV) (Article 2.4).
Advance Notice Testing:
- Not usually allowed. Exceptions: minors, disabled, high-security areas, or bio-bubble
situations.

### Sample Handling and Analysis
- Samples sealed and transported to WADA-accredited laboratory within 24 hrs.

- Must be kept at controlled room temperature (not frozen).

- Two samples: A (30 ml) and B (60 ml).
If Sample A is positive → termed AAF (Adverse Analytical Finding).
- NADA decodes athlete name and checks for TUE (Therapeutic Use Exemption).

<!-- page 3 -->

If Sample B also positive → athlete notified and provisionally suspended.
- Decision made by Anti-Doping Disciplinary Panel (ADDP).
- Athlete can appeal within 14 days to the Anti-Doping Appeal Panel (ADAP).

Therapeutic Use Exemption (TUE)
Definition: Authorization for athletes to use banned substances for legitimate medical
reasons.
### 4 Criteria for Granting TUE:
1. Drug required to treat a diagnosed medical condition.
2. No significant performance enhancement from use.
3. No reasonable therapeutic alternative.
4. Use not due to prior banned substance use.
Application Process:
- Submit medical documents (reports, imaging, dosage, route, duration).
- Apply to NADA or respective International Federation at least 30 days before
event.
- For emergencies → retroactive TUE.
Duration: Temporary; must be renewed periodically.

### Intravenous Infusions & Injections
- >100 mL/12 hrs = prohibited method (even if normal saline).

- Exceptions (no TUE required): hospital treatment, surgery, clinical diagnostic procedures.

- For non-hospital cases → TUE required.

- Fluids should be at room temperature, not refrigerated.
Retroactive TUE allowed if:
- Emergency (e.g., dehydration, anaphylaxis).
- Life-saving treatment given before approval possible.
