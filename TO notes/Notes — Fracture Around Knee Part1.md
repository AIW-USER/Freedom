Fractures Around the Knee – Part 1 – Dr. Anurag
Tiwari
Overview
This lecture covers Distal Femur Fractures and Tibial Plateau Fractures. Both are
intra articular injuries requiring precise anatomical reduction, careful planning, and strict
attention to soft tissue status.
Faculty Remark: “Never decide treatment from the X ray alone. Look at the limb
first. Soft tissue decides your plan.”

## Distal Femur Fractures
Definition
- Fracture within transcondylar width of the knee (square drawn between medial

Epidemiology
and lateral epicondyles).

- Young males: high energy trauma.
- Elderly females: osteoporosis related fractures.

Surgical Anatomy
### Key Points
- Distal femur is trapezoidal (anterior surface narrower than posterior).
- Medial condyle is more distal than lateral, producing physiological valgus.
- Lateral surface is angled 10 degrees; medial 25 degrees.
### Vascular Anatomy
- Femoral vessels enter posterior compartment through adductor hiatus, 10 to 12 cm above knee.
- Medial plating must respect this zone.
### Muscle Displacement Pattern

| Muscle | Effect |
|---|---|
| Quadriceps & Hamstrings | Shortening |
| Gastrocnemius | Apex posterior angulation |
| Adductor magnus | Varus displacement |

Clinical Relevance: Posterior spike of distal fragment may injure popliteal artery.
Classification (AO – Most Used)
### AO 33
- A – Extra articular
  - A1 simple, A2 wedge, A3 comminuted
- B – Partial articular
  - B1 lateral condyle, B2 medial condyle, B3 Hoffa (coronal)
- C – Complete articular
  - C1 simple, C2 metaphyseal comminution, C3 intra articular comminution

### Clinical Evaluation
- Pain, swelling, deformity, inability to bear weight.
- Always check:
  - Distal pulses (risk of popliteal artery injury)
  - Neurology
  - Open wounds
  - Skin condition and blisters

Imaging
X ray
- AP and lateral usually sufficient.
- Hoffa fractures missed in 30 to 40 percent on X ray.
### CT Scan
- Mandatory in intra articular fractures.
- Determines fracture personality and identifies coronal fractures.

### Principles of Management
- Almost all cases need surgery unless undisplaced or medically unfit.
- Options:
  - Plate (DFLCP, DCS, Blade plate)
  - Retrograde distal femoral nail
  - External fixator (damage control)

<!-- page 3 -->

Plate Fixation – Key Concepts
### Blade Plate
- Technically demanding, obsolete.
### Dynamic Condylar Screw (DCS)
- Large diameter screw (12.5 mm), bone consuming.
- Needs at least 4 cm intact distal fragment.
### Distal Femur Locking Plate (DFLCP)
- Gold standard.
- Combi holes allow locking and non locking screws.
### Surgical Approaches

| Approach | Use |
|---|---|
| Lateral | Plate application, metaphyseal work |
| Lateral parapatellar | Best for intra articular visualization |
| Anterolateral (Schwarzbuckler) | Combined benefits of both above |
| Medial parapatellar / Subvastus | Medial column fractures |

### Reduction Tips
- Use K wires or Steinmann pin as joystick.
- Correct apex posterior angulation with knee flexion and bolster.
- Distal most screw must be parallel to articular surface.
- Anterior screws must be short due to trapezoidal anatomy.
- Insert non locking screws first, then locking.
### Far Cortical Locking (FCL)
- Reduces construct stiffness.
- Promotes callus formation.
### LISS / MIPO
- Minimally invasive technique preserving biology.

Retrograde Distal Femur Nail
### Entry Point
- AP view: just anterior and medial to PCL attachment.
- Lateral view: at apex of Blumensaat line.

<!-- page 4 -->

Advantages
- Load sharing, biologically friendly.
- Less soft tissue disruption.
### Nail Length
- Should reach up to or above lesser trochanter.

### Indications for External Fixator
- Damage control orthopaedics.
- Grade III open fractures.
- Severe soft tissue swelling, blisters.
- Vascular injury requiring urgent stabilization.
- Pins must be placed far from future incision line.

### Complications (Distal Femur)
- Malunion
- Stiffness
- Symptomatic hardware
- Non union
- Infection
- Implant failure

## Tibial Plateau Fractures
Most Important Principle
Dr. Anurag: “Tibial plateau treatment is guided by soft tissue status, not X rays.”
Epidemiology
- Bimodal: young males (high energy), elderly females (osteoporosis).

Anatomy
- Lateral plateau is convex and higher.
- Medial plateau is concave and lower.
- Posterior tibial slope: 3 to 10 degrees (average 7).

<!-- page 5 -->

Mechanism

| Force | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Valgus + axial | Lateral split, split depression |
| Varus | Medial plateau |
| Flexion + Varus + IR | Posteromedial fragment |

### Clinical Evaluation
- Soft tissue examination is critical.
- Look for:
  - Open wounds
  - Blisters
  - Compartment syndrome
  - Distal pulses
  - Neurology

Imaging
X ray
- Best AP view obtained with 10 to 15 degree caudal tilt (beam parallel to slope).
### CT Scan
- Essential for fracture mapping.
- Determines depression, split, posteromedial involvement.
- Always done after spanning external fixator.
### MRI – Indications
- Suspected occult fracture.
- Associated meniscal or ligament injury.

Classifications
### Schatzker Classification (Most Used)
1. Split
2. Split + depression (most common)
3. Depression only
4. Medial condyle
5. Bicondylar
6. Metaphyseal diaphyseal dissociation

<!-- page 6 -->

Limitations
- Only AP dependent.
- Does not classify posterior fragments.
### Three Column Classification (Luo)
- Based on CT axial cuts.
- Columns: Medial, Lateral, Posterior.
- Zero column: pure depression (Schatzker III).
### Approach Based on Columns

| Column | Approach |
|---|---|
| Lateral | Anterolateral |
| Medial/Postromedial | Posteromedial |
| Posterolateral | Frosch approach |

### Treatment Principles
- Soft tissue dictates timing.
- Span, Scan, Plan protocol:
  1. Knee spanning external fixator.
  2. CT scan in reduced position.
  3. Definitive fixation.
### Treatment by Schatzker Type

| Type | Management |
|---|---|
| I Split | Percutaneous CC screws or plate |
| II Split depression | Elevation + graft + plate |
| III Depression | Elevation + graft + raft screws |
| IV Medial | Medial plating |
| V Bicondylar | Dual plating |
| VI Shaft dissociation | Dual plating |

Surgical Approaches
### Anterolateral Approach
- Incision 1 cm lateral to tibial crest.
- Submeniscal arthrotomy for direct articular visualization.
### Posteromedial Approach
- Knee flexion, hip external rotation.

<!-- page 7 -->

- Incision 1 to 2 cm posterior to medial border of tibia.
- Buttress plating of posteromedial fragment.
### Posterolateral (Frosch) Approach
- Between biceps femoris and lateral gastrocnemius.
- Protect common peroneal nerve.
- Plate should not extend more than 5 cm from joint line.

### Articular Reduction Techniques
- Direct reduction through submeniscal arthrotomy.
- C arm guided indirect reduction.
- Arthroscopy assisted reduction.
### Elevation of Depression
- Via medial metaphyseal window.
- Fill defect with autograft, allograft, or Ca phosphate substitute.

### External Fixator – Indications
- Blisters
- Open fractures
- Compartment syndrome
- Severe swelling
- Two stage protocol recommended.

### Complications (Tibial Plateau)
- Malunion
- Post traumatic arthritis
- Compartment syndrome
- Non union
- Stiffness
- Infection
- Symptomatic hardware

### Viva and Exam Pearls
- Distal femur: Hoffa is a coronal plane fracture.
- In distal femur plating: distal screw parallel to joint surface.
- Tibial plateau: soft tissue status is paramount.
- Caudal tilt AP X ray improves visualization.

<!-- page 8 -->

- Schatzker II most common.
- Three column helps plan approach.
- Span–Scan–Plan for high energy injuries.

Final Faculty Insight: “Know your soft tissues, know your anatomy, and every
fixation becomes logical.”
