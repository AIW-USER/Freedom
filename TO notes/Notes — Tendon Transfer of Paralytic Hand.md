Tendon Transfer of Paralytic Hand – Dr. Amit Mittal
Overview / Key Concepts
Tendon transfer in paralytic hand is a functional reconstructive strategy, not an
anatomic restoration. The aim is to convert available muscle power into useful hand
function, especially in patients with tetraplegia due to cervical spinal cord injury.
The success of tendon transfer depends on: - Accurate neurological level assessment -
Identification of muscles with Grade 4 or more power - Clear understanding of
functional priorities - Appropriate sequencing of tendon transfers and tenodesis

What is a Paralytic Hand?
- Paralytic hand commonly refers to tetraplegia, where both upper and lower limbs are affected
- Typically results from cervical spinal cord injury
- Lower limb injury alone does not cause paralytic hand
### Key Concept
- Upper limb paralysis in spinal cord injury is due to involvement of anterior horn cells and their longitudinal columns
- Sensory involvement depends on width of cord injury, not just length

### Neurological Basis
- Motor nuclei of anterior horn cells are arranged cephalocaudal (C5 → T1)
- Predictable muscle involvement based on level of injury
### Segmental Muscle Functions (Simplified)

| Segment | Major Function |
|---|---|
| C5 | Elbow flexion |
| C6 | Wrist extension |
| C7 | Elbow extension |
| C8 | Finger flexion |
| T1 | Intrinsic hand muscles |

Above the injured segment: normal function At the level: absent or weak function Below
the level: flaccid or spastic muscles

### Metamere Concept
- Metamere is the entire longitudinal and transverse segment of injured spinal cord
- Explains variability in muscle involvement
- Determines whether paralysis is pure motor or sensorimotor

### Muscle Strength and Recovery
- Muscle grading is essential
- Only Grade 4 or higher muscles are considered functional and transferable
### Recovery Timeline
- Grade 2 power at 1 month → full recovery by ~6 months
- Grade 1 power at presentation → may recover to Grade 3–4 by 1 year
- Triceps recovery is unpredictable
Final reconstruction should be planned after 1 year, when neurological recovery
plateaus

Functional Muscle Grouping (Dr. Mittal Classification)
Patients are classified based on the most distal muscle having Grade 4 power

| Group | Muscle with Grade ≥4 |
|---|---|
| Group 0 | No muscle below elbow |
| Group 1 | Brachioradialis |
| Group 2 | ECRL |
| Group 3 | ECRB |
| Group 4 | Pronator teres |
| Group 5 | FCR |
| Group 6 | EDC |
| Group 7 | EPL |
| Group 8 | FDP |
| Group 9 | FPL |

If a distal muscle has Grade 4 power, all proximal muscles are assumed functional

### Principles of Tendon Transfer
1. Donor muscle must have Grade ≥4 power
2. Donor should have expendable or duplicate function
3. One tendon, one function
4. Straight line of pull preferred
5. Synergistic transfer improves outcomes
A muscle should never be transferred if it is the only muscle performing a critical
function

Functional Priorities in Paralytic Hand
Order of reconstruction:
1. Elbow extension
2. Wrist extension
3. Thumb stability and pinch
4. Finger grasp
5. Thumb extension
6. Finger extension
Active movement is restored first, remaining functions are supplemented by tenodesis

### Tenodesis Concept
- Passive tightening of tendons across the wrist
- Wrist motion produces finger and thumb movement
### Common Tenodesis Applications
- Finger flexion: FDP, FDS tightened across wrist
- Thumb flexion: FPL tenodesis
- Thumb extension: EPL tenodesis
- Finger extension: EDC tenodesis
Tenodesis is weaker than active transfer but functionally useful
### Management of Claw Hand (Intrinsic Minus Hand)
- MP joint hyperextension
- IP joint flexion
### Correction Strategy
- FDS tenodesis across wrist
- Restores MP flexion and prevents clawing

Elbow Extension Restoration
Options
- Posterior deltoid to triceps transfer
- Biceps rerouting
- Triceps reconstruction when partial power exists
Elbow extension is the single most important function for independent transfers

### Wrist Extension Restoration
- PT to ECRB transfer
- Essential for effective tenodesis of fingers
Without wrist extension, hand grasp is not functional

### Contraindications to Tendon Transfer
- Fixed joint contractures
- Poor passive range of motion

<!-- page 5 -->

- Ongoing neurological recovery (<1 year)
- Poor patient motivation or compliance

### Role of Neuroprosthesis
- Electrical stimulation of intact reflex arcs
- Used when donor muscles are limited
- Electrodes stimulate transferred or native muscles
- Adjunct to tendon transfer, not a replacement

Faculty Quote
“In paralytic hand surgery, we do not restore anatomy. We restore priority-based
function.” – Dr. Amit Mittal

### Exam Pearls
- Tendon transfer is planned only after neurological recovery stabilizes
- Grade 4 muscle power is mandatory for transfer
- Functional classification is based on the most distal Grade 4 muscle
- Elbow extension is the first functional priority
- Wrist extension is essential for tenodesis grip
- Tenodesis provides passive but useful hand function
- Contracture is an absolute contraindication to tendon transfer
