CONCEPT OF KINETIC CHAIN
Definition: The kinetic chain refers to a series of interconnected segments (links) that
work together to generate and transfer energy efficiently during motion.
Functional Components: 1. Optimized Anatomy: Strength, flexibility, and power of
functional segments.
2. Sequential Force Generation: Energy transferred from one link to another in proper sequence.
3. Efficient Motor Patterns: Ensures minimal energy loss and proper coordination.
Key Links in Throwing:
Feet → Lower Extremities → Hip & Pelvis → Trunk → Scapulothoracic Articulation →
Shoulder → Elbow → Wrist → Hand.
Each segment functions independently but must coordinate synchronously
to maximize energy transfer.

HIP–SHOULDER SEPARATION (HSS)
Definition: The rotational angle created between the hips and torso during the stride
and wind-up phases.
- Acts like a rubber band storing elastic energy.

- Efficient release → accelerates torso toward hips → whip-like kinetic transfer.
- Range: 30–55° (peak up to 67°). Efficient “closing of the gap” allows better sequencing, anatomical positioning, and energy transfer.
If inefficient:
- Caused by poor hip deceleration or timing errors.
- Leads to improper load transfer and shoulder/elbow overload.

### PROXIMAL-TO-DISTAL SEQUENCING
- Motion starts at proximal segments → progresses to distal ones.
- Each distal link activates when proximal velocity peaks.

<!-- page 3 -->

- Enhances endpoint speed and throwing efficiency.
- Disturbance = loss of velocity, injury, or overuse stress. In throwers, efficient sequencing means maximum velocity with minimum joint strain.

### BIOMECHANICAL FACTORS IN KINETIC CHAIN

| Component | Key Role |
|---|---|
| Core Strength | Transfers energy between lower and upper extremities. |
| Hip Strength/ROM | Generates ~50% of total throwing energy. |
| Scapular Kinematics | Maintains glenohumeral stability and positioning. |
| Shoulder Strength & ROM | Enables safe acceleration and deceleration. |
| Knee & Ankle Mobility | Provides stable base for force initiation. |

Deficits in any of these → faulty energy transfer → shoulder/elbow stress.

KINETIC CHAIN IN THROWING PHASES
### 1⃣ Wind-Up Phase
- Begins with initial movement; ends when lead leg reaches max knee height (“balance point”).

<!-- page 4 -->

- Lead leg: Iliopsoas, rectus femoris, pectineus, sartorius elevate leg.

- Stance leg: Ankle joint provides stability and proprioception.
Faults: - Chronic ankle instability → postural sway → poor balance.
- Dorsiflexion restriction → altered COG → risk of kinetic misalignment.
Pelvic & Hip Role: - TFL, gluteus medius/minimus contract isometrically to stabilize
pelvis. - Poor lumbopelvic control → ↑ shoulder torque & elbow valgus stress.
Weak glutes or core = faulty wind-up → cascading injury risk.
Corrective Exercises:
Banded lateral walks, Bird-dogs, Forward/lateral lunges (preferably on a raised
platform).

### 2⃣ Stride Phase
- Begins with shoulder horizontal abduction (~90°) → ends with front foot contact.
- Muscle activation: Ankles, hips, core, thoracic spine, shoulder engage sequentially.
- Deficiency → “Catch-up phenomenon” → compensatory shoulder load.
Key Issues: - Poor lumbar control, limited thoracic rotation, weak core. - May cause
repetitive lumbar stress fractures, disc degeneration, or paraspinal spasm.
Rehab Focus:
Maintain neutral spine, encourage thoracic mobility & lumbar stability.

### 3⃣ Cocking Phase
- From foot strike to maximum external rotation (MER) (150–180°).

- Early Cocking: Pelvic rotation begins → trunk rotation lags → core stabilization essential.
- Late Cocking: Energy transfer from trunk → arm.
Muscle Activity: - Deltoid (maintains 90° abduction). - Rotator cuff resists compressive
load. - Scapular stabilizers (levator scapulae, serratus anterior, trapezius, rhomboids)
maintain glenoid stability.
Common Injury Link: Scapular Dyskinesis → impingement risk.
“Most shoulder injuries start with a scapula that forgot its job.”
Correction: Serratus strengthening, lower trap activation, pec minor & levator
stretching.

<!-- page 5 -->

### 4⃣ Late Cocking Phase
- Trunk energy transferred to throwing arm → horizontal adduction.
- External rotators: Infraspinatus, teres minor (concentric).

- Internal rotators: Subscapularis, teres major, pectoralis major (eccentric control).
Thrower’s Paradox: - Shoulder capsule must be loose enough for external rotation
yet stable enough to avoid subluxation.
Prevention: - Improve ROM in hip/thoracic spine instead of excessive GH rotation.
- Strengthen biceps tendon support.

### 5⃣ Acceleration Phase
- From MER → ball release.
- Trunk flexes forward via rectus abdominis & obliques.
- Arm internally rotates; elbow extends concentrically (triceps).
- Peak arm velocity ≈ 7000°/s → one of the fastest human movements.
Key Points: - Lag between shoulder rotation and elbow extension reduces inertia.
- Wrist flexors bring hand from hyperextension → neutral. - Rotator cuff & scapular
muscles stabilize joint.
This is the most injury-prone phase due to high torque.
### 6⃣ Deceleration Phase
- From ball release → end of humeral internal rotation.
- Muscles: Teres minor, infraspinatus, posterior deltoid (eccentric control).

- Biceps & brachialis: Slow elbow extension.

- Trapezius & rhomboids: Stabilize scapula.
### 7⃣ Follow-Through Phase
- Completion of motion; all segments link for smooth energy dissipation.
- Joint load is minimal → lowest injury risk.

MODERN THROWING ANALYSIS
### Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
- Ball Speed

- Stride Length

- Ground Reaction Forces (Lead & Back Leg)

- Shoulder Abduction / Rotation Angles

- Hip–Shoulder Separation

- Pelvic & Trunk Rotation Velocity

- Elbow & Knee Angular Velocities

- Thorax Angular Velocity & Trunk Flexion Angles
Tools: Motion capture systems, marker-based analysis, surface EMG.

### RETURN-TO-SPORT CRITERIA
1. Pain-free & no swelling.

2. Restored ROM & strength (within 10% of contralateral side).

3. Normal scapular kinematics.

4. Asymptomatic plyometric progression.

5. Interval throwing with workload monitoring.

6. Mechanical & kinetic reassessment before full competition. “Return when the chain moves as one — not when the pain just stops.”
### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Kinetic chain = ground-up coordination of anatomy, sequence & control.

- 50% of throwing power originates from hip & core.

<!-- page 7 -->

- Poor hip ROM or weak glutes → shoulder & elbow overload.

- Scapular control is the cornerstone of shoulder preservation.

- Focus rehab on core, glutes, scapular stability, and sequencing drills. “Train the chain — from ground to grip.”
