Benign Bone Tumors – Dr. Rajat Gupta
Overview
Benign bone tumors encompass a wide spectrum of lesions ranging from simple cysts
to biologically aggressive tumors. Diagnosis relies on integrating clinical features,
radiological patterns, and pathology correlation. Management varies from simple
observation to extended curettage and reconstructive procedures.
Faculty Remark: “Principles are easy to mug up, but their practical application
makes all the difference.”

### Cystic Lesions
- Unicameral bone cyst (UBC)

### Fibrous Lesions

### Cartilaginous Lesions

## Cystic Bone Lesions
Unicameral Bone Cyst (UBC)
### Key Features
- First two decades.
- Most common sites: proximal humerus, proximal femur in children; ilium and calcaneum in adults.
- Often incidental or presents with pain or pathological fracture.
- Fallen fragment sign indicates fracture.
Radiology
- Geographical, central, metaphyseal or metaphyseodiaphyseal.
- Narrow transition zone.
- No periosteal reaction or soft tissue mass.
Treatment
- Upper limb, asymptomatic: Observation.
- Symptomatic: Aspiration plus scraping (Q‑Ropsy). Optional BMA or steroids.
- Lower limb pathological fracture: Curettage + bone grafting + fixation.

Aneurysmal Bone Cyst (ABC)
### Key Features
- First two decades; female predominance.
- Common sites: proximal humerus, proximal tibia, distal femur; spine posterior elements.
- Presents with pain, swelling, or pathological fracture.
Radiology
- Geographical, eccentrically located.
- Honeycomb appearance.
- MRI shows fluid-fluid levels.
- USP6 gene rearrangement common.
Differentials
- UBC
- Telangiectatic osteosarcoma
- GCT
- Chondroblastoma

<!-- page 3 -->

Treatment
- Gold standard: Sclerotherapy (3% polidocanol).
- Curopsy (limited curettage) also effective.
- Curettage + grafting for pathological fractures.

## Fibrous Lesions
Fibrous Dysplasia
### Key Features
- Any bone; long bones, ribs, pelvis, craniofacial common.
- Monoostotic or polyostotic.
- Associated syndromes:
  - McCune Albright: Café au lait + endocrine.
  - Mazabraud: Myxomas.

Radiology
- Classic ground glass appearance.
- Parrot beak sign (fatigue fracture femoral neck).
- Rind sign – thick sclerotic rim.
Treatment
- Observation.
- Bisphosphonates for pain.
- Prophylactic fixation for high stress locations.
- Curettage + cortical graft or allograft.

Non Ossifying Fibroma (NOF)
### Key Features
- Children and adolescents.
- Metaphyseal cortical lesion.
- Incidental finding; can be multifocal.
Radiology
- Eccentric, well defined, sclerotic/creanated margins.
- Bunch of grapes appearance.
- No marrow edema.

<!-- page 4 -->

Treatment
- Observation.
- Curettage + grafting if large or fractured.

Osteofibrous Dysplasia (OFD)
### Key Features
- Children; diaphyseal tibia > fibula.
- Painless swelling, tibial bowing.
- Cortical lesion.
Treatment
- Observation.
- Correction for deformity.
- Conservative management for fractures.

## Cartilaginous Lesions
Osteochondroma
### Key Features
- Most common benign bone tumor.
- Cartilage-capped bony projection; sessile or pedunculated.
- Around knee and proximal humerus common.
- Hereditary multiple exostosis (HME): EXT1/EXT2 mutations.
Symptoms
- Painless swelling.
- Cosmetic concerns.
- Mechanical symptoms.
- Malignant transformation: 1 percent in solitary, 5 percent in HME.
Radiology
- Marrow continuity is hallmark.
- MRI: cartilage cap thickening suggests malignancy.
Treatment
- Observation.
- En bloc resection if symptomatic or malignant transformation.

<!-- page 5 -->

Enchondroma
### Key Features
- Second most common benign bone tumor.
- Hand bones most common; may be multiple (Ollier’s disease).
- Can be associated with Maffucci syndrome.
Radiology
- Tubular bones: purely lucent or popcorn calcification.
- Small bones may show cortical erosion.
- Long bones: popcorn calcification.
- Malignant transformation features:
  - Cortical erosion
  - 5 cm size
  - Endosteal scalloping
  - Marrow edema
  - Soft tissue mass
Treatment
- Observation.
- Extended curettage if symptomatic.

## Bone Forming Lesions
Osteoid Osteoma
### Key Features
- Young patients.
- Night pain relieved by NSAIDs.
Radiology
- CT is gold standard.
- Nidus with vessel sign.
- Bone scan: double density sign.
Treatment
- CT guided RFA is gold standard.
- CT guided percutaneous drilling as alternative.

<!-- page 6 -->

Osteoblastoma
### Key Features
- Second or third decade.
- Spine posterior elements common.
- Radiology variable: lytic, sclerotic or mixed.
Treatment
- Extended curettage.

## Fatty Lesions
Intraosseous Lipoma
### Key Features
- Well defined lytic lesion.
- Calcaneum, proximal tibia, proximal femur common.
- Central calcification: cockade sign.
Treatment
- Observation.

## Chondroblastoma
### Key Features
- Codman tumor.
- Ages 10–25.
- Epiphyseal or apophyseal.
- Chicken wire calcification on histology.
Radiology
- Epiphyseal, geographic.
- Chondroid matrix in ~50 percent.
- Narrow transition, no periosteal reaction.
Treatment
- Extended curettage.
- RFA for smaller lesions.

### Exam Pearls
- UBC: fallen fragment sign.
- ABC: fluid-fluid levels.
- Fibrous dysplasia: ground glass appearance, parrot beak, rind sign.
- NOF: eccentric cortical lesion with sclerotic margins.
- Osteochondroma: marrow continuity.
- Enchondroma vs infarct: enchondroma has no central lucency.
- Osteoid osteoma: double density sign; RFA is gold standard.
- Chondroblastoma: epiphyseal in youth with chicken wire calcification.

Final Faculty Insight: “Benign tumors may be benign, but mistakes in biopsy,
curettage, or planning can cause lifelong problems. Respect the basics.”
