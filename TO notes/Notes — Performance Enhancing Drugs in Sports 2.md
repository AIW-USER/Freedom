DIETARY SUPPLEMENTS – REGULATION & SAFETY
Legislation: Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act (DSHEA, 1994)
Defined supplements as: - Not regular food or meal substitutes.
- Must contain at least one of the following: vitamins, minerals, herbs, amino acids,
or dietary substances.
Important Points: - No requirement for proof of safety, efficacy, or quality before
marketing.
- Manufacturers self-regulate and are not bound to test for contaminants.
- FDA only intervenes after adverse events are reported.
Quality Assurance: - Look for USP Seal (US Pharmacopeia) – certifies purity &
composition.
- USADA Recommendation: Use NSF Certified for Sport® supplements.
- Prefer reputed brands over unverified online products.

ALLOWED vs. PROHIBITED SUPPLEMENTS

| Allowed | Prohibited |
|---|---|
| Non-ergogenic nutritional supplements | Ergogenic supplements (performance enhancing) |
| Essential nutrients | Androstenedione, Chinese Ephedra |
| Food constituents sold as dietary supplements | Any containing banned or unsafe ingredients |

PLACEBO EFFECT IN SPORTS PERFORMANCE
Sometimes performance improves due to belief, not physiology.
Example: - Weightlifters injected with saline (believed it was steroids) → gained lean
mass.

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| True physiological benefits | Expense & side effects |
| Helps meet nutrition goals | Risk of doping violations |
| Aids health & recovery | Misplaced training priorities |

MCQ Tip: The best reason to take supplements – to aid recovery from
training (not to replace diet or enhance performance alone).

CAFFEINE (Revisited)
- Removed from WADA banned list in 2004.

- Earlier limit: 12 µg/mL urine (≈700 mg caffeine for a 70 kg athlete).

- Family: Methylxanthines.

| Source | Content (per serving) |
|---|---|
| Coffee | 80–150 mg |
| Tea | 30–50 mg |
| Cola/Energy Drinks | 30–200 mg |
| Medications | 100–200 mg/tablet |

### Mechanism of Action
- Adenosine receptor antagonist → ↑ neuronal firing, ↑ catecholamines.

- Enhances: alertness, fat oxidation, voluntary activation.

- Spares muscle glycogen by increasing FFA mobilization.

- Improves Ca²⁺ release and sensitivity in sarcoplasmic reticulum.

| Effect | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| ↑ Muscle force | Enhanced Ca²⁺ kinetics |
| ↓ Perceived exertion | Central stimulant effect |
| ↑ Endurance | Fat oxidation + glycogen sparing |

MCQ: Moderate caffeine doses improve endurance by increasing plasma K⁺ (not
decreasing Ca²⁺ sensitivity).

<!-- page 3 -->

Side Effects: Insomnia, tremors, GI upset, dehydration.
Caution: >7 mg/kg → impairs hydration in heat.

BUFFERING AGENTS – BICARBONATE & BETA-ALANINE
### Sodium Bicarbonate Loading
- Buffers H⁺ → delays fatigue in high-intensity exercise.
- Protocol: 0.3 g/kg 1–2 hrs pre-event (~4–5 tsp powder).

- Side Effects: GI distress, nausea, hyperosmotic diarrhea.

| Duration | Dosage |
|---|---|
| 5–6 days | 500 mg/kg/day in 4 divided doses |

MCQ: Main side effect – GI discomfort and diarrhea.
### Beta-Alanine Supplementation
- Precursor of carnosine, a muscle buffer improving pH control.

- Dose: 1.6–6.4 g/day (sustained release preferred – CarnoSyn™).

- Side Effect: Paraesthesia (>800 mg per dose).

- Why not Carnosine directly? → Hydrolyzed in gut, ineffective orally. Note: Beta-alanine elevates both Type I & II muscle fiber carnosine content.
CREATINE
Mechanism: Maintains ATP:ADP ratio during high-intensity exercise via
phosphocreatine (PCr) shuttle.

| Step | Reaction |
|---|---|
| PCr + ADP + H⁺ → ATP + Cr | Catalyzed by creatine kinase (CK) |

Dosage: 5 g bolus raises plasma creatine 20–30× to 600–900 µmol/L within 15 min.

| Phase | Dose | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Loading | 20 g/day (4×5 g) | 5–7 days |
| Maintenance | 3–5 g/day | 12+ weeks |

Effect: ↑ Strength, power, short-term performance.
Side Effects: Muscle cramps, weight gain, potential renal strain.
Status: Legal but ethically debated.

<!-- page 4 -->

MCQ: NCAA forbids teams from providing creatine, though not banned by
WADA.

CARNOSINE & BETA-ALANINE
Function: Cytoplasmic dipeptide (β-alanyl-L-histidine) → improves
excitation-contraction coupling. - Found in muscle (Type II fibers) & brain. - Dietary
sources: White meat (poultry, fish). - Vegetarians → lower carnosine stores.

| Limiting Factor | β-alanine availability |
|---|---|
| Effective Dose | 3–6 g/day (divided) |
| Effect | ↑ Buffering → delay fatigue |

Dr. Shikha: “Don’t just train your muscles; train your chemistry to support
them.”

ARGININE
Roles: 1. Stimulates Growth Hormone (suppresses somatostatin).
2. Synthesizes Creatine (with Glycine & Methionine).
3. Nitric Oxide (NO) precursor → vasodilation & fuel delivery.

| Dose | 12 g/day for 8 weeks |
|---|---|
| Result | ↑ Strength, Peak Power; no change in body composition or VO₂ max |

Mechanism: ↑ Calcium release & muscle perfusion via NO pathways.
MCQ: Despite NO synthesis, arginine does not significantly increase muscle NO
levels in trials.

HMB (β-Hydroxy β-Methylbutyrate)
Type: Metabolite of Leucine; anticatabolic supplement.

| Dose | 1.5–3 g/day |
|---|---|
| Effect | ↑ Muscle mass, ↓ damage, faster |

recovery
Legal Status Allowed (Not on WADA list)
Natural Sources Alfalfa, Asparagus, Avocado,
Cauliflower, Catfish

<!-- page 5 -->

Mechanism: - Activates mTORC1 → ↑ protein synthesis.
- Inhibits Ubiquitin-Proteasome system → ↓ protein breakdown.
- Reduces apoptosis of skeletal muscle.
MCQ: HMB improves both strength (resistance training) and endurance
(cycling, rowing).

WHEY PROTEIN
Composition: β-lactoglobulin, α-lactalbumin, immunoglobulins, BSA, lactoferrin,
enzymes.

| Effect | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| ↑ Lean mass & strength | Rapid digestion → ↑ MPS via mTOR signaling |
| ↓ Fat mass | ↑ Glutathione (antioxidant) & reduced oxidative stress |
| ↑ Dopamine | via L-Tyrosine → improved mood |

Dose: 20 g/day for 3 months.
Carbohydrate ratio: 3:1 (CHO:Protein).
Leucine content: 2–3 g (20–25 mg/kg ideal for optimal MPS).
MCQ: Whey protein enhances performance mainly by reducing oxidative
stress and fatigue.

EPHEDRINE
Source: Ma Huang shrub.
Mechanism: Sympathomimetic & CNS stimulant → catecholamine release →
thermogenesis & lipolysis.

| Effect | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| ↑ Endurance | ↑ Fat oxidation + glycogen sparing |
| ↓ Fat mass | Appetite suppression |
| ↑ VO₂ | Thermogenic effect |

Side Effects: Hypertension, anxiety, cardiac arrhythmia.
Status: Banned by FDA (2004) for health risks.

L-CARNITINE
Structure: Quaternary amine; essential for fatty acid oxidation in liver & heart.

<!-- page 6 -->

Mechanism: - Transfers long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for β-oxidation.
- Buffers free CoA/acetyl-CoA ratio, maintaining metabolic balance.

| Benefit | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| ↑ Power Output | Improved ATP generation |
| ↓ Muscle Damage | ↓ CK levels; enhanced repair |
| ↑ Blood Flow | Protects endothelium; ↑ oxygen supply |
| ↓ Oxidative Stress | Especially after eccentric exercise |
| Dose | 1–2 g/day |

MCQ: L-Carnitine improves recovery by enhancing oxygen delivery and
reducing CK leakage.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Legal Ergogenic Aids: Caffeine, Creatine, Whey, Beta-Alanine, Carnitine, HMB.

- Banned Substances: Ephedrine, Anabolic Steroids, EPO.

- Caution: Overuse leads to renal, hepatic, or cardiovascular risk.

- MCQ Key Recall:
  - Beta-alanine → buffering agent.

  - Creatine → ATP regeneration.

  - HMB → mTOR activation.

  - Arginine → GH & NO.

  - Caffeine → adenosine antagonist.
Dr. Shikha’s Final Remark:
“Supplements are tools, not miracles. Discipline and recovery remain the best
ergogenic aids.”
