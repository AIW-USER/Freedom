Overview
This lecture introduces the fundamental concepts of bone tumors, focusing on
tumor-like lesions, benign and malignant tumors, classical radiological patterns, biopsy
principles, and key diagnostic clues. The purpose is to prepare students with a strong
foundation before advanced oncology lectures.
Faculty Remark: “Most students know how to identify osteosarcoma or Ewing’s, but
they miss the basic text, the sites, and the radiology. This lecture fills that gap.”

## Fundamental Concepts in Bone Tumors
### Age Distribution
- Most bone tumors occur between 10 to 25 years.
- Few tumors occur in older age: chondrosarcoma, hemangioma, metastasis.
- Osteosarcoma shows bimodal distribution (10–20 years and 40–60 years).
### Bone Location Clues
- Metaphysis – majority of tumors.
- Epiphysis – chondroblastoma, giant cell tumor.
- Diaphysis – Ewing sarcoma, osteoblastoma, osteoid osteoma.
### Radiology Basics
- Lytic lesions → tumors from cartilage cells.
- Sclerotic lesions → tumors from osteoblasts.
- Cartilage lesions show calcification, especially after growth plate fusion.

## Important Classification Systems
### Enneking Classification (Benign)
- Latent – thick sclerotic rim.
- Active – thin rim.
- Aggressive – breaching cortex.
### Enneking Classification (Malignant)
- Stage 1A/1B – low grade (intra, extra compartmental).

- Stage 2A/2B – high grade (intra, extra compartmental).
- Stage 3 – distant metastasis.
WHO Classification
Based on cell of origin: chondroblasts, osteoblasts, osteoclasts, vascular, marrow.

## Tumor Like Lesions
### Fibrous Cortical Defect
- Commonest lesion of bone.
- Age: 10–25 years.
- Site: Distal femur metaphysis.
- Incidental finding, eccentric lytic lesion.
- No treatment.

### Non Ossifying Fibroma
- Larger version of FCD.
- Commonest reported lesion.
- Lobulated, eccentric metaphyseal lytic lesion.
### DD: Chondromyxoid Fibroma
- Similar radiology, but proximal tibia metaphysis.
- No calcification.

### Fibrous Dysplasia
- Combination of fibrous tissue + woven bone.
- Site: Proximal femur / neck region.
### Radiological Signs
- Ground glass appearance.
- Rind sign – sclerotic rim.
- Shepherd crook deformity – late stage deformity.
Biopsy
- Chinese letter pattern.
Treatment
- Excisional curettage + bone grafting.

Syndromes
- McCune Albright syndrome: polyostotic FD, precocious puberty, café au lait spots (irregular margins). turn34file1
- Mazabraud syndrome: polyostotic FD + intramuscular myxomas.

Bone Cysts
### Simple Bone Cyst (SBC)
- Children and adolescents.
- Proximal humerus common.
- Central location, symmetrical expansion.
- Fallen leaf sign and trapdoor sign.
- Aspiration: straw colored fluid.
- Treatment: steroid injection first, then curettage + grafting.
### Aneurysmal Bone Cyst (ABC)
- Eccentric, expansile, septated.
- Sites: femur or tibia.
- MRI: double density fluid levels.
- Aspiration: blood filled.
- Treatment: extended curettage (liquid nitrogen is most potent adjuvant).
### Pathological Fracture in Cyst
- Treat fracture only – fracture often heals the cyst.

## Osteochondroma (Exostosis)
- Growth plate aberration.
- Sessile or pedunculated.
- Growth stops at puberty.
- Rare malignant transformation → chondrosarcoma.
### Warning Signs for Malignancy
- Cartilage cap > 2 cm.
Treatment
- Only symptomatic cases → extraperiosteal excision + wedge of normal bone.
### Multiple Hereditary Exostosis
- Autosomal dominant.
- Malignant potential ~ 5 percent.

## Benign Tumors
Enchondroma
- Commonest tumor of hand.
- Lytic, expansile, septate.
- DD: Spina ventosa (TB).
- Treatment: Curettage + graft.

Chondroblastoma
- Epiphyseal tumor (distal femur classic).
- Calcification → chicken wire calcification.
- Also called Codman’s tumor.

### Osteoid Osteoma
- Small nidus < 1.5 cm.
- Night pain relieved by aspirin.
- Treatment: Radiofrequency ablation.

Osteoblastoma
- Nidus > 1.5 cm, aggressive.
- Common in spine (sacrum).
- Requires extended curettage.

## Giant Cell Tumor (GCT)
- Locally aggressive, 20–40 years.
- Epiphyseal lytic lesion.
- Soap bubble appearance.
- Pulsatile.
- Treatment: extended curettage.
### Sandwich Technique
- Used when lesion is < 1 cm from subchondral bone.
### Variant Closest to GCT
- Aneurysmal bone cyst (ABC).

## Malignant Tumors
Chondrosarcoma
- Age: 40–60 years.
- Sites: Pelvis, proximal femur.
- Popcorn calcification.
- Treatment: wide / radical excision.

Osteosarcoma
- Bimodal distribution.
- Site: Distal femur metaphysis.
- Radiology: Codman triangle, sunburst appearance.
- Most radioresistant tumor.
- Treatment: Neoadjuvant chemo → wide excision / limb salvage.

### Ewing Sarcoma
- 10–15 years, diaphysis of femur.
- Radiology: Onion peel lamellated periosteal reaction.
- Biopsy: small round blue cells, pseudo rosettes.
- Markers: CD99 / MIC2, S100, NSE.
- Translocation: t(11;22) in 90 percent cells.
- Most chemosensitive tumor.

## Hemangioma
- Spine commonest site.
- X-ray: corduroy appearance.
- CT: polka dot pattern.
- Treatment: radiotherapy.

## Radiology Patterns Summary

| Pattern | Description | Seen In |
|---|---|---|
| Geographic with narrow zone | Well defined | Benign tumors, GCT, ABC |
| Moth eaten | Multiple small lytic areas | Osteosarcoma |
| Permeative | Poor borders | Ewing, chondrosarcoma |

### Exam Pearls
- Hand lesion → enchondroma until proven otherwise.
- Distal radius epiphyseal lesion → GCT.
- Proximal humerus cyst → simple bone cyst.
- Calcification only in cartilage tumors.
- Cartilage cap > 2 cm suggests chondrosarcoma.
- Pain relief with aspirin → osteoid osteoma.
- ONION PEEL → Ewing; SUNBURST → Osteosarcoma.
- Metastasis is the commonest bone tumor overall.
- Breast → mixed lytic sclerotic metastasis.

Final Faculty Insight: “Master the basics. Most errors in exams and clinics occur
not in diagnosis of the big tumors but in misunderstanding simple lesions.”
