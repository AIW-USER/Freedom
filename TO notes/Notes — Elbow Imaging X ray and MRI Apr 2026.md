Elbow Imaging: X-ray and MRI – Dr. Nafisa Shakir
Batta (Apr 2026)
### Overview / Key Concepts
- Part 1: elbow X-rays, especially the paediatric elbow (ossification centres, supracondylar, lateral condyle and epicondyle injuries) and occult radial head fractures. Part 2: normal MRI anatomy of the elbow, normal variants that mimic disease, and cases (distal biceps rupture, flexor and extensor origin disease, OCD of the capitellum).
- CT has little role except in deformity. Know normal anatomy and pitfalls before calling something abnormal.

### Elbow X-ray Anatomy
- Lateral condyle (larger) carries the capitellum and lateral epicondyle; medial side = medial epicondyle and trochlea. Lateral condyle fractures in children involve the lateral epicondyle and capitellum; a "medial condyle fracture" is really a medial epicondyle or trochlear injury.
- Coronoid fossa (anterior) receives the coronoid in flexion; olecranon fossa (posterior) receives the olecranon in extension.
- Lateral view: radial head/neck/shaft, coronoid, ulnotrochlear joint, olecranon. An oblique view helps where bones overlap.

Paediatric Elbow
Ossification centres: CRITOE

| Centre | Approximate age (years) |
|---|---|
| Capitellum | 1 (about 2) |
| Radial head | 3 (about 4) |
| Internal (medial) epicondyle | 5 |
| Trochlea | 7 (about 9) |
| Olecranon | 9 |
| External (lateral) epicondyle | 11 |

- Ages vary between sources (1-3-5-7-9-11 or 2-4-6-8-10-12); the ORDER is what matters. If the trochlea is seen, the medial epicondyle must be visible; if it is not in place, suspect avulsion with entrapment.
- Cartilage is invisible on X-ray, so young elbows look blunted; the radial head apophysis is not a fracture.

Fractures
- Fat pads: elevated anterior ("sail sign") or visible posterior fat pad = haemarthrosis; a fat- fluid level = lipohaemarthrosis, implying an intra-articular fracture.
- Supracondylar fracture: the anterior humeral line should pass through the middle third of the capitellum; with posterior displacement it passes through the anterior part or misses it. Grade with Gartland types I–IV.
- Lateral condyle fracture: a small bony flake may hide a fracture running into cartilage; MRI is essential when the X-ray does not answer the question.
- Epicondyle avulsions: lateral or medial epicondyle ossific centre avulsion; in adolescent athletes compare with the other side (physis wider on the injured side); ultrasound can help but is operator dependent.

Occult radial head and neck fractures
- After any elbow trauma request AP, lateral AND external oblique views: the external oblique (and the Coyle view: a lateral with the beam angled 45°) shows the radial head free of the ulna.
- If X-rays are negative but suspicion remains (especially in children), do a quick fracture- screening MRI; it may also show associated injuries such as a coronoid tip fracture.

### MRI Technique
- Elbow imaged prone in the "superman" position, away from the chest and in the centre of the magnet; large field-of-view imaging of shoulder, arm and elbow together gives poor images (respiratory artefact).
- Distal biceps protocol: two scans (shoulder to arm supine, then arm and elbow prone).
- Sequences: coronal T1 and PD FS, optional coronal GRE (trabeculae, bone-cartilage interface, faint calcification), sagittal PD FS, axial PD and PD FS.

### Normal MRI Anatomy and Pitfalls

| Structure | Normal appearance / pitfall |
|---|---|
| Ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) | Anterior band (key), posterior band (floor of the cubital tunnel), transverse band (rarely seen). Grey, fan-shaped humeral end with fibrofatty tissue is NORMAL; dark and cord-like at the ulnar (sublime tubercle) end |
| Radial collateral ligament | Cord-like; a small sulcus at the radial side is normal |
| Lateral ulnar collateral ligament | From the lateral epicondyle behind the radius to the ulna; often hard to see even when intact |
| Radiocapitellar (synovial) plica | Normal fold projecting into the radiocapitellar joint |
| Capitellar pseudodefect | Normal groove at the posterior capitellum; do not call it OCD |
| Ulnar "bare area" | Normal cartilage gap in the trochlear notch of the ulna |
| Radial head fovea | Central cartilage thinning can be normal |
| Annular ligament | Dark band around the radial head from the ulnar tubercle; lost with a posterior tubercle fracture |
| Anconeus | Normal muscle posterolaterally |
| Cubital tunnel | Ulnar nerve in fat; floor = posterior band of the UCL |

- Muscles on axial images: biceps and brachialis anterior (brachialis has a broad muscular and tendinous ulnar insertion), triceps posterior, brachioradialis laterally, pronator teres medially, supinator wrapping the radius, common extensor origin laterally, common flexor origin medially.
- Distal biceps: short and long heads stay separate down to the radial tuberosity; follow the tendon all the way to its insertion. Lacertus fibrosus runs medially.
- Do not confuse the brachial artery (flow void) and veins or the median nerve with the biceps tendon.

Cases

| Case | Findings |
|---|---|
| Boxer, gym injury, Popeye sign | Fluid in the biceps-brachialis plane, grey/absent tendon, no insertion at the radial tuberosity: full- thickness distal biceps rupture; measure retraction from the tuberosity |
| Medial elbow pain | Oedematous, heterogeneous common flexor origin with faint calcification (GRE): calcific tendinosis; epitrochlear lymph node; radial head chondromalacia |
| Pain on supination | Thickened distal biceps with fluid around it: radiobicipital bursitis / tendinosis |
| Lateral elbow pain | Empty common extensor origin, torn and retracted extensors (ligaments intact); degenerate tendons may respond to tenotomy |
| Young athlete | Osteochondritis dissecans of the capitellum: ANTERIOR capitellar cartilage and subchondral irregularity on sagittal images (unlike the posterior pseudodefect) |

### Exam & Viva Pearls
- CRITOE order; trochlea seen but medial epicondyle not = look for entrapment.
- Anterior humeral line through the middle third of the capitellum.
- Sail sign / posterior fat pad = effusion; fat-fluid level = intra-articular fracture.
- Request an external oblique view after elbow trauma.
- Capitellar pseudodefect is posterior and normal; OCD is anterior.
- Grey humeral end of the UCL is normal.

### Take-Home Summary
- Learn the normal paediatric and MRI anatomy first; use CRITOE, fat pads, the anterior humeral line and oblique views on X-ray, and on MRI follow each ligament and tendon to its attachment while ignoring the known pseudo-lesions.
