Faculty: Dr. C S Vishnu Prasath

### OVERVIEW
- Goal: Understand bladder physiology and its rehabilitation in spinal cord injury (SCI) patients.
- Importance: Proper bladder rehab enables near-normal social functioning and prevents renal complications.
- Topics: Bladder control, nerve supply, neurogenic bladder types, assessment, pharmacological and surgical management. “A well-rehabilitated bladder decides the quality of life in a spinal injury patient.” – Dr. C S Vishnu Prasath

### BLADDER ANATOMY & CONTROL
- Components: Detrusor muscle, internal sphincter (involuntary), external sphincter (voluntary).
- Males: Both internal & external sphincters present.
- Females: Only external sphincter → shorter urethra → higher UTI risk but easier rehab.

PHYSIOLOGY OF MICTURITION
### Storage Phase
- Low detrusor pressure, high urethral tone.
- Sympathetic dominance:
  - β3 → relax detrusor.
  - α1 → contract internal sphincter.
- Somatic control: Pudendal nerve keeps external sphincter contracted.
### Voiding Phase
- Triggered when bladder volume >300 ml.
- Stretch receptors → pons (micturition center) → activate parasympathetic + inhibit sympathetic.
- Parasympathetic (M3): Detrusor contraction.
- Sympathetic inhibition: Internal sphincter relaxation.
- Voluntary control: Pudendal inhibition → external sphincter relaxation. Normal synergy = detrusor contracts while sphincters relax. Loss = dyssynergia.

EVALUATION OF NEUROGENIC BLADDER
### Goals of Management
1. Protect upper tracts – avoid pressures >40 cm H₂O.
2. Ensure complete regular emptying – prevent stasis.
3. Prevent complications: UTI, stones, reflux, dysreflexia.
4. Maintain continence.
5. Adapt to patient lifestyle.
### Clinical & Investigative Work-up
- Bladder diary, physical exam.
- Urinalysis, culture, renal function (GFR).
- Ultrasound KUB, CT abdomen, renal scintigraphy.
- Urodynamic Study (UDS): Gold standard for detrusor–sphincter dyssynergia.
  - Measures: first sensation, bladder capacity, compliance, detrusor pressure, leak point, flow rate.
  - Normal: Low detrusor pressure during filling; rises only during voiding.
DSD pattern = simultaneous detrusor + sphincter contraction on UDS.

MANAGEMENT STRATEGIES
1⃣ Conservative / Non-Surgical
A. Behavioral Therapy

<!-- page 4 -->

- Reflex voiding stimulation: running water, suprapubic tapping, Creden/Valsalva maneuvers.
- Works better in females and LMN-type bladders.
B. Catheterization Methods

| Type | Indication | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Intermittent | Preferred | Every 4–6 hours, <500 ml |
| Catheterization (ICP) | long-term | drained each time |
| Indwelling Catheter | Acute SCI / early phase | Temporary; infection risk |
| Suprapubic Catheter | Urethral injury, sexual rehab | Keeps perineum free |
| Condom Catheter | Overflow incontinence | Males, low-resistance bladders |

ICP = gold standard for rehabilitation; PVR <50 ml indicates good recovery.

2⃣ Surgical Management
### A. Increase Bladder Storage Capacity
- Botulinum toxin – minimally invasive, increases capacity.
- Cystoplasty / Enterocystoplasty – augment with ileum or stomach.
- Autoaugmentation (Detrusor Myotomy/Myectomy) – detrusor relaxation.
### B. Increase Bladder Outlet Resistance
- Artificial sphincter (inflatable cuff + hand pump).
- Slings / Bulking agents – collagen, PTFE, hyaluronic acid.
- Neuromodulation – sacral nerve stimulation.
C. Salvage Procedures

<!-- page 5 -->

- Urethral stents (Urolume).
- Urinary diversion / ileal conduit.
- Experimental: Latissimus dorsi microvascular transplant, nerve grafting, tissue engineering.

### KEY DIFFERENCES: UMN vs LMN BLADDER

| Feature | UMN Bladder | LMN Bladder |
|---|---|---|
| Tone | Spastic | Flaccid |
| Detrusor | Overactive | Underactive |
| Sphincter | Overactive → DSD | Underactive → leakage |
| Pressure | High | Low |
| Capacity | Low | High |
| Risk | Reflux → renal damage | Infection → stasis |

UMN: Treat overactivity (α-blockers, antimuscarinics, botox).
LMN: Prevent stasis (intermittent catheterization).

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Parasympathetic → Pelvic (M3) → contraction.
- Sympathetic → Hypogastric (β3 relax, α1 contract).
- Somatic → Pudendal → voluntary control.
- DSD = UMN lesion, Atonic bladder = LMN lesion.
- UDS = gold standard for bladder dysfunction assessment.
- Goals: Protect kidneys, ensure complete emptying, avoid high detrusor pressure.
- ICP protocol: 4–6 hourly; residual <50 ml = success.
