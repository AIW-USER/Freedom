Overview / Key Concepts
Sensitivity and specificity are core measures used to evaluate the validity and accuracy
of diagnostic and screening tests. These parameters determine how well a test
identifies diseased and non-diseased individuals and guide clinical decision-making.
This lecture covers: - Concept of screening tests - Definitions of sensitivity and
specificity - 2×2 diagnostic table - Interpretation and clinical relevance - Trade-off
between sensitivity and specificity - Positive and negative predictive values - Common
exam-oriented scenarios

### Screening Test
- Aim is to detect disease among apparently healthy individuals
- Used before confirmatory testing
- Accuracy of a screening test is assessed using:
  - Sensitivity
  - Specificity

2×2 Diagnostic Table (Foundation)
Test Result Disease Status | Disease Present | Disease Absent |
Test Positive | True Positive (TP) | False Positive (FP) |
Test Negative | False Negative (FN) | True Negative (TN) |
Key totals: - Total diseased = TP + FN - Total non-diseased = FP + TN
Faculty Quote “If you understand this table clearly, all formulas become intuitive and
you do not need to memorise anything.”

Sensitivity
Definition
Sensitivity is the ability of a test to correctly identify individuals who have the disease.
- Focuses on true positives
- Expressed as a percentage

Formula
Sensitivity = TP / (TP + FN)

<!-- page 2 -->

Interpretation
- High sensitivity means fewer false negatives
- A sensitivity of 75% means:
  - 75% of diseased individuals are correctly identified
  - 25% are missed (false negative rate)

### Importance of Sensitivity
- Accurate diagnosis
- Early disease detection
- Reduces missed cases
- Useful for screening tests
Sensitive tests are preferred when missing a disease has serious consequences.

### Limitations of Sensitivity
- High sensitivity alone does not ensure accuracy
- Must be interpreted with specificity
- Can be influenced by disease prevalence
- Highly sensitive tests may increase false positives

Specificity
Definition
Specificity is the ability of a test to correctly identify individuals who do not have the
disease.
- Focuses on true negatives
- Confirms disease-free status

Formula
Specificity = TN / (TN + FP)

Interpretation
- High specificity means fewer false positives
- A specificity of 90% means:

<!-- page 3 -->

- 90% of healthy individuals are correctly identified
  - 10% are incorrectly labelled diseased

### Importance of Specificity
- Avoids unnecessary treatment
- Reduces patient anxiety
- Important for confirmatory tests

Sensitivity vs Specificity
Sensitivity | Specificity |
Identifies true positives | Identifies true negatives |
Minimizes false negatives | Minimizes false positives |
Important for screening | Important for confirmation |
There is often a trade-off: - Increasing sensitivity reduces specificity - Increasing
specificity reduces sensitivity

### Choosing the Right Parameter
- Screening test → Sensitivity is more important
- Confirmatory test → Specificity is more important
Clinical reasoning determines the balance between the two.

Predictive Values
Predictive values assess test performance from the test-result perspective.

### Positive Predictive Value (PPV)
- Probability that a person with a positive test truly has the disease
PPV = TP / (TP + FP)

### Negative Predictive Value (NPV)
- Probability that a person with a negative test truly does not have the disease
NPV = TN / (TN + FN)

<!-- page 4 -->

### Key Difference
- Sensitivity and specificity depend on disease status
- PPV and NPV depend on test results
- Predictive values are affected by disease prevalence

### Common Pitfalls
- Confusing sensitivity with PPV
- Ignoring disease prevalence while interpreting PPV and NPV
- Using screening tests for definitive diagnosis
- Assuming high sensitivity equals high accuracy

### Exam Pearls
- Sensitivity focuses on true positives
- Specificity focuses on true negatives
- Screening tests require high sensitivity
- Confirmatory tests require high specificity
- False negative rate = 1 − sensitivity
- False positive rate = 1 − specificity
- PPV answers: out of test positives, how many truly diseased
- NPV answers: out of test negatives, how many truly disease-free
- Accuracy depends on both sensitivity and specificity
