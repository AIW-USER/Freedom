Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Thoracolumbar Spine Injury
Faculty: Dr. C S Vishnu Prasath

### OVERVIEW
- Thoracolumbar fractures are one of the most common spinal injuries encountered in trauma settings.
- Important for both DNB/FNB exams and clinical practice.
- Understanding stability, classification, and management protocols is key.
- The goal is to protect the spinal cord, maintain alignment, and restore stability. “A broken spine doesn’t always mean broken stability — know when to fix and when to brace.” – Dr. Vishnu

ANATOMY & BIOMECHANICS
### Functions of the Spinal Column
- Protects the spinal cord.
- Provides flexibility and stability.
- Supports load transfer between upper and lower limbs.
### Structural Components
- Anterior elements: Vertebral bodies, intervertebral discs, anterior and posterior longitudinal ligaments.
- Posterior elements: Lamina, pedicles, spinous and transverse processes, facet joints, and their capsules.
- Ligamentous support:
  - Anterior longitudinal ligament (ALL)
  - Posterior longitudinal ligament (PLL)
  - Ligamentum flavum
  - Interspinous & supraspinous ligaments
  - Facet joint capsules
### Biomechanical Concept (Ring/Bangle Analogy)
- The spinal column behaves like a ring:
  - One break = stable.
  - Two breaks = unstable.
- Stability depends on integrity of both anterior and posterior elements.

### MECHANISM OF INJURY
- Anterior compression & posterior tension — like a weightlifting crane.
- Failure of posterior tension band indicates instability.
- Common mechanisms:
  - Compression
  - Flexion-distraction
  - Translation/dislocation
  - Rotation/torsion

DEFINITION OF STABILITY
White & Panjabi (Classic Definition):
Loss of the spine’s ability under physiological loads to maintain vertebral
relationships without damage or irritation to the spinal cord or nerve roots.

<!-- page 3 -->

Stable: Maintains cord protection during physiological motion. Unstable: Risk of
neurological injury during normal motion.

INITIAL ASSESSMENT & EMERGENCY RESPONSE
### At Accident Site
- Handle patient carefully – improper transport can convert stable to unstable.
- Spine mode transfer: Use log roll technique or sheet lift to prevent twisting.
- Assume spinal injury in all unconscious or head-injury patients → apply cervical collar.
### In Emergency Room
- ABC Protocol:
  - A: Airway with cervical spine protection.
  - B: Breathing → assess for rib injury or hemothorax.
  - C: Circulation → differentiate hypovolemic vs neurogenic shock.

### Neurogenic Shock
- Seen in injuries above T5.
- Pathophysiology: Loss of sympathetic tone → bradycardia + hypotension.
- Treatment: Vasopressors (dopamine, noradrenaline). Avoid fluid overload.
### Spinal Shock
- Temporary physiological shutdown of the cord.
- Characterized by flaccid paralysis, areflexia, sensory loss.
- Lasts 24–48 hrs.
- End sign: Return of bulbocavernosus reflex.

NEUROLOGICAL ASSESSMENT
### Key Components:
1. Motor power
2. Sensory function
3. Reflexes
### Sacral Sparing
- Indicates incomplete injury.
- Signs:
  - Perianal sensation (S3)
  - Voluntary anal contraction (S2)
  - Great toe flexion (S1)

| Grade | Description |
|---|---|
| A | Complete – No motor/sensory function below injury |
| B | Sensory only |
| C | Motor <3/5 |
| D | Motor ≥3/5 but not normal |
| E | Normal neurology |

Prognosis: Better in incomplete injuries (C/D) than in complete ones (A).

### IMAGING PROTOCOLS

| Modality | Indication |
|---|---|
| X-ray (AP & Lateral) | First-line for screening; 80% diagnostic value |
| CT Scan | Bony detail, fracture pattern, pedicle integrity |
| MRI | Neurological deficit, PLC injury, cord edema |

### When to Order MRI/CT
- 50% loss of vertebral body height.
- 20° kyphotic angulation.
- Any translation/dislocation.
- Suspected PLC or neurological deficit.

MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES
### Non-Operative (Stable Fractures)
- Goal: Preserve neurology, prevent deformity progression, and allow mobilization.
- Brace: Thoracolumbar Spinal Orthosis (TLSO) / Taylor’s Brace.
- Duration: 8–12 weeks.
- Indications:
  - Compression fractures without neurological deficit.
  - Burst fractures with intact PLC.

### Fixation Principle:
- Posterior pedicle screw fixation is standard.
- Decompression only if neurological deficit present.
- Include fractured vertebra screw for stronger construct.
- Use transpedicular decompression if anterior fragments compress cord.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Most common level: T12–L1.
- Unstable indicators: >50% height loss, >20° kyphosis, posterior element fracture.
- TLICS ≥5 → Surgery.
- Flexion-distraction = Chance fracture.
- Sacral sparing → incomplete injury → good prognosis.
- Bulbocavernosus reflex → end of spinal shock.
- Neurogenic shock: Bradycardia + Hypotension.
- Brace: TLSO (not LS belt) for T5–L3 fractures.
- Posterior-only fixation: standard for most injuries.
