OVERVIEW
Focus: Practical applications and best practices of taping in sports medicine for injury
prevention, rehabilitation, and performance enhancement.
Dr. Shikha’s Quote: “Taping isn’t treatment — it’s facilitation. It reminds the
body how to move right.”

### PURPOSE OF TAPING
1. Support & Stability: Reinforce ligaments and joint structures.

2. Proprioception: Improve joint awareness and neuromuscular control.

3. Pain Reduction: Decompress soft tissues and offload stress zones.

4. Swelling Control: Promote lymphatic drainage.

5. Prevention: Prevent recurrence of sprains and overuse injuries.

### Ideal Taping Outcome:
- Stabilizes without restricting movement.

- Reinforces function, not immobilization.

- Acts as an external neuromuscular cue.

RIGID TAPING – PRINCIPLES & APPLICATIONS
Purpose: Restrict motion, prevent re-injury, support ligamentous healing.

<!-- page 3 -->

### Ankle Taping (Standard 8-Figure Technique)
1. Anchor: 2 wraps above malleoli.

2. Stirrups: Medial → lateral (limit inversion).

3. Figure-of-Eight: Around heel and forefoot.

4. Heel Lock: Reinforces talocrural stability.

5. Closure: Finish with elastic adhesive bandage for compression. Common Error: Over-tightening — leads to circulation compromise.
Benefits: - ↓ Inversion sprains recurrence (by 50–70%).
- ↑ Proprioceptive feedback.
Limitations: - Effectiveness decreases after 20–30 min (tape stretch, sweat).
- Can cause skin irritation with prolonged use.

McCONNELL TAPING (ALIGNMENT TAPING)
Concept: Developed by Jenny McConnell (Australia).
Tape Type: Rigid brown zinc oxide tape with hypoallergenic underwrap (Fixomull).

Application Example – Patellofemoral Pain Syndrome (PFPS)
Goals: 1. Correct patellar glide, tilt, and rotation.
2. Relieve pain during functional activities.
3. Improve quadriceps control.
Technique: - Apply Fixomull base (protects skin).
- Use rigid brown tape → pull medially to realign patella.
- Adjust tilt/rotation via tape direction.
- Test movement immediately — patient feedback is key.
Duration: Max 18–24 hrs; remove to avoid skin maceration.
“Alignment first, activation next — McConnell is about retraining, not
restricting.”

MULLIGAN TAPING (MOBILIZATION WITH MOVEMENT)
Inventor: Brian Mulligan (Physiotherapist, NZ).
Principle: “Reposition the joint — restore pain-free movement.”

<!-- page 4 -->

Technique: - Rigid white tape (75 mm) applied while maintaining a pain-free glide.
- Used in conjunction with Mobilization with Movement (MWM).
- Common uses: Lateral epicondylitis, PFPS, shoulder impingement, ankle instability.
Example: - Tennis Elbow: Glide humeral head medially while applying rigid tape →
pain-free grip restored.
Combines passive joint glide + active patient movement.
LOW-DYE TAPING (FOOT POSTURE CORRECTION)
Purpose: Support medial longitudinal arch, offload plantar fascia.
Technique Steps: 1. Anchor around forefoot and heel.
2. Apply 3–4 strips from lateral → medial arch, overlapping 50%.
3. Lock with circumferential tape over midfoot.
Indications: - Plantar fasciitis
- Flatfoot (pes planus)
- Medial tibial stress syndrome (MTSS)
Duration: Up to 24–48 hrs (remove for skin airing).
“Every step starts from the arch — support it, and you prevent half the
injuries.”

“Lift the skin, not the joint — decompression heals.”
### TAPE REMOVAL & SKIN CARE
- Remove slowly along hair growth direction.
- Use warm water / oil to reduce adhesive trauma.
- Avoid alcohol wipes (dry out skin).

- Re-tape only after 24-hour gap for skin recovery.

### CONTRAINDICATIONS & PRECAUTIONS

| Contraindication | Reason |
|---|---|
| Open wounds | Infection risk |
| Skin allergies / eczema | Irritation risk |
| DVT / Thrombophlebitis | Risk of embolism |
| Malignancy / Active infection | Contraindicated |
| Fragile / Elderly skin | Tear risk |

Precautions: - Test patch 24 hrs prior (for allergy).
- Avoid excessive stretch or circulation block.
- Stop immediately if pain, redness, or tingling occurs.

### BEST PRACTICE RECOMMENDATIONS
1. Assess patient biomechanics before taping.

2. Apply tape over clean, dry skin (no lotions).

3. Combine manual therapy + taping + exercise rehab.

4. Retape after rest periods; monitor skin integrity.

<!-- page 6 -->

5. Taping should facilitate movement, not replace strengthening. “A good tape is silent — the athlete shouldn’t feel it, just perform better.”
### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Taping supports stability, proprioception, and pain modulation.

- Choose technique based on purpose: Rigid (stability) vs Kinesio (mobility).

- Maintain proper tension & alignment to avoid adverse effects.

- Combine taping with rehab for long-term benefits. “Taping is an art guided by anatomy and purpose — master both.”
