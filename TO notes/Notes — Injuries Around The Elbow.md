Injuries Around the Elbow – Dr. Anurag Tiwari

Overview
- The elbow is a complex hinge–pivot joint responsible for both flexion–extension and supination–pronation.
- Injuries range from isolated fractures (radial head, olecranon, coronoid) to complex fracture–dislocations.
- Stability depends on bony anatomy, ligamentous integrity, and muscular support.
Dr. Anurag’s Remark:
“Elbow stability is about restoring relationships — between bone, ligament, and motion.
Miss one, and you lose all.”

Radial Head Fractures
Mechanism
  - Axial load on forearm with elbow extended and pronated.
  - May be isolated or part of the terrible triad.
### Mason Classification (Modified Hotchkiss)

| Type | Description | Management |
|---|---|---|
| I | Non-displaced | Sling, early motion |
| II | >2 mm displacement, stable | ORIF |
| III | Comminuted, non-reconstructable | Radial head replacement |
| IV | With dislocation | ORIF / Replacement + LCL repair |

Coronoid Fractures
Function
- Anterior bony buttress preventing posterior dislocation.
- Injury leads to instability (especially PLRI or PMRI).
Classification

| System | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Regan–Morrey | I – Tip, II – <50%, III – >50% | Based on height |
| O’Driscoll | I – Tip, II – Anteromedial facet, III – Basal | Mechanism-based |

### Fixation Options

| Type | Fixation |
|---|---|
| Tip fracture | Suture anchor or transosseous sutures |
| Anteromedial facet | Buttress plate (medial approach) |
| Basal fracture | Screws + plate |

Approaches: * Hotchkiss (Over-the-top) – between FCU and pronator teres. * FCU
split – exposes ulnar nerve, used for MCL repair.

### Olecranon Fractures

| Type | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Undisplaced (<2 mm) | Posterior slab |
| Displaced (>2 mm) | ORIF (TBW or plate) |
| Comminuted / unstable | Plate fixation |

### Fixation Tips
- K-wires toward anteromedial cortex.
- Engage opposite cortex to avoid backing out.
- Convert triceps force into compression (TBW principle).

### Elbow Dislocations

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Simple | Ligamentous only |
| Complex | With fractures (radial head, coronoid, olecranon) |

Mechanism
- FOOSH → Valgus + axial + rotational stress.
### Reduction – Parvin’s Technique
1. Flex elbow to 30°.
2. Apply traction–countertraction.
3. Correct mediolateral shift.
4. Supinate forearm and push olecranon anteriorly while flexing.
### Immobilization Position

| Repair | Forearm Position |
|---|---|
| LCL repaired, MCL intact | Pronation |
| MCL repaired, LCL intact | Supination |
| Both repaired | Neutral |

Most common nerve injured: Ulnar.

### Terrible Triad Injury

| Components | Injury Sequence |
|---|---|
| 1 | Posterior dislocation |
| 2 | Radial head fracture |

| Components | Injury Sequence |
|---|---|
| 3 | Coronoid fracture |

Mechanism
VAS (Valgus, Axial, Supination) → LUCL rupture → radial head + coronoid.

### Treatment Sequence (Pugh–McKee Protocol)
1. Coronoid fixation (suture/plate).
2. Radial head fixation or replacement.
3. LCL repair (mandatory).
4. MCL repair if instability persists.
5. Hinged external fixator if residual instability.
Approach: Lateral (Kocher) ± Medial (Hotchkiss) depending on fracture.
Hinge Axis: Line from lateral epicondyle → medial trochlear sulcus.

### Chronic Instability

| Type | Pathology | Reconstruction |
|---|---|---|
| PLRI | LUCL insufficiency | LUCL reconstruction (Palmaris longus graft) |
| PMRI | MCL insufficiency | MCL reconstruction (Jobe/Docking) |

Tests: * Pivot Shift Test – PLRI. * Valgus Stress Test – MCL.

Complications

| Complication | Notes |
|---|---|
| Instability | Missed ligamentous injuries |
| Stiffness | Most common late issue |
| Heterotopic ossification | Especially after trauma or surgery |
| Ulnar neuropathy | Common in complex cases |
| Hardware prominence / loosening | Especially in radial head fixation |
| Post-traumatic arthritis | Due to malreduction |

### Exam / Viva Pearls
- Valgus stabilizer: MCL anterior bundle.
- Varus stabilizer: Coronoid process.
- PLRI stabilizer: LUCL.
- Regan–Morrey: Height-based coronoid classification.
- O’Driscoll: Facet-based coronoid classification.
- Terrible triad sequence: Coronoid → Radial head → LCL → MCL.
- Posterior fat pad sign: Pathognomonic for intra-articular fracture.
- Safe zone (Caputo): 90° between radial styloid & Lister’s tubercle.
- Pulse-less but pink hand: Observe, not explore.
Final Faculty Insight:
“Fixation gives structure, but motion gives life — stable, concentric, and moving elbow is
the true endpoint of surgery.”
