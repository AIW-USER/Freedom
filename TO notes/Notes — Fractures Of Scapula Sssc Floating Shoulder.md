Fractures of Scapula, SSSC Injuries, and Floating Shoulder –
Dr. Daivik T Shetty

### Key Concepts / Overview
- This lecture explores Scapular Fractures, Superior Shoulder Suspensory Complex (SSSC) Injuries, and the concept of the Floating Shoulder.
- The floating shoulder is a complex injury involving both bony and ligamentous disruptions.
- Understanding the SSSC as an osseoligamentous ring connecting the upper limb to the axial skeleton is key to diagnosis and management.
Dr. Daivik’s Remark:
“Every floating shoulder is a story of disrupted balance between bone, ligament, and
motion. Fix the balance, not just the bone.”

### Historical Background

| Timeline | Key Discovery |
|---|---|
| 250 million years ago | Oldest recorded scapular fracture (in dinosaur Yangchuanosaurus hepingensi) |
| 1799 | First scientific study on scapular fracture by Karl August Vogt – described a case of ipsilateral clavicle and scapula fracture, introducing the concept of ‘unstable shoulder’, precursor to floating shoulder |

### Evolution of the Floating Shoulder Concept

| Year | Author(s) | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1975 | Ganz & Noesberger | Defined Unstable Shoulder as fracture of scapular neck with clavicular fracture or AC dislocation + rupture of coracoclavicular and coracoacromial ligaments |
| 1980s | Herscovici et al. | Coined the term Floating Shoulder – ipsilateral midshaft clavicle + scapular neck fracture |

| Year | Author(s) | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1993 | Goss | Expanded concept to Superior Shoulder Suspensory Complex (SSSC) injuries |
| 2006 | Goss (revised) | Defined floating shoulder as double disruption of the SSSC |

Earlier definitions ignored ligamentous injuries, but modern understanding
emphasizes combined osseoligamentous disruption.

### Mechanism of Injury
- High-velocity trauma – especially road traffic accidents.
- Often associated with polytrauma – chest injuries, rib fractures, pneumothorax/hemothorax.
- Commonly involves glenoid neck and clavicle fractures with ligamentous rupture.

Superior Shoulder Suspensory Complex (SSSC)
Definition
An osseoligamentous ring that connects the upper limb to the axial skeleton,
providing dynamic stability to the shoulder girdle.

Components
1. Glenoid process

2. Coracoid process

3. Coracoclavicular ligament

4. Distal clavicle

5. Acromioclavicular joint

6. Acromion process

7. (Later additions) – Coracoacromial ligament, as an essential stabilizer.

<!-- page 3 -->

### Struts of SSSC

| Strut | Structure |
|---|---|
| Superior Strut | Middle third and distal end of the clavicle |
| Inferior Strut | Junction of lateral scapular body and medial glenoid neck |

Subdivisions
Division Structures Included
1. Clavicular–AC joint–Acromial strut
2. Three-process–Scapular body junction
3. Clavicular–Coracoclavicular ligament–Coracoid (C4 linkage)

Secondary support: Coracoacromial ligament.

### The Ring Concept
- The SSSC behaves like the pelvic ring. Disruption at one point usually implies injury elsewhere in the ring.
- A double disruption leads to loss of continuity and instability — the hallmark of a floating shoulder.

### Biomechanical Significance
- The clavicle is the only bony link between the axial skeleton and the upper limb.
- The scapula is suspended from the clavicle via the coracoclavicular ligaments and AC joint.
- Limited motion at AC joint & CC ligaments maintains stability while allowing physiological scapular movement.

### Double Disruption Theory (Goss, 2006)

| Principle | Definition |
|---|---|
| Double disruption | When the SSSC ring is broken at two points or one point + strut fracture → instability |

<!-- page 4 -->

| Principle | Definition |
|---|---|
| Floating shoulder | A type of double disruption involving glenoid neck and clavicle or AC disruption |

### Effects of Double Disruption
- Delayed union, malunion, or nonunion
- Drooping of shoulder (neurovascular stretch)
- Subacromial impingement and weakness
- Altered glenohumeral–acromial alignment

### Clinical Implications
- High association with brachial plexus, axillary artery/vein injuries.
- Usually part of polytrauma.
- Conservative management of clavicle alone can fail if SSSC integrity is disrupted.
Faculty Note:
“If two links of the chain are broken, don’t expect the rest to hold. Fix the ring, not just
the fracture.”

### Anatomy – Proximal and Distal Fragments

| Fragment | Structures |
|---|---|
| Distal Fragment | Glenoid and coracoid process |
| Proximal Fragment | Acromion, scapular spine, and scapular body |

Connection: * Coracoacromial ligament connects distal to proximal fragment.
- Clavicular shaft and CC ligament connect to axial skeleton.
Disruption of these attachments → Floating Shoulder.

### Angular Measurement
1. Glenopolar Angle (GPA)
- Formed by: Line connecting cranial glenoid to caudal scapular tip and another line connecting cranial–caudal glenoid points.
- Normal: 30–45°

- <20°: Severe rotational malalignment (Romero et al.) → Surgical indication.
2. Glenoid Inclination Angle
- Measured on AP view between perpendiculars to glenoid and medial scapular border.
- Assesses caudal tilt of glenoid after fracture.

### Indications for Surgical Fixation

| Parameter | Indication for Surgery |
|---|---|

| Articular Step-off | >4–5 mm or >20% glenoid fragment involvement |

Management Principles
Non-Operative
- Minimally displaced fractures (<5 mm, well-aligned GPA >30°).
- Immobilization (sling/shoulder immobilizer).
Operative
Indications: * Double disruption (floating shoulder) * Displacement or angulation >
defined parameters * Associated neurovascular risk

### Surgical Strategy
Step Principle
1. Fix Clavicle First Restores length and indirectly reduces scapular fracture
2. Assess Scapula If still displaced → fix scapula
3. Individualize Surgeon discretion; depends on displacement
Sequence and alignment

### Common Fixation Sequence
1. Clavicle plating – anatomical reduction, restores tension.

2. Scapular fixation – only if GPA <20°, severe lateralization, or persistent displacement.
Key Insight: Fixing clavicle often realigns glenoid neck due to ligamentous
continuity.

### Surgical Approaches

| Approach | Features |
|---|---|
| Judet (Posterior) Approach | Workhorse; lateral position; inverted L incision; interval between teres minor & infraspinatus |
| Vertical / Reverse L Incision | Minimal dissection, used for simple fractures |

### Structures to Protect:
- Suprascapular nerve (at infraspinatus border)
- Axillary nerve and circumflex vessels (near axilla)

### Algorithm for Management of SSSC / Floating Shoulder
1. Obtain X-ray + CT with 3D reconstruction.

2. Assess displacement, GPA, angulation, lateralization.

3. If <5 mm displacement / GPA >30° → Conservative.

<!-- page 8 -->

4. If GPA <20°, >20 mm lateralization, >45° angulation, or 100% translation → Operative. Step 1: Fix clavicle → reassess scapula → fix if malalignment persists.

### Exam / Viva Pearls
- Floating shoulder = double disruption of SSSC.
- GPA <20° → indicates surgical fixation.
- SSSC components = glenoid, coracoid, CC ligament, distal clavicle, AC joint, acromion, CA ligament.
- Superior strut = clavicle; Inferior strut = scapular body–glenoid junction.
- Judet approach – between teres minor & infraspinatus.
- Pelvic ring analogy: one break → look for another.
Final Faculty Insight:
“The stability of your shoulder rests on an invisible ring. Once that ring breaks in two,
you must mend it — not just align it.”
