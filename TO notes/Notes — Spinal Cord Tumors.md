Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Spinal Cord
Tumors (2025)
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Spinal cord tumors are neoplasms arising within or adjacent to the spinal canal,
producing symptoms primarily through cord compression rather than invasion.
Dr. Vishnu’s Quote:
“Every spinal tumor teaches humility — because you are working millimeters
away from disaster.”
Key Facts: - Most primary CNS spinal tumors are benign (unlike intracranial
counterparts). - Presentation is due to compression, not infiltration. - Classification is
based on anatomic compartment: 1. Extradural (55%)
2. Intradural Extramedullary (40%)
3. Intramedullary (5%)

EXTRADURAL TUMORS (55%)
Most common: Metastatic deposits from breast, lung, prostate, kidney, thyroid.

Others: - Primary vertebral tumors – Chordoma, Osteoid osteoma, Osteoblastoma,
ABC, Chondrosarcoma, Osteochondroma, GCT. - Hematologic – Plasmacytoma,
Multiple Myeloma. - Pediatric – Ewing’s sarcoma.
Features: - Pain (nocturnal, non-mechanical)
- Neurological deficit due to compression
- Pathologic vertebral collapse or deformity
Radiology:
Destructive vertebral lesion with paraspinal mass; often osteolytic (except prostate =
osteoblastic).

INTRADURAL EXTRAMEDULLARY TUMORS (40%)
### 1⃣ Meningioma

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Epidemiology | Peak 50–70 yrs, Female:Male = 4:1 (1:1 lumbar) |
| Location | 82% thoracic, 15% cervical, 2% lumbar |
| Pathology | Arises from meninges; 90% intradural, 5% extradural |
| Clinical | Long tract sensory deficit → motor deficit → bladder involvement (51%) |
| Special Note | May enlarge rapidly during pregnancy due to progesterone sensitivity |
| Imaging | Contrast-enhancing, calcified lesion on MRI/CT |
| Treatment | Surgical excision + dural repair; recurrence ~7% after complete excision |

Exam Tip: Acute neurological deterioration during pregnancy → Think
spinal meningioma.

Dumbbell Tumors:
Hourglass-shaped lesions with intraspinal, foraminal, and extraforaminal
components.
Characteristic: Widened neural foramen on X-ray.
Classification (Asazuma): - Type I: Intradural + extradural (constriction at dura)
- Type II (a–c): Extradural ± paravertebral extension
- Type III (a–b): Both dural and foraminal constriction
- Type IV–VI: Vertebral or laminar invasion / bone erosion

with extradural extension.

INTRAMEDULLARY SPINAL CORD TUMORS (5%)
### Common Types: 1. Ependymoma (45%)
2. Astrocytoma (40%)
3. Others: Hemangioblastoma, Lipoma, Dermoid/Epidermoid

### 1⃣ Ependymoma

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Origin | From ependymal cells lining central canal |
| Location | Conus, filum terminale (myxopapillary type most common) |
| Age / Sex | 3rd–6th decade; slight male predominance |
| Association | NF2; may show drop metastasis through CSF |

<!-- page 4 -->

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Imaging | Sharply demarcated, encapsulated, contrast-enhancing; cystic degeneration in 50% |
| Treatment | Gross total excision (encapsulated → feasible) |
| Key Surgical Tip | Identify filum terminale by its whiter color & squiggly surface vessel before cutting |

Remember: “Drop metastasis” is typical of ependymoma — hence, full
neuraxis MRI is mandatory.

### 2⃣ Astrocytoma

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Age | Common in children (most frequent pediatric IMSCT) |
| Site | Thoracic > Cervical |
| Nature | Infiltrative, eccentrically located, poorly demarcated |
| Histology | Low:High-grade = 3:1 |
| Imaging | Cord expansion, cystic in ~40% |
| Surgery | Difficult – no clear plane from cord; limited excision/biopsy for high grade |
| Adjuncts | Radiotherapy ± Chemotherapy for high-grade lesions |

High surgical risk due to infiltration and rotation of cord – greatest challenge
in intramedullary tumors.

### 3⃣ Other Rare Intramedullary Lesions

| Tumor | Key Feature |
|---|---|
| Dermoid / Epidermoid | Common at conus; may be mixed intradural–intramedullary |
| Lipoma | Often with spinal dysraphism; causes tethered cord, bladder issues |
| Hemangioblastoma | Non-infiltrative, highly vascular, associated with VHL disease (33%) |

DIAGNOSIS & RADIOLOGY
MRI: Investigation of choice for all spinal tumors.
- Meningioma: Isointense, calcified, dural tail sign.
- Schwannoma: T2 hyperintense, cystic change, foraminal widening.
- Ependymoma: Central, well-defined, contrast-enhancing.
- Astrocytoma: Diffuse, infiltrative, poorly defined.
CT Scan: Useful for bone erosion, foraminal widening.
CSF Analysis: - ↑ Protein (95% cases).
- Froin’s syndrome = clotting + xanthochromia (complete block).
- Queckenstedt’s test: failure of pressure rise = block.

### MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES

| Category | Approach |
|---|---|
| Asymptomatic lesions | Observe with MRI every 6–12 months. |
| Symptomatic (compression / myelopathy) | Surgical excision as early as possible. |
| Low-grade Astrocytoma / | Microsurgical resection under |
| Ependymoma | SSEP/MEP monitoring. |
| High-grade Astrocytoma | Biopsy ± limited excision + RT/CT. |

Intraoperative Guidelines: - Myelotomy: Midline or paramedian to avoid posterior
median vein.
- Use of Ultrasound/Laser: For tumor debulking from within.
- Stop excision if MEP amplitude ↓ ≥50%.

### PROGNOSIS
- Better outcomes if initial neurological deficit is minimal.

- Recurrence depends on extent of excision and tumor biology.

- Ependymoma → best prognosis, Astrocytoma → guarded.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Most common spinal tumor overall → Metastasis.

<!-- page 6 -->

- Most common intradural extramedullary → Meningioma & Schwannoma (40% each).

- Most common intramedullary (adult) → Ependymoma.

- Most common intramedullary (child) → Astrocytoma.

- Pregnancy → meningioma may acutely enlarge.

- Dumbbell tumor → widening of neural foramen.

- Ependymoma → drop metastasis, myxopapillary at filum.

- Astrocytoma → infiltrative, no clear plane.

- Hemangioblastoma → associated with VHL.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Classify by compartment to guide differential diagnosis.

- MRI is diagnostic gold standard.

- Ependymoma – encapsulated and resectable; Astrocytoma – infiltrative and difficult.

- Always evaluate entire neuraxis for CSF seeding.

- Surgery guided by neuromonitoring; stop if MEPs drop ≥50%.

- Goal: Decompression, maximal safe excision, and neurological preservation.
