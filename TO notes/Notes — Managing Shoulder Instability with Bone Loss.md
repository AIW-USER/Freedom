### OVERVIEW
- This lecture focuses on management strategies once bone loss has been identified — covering glenoid and humeral defects, as well as bipolar lesions.
- Goal: Choose the appropriate soft-tissue vs bony reconstruction based on quantification of bone loss and risk factors. Quote: “When bone is lost, stability cannot be regained by sutures alone — bone must be replaced by bone.” – Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### WHY BANKART FAILS IN PRESENCE OF BONE LOSS
- Rowe (1978): First reported that bone loss correlates with Bankart failure.
- Burkhart & De Beer (2000): Contact athletes with glenoid defects had higher recurrence post-Bankart.
- Critical threshold: >21% glenoid width loss → isolated Bankart repair fails.

SURGICAL OPTIONS FOR GLENOID BONE LOSS
### 1⃣ Bristow Procedure (Helfet, 1958)
- Transfer tip of coracoid to glenoid neck medially.
- Mechanism: Sling effect via conjoined tendon (coracobrachialis + short head of biceps).
- Limitation: Non-anatomic, relies on soft-tissue restraint; poor bone coverage.
### 2⃣ Latarjet Procedure (Michel Latarjet, 1954)
- Coracoid osteotomy (2–3 cm) transferred along its long axis to anteroinferior glenoid neck.
- Combines osseous extension + dynamic sling effect + capsular reinforcement.
### Triple Stabilizing Effect (Patte):
1. Osseous block – restores glenoid arc.
2. Conjoint tendon sling – resists anterior translation in abduction + ER.
3. Tenodesis effect – conjoined tendon + lower subscapularis reinforce capsule. Gold Standard for significant anterior glenoid bone loss (>25%).
### 3⃣ Congruent-Arc Latarjet (De Beer)
- Coracoid rotated 90° so inferior surface becomes articular, improving curvature match.
- Provides wider articular arc and better graft–glenoid congruence.

### Arthroscopic Latarjet (Lafosse)
- Five stages: exposure → coracoid prep → drilling/osteotomy → transfer → fixation.
- Advantages: Better visualization, precise positioning, less overhang.
- Outcomes: Lafosse & Boyle (2010) – 91% excellent results; 80% grafts flush on CT.

BIPOLAR BONE LOSS (Glenoid + Humerus)
### Di Giacomo, Itoi, Burkhart (2014) – Instability Classification
Glenoid Hill–Sachs

| Defect | Lesion | Recommended Procedure |
|---|---|---|
| <25% | On-track | Arthroscopic Bankart |
| <25% | Off-track | Bankart + Remplissage |
| >25% | On-track | Latarjet |
| >25% | Off-track | Latarjet ± Humeral procedure (Remplissage / bone graft) |

Summary Principle: “On-track → soft-tissue safe; Off-track → needs bone.”

### KEY TAKEAWAYS
- ISIS >6 → bony procedure required.
- Critical Glenoid defect: >21% → Bankart fails.
- Latarjet = Gold standard (25–40% glenoid loss).
- ICBG / Allograft: For >40% or revision cases.
- Remplissage: Effective for moderate Hill–Sachs; minimal ROM loss.
- Rotational osteotomy: For large young lesions.
- RSA / Arthroplasty: For elderly with >40% humeral loss.
- Always assess On-track vs Off-track before final decision.
