Horizons of Limb Salvage Surgery – Dr. Rajat Gupta
Overview
Limb Salvage Surgery (LSS) aims to preserve limb function and quality of life while
achieving adequate oncologic clearance. Modern advances in imaging,
chemotherapy, prosthetics, and surgical precision have expanded the indications for
LSS significantly.
Faculty Remark: “Limb salvage is not about saving the limb at all costs. It is about
saving the patient, then the limb, and only then the function.”

## Evolution & Goals of Limb Salvage Surgery
### Core Objectives
- Achieve wide surgical margins.
- Maintain local disease control.
- Ensure meaningful limb function.
- Avoid undue complications.
### Why LSS Has Expanded
- Better imaging (MRI, PET CT).
- Advances in chemotherapy.
- Precise surgical techniques.
- Customizable and modular prostheses.
- Reconstruction options for bone and soft tissue defects.

## Oncologic Principles
### Requirements for Successful LSS
- Tumor confined in a manner that allows wide excision.
- Major neurovascular structures not encased.
- No uncontrolled infection.
- Patient fit for complex surgery.
Contraindications
- Unclear margins.
- Neurovascular involvement that compromises oncologic safety.
- Compartment contamination after improper biopsy.
- Fungating or uncontrolled local disease.

## Preoperative Evaluation
Imaging
- MRI: Gold standard for local staging.
- CT chest: Lung metastasis evaluation.
- PET CT: Metabolic activity and skip lesions.
- Assess muscle compartments and joint involvement.
### Biopsy Planning
- Ideally core needle biopsy.
- Must be on the planned resection incision line.
- Avoid contamination of uninvolved compartments.
“A bad biopsy can turn a salvageable limb into an amputation.”
## Surgical Planning
### Resection Principles
- Achieve wide margins.
- Preserve maximum functional anatomy.
- Identify neurovascular structures early.
- Plan reconstructive strategy before starting resection.
### Reconstruction Options
- Megaprosthesis.
- Allograft.
- Allograft prosthetic composite.
- Biological reconstructions (vascularized fibula, induced membrane).
- Rotationplasty.
- Extracorporeal irradiation and reimplantation.

## Megaprosthesis Reconstruction
Indications
- Large segmental bone loss.
- Joint around the tumor requiring resection.
- Need for immediate stability and early mobilization.

<!-- page 3 -->

Types
- Modular megaprosthesis (most common).
- Custom made prosthesis.
- Cemented or cementless stems.
Advantages
- Early mobilization.
- Predictable outcomes.
- Low mechanical failure in modern designs.
Limitations
- Infection risk.
- Aseptic loosening over years.
- Extensor mechanism reconstruction challenges (knee).

## Rotationplasty
Concept
- Remove diseased femur and knee, rotate lower limb by 180 degrees, and attach tibia to femur.
- Ankle functions as new knee joint.
Indications
- Children needing durable reconstruction.
- Massive resection where prosthesis will fail.
- Poor soft tissue envelope.
Advantages
- Durable functional limb.
- Excellent activity levels.
Limitations
- Cosmetic acceptance.
- Requires extensive physiotherapy.

## Biological Reconstruction
### Vascularized Fibular Graft
- Used for defects requiring biological healing.
- High union rates.

<!-- page 4 -->

### Extracorporeal Irradiation & Reimplantation
- Patient’s bone removed, irradiated, and reimplanted.
- Maintains anatomical geometry.
Allograft
- Structural stability.
- Can combine with prosthesis (APC).
Challenges
- Longer healing.
- Infection risk.
- Graft fractures.

## Soft Tissue Reconstruction
### Why Soft Tissue Matters
- Provides prosthesis coverage.
- Reduces infection.
- Protects neurovascular structures.
Techniques
- Local rotational flaps.
- Free flaps.
- Gastrocnemius flap for knee defects.
“A good flap can save a bad reconstruction, but a bad flap can ruin a perfect
surgery.”

## Complications in Limb Salvage Surgery
Early
- Infection.
- Wound necrosis.
- Neurovascular injury.
Late
- Aseptic loosening.
- Implant breakage.
- Local recurrence.
- Failure of biological reconstructions.

<!-- page 5 -->

### Exam Pearls
- LSS is contraindicated when wide margins cannot be achieved.
- Bad biopsy ruins salvage options.
- Megaprosthesis is preferred for large segmental defects.
- Rotationplasty is excellent for children with major resections.
- MRI is the most important investigation for local staging.
- Soft tissue reconstruction strongly influences infection rates.
- Aim is safe margin first, function later.

Final Faculty Insight: “Limb salvage surgery succeeds when we balance oncologic
safety with functional ambition. The margin decides survival, the reconstruction decides
quality of life.”
