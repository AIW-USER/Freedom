Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Treatment of
Idiopathic Scoliosis (2025)
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Idiopathic scoliosis refers to a three-dimensional deformity of the spine
characterized by a lateral curvature (Cobb’s angle >10°) with vertebral rotation,
occurring without any underlying congenital, neuromuscular, or syndromic cause.
Dr. Vishnu’s Quote:
“Treat the curve, but understand the growth — scoliosis is as much about
timing as it is about correction.”
Epidemiology:
- Most common type of scoliosis (80% of all).
- Predominantly affects adolescent girls (female:male = 6:1).
- Commonly right thoracic curve (Lenke Type 1).
Goal of Treatment:
Prevent curve progression, maintain spinal balance, and achieve a cosmetically and
functionally balanced spine at skeletal maturity.

CLASSIFICATION
### 1⃣ Based on Age of Onset (James’ Classification)

| Type | Age Range |
|---|---|
| Infantile | 0–3 years |
| Juvenile | 4–9 years |
| Adolescent (AIS) | 10–20 years |

AIS accounts for nearly 90% of idiopathic scoliosis cases.
### 2⃣ Based on Curve Pattern (Lenke Classification – 2001)

| Type | Curve Pattern |
|---|---|
| 1 | Main Thoracic |
| 2 | Double Thoracic |
| 3 | Double Major |

| Type | Curve Pattern |
|---|---|
| 4 | Triple Major |

| 6 | TL/Lumbar + Main Thoracic |

Modifiers: Lumbar modifier (A/B/C) and Sagittal modifier (-, N, +)

### NATURAL HISTORY & PROGRESSION
- Before skeletal maturity: Curves >25° are likely to progress.

- After maturity: Thoracic >50° or Lumbar >40° continue to progress 1–2°/year.
### Risk Factors for Progression

| Factor | Impact |
|---|---|
| Female sex | ↑ risk of progression |
| Curve magnitude | >25° before Risser 2 → high risk |
| Curve pattern | Thoracic > Lumbar |
| Growth potential | Risser 0–1 → highest risk |

Key Principle:
The smaller and younger the patient, the more growth remaining — and higher the risk
of progression.

RADIOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT
### Cobb’s Angle Measurement
- <25° → Observation

- 25–45° → Bracing

- 45° → Surgical indication
### Risser Grading (Iliac Apophysis Ossification)

| Grade | Ossification | Growth Potential |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | None | Immature |
| 1–2 | 25–50% | Peak growth (pubertal spurt) |
| 3 | 75% | Slowing growth |
| 4 | 100% | Nearly mature |

<!-- page 3 -->

| Grade | Ossification | Growth Potential |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Fusion complete | Mature |

### Skeletal Maturity Indicators
- Risser sign.

- Triradiate cartilage closure (onset of puberty).

- Fusion of greater trochanteric epiphysis (end of growth).

### MANAGEMENT ALGORITHM

| Cobb Angle | Skeletal Maturity | Management |
|---|---|---|
| <25° | Immature | Observation every 6 months |
| 25–45° | Immature & flexible | Bracing (TLSO/CTLSO) |
| >45° | Mature or Progressive | Surgery |

Goals:
Prevent progression, achieve balanced correction, and preserve motion segments
whenever possible.

### OBSERVATION
- For mild curves (<25°) without progression.

- X-rays every 6 months until skeletal maturity.

- Watch for:
  - Increase ≥5° in 6 months.

  - Curve changing pattern or side.

BRACING
Indication: - Skeletally immature (Risser 0–2).
- 25°–45° Cobb angle.
- Flexible curve pattern.
Goal:
Prevent curve progression — does not correct the deformity.

Duration:
16–23 hours/day (minimum 12 hrs). Continue till Risser 4–5 or 2 years post-menarche.
Gradual weaning over 6–9 months.
Bracing Contraindications: - Skeletally mature.
- Curve >45°.
- Rigid curves.
- Obesity or poor compliance.
Success Criteria: - Curve progression ≤5°.
- Avoidance of surgery.

SURGICAL MANAGEMENT
Indications: - Curve >45° with progression.
- Failed bracing.
- Pain or cosmetic deformity.
- Pulmonary compromise.

### Instrumentation Evolution

| Generation | System | Limitation / Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | Harrington rods | Distraction-based; loss of lordosis (flat back) |

| Generation | System | Limitation / Benefit |
|---|---|---|
| 2nd | Luque / Hartshill | Sublaminar wires; limited derotation |
| 3rd | Pedicle screw system | 3D correction + translation; gold standard |

Correction Techniques: - Ponte’s osteotomy: Posterior release by removing facets.
- Cantilever technique: Rod levering for alignment.
- Rod rotation (Derotation): 3D correction – converts coronal deformity into sagittal
alignment.
Intraoperative Neuromonitoring: | Type | Function | Interpretation |
|——|———–|—————-| | SSEP | Sensory tracts | Continuous; ↓ amplitude =

If neuromonitoring changes: check BP, Hb, positioning, temperature → consider
wake-up test.

### POSTOPERATIVE CARE
  - Early mobilization (within 48 hrs).

  - Avoid prolonged bracing unless fusion questionable.

  - Follow-up X-rays at 3, 6, and 12 months.

  - Regular physiotherapy for posture correction and strengthening.

### COMPLICATIONS
  - Neurologic injury: cord or root traction.

  - Pseudoarthrosis: 1–2%.

  - Infection: 1–2%.

  - Flat back syndrome: especially with Harrington rods.

  - Crankshaft phenomenon: anterior growth continues post posterior fusion in <10 yrs.

  - SMA syndrome: duodenal compression due to acute spinal correction.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Scoliosis = Cobb’s >10° + vertebral rotation.

- AIS → 10–20 yrs, right thoracic, female predominance.

- Risser 0–2 = active growth → high progression risk.

- Bracing = 25–45°, flexible, immature.

- Surgery = >45° or progression post-maturity.

- Pedicle screw constructs = gold standard.

- Posterior approach = most common.

- Crankshaft phenomenon = anterior column growth after posterior fusion.

- Use neuromonitoring (SSEP/MEP) in all cases.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Observe → Brace → Operate – stepwise treatment.

- AIS is primarily a growth-dependent deformity — timing of intervention is key.

- Posterior Spinal Fusion with pedicle screws = current gold standard.

- Bracing prevents progression, not correction.

- Aim: Stable, balanced, and aesthetically acceptable spine at maturity.
