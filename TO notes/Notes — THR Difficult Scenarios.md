Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

Overview
This lecture discusses THR in complex conditions like DDH, protrusio acetabuli,
ankylosis, arthrodesis, and cases with bone defects. It also covers exposure
techniques, trochanteric osteotomy types, and management of severe deformities.
These concepts are critical for DNB/FNB exams and revision arthroplasty practice.

GENERALhavePRINCIPLES
- Always FOR DIFFICULT THR multiple implant options bone grafts and ready.
- Posterior approach preferred → extensile exposure.
- Explore and protect the sciatic nerve before implant placement.
- Add trochanteric osteotomy for difficult exposure (especially acetabular side).
- Delay weight bearing for 2–3 months to allow osseointegration (especially in uncemented implants).

DEVELOPMENTAL DYSPLASIA OF HIP (DDH)
### Classification Systems
1. Crowe’s Classification – based on % of subluxation: <50%, 50–75%, 75–100%, >100%.
2. Hartofilakidis Classification:
  - Dysplasia – head partially contained in true acetabulum.
  - Low Dislocation – false acetabulum above true one.
  - High Dislocation – head migrated completely posterosuperiorly.
MCQ Tip: Hartofilakidis preferred in clinical use for surgical planning.
### Surgical Strategy
- Use posterior approach with trochanteric osteotomy if needed.
- Identify true acetabulum using inferior margin and acetabular notch.
- Medialize cup to re-establish anatomic hip center.
- Handle anterior wall carefully → thin in DDH.
- Cementless cup preferred; cemented cup acceptable if poor bone stock.

### Cotyloplasty Technique
- Deliberate perforation of medial wall → medialization and cup centralization.
- Pack autograft (head chips) beneath cup for bony support.
### Femoral Shortening (Dunn & Hess Technique)
- Osteotomy just distal to lesser trochanter; remove bone segment for controlled shortening.
- GT distalization → attach GT fragment laterally for abductor tension.
### Acetabular Component Tips
- Use small acetabular cups (<40 mm) and small femoral heads (≤28 mm).
- Use anatomical stems with anterior bow (Khanduja type) for narrow canals.

PROTRUSIO ACETABULI
### Definition & Etiology
- Definition: Femoral head lies medial to Köhler’s line.
- Primary (Idiopathic) – bilateral, seen in women (arthrokatadysis).
- Secondary – due to RA, Paget’s, post-traumatic, or old hemiarthroplasty (Austin Moore/Thompson prosthesis).

### Surgical Challenges
- Difficult dislocation (head incarcerated inside pelvis).
- Fragile medial wall → risk of cup migration.

### Surgical Strategy
1. Remove posterior acetabular wall osteophytes for posterior dislocation.
2. Use double femoral neck osteotomy (divergent cuts) to aid head extraction.
3. Reconstruct true hip center using Köhler’s & Shenton’s lines intersection.
4. Rebuild medial wall with:
  - Morselized autograft (from femoral head) ± wire mesh or tantalum chips.
  - Optional anti-protrusio cage if large defect.
5. Reverse reaming over graft for impaction. Key Concept: Unlike standard THR (medialize), in protrusio you err towards lateralization to maintain horizontal offset & prevent over-medialization.

ANKYLOSIS (FUSED HIP)
Challenges
- Difficult dislocation; fused joint.
- Abductors may be weak/atrophic → affects post-op gait.

### Surgical Strategy
1. Double femoral neck osteotomy (divergent cuts) to aid dislocation.
2. Ream under C-arm guidance after determining depth via drill.
3. Identify acetabular center using Shenton’s & Köhler’s lines.
4. Pre-op check of abductors:
  - No role of EMG/MRI; clinical palpation in lateral decubitus with knee flexed.
5. Counseling: Abductor recovery may take up to 2 years; pain persists till recovery.

ARTHRODESIS (FUSED HIP SURGICALLY)
### Indications for Conversion to THR
- Severe low back pain or ipsilateral knee pain.
- Contralateral hip pain due to altered mechanics.
- Painful pseudoarthrosis. NOT an indication: Fusion in improper position → corrective osteotomy, not THR.

Prognosis
- Poorest among all difficult THR scenarios.
- High risk of heterotopic ossification and infection.

MANAGEMENT OF BONE DEFECTS (REVISION THR CONTEXT)
Causes
- Osteolysis (poly wear, infection, loosening)
- Excessive bone removal in prior surgery
- Dysplasia / trauma / old fractures

KEYDDHEXAM

PEARLS
Classification: Hartofilakidis (clinical relevance).
- Cotyloplasty: Controlled medial wall perforation for medialization.
- Dunn & Hess: Femoral shortening distal to LT + GT distalization.
- Protrusio: Err toward lateralization.
- Ankylosis: Check abductors clinically, not radiologically.
- Arthrodesis: Improper fusion → corrective osteotomy, not THR.
- Paprosky (Femur): IIIB = Distal fluted stem.
- Cup-cage construct: Graft → Cage → Cement → Cup sequence.
- Delayed WB: 2–3 months in all complex cases.
