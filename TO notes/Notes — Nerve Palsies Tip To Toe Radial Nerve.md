Nerve Palsies Tip to Toe – Radial Nerve –
Dr. Priyanka Sharma
Overview / Key Concepts
The radial nerve is the primary extensor nerve of the upper limb, responsible for
elbow, wrist, finger, and thumb extension. Radial nerve palsy leads to wrist drop,
finger drop, and loss of power grip, while sensory loss is usually less disabling than
motor loss.
Unlike median and ulnar nerve palsies, radial nerve injury is most often discussed as: -
Complete radial nerve palsy, or - Posterior interosseous nerve (PIN) palsy
This lecture emphasizes level of injury, internal splinting, and tendon transfer
strategies, which are frequently tested in exams.

Anatomy of the Radial Nerve
### Root Value
- C5 to T1
- Arises from posterior cord of brachial plexus

Course
Axilla
- Lies posterior to axillary artery
- Exits via triangular interval
- Branches to long and lateral heads of triceps
### Arm (Radial / Spiral Groove)
- Gives branch to medial head of triceps
- Closely related to deep brachial artery
- Common site of injury in fracture shaft humerus
### Elbow and Forearm
- Crosses anterior to lateral epicondyle
- Enters cubital fossa
- Divides into:
  - Deep branch (motor)
  - Superficial branch (sensory)

<!-- page 2 -->

### Posterior Interosseous Nerve (PIN)
- Continuation of deep branch after passing under supinator
- Compression occurs at Arcade of Frohse
- Causes finger and thumb drop without sensory loss

Motor Innervation
### Proximal Branches
- Triceps brachii
- Anconeus
- Brachioradialis
- Extensor carpi radialis longus (ECRL)
### Deep Branch
- Extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB)
- Supinator
### PIN Muscles
- Abductor pollicis longus (APL)
- Extensor pollicis brevis (EPB)
- Extensor pollicis longus (EPL)
- Extensor carpi ulnaris (ECU)
- Extensor digitorum communis (EDC)
- Extensor digiti minimi (EDM)
- Extensor indicis proprius (EIP)

### Sensory Supply
- Lower lateral cutaneous nerve of arm
- Posterior cutaneous nerve of arm
- Posterior cutaneous nerve of forearm
- Superficial radial nerve to:
  - Dorsum of hand
  - First web space (key sensory area)

### Etiology of Radial Nerve Palsy
- Saturday night palsy
- Crutch paralysis
- Fracture shaft humerus (most common)
- Wartenberg syndrome (compression by brachioradialis)

<!-- page 3 -->

- Posterior interosseous nerve palsy
- Supinator syndrome (Arcade of Frohse)

### Clinical Features
- Loss of elbow extension (high lesions)
- Weak supination
- Wrist drop
- Finger drop
- Thumb extension loss

Diagnosis
- Clinical examination
- Nerve conduction studies
- Electromyography
- MRI (selected cases)

Nerve Repair Options
### Primary Repair
- Epineural repair (gold standard)
### Nerve Grafting
- Cable nerve graft when gap present
- Sural nerve commonly used

### Motor Nerve Transfers
- Median nerve fascicles to radial nerve
- FDS or FCR fascicles to:
  - PIN
  - ECRB
Branch to pronator teres and AIN must be preserved
### Sensory Nerve Transfers
- Median or ulnar nerve fascicles to radial sensory nerve
- Key area: first web space

<!-- page 4 -->

### LABC Transfer
- Lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve to radial sensory nerve
- Does not fully restore sensation
- Reduces painful neuroma and phantom pain

### Tendon Transfer Principles
1. Supple joints
2. Good soft tissue cover
3. Adequate excursion
4. Donor power ≥4/5
5. Expendable donor
6. Straight line of pull
7. Synergy and tenodesis
8. Single function per transfer

### Goals of Tendon Transfer in Radial Nerve Palsy
- Wrist extension
- Finger extension
- Thumb extension and radial abduction
- Wrist stabilization

Internal Splinting (Radial Nerve Specific)
Concept
- Early tendon transfer done at time of nerve repair
- Prevents stretching of denervated extensors
- Improves power grip while awaiting nerve recovery
Procedure
- Pronator teres to ECRB (end-to-side)
Difference from Formal Transfer
- ECRB insertion preserved
- ECRB reinnervation expected

Formal Tendon Transfers (Secondary Reconstruction)
### Modified Robert Jones Procedure
- PT → ECRL and ECRB

<!-- page 5 -->

- FCU → EDC (3rd, 4th, 5th fingers)
- FCR → EDC (index), EIP, EPL
FCU expendability is debated
### Standard FCU Transfer
- PT → ECRB
- FCU → EDC
- Palmaris longus → EPL
### Single FCU Split Transfer
- FCU split to EDC and EPL

### Brand FCR Transfer
- PT → ECRB
- FCR → EDC
- Palmaris longus → EPL

### Boyes Superficialis Transfer
- PT → ECRB
- FDS (ring) → EDC (3,4,5)
- FDS (middle) → EIP and EPL
- FCR → APL and EPB

Free Functional Muscle Transfer
Indications
- Severe muscle loss (e.g., Volkmann ischemic contracture)
Procedure
- Gracilis transfer to EDC or wrist extensors
- Motor: PIN (if intact)
- Vascular: Posterior interosseous artery or radial recurrent branch

### Postoperative Immobilization
- Long arm splint
- Elbow in resting position

<!-- page 6 -->

- Forearm: 15–30° pronation
- Wrist: ~45° extension
- MCP joints: 10–15° flexion
- Thumb: maximum extension and abduction
- Cast removal: 4–6 weeks
- Followed by short arm splint and supervised physiotherapy

Faculty Quote
“Internal splinting is unique to radial nerve palsy and protects extensor function while
the nerve recovers.” – Dr. Priyanka Sharma

### Exam Pearls
- Radial nerve palsy causes wrist and finger drop
- PIN palsy has no sensory loss
- Fracture shaft humerus is the most common cause
- First web space sensation is key sensory area
- Internal splinting is specific for radial nerve palsy
- PT to ECRB is the constant transfer in all procedures
- FCU expendability is controversial
- Gracilis free flap can restore finger extension in selected cases
