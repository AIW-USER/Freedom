Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

OVERVIEW
Revision ACL reconstruction is a technically demanding surgery performed after
failed primary ACL reconstruction. It requires precise tunnel assessment, graft
selection, and correction of all contributing factors to ensure stability and function.
Quote: “Revision ACL begins with a prayer — precision, planning, and
patience are your only allies.” – Dr. Mukul Mohindra

COMMON CAUSES OF FAILURE
### 1⃣ Technical Errors – Malpositioned Tunnels
- Commonest cause (>50% of failures).
- Vertical femoral tunnel → persistent rotatory instability.
- Anterior tibial tunnel → extension block (notch impingement / nutcracker knee).

<!-- page 2 -->

- Anterior femoral tunnel → captured knee (short graft length, tight in extension).
- Posterior tibial tunnel → flexion loss (rare).
### 2⃣ Missed Associated Ligament Injuries
- Anterolateral ligament (ALL) – most commonly missed.
- Posterolateral corner (PLC) injuries – often coexist with ACL tears. If unrecognized, these lead to persistent pivoting and instability despite graft integrity.
### 3⃣ Re-Rupture
- Common in contact sports (trauma) or with high posterior tibial slope (>12°).
- Consider simultaneous HTO to correct slope in revision setting.
### 4⃣ Alignment & Anatomy Errors
- Genu valgum / increased tibial slope → repeat failures.
- Always correct malalignment before or along with revision.

TUNNEL ASSESSMENT & RADIOLOGIC MARKERS
### Tibial Tunnel
- Should exit between tibial spines at 30–45° angle (AP view).
- Lateral view: Tunnel should lie posterior to Blumensaat line.
### Femoral Tunnel
- Ideal position = 10:30 (right knee) or 1:30 (left knee) clock-face.
- On lateral X-ray: locate posterior 1/4th of condyle – tunnel centered there.
- Arthroscopic landmark: Resident’s ridge → bifurcate ridge. Malposition is best detected by Rosenberg view + MRI tunnel mapping.

Step 1: Categorize the Failure
Identify whether failure is:
- Instability (tunnel error / graft re-rupture)
- Motion loss (arthrofibrosis / cyclops)
- Extensor dysfunction
- OA progression

### Step 3: Tunnel Planning
- New tibial tunnel should be more lateral & posterior to old one.
- Prefer outside-in drilling in revision (better control, avoids merging). Always visualize old tunnel arthroscopically; preserve hardware unless interfering.

### Avoid Tunnel Dilatation
- Occurs due to synovial seepage or graft micromotion.
- Prevent via:
  - Screw placement near joint line.
  - Preservation of tibial stump.
  - Correct tunnel diameter & snug fit.
Bungee cord effect – longitudinal graft motion. Windshield wiper effect –
transverse graft motion.
Both → cause tunnel widening & graft loosening.

### Arthrolysis Sequence (Arthroscopic)
1. Release suprapatellar pouch (improves flexion).
2. Clear medial/lateral gutters (adds flexion).
3. Anterior interval release (removes fat pad – restores extension).
4. Retinacular release (improves patellar mobility).
5. Notch clearance (removes cyclops/fibrosis).
6. Manipulation under anesthesia (MUA) – only after arthrolysis. Extension loss is less tolerated than flexion loss → always correct first.

### QUADRICEPS INHIBITION & ACL REFLEX
- Mechanoreceptors in proximal ACL control reciprocal activation.
- Injury → continuous hamstring activation + quad inhibition → extensor lag.
- Reflex disruption → VMO atrophy & anterior knee pain.

### KEY PEARLS
- Tunnel malposition = 50% of failures.
- Check posterior tibial slope (>12° → consider HTO).
- Re-rupture: assess for missed ALL/PLC.
- Preserve ACL stump → prevents tunnel widening.
- Hybrid fixation (cortical + aperture) preferred.

<!-- page 6 -->

- Rosenberg view: early OA detection.
- Cyclops vs Cyclopoid: hard = symptomatic.
- Arthrolysis → MUA sequence, not reverse.
- Loss of extension worse than loss of flexion.
- Two-stage revision: tunnel >8 mm or merged.
- Graft choice: contralateral hamstring → quadriceps → peroneus.
