Septic Arthritis and Its Sequelae – Dr. Shalin Shah
Overview / Key Concepts
Septic arthritis is an acute bacterial infection of synovial joints that rapidly destroys
cartilage and can extend into the metaphysis. It is more common than osteomyelitis in
infancy and childhood, especially under 5 years. Early diagnosis and urgent surgical
lavage are critical to prevent long term sequelae such as deformity, limb length
discrepancy and hip instability.

Pathophysiology
### Modes of Infection
- Hematogenous spread
- Direct inoculation
- Adjacent infection
Children under 18 months have transphyseal spread, allowing metaphyseal infection
to enter the joint.

### Common Joints Affected
- Hip (most common)
- Knee
- Shoulder
- Ankle
- Elbow
### Destruction Mechanism
- Bacteria and activated neutrophils release proteolytic enzymes (MMPs).
- Rapid degradation of hyaline cartilage.
- Damage begins within 8 hours, making this a surgical emergency.
Consequences
- Increased intracapsular pressure causes ischemia of femoral head.
- Growth plate damage leads to deformities.
- Capsular and ligamentous destruction may cause dislocation.

Clinical Presentation
History
- NICU admission

<!-- page 2 -->

- Long term antibiotics masking symptoms
- Decreased feeding
- Immunocompromised child
Examination
- Irritability
- Pseudoparalysis
- Failure to thrive
- Fever
- Limb held in position of comfort (hip in flexion, abduction, external rotation)
### Laboratory Markers
- Elevated total leukocyte count
- ESR > 40 mm per hour
- CRP > 20 mg per L
- Blood culture (positive in 60 percent)
- PCR increases diagnostic yield
### Differential Diagnosis
- Transient synovitis
- Osteomyelitis
- Juvenile arthritis
- Lyme disease (rare in India)

Diagnostic Criteria
### Kocher Criteria
1. Inability to bear weight
2. History of fever
3. ESR > 40 mm per hour
4. WBC > 12000 per microlitre
Presence of all four strongly predicts septic arthritis.

### Caird Predictors
- Fever is the strongest predictor.
- Followed by CRP, ESR and refusal to bear weight.

Imaging
Radiographs
- Joint space widening

<!-- page 3 -->

- Lateral subluxation of head
- Metaphyseal erosion in late stages
Ultrasonography
- Detects effusion
- Identifies septations
### MRI
- Gold standard for marrow edema and early osteomyelitis
- Essential for detecting obturator internus abscess
### Obturator Internus Abscess
- Often mistaken for septic hip
- Requires drainage via subadductor approach

Principles of Management
Surgical Emergency
Cartilage destruction begins within 8 hours, so drainage must be early.

### Aims of Treatment
- Reduce intra-articular pressure
- Prevent growth plate damage
- Reduce bacterial load
- Prevent joint destruction
### Standard Treatment
- Intravenous antibiotics
- Arthrotomy and lavage
- Immobilization in cast
- Drill metaphysis if MRI unavailable
### Indications for Arthrotomy
- Any hip septic arthritis
- Any delayed presentation
- Any obturator internus abscess
### Recent Advances
- Arthroscopic drainage of pediatric hip without traction

Sequelae of Septic Arthritis
### Natural History
- Damage and final outcome difficult to predict
- Symptoms may appear years later
### Clinical Complaints
- Pain
- Stiffness
- Limb length discrepancy
- Limp
- Back or knee pain
- Emotional and psychosocial burden
Possible Pathologic Manifestations
Acetabulum: dysplasia, osteomyelitis, late degeneration
Epiphysis and Physis: - AVN - Growth arrest - Overgrowth or undergrowth - Coxa vara
- Coxa valga - Excessive femoral anteversion or retroversion
Proximal Femur: pseudoarthrosis, pathological fracture, osteomyelitis

### Growth Plate Deformity Patterns
- Inferior physis affected: coxa vara
- Superior physis affected: coxa valga
- Anterior physis: femoral anteversion
- Posterior physis: retroversion

Classification Systems
Hunka Classification
Type I to V based on severity, ranging from minimal involvement to total destruction with
dislocation.

Choi Classification
Focuses on timing and ossification center appearance, including Perthes like hips.

Forlin and Milani
Simplified into located and dislocated hips and degree of head involvement.

<!-- page 5 -->

Johari Classification
Most comprehensive Indian classification. - Group 1: loss of capital femoral epiphysis -
Group 2: unstable hips without CFE - Group 3: unstable hips with CFE - Group 4: AVN
and physis deformities - Group 5: pseudoarthrosis

Management of Sequelae
Principles
1. Restore cephalic coverage and congruity
2. Correct limb length discrepancy
3. Improve abductor function
4. Correct malalignment
5. Increase hip stability
Procedures
1. Cephalic Coverage and Congruity
- Abduction cast
- Traction
- Osteochondroplasty
2. Limb Length Discrepancy
- Shoe raise
- Contralateral epiphysiodesis
- Ipsilateral lengthening via external fixator
3. Abductor Insufficiency
- GT epiphysiodesis (<7 years)
- GT advancement or distalization (>7 years)
4. Malalignment
- Pelvic osteotomies
- Femoral valgus or varus osteotomies
5. Instability
- Pelvic and femoral osteotomies
- Neck reconstruction procedures
- Trochanteric arthroplasty

Ilizarov Hip Reconstruction (IHR)
Addresses: - Abductor lurch - Limb length discrepancy - Hip instability

<!-- page 6 -->

Components
1. Proximal femoral pelvic support osteotomy (valgus)
2. Distal femoral varus osteotomy
3. Gradual lengthening
Indications
- DDH sequelae
- Traumatic hip dislocation sequelae
- Post septic arthritis sequelae
- Head or neck absence
- Skeletal dysplasias
Contraindications
- Age <12 years
- Young adults eligible for THR
- Chronic paralytic hips

Faculty Quote
“Early recognition and aggressive management prevent lifelong disability. Once
sequelae set in, treatment must be tailored to the child’s anatomy and biomechanics.”

### Exam Pearls
- Hip is the most common joint affected in pediatric septic arthritis.
- Cartilage destruction starts within 8 hours.
- Kocher criteria do not include CRP.
- Fever is the strongest predictor.
- MRI is mandatory in delayed presentation.
- Obturator internus abscess must be ruled out.
- Growth plate damage determines long term deformity.
- Choi classification is most clinically applied.
- Ilizarov hip reconstruction corrects LLD, instability and abductor lurch simultaneously.
