Intertrochanteric Fractures – Dr. Daivik T Shetty
Overview
Intertrochanteric fractures are extracapsular fractures of the proximal femur, occurring
between the greater and lesser trochanters. They account for nearly half of all hip
fractures and are most common in elderly osteoporotic women. These fractures require
timely stabilization to restore mobility and prevent complications.
Faculty Remark: “Understand the fracture pattern, understand the forces, and your
implant choice becomes simple.” – Dr. Daivik

### Epidemiology and Risk Factors
- More common in older adults with trivial trauma.
- Women more affected due to postmenopausal osteoporosis.
- High risk patients: neurologic illness, impaired gait, poor vision, malnutrition, alcohol use.
### Cumming’s Four Factors Influencing Hip Fractures
1. Faller orientation during impact.
2. Soft tissue coverage over the hip.
3. Adequacy of protective responses.
4. Residual bone strength at the hip.

Anatomy and Pathoanatomy
### Key Structures
- Combination of cortical and cancellous bone.
- Calcar femorale (Adams arch): Strong posteromedial strut crucial for stability.
- Hip capsule: anterior attachment at intertrochanteric line, posteriorly ends 1.5 inches proximal to crest.
- Musculotendinous attachments: GT (gluteus medius/minimus), LT (iliopsoas), short external rotators, vastus lateralis.
### Deforming Forces

| Muscle | Effect |
|---|---|
| Gluteus medius, external rotators | Abduction, external rotation, proximal migration |
| Iliopsoas | Flexion of proximal fragment |

| Muscle | Effect |
|---|---|
| Adductors, hamstrings | Posterior, medial displacement of shaft, shortening |

### Classification Systems
1. Boyd and Griffin
- Type 1: Stable two part.
- Type 2: Unstable, comminuted.
- Type 3: Reverse oblique.
- Type 4: Intertrochanteric with subtrochanteric extension.
2. Evans Description
- Type 1: Stable fractures (undisplaced, reducible displaced).
- Type 2: Reverse oblique fractures.
3. Jensen Modification
- Type 3: GT involvement.
- Type 4: LT involvement.
- Type 5: Both GT and LT.
- Type R: Reverse oblique.
4. AO Classification (Old)
- A1: Simple pertrochanteric.
- A2: Multi fragmentary.
- A3: Reverse oblique.
### AO Classification (2018 – Revised)

| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| 31A1 | Simple fracture, lateral wall intact (>20.5 mm) |
| 31A2 | Multifragmentary fracture, lateral wall incompetent (<20.5 mm) |
| 31A3 | Reverse oblique |

### Importance of Lateral Wall
- Lateral wall thickness <20.5 mm → unstable fracture → PFN preferred.

Clinical Features and Imaging
Presentation
- Pain, inability to bear weight.
- Leg shortened, externally rotated.
Imaging
- X ray pelvis with hip AP.
- Lateral view for posterior comminution.
- CT scan recommended for assessing:
  - Lateral wall thickness.
  - Posteromedial comminution.
  - Reverse obliquity.
  - 3D planning.

Management Principles
### Conservative Management
- Rare, reserved for non ambulatory or severe comorbid patients.
- Traction 6 to 8 weeks with complications of recumbency.
### Isolated GT / LT Fractures
- Usually conservative.
- LT fracture secondary to tumor → internal fixation.

Choice of Implant
### Historical Progression
- Jewett nail → Sliding nails → DHS → Condylocephalic nails → Gamma nail → Proximal femoral nail (current gold standard).
### Current Options

| Implant | Notes |
|---|---|
| Dynamic Hip Screw (DHS) | Works well in stable fractures with intact lateral wall |
| Proximal Femur Nail (PFN) | Preferred in unstable fractures, reverse oblique, lateral wall deficiency |
| Proximal Femur Locking Plate | Reserved for young with narrow canal or complex patterns |
| Angle Blade Plate | Good in reverse oblique |

| Implant | Notes |

| Medoff plate | Biaxial dynamization, A3 fractures |

Dynamic Hip Screw (DHS)
### Richard Screw Dimensions
- Thread diameter: 14 mm
- Core diameter: 8 mm
- Thread length: 22 mm
- Barrel: 25 mm (short) or 38 mm (long)
### Barrel Selection Rule
- Lag screw > 85 mm → long barrel.
- Lag screw < 85 mm → short barrel.
### Importance of Lateral Support
- If lateral wall is deficient:
  - Add TSP (Trochanteric Stabilization Plate)
  - Use short barrel
  - Use 5 hole side plate

Cephalomedullary Nails (PFN)
Advantages
- Acts as lateral buttress.
- Better mechanical advantage.
- Controls medialization.
- Superior in reverse oblique and unstable patterns.
Complications
- Z effect and reverse Z effect.
  - Prox screw migration and distal screw penetration.
  - More common with posteromedial comminution.
### Cement Augmented PFN
- Used in severe osteoporosis.
- Cement extrudes through fenestrated screws to improve anchorage.

### Assessing Quality of Fixation
1. Garden Alignment Index
- AP: 160 to 180 degrees.
- Lateral: 180 degrees.
2. Tip Apex Distance (TAD)
- AP + lateral distance < 25 mm.
3. Parker Ratio
- Measures screw position.
- AP: 0 is inferior, 100 is superior.
- Lateral: 0 posterior, 100 anterior.

Which Implant to Choose?
### Based on Fracture Pattern

| Pattern | Preferred Implant |
|---|---|
| Stable, good lateral wall | DHS |
| Lateral wall incompetent | PFN or DHS+TSP |
| Reverse oblique | PFN |
| Posteromedial comminution | PFN or valgus osteotomy |
| Subtrochanteric extension | Long PFN + cerclage |

### Based on Patient Profile

| Scenario | Implant |
|---|---|
| Young, stable fracture | DHS |
| Narrow canal | Proximal femur locking plate |
| Elderly with arthritis | Arthroplasty |
| Others | PFN |

### Viva and Exam Pearls
- Lateral wall thickness <20.5 mm defines unstable IT fracture.
- PFN is preferred in reverse oblique fractures.
- TAD must be <25 mm to prevent cutout.
- Calcar femorale integrity is critical for stability.
- Posteromedial comminution is a major instability factor.
- Z effect seen in PFN with two screw design.
