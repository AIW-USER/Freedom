Calcaneum Fracture – Dr. Rahul Upadhyay
Overview
Calcaneal fractures are the most common tarsal fractures and often occur in high
energy trauma. Up to 75 percent are displaced intraarticular injuries, and most patients
are young, working adults. Mismanagement leads to lifelong pain, loss of function, and
major socioeconomic impact.
Faculty Remark: “Understand the 3D anatomy of the calcaneus. Without that, every
reduction becomes suboptimal.”

Anatomy Essentials
Calcaneus has six surfaces, each of which must be reconstructed for anatomical
reduction.

### Superior Surface
- Contains three articular facets:
  - Posterior facet (largest, most important for subtalar joint mechanics).
  - Middle facet.
  - Anterior facet.
- These facets are the key reference points during reduction.
### Anterior Surface
- Saddle shaped.
- Forms the calcaneocuboid (CC) joint.
### Lateral Surface
- Peroneal tubercle.
- Groove for peroneal tendons.
- Lateral wall often bulges in displaced fractures.
### Medial Surface
- Contains the sustentaculum tali, the “stable fragment” in most fractures.
- Strong attachment of deltoid ligament.
### Inferior Surface
- Calcaneal tuberosity.
- Fat pad provides shock absorption.

### Why the Sustentaculum Tali Matters
- In ~60–70 percent of fractures it remains intact.
- Reconstruction begins from this fragment.
- If fractured, medial approach is required.

### Mechanism of Injury
- Fall from height (most common).
- Road traffic accidents.
Essex Lopresti Patterns
Primary fracture line - Always lateral, due to lateral process of talus impacting
calcaneus. - Divides bone into: - Medial sustentacular fragment. - Anterolateral
fragment.
Secondary fracture line determines type: - Joint depression type – fracture line
separates posterior facet from tuberosity. - Tongue type – posterior facet remains
attached to tuberosity.
(Visualised clearly on models and CT images in transcript pages 7–12.)

Radiographic Evaluation
### Recommended X-rays
- Foot AP and lateral.
- Axial heel (Harris view).
- Ankle AP and mortise views.
- Full foot views to assess missed Lisfranc injuries.
- Spine and pelvis due to high association (10–21 percent) with other injuries.
Key Radiographic Angles
### Böhler’s Angle
- Normal: 25–40 degrees.
- Decreased in most fractures.
### Crucial Angle of Gissane
- Normal: 120–145 degrees.
- Increased in joint depression fractures.
### Double Density Sign
- Indicates partial depression of posterior facet.

<!-- page 3 -->

Harris Axial View
Assesses: - Varus. - Widening. - Articular surface alignment.

### Broden Views
- Evaluate posterior facet reduction intraoperatively.

CT Scan Essentials
CT is mandatory.
Correct technique: - ≤2 mm slice interval. - 30 degree semicoronal cuts (critical for
Sanders classification). - Axial cuts to identify: - Anterolateral fragment. - CC joint
involvement. - Sustentacular fracture. - Sagittal cuts for tuberosity alignment and facet
depression.
Faculty Insight: “If CT is not done in 30 degree semicoronal, your Sanders
classification becomes unreliable.”

## Sanders Classification (Semicoronal CT)
- Type I: Nondisplaced.
- Type II: One fracture line → two articular fragments.
- Type III: Two fracture lines → three fragments.
- Type IV: Highly comminuted.
Sanders type guides management, not prognosis alone.

### Clinical Evaluation
- Soft tissue status (swelling, blisters).
- Compartment syndrome of foot (4 compartments: medial, lateral, superficial central, deep central).
- Plantar ecchymosis → think midfoot injury.
- Assess associated spine injuries.
Wrinkle sign is essential before doing extensile approach.

Non Operative Management
Only indicated in: - Extraarticular undisplaced fractures. - Sanders Type I fractures. -
Severe PVD. - Uncontrolled diabetes. - Elderly minimally ambulatory patients. -
Extensive open wounds. - Life threatening polytrauma.

<!-- page 4 -->

Protocol: - Below knee slab → cast/boot after swelling settles. - ROM at 4 weeks. -
NWB 6–12 weeks.

Operative Management
Most calcaneal fractures must be operated (70–80 percent require surgery).

Approaches
- Extensile lateral approach (ELA) – gold standard for full visualization.
- Sinus tarsi approach – minimal invasive, excellent for early surgery.
- Percutaneous fixation – selected patterns.
- Medial approach – sustentacular involvement.
- Combined medial lateral – complex fractures.

### Goals of Surgery
1. Restore posterior facet anatomy.
2. Restore calcaneal height.
3. Correct heel alignment.
4. Reduce lateral wall bulge.
5. Correct talar inclination.
Talar Attitude: - A horizontal talus postoperatively indicates inadequate reduction.
- Compare with contralateral side.

### Intraoperative Principles
1. Reduction Sequence
1. Posterior facet reconstruction.
2. Medial sustentacular alignment.
3. Correct varus/valgus.
4. Restore height.
5. Fix tuberosity.
6. Address CC joint.
2. Lateral Wall Osteotomy
- Essential for depressed facets.
- Remove wall, elevate facet from neutral triangle, then fix.
3. Use of Retractors
- Minimal use to prevent flap necrosis.

<!-- page 5 -->

- Thick full thickness flap essential in ELA.
4. Flap Handling
- Never use toothed forceps.
- Hold flap from inside.
5. Suturing Technique
- Use Allgöwer Donati sutures to preserve vascularity.
6. No Bone Grafting Needed
- Calcaneus is cancellous and void fills naturally.
“If you can reconstruct the facet, grafting is pointless.”
### Pearls for Sinus Tarsi Approach
- Can be done on day 1.
- After 7–10 days, fragments become sticky and reduction becomes difficult.
- Best for Sanders II and III patterns.

### Indications for Primary Subtalar Fusion
- Sanders Type IV where anatomical reconstruction not feasible.
- Chronic malunion with arthritis.
- Poor bone stock.

### Malunited Calcaneus – Causes of Pain
1. Subfibular impingement.
2. Lateral wall blowout.
3. Loss of height → horizontal talus.
4. Heel varus.
5. Facet incongruity (least common source of pain).

### Exam Pearls
- Primary fracture line is lateral.
- Joint depression vs tongue type → determined by secondary fracture line.
- Böhler angle decreases, Gissane increases in most fractures.
- Double density sign → partial facet depression.
- Sanders classification uses 30 degree semicoronal CT.
- Sustentaculum tali → stable fragment unless broken.

<!-- page 6 -->

- Sinus tarsi → early surgery. Extensile → wait for wrinkle sign.
- Most reliable intraoperative view for facet reduction → Broden view.
- Calcaneus unites fast → after 3 weeks call it malunited.

Final Faculty Insight: “Calcaneal reconstruction is a 3D puzzle. If the talus looks
right, the calcaneus is right.”
