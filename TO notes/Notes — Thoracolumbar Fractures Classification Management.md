### OVERVIEW
- Thoracolumbar junction (T9–L2) is the most common site of spinal fractures due to the transition between rigid thoracic and mobile lumbar spine.
- Common causes: Road traffic accidents, falls, sports trauma.
- Objective: understand biomechanics, stability concepts, classification, and management. “A single break may be stable, but when the ring breaks in two places, instability is inevitable.” – Dr. C S Vishnu Prasath

FUNCTION & STABILITY OF THE SPINAL COLUMN
Functions:
1. Protects the spinal cord.
2. Provides flexibility for motion.
3. Supports axial load of the body.

### Ligamentous Support:

| Ligament | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Anterior Longitudinal | Anterior to vertebral | Prevents |
| Ligament (ALL) | body | hyperextension |
| Posterior Longitudinal | Posterior to vertebral | Prevents |
| Ligament (PLL) | body | hyperflexion |
| Ligamentum Flavum | Between laminae | Elastic recoil, limits flexion |

Interspinous & Between and over Posterior tension
Supraspinous Ligaments spinous processes band
Mnemonic: ALL, PLL, Flavum, Interspinous, Supraspinous = Stability Shield.
### Bangle Analogy (Dr. Vishnu)
- Vertebral column = ring.
- One break = stable.
- Two or more breaks = unstable → spinal cord at risk.

### MECHANISM OF INJURY
- Flexion: anterior compression / wedge fractures.
- Axial load: burst fractures.
- Flexion-distraction: seatbelt/chance injuries.
- Rotation / Translation: fracture-dislocation, highly unstable.
### Forces Acting on Spine:
- Anterior: Compression.
- Posterior: Tension.
- Instability: Failure of both forces maintaining balance.

DEFINITION OF SPINAL INSTABILITY (Panjabi et al.)
Loss of the spine’s ability under physiological loads to maintain vertebral alignment
such that:
- No spinal cord or nerve root damage occurs.
- No incapacitating pain or deformity develops. Simple rule: Under normal load, if the cord isn’t protected → unstable.

INITIAL ASSESSMENT & EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT
### Accident Site
- Do not lift manually. Use log roll or spine board.
- Immobilize cervical spine with Philadelphia collar.
- If unavailable → place pillows or sandbags on both sides of the head.

<!-- page 3 -->

### In Emergency Room
- Follow ATLS (Airway, Breathing, Circulation).
- C4 involvement → diaphragm paralysis (phrenic nerve).
- Neurogenic shock:
  - Hypotension + Bradycardia due to sympathetic block.
  - Treat with vasopressors (dopamine), not IV fluids.
  - Maintain MAP > 80–85 mmHg for cord perfusion.

NEUROLOGICAL ASSESSMENT
Identify Incomplete Cord Injury – Sacral Sparing
Check for:
- Perianal sensation.
- Voluntary anal contraction.
- Great toe flexion. Presence = incomplete injury → better prognosis.
### Spinal Shock
- Temporary cord shutdown → areflexia, flaccid paralysis, sensory loss.
- Resolves in 24–48 hours.
- Return of bulbocavernosus reflex → end of spinal shock. Absent reflex even after recovery → suspect conus medullaris or cauda equina lesion.
### ASIA Grading System

| Grade | Definition |
|---|---|
| A | Complete – no motor/sensory function |
| B | Sensory only preserved |
| C | Motor preserved but <3/5 strength in >50% muscles |
| D | Motor preserved with ≥3/5 in >50% muscles |
| E | Normal motor & sensory |

### X-ray Clues
- Height loss >50% = instability.
- Kyphosis >20° = unstable.
- Fracture-dislocation = unstable.
- Pedicle break + posterior line disruption = suspect burst.

### 1⃣ Dennis Three-Column Concept

- 1 Column = Stable
- ≥2 Columns = Unstable

### 2⃣ AO (Magerl) Classification

| Type | Mechanism | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| A | Compression | Wedge / Burst |
| B | Distraction | Flexion-Distraction / Chance injury |
| C | Rotation / Translation | Fracture-dislocation, most unstable |

A = Axial, B = Bending, C = Combined Rotation.

3⃣ TLICS (Thoracolumbar Injury Classification & Severity Score)
Parameters: Morphology, Posterior Ligamentous Complex (PLC), Neurological status.

| Parameter | Score |
|---|---|
| Morphology | Compression = 1, Burst = 2, Translation/Rotation = 3, Distraction = 4 |
| PLC Integrity | Intact = 0, Indeterminate = 2, Disrupted = 3 |
| Neurological | Intact = 0, Root = 2, Incomplete = 3, Complete = 2, |
| Status | Cauda Equina = 3 |

### Decision Guide:
- ≤3: Conservative
- 4: Surgeon’s discretion
- ≥5: Surgical fixation

MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES
### Non-operative (Stable Fractures)
- Indications: Compression or stable burst fractures, intact PLC, no neuro deficit.
- Immobilization:
  - Below L3: Lumbosacral belt.
  - Above L3: Taylor’s brace (TLSO).
- Bed rest for 1–2 weeks → gradual mobilization.
- Goal: Preserve neurology, prevent deformity, reduce pain.
### Operative (Unstable Fractures)

| Type | Construct | Technique |
|---|---|---|
| Unstable Burst | Short-segment (1 above, 1 below + fracture screw) | Posterior pedicle screw fixation |
| Flexion-Distraction / Dislocation | Long-segment (2 above, 2 below) | Posterior fixation + decompression |
| Neurological | Transpedicular | Posterior approach |
| Deficit + Burst | decompression | preferred |

Anterior approach (corpectomy) rarely needed now; transpedicular route
preferred.

### TECHNICAL NOTES
- Include the fracture-level screw if pedicles are intact → improves stability.
- In fracture vertebra, use shorter and thinner screws.
- Maintain sagittal alignment and avoid excessive rod contouring.

<!-- page 6 -->

### ♂ REHABILITATION
- Early mobilization is key — even in conservative care.
- Physiotherapy: Core strengthening, gait training, respiratory exercises.
- Avoid prolonged bed rest → risk of deconditioning and DVT.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Most common fracture level: T12–L1.
- >50% height loss = unstable.
- Kyphosis >20° = unstable.
- TLICS ≥5 → surgery.
- Flexion-distraction → 2 above, 2 below fixation.
- Burst fracture → include fracture screw.
- Maintain MAP >85 mmHg post-injury.
- Sacral sparing = incomplete injury → good prognosis.
- Bulbocavernosus reflex → signals end of spinal shock.
