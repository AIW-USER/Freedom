Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – PCL Reconstruction & Posterolateral Corner
(PLC) Injuries
Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

OVERVIEW
Posterior Cruciate Ligament (PCL) injuries are less frequent than ACL injuries but are
critical for posterior and rotational stability of the knee. They often coexist with
Posterolateral Corner (PLC) injuries, and mismanagement can lead to chronic
instability and early arthritis.
Quote: “A missed PLC injury is the most common cause of failed PCL
reconstruction.” – Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### ANATOMY OF PCL
- Length: ~38 mm (Girgis et al., Clin Orthop Rel Res 1975)
- Origin: Broad crescent-shaped area on medial femoral condyle in intercondylar notch.
- Insertion: Depression between tibial plateaus (PCL facet) ~1–1.5 cm below posterior rim.
- Structure: Extra-synovial, within its own synovial sheath.
- Strength: ~1.5× stronger than ACL.
Bundles:

| Bundle | Function |
|---|---|
| Anterolateral (AL) | Tight in flexion – main stabilizer |
| Posteromedial (PM) | Tight in extension |

### Meniscofemoral Ligaments:
- Ligament of Humphrey (anterior) and Ligament of Wrisberg (posterior).
- Contribute ~17% to posterior drawer stability. Thicker bundle: Anterolateral bundle.

### BIOMECHANICS

| Function | Detail |
|---|---|
| Primary Restraint | Posterior translation of tibia (especially at 90° flexion) |
| Secondary Restraint | External rotation & varus stress |

Isolated PLC injury: 3–5 mm at 30°.

### POSTEROLATERAL CORNER (PLC) – COMPONENTS

| Structure | Function |
|---|---|
| Fibular Collateral Ligament (FCL) | Primary varus restraint |
| Popliteus tendon | External rotation control |
| Popliteofibular ligament (PFL) | Assists posterolateral stability |
| Arcuate ligament complex | Posterior stability |
| Lateral capsule | Secondary restraint |

Primary role: Resists varus & external rotation forces.

RADIOLOGY & IMAGING
X-Rays
- Rosenberg view (45° flexion WB): Detects posterior translation.
- Arcuate Sign: Avulsion fracture of fibular styloid → PLC injury. Arthritis in PCL-deficient knee: Posteromedial compartment.
### MRI
- Best modality for PCL + PLC evaluation.
- Normal PCL: 6 mm low-signal curvilinear band.
- Tear: Increased signal, thickened (>6 mm) or discontinuity.
- Partial tear: One bundle disrupted.
- Tram Track sign: Mucoid degeneration.
- Double PCL sign: Bucket-handle meniscal tear.
### CT / Stress Radiography
- Quantifies posterior laxity.
- Kneeling view: Most reliable.
- Telos stress view: Gold standard; >11 mm translation or >3 mm side difference = abnormal.

### SURGICAL TECHNIQUES
1. Transtibial (TT) Technique
- Traditional approach.
- Tibial tunnel drilled from anterior tibia to posterior facet.
- Risks: Killer curve (sharp bend → graft abrasion).

<!-- page 4 -->

2. Tibial Inlay Technique
- Posterior incision; graft fixed directly at tibial insertion.
- Advantage: Avoids killer curve; better biomechanics.
- Disadvantage: Technically demanding; neurovascular risk.
3. Arthroscopic Reconstruction (TT or Inlay)
- Single- or double-bundle using hamstring / BTB graft.
- Femoral tunnel at anterolateral bundle footprint.
- Fix graft at 90° flexion.

PLC RECONSTRUCTION TECHNIQUES
### Larson’s Technique (Modified)
- Reconstructs LCL + popliteus tendon using single graft.
### LaPrade’s Technique
- Anatomic reconstruction using two grafts (FCL + PFL + Popliteus).
### Fanelli Grading of PLC Injury:

| Grade | Structures Involved |
|---|---|
| A | PFL, Popliteus |
| B | PFL + Popliteus + FCL |
| C | PFL + Popliteus + FCL + Capsule ± Cruciate |

Always reconstruct PLC with PCL to prevent graft overload.

### ROLE OF HIGH TIBIAL OSTEOTOMY (HTO)
- Indicated in chronic PCL/PLC-deficient varus knees.
- Goal: Correct varus & increase posterior tibial slope → enhances posterior stability.
- Timing: Perform HTO first, wait 6–8 months, then ligament reconstruction if instability persists.

### COMPLICATIONS

| Complication | Cause |
|---|---|
| Residual posterior laxity | Missed PLC injury |
| Neurovascular injury | Posterior dissection / tibial inlay |
| Tunnel widening | Improper fixation |
| Loss of motion / stiffness | Inadequate rehab |
| Graft abrasion | Killer curve in TT technique |

### EXAM PEARLS & TAKEAWAYS
- PCL: Stronger than ACL (1.5× tensile strength).
- Main restraint: Posterior tibial translation.
- Posteromedial bundle: tight in extension.
- Posterolateral bundle: tight in flexion.
- Meniscofemoral ligaments add to stability.
- Posterior Drawer Test = most reliable clinical test.
- PLC injury → ↑ external rotation (Dial test).
- PCL deficiency OA: Posteromedial compartment.
- Killer curve avoided in tibial inlay technique.
- HTO before ligament reconstruction in chronic varus knees.
