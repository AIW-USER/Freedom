Understanding Pelvic Anatomy and Pelvic Fracture
Classification – Dr. Sanjay Arora
Overview
This lecture explains the complete pelvic anatomy, ligamentous support, muscular
relations, radiological evaluation, and the full framework of Young–Burgess and Tile
classifications. A clear understanding of pelvic biomechanics and ring stability is
essential for managing pelvic injuries.
Faculty Remark: “Pelvic stability comes from the ring, the keystone sacrum, and the
suspension bridge of ligaments. Understand these three and every fracture makes
sense.”

## Pelvic Anatomy
### Bones of the Pelvis
- Three bones fuse at triradiate cartilage:
  - Ilium
  - Ischium
  - Pubis
- Sacrum articulates with both innominate bones at SI joints.
- Anteriorly joined at pubic symphysis.
### True vs False Pelvis
- False pelvis: above pelvic inlet.
- True pelvis: between pelvic inlet and outlet, houses pelvic viscera.

Ligamentous Anatomy
### Key Ligaments
- Sacrotuberous ligament: resists vertical shear.
- Sacrospinous ligament: resists external rotation.
- Anterior SI ligaments: resist external rotation.
- Posterior SI ligaments: major stabilizers of posterior ring.
- Iliolumbar ligament: from L5 transverse process to ilium, crucial in lateral compression injuries.
- Symphyseal ligaments: stabilize anterior ring.

Exam Pearl: L5 transverse process fracture suggests iliolumbar ligament injury
→ high energy lateral compression.

Muscular Anatomy
Anterior
- Iliopsoas (hip flexor) over pelvic brim.
Posterior
- Piriformis (key landmark, protects sciatic nerve)
- Gemelli and obturator internus
- Quadratus femoris

## Radiological Evaluation of Pelvis
### Standard Views
1. AP Pelvis (diagnosis in 90 percent)
- Checks ring disruption.
- Symphysis width, SI widening, pubic rami fractures.
2. Pelvic Inlet View (40° caudal)
Shows: - Sacral fractures. - AP displacement. - Rotational deformity. - Pubic diastasis
clearly seen.

3. Pelvic Outlet View (40° cephalic)
Shows: - Vertical displacement. - Sacral foramina clearly round. - Posterior iliac wing
fractures.

### CT Scan
- Superior evaluation of:
  - Sacrum and foramina.
  - Extent of SI diastasis.
  - Vertical displacement.
  - 3D reconstruction for ring mapping.
Exam Pearl: Outlet view is best to assess vertical displacement.

## Pelvic Biomechanics
### Weight Bearing Axis
1. SI joints
2. Sacrum (keystone)
3. Sciatic buttress
4. Pelvic brim
5. Acetabulum (standing)
6. Ischium (sitting)
7. Pubic symphysis and anterior strut
### Three Stabilizing Elements
- Ring structure
- Keystone sacrum
- Suspension bridge (ligamentous complex)
Instability arises when disruption involves these supporting units.

## Young–Burgess Classification
Mechanism based classification: APC, LC, VS, CM.

1. Anteroposterior Compression (APC)
### APC I
- Ligaments stretched, not torn.
- Pubic diastasis < 2.5 cm.
- Rotationally unstable, vertically stable.
### APC II
- Sacrotuberous, sacrospinous, anterior SI ligaments disrupted.
- Posterior SI complex intact.
- Diastasis > 2.5 cm.
- Rotationally unstable, vertically stable.
### APC III
- Complete iliosacral dissociation.
- Disruption of posterior SI ligaments.
- Rotationally and vertically unstable.
- High risk of vascular injury.

<!-- page 4 -->

2. Lateral Compression (LC)
LC I (most common)
- Anterior rami fracture.
- Posterior ligaments injured.
- ST and SS intact.
- Usually stable.
### LC II
- Ipsilateral anterior ring + posterior iliac wing fracture.
- Posterior ligaments disrupted.
LC III – “Wind swept pelvis”
- LC on one side + external rotation injury on opposite side.
- Very unstable.

### 3. Vertical Shear (VS)
- Fall from height.
- Vertical orientation fractures through rami and SI.
- Entire hemipelvis displaced superiorly.
- Completely unstable.

### 4. Combined Mechanism
- Combination of vectors (ejection/landing).
- LC/VS or APC/VS patterns.

## Tile Classification (Stability Based)
Type A – Stable (posterior arch intact)
- A1: Avulsion injuries.
- A2: Iliac wing or anterior arch fracture.
- A3: Transverse sacral or coccygeal fractures.
Type B – Partially Stable (rotational instability)
- B1: Open book (external rotation).
- B2: Lateral compression.
  - B2-1: Ipsilateral.

<!-- page 5 -->

- B2-2: Contralateral (bucket handle).
- B3: Bilateral.
Type C – Completely Unstable (rotational + vertical)
- C1: Unilateral (C1.1 iliac fracture, C1.2 SI dislocation, C1.3 sacral fracture).
- C2: Bilateral, one side B, one side C.
- C3: Bilateral complete.
Exam Pearl: Tile system is best for surgeons specialising in pelvic trauma.
## Sacral Fracture Classification – Denis
- Zone 1: Lateral to foramina.
- Zone 2: Through foramina.
- Zone 3: Medial to foramina (highest neurological deficit).

### Viva and Exam Pearls
- Sacrotuberous resists shear; sacrospinous resists external rotation.
- L5 transverse process fracture → iliolumbar ligament injury.
- Outlet view shows vertical displacement; inlet shows AP displacement.
- APC injury: pubic diastasis thresholds important (<2.5 vs >2.5 cm).
- LC3 = wind swept pelvis.
- Tile C = completely unstable.
- Denis Zone 3 has highest neuro deficit.

Final Faculty Insight: “Understand the ring, understand the vectors, and the pelvis
will tell you the diagnosis itself.”
