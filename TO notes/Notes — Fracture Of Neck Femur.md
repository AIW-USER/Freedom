Fracture of Neck Femur – Dr. Daivik T Shetty
Introduction
Neck of femur fractures form a major part of orthopedic trauma. Around 55 to 59
percent of hip fractures are intracapsular. These fractures are often referred to as
unsolved because of high complication rates including non union and avascular
necrosis.
Faculty Quote: “We come to the world under the brim of the pelvis and we go out
through the neck of the femur.” – Watson Jones (shared by Dr. Daivik)

Risk Factors
### Non Modifiable
- Age
- Sex
- Ethnicity
- Family history
Modifiable
- Weight
- Smoking
- Alcohol intake
- Low physical activity
### Secondary Causes
- HIV
- Primary hyperparathyroidism
- Chronic liver disease
- Spinal injury
### Medication Related
- PPIs
- Anti epileptics
- Aromatase inhibitors
- Inhaled corticosteroids
- Thiazolidinediones
- GRH inhibitors

### Pathophysiology and Why It Is an Unsolved Fracture
- Poor blood supply (over reliance on medial circumflex femoral artery)
- Must heal by direct endosteal healing due to absence of cambium layer
- Angiogenesis inhibiting factors in synovial fluid
- High mechanical and biological failure rates
- High velocity injuries in younger patients
### Healing Process
- Dependent on endosteal pluripotent stem cells
- Requires near perfect anatomical reduction
- Two components of revascularization:
  - Creeping substitution
  - Vascular ingrowth

### Mechanism of Injury
- Axial loading in flexion and abduction
- Sideways fall causing force transmission through the greater trochanter
- External rotation injury causing increased tension on anterior capsule and iliofemoral ligament leading to posterior comminution
- Bimodal: trivial fall in elderly and high velocity trauma in young patients

Clinical Features
Symptoms
- Severe hip pain
- Inability to bear weight
- Painful hip movements
Signs
- Undisplaced: painful range of motion
- Displaced: shortened and externally rotated limb
- External rotation: less than 45 degrees
- Shortening: less than one inch
- Minimal swelling or bruising (intracapsular fracture)
- Neurovascular deficits rare

Imaging
X ray
- AP hip view with 10 to 15 degrees internal rotation
- Lateral view for posterior comminution
- Radiological features:
  - Break in medial cortex
  - Prominent lesser trochanter (external rotation)
  - Overriding of greater trochanter
  - Break in trabeculae and Shenton’s line
### MRI
- For occult fractures
- Dynamic MRI for vascularity assessment
### CT Scan
- Better visualization of posterior comminution and head rotation
### Bone Scan / PET CT
- Early prediction of AVN

Applied Anatomy
### Neck Shaft Angle
- Men: 130 degrees
- Women: 128 degrees
### Femoral Neck Anteversion
- 15 to 25 degrees
### Blood Supply
- Medial circumflex femoral artery is primary supply.
- Superior and anterior retinacular arteries often torn in fractures.
- Inferior retinacular artery usually preserved.

### Classification Systems
1. Anatomical
- Subcapital
- Transcervical
- Basicervical

<!-- page 4 -->

2. Garden
- Type 1: incomplete, valgus impacted
- Type 2: complete, undisplaced
- Type 3: partially displaced
- Type 4: completely displaced
3. Pauwels
- Type 1: < 30 degrees, compressive forces dominate
- Type 2: 30 to 50 degrees
- Type 3: > 50 degrees, shearing forces dominate, worst prognosis
4. AO Classification
- 31 B1: subcapital
- 31 B2: transcervical
- 31 B3: basicervical
- Subdivided based on Garden and Pauwels

Stress and Fatigue Fractures
### Type A: Tension
- Superolateral radiolucent crack
- Progresses to displacement
- Requires internal fixation
### Type B: Compression
- Inferomedial sclerosis with callus
- Managed non operatively

Treatment Principles
Conservative Management
Only in: - Terminal illness - Severe comorbidity - Non ambulatory patients

### Valgus Impacted Fractures
- Treated operatively with cannulated screws to prevent slippage

Undisplaced Intracapsular Fracture
### Preferred Options
- Cannulated cancellous screws

<!-- page 5 -->

- Sliding hip screw in selected cases
### Arthroplasty Consideration
- Pre existing OA
- Chronic renal failure
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Steroid induced osteoporosis

Displaced Intracapsular Fracture
### Younger Patients
- Attempt closed reduction
- Two attempts only, then open reduction
- Fixation with CC screws or DHS
Reduction Methods
### Whitman Technique (Extension)
- Abduction and internal rotation
- Relaxes capsule and abductors
### Leadbetter Method (Flexion)
- Hip flexed to 90 degrees
- Slight adduction and internal rotation
- Gradual extension
### Flynn Method (Atraumatic)
- Flexion to 90 degrees
- Lateral traction for disimpaction
- Internal rotation and extension
### Confirmation of Reduction
- Lowell lines (smooth S curve)
- Garden alignment index: 160 degrees AP, 180 degrees lateral

Implant Options
### Cannulated Screws
- Inverted triangle configuration
- 5 to 10 mm below subchondral bone
### Dynamic Hip Screw
- Controlled collapse
- Preferred with posterior comminution

<!-- page 6 -->

### Moore/Knowles Pins
- Used in children

### Pauwels Type 3 Management
- Vertical fracture line has highest shear
- Simple screw fixation fails
- Solution: Valgus osteotomy
  - Converts shear forces to compression

Special Scenarios in Young Patients
### Head Viability on MRI
- Viable head: abduction osteotomy, McMurray osteotomy, Meyer’s muscle pedicle graft
- Non viable head: hemi or total hip replacement
### Osteotomy Concepts
- Valgus osteotomy: decreases Pauwels angle
- McMurray osteotomy: armchair effect for load transmission
### Biological Augments
- Muscle pedicle grafts (Meyer, Bakshi)
- Vascularised fibular graft

Recent Advances
### Medial Plating
- Buttress plate to prevent varus
### Biplane Double Supported Screw Fixation
- Prevents varus collapse in osteoporotic bone
### Femoral Neck System
- Small footprint
- Anti rotation screw
### Tronzo Screw System
- Controlled shaft region compression
### Targon FN
- Limits telescoping and femoral shortening

<!-- page 7 -->

### Viva and Exam Pearls
- Garden 1 and 2: undisplaced, Garden 3 and 4: displaced
- Pauwels 3: worst prognosis due to shear stress
- Inferior retinacular artery most preserved in displaced fractures
- CT scan essential for posterior comminution
- Do MRI in young chronic cases to assess head viability
- Two attempts only for closed reduction
- Valgus osteotomy is key for Pauwels 3

Faculty Key Takeaway
“Neck of femur fracture demands anatomical reduction and stable fixation. Any
compromise increases the risk of non union and AVN.”
