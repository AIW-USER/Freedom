OVERVIEW
Definition:
Complications of spine surgery include any adverse event causing neurological,
mechanical, or systemic morbidity related to spinal procedures.
Dr. Vishnu’s Quote:
“Deformity correction is not carpenter work — there’s a living cord inside that
bone; respect it.”
Major Complication Categories: 1. Neurological deficits (peri‑operative or delayed)
2. Dural tears and CSF leak
3. Vascular injury
4. Pseudoarthrosis (failed fusion)
5. Surgical‑site infection (SSI)

NEUROLOGICAL DEFICITS
Causes
- Positioning injury

- During decompression (traction/ischemia)

- Instrumentation (pedicle breach, screw malposition)

- Deformity correction maneuvers

- Post‑operative hematoma or vascular occlusion
### Peri‑operative Spinal Cord Injury (POSCI)

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Incidence | 0–10 % overall; ↑ with deformity or intramedullary tumor surgery |
| High‑risk cases | Complex deformity, intramedullary tumor, pre‑existing myelopathy |

<!-- page 2 -->

Risk Factors
Patient‑related: coagulopathy, radiation, cardiac disease, congenital/neurocutaneous

disorder, severe stenosis.
Operative: hypotension (MAP < 55 mmHg), blood loss, instability, revision/multilevel
cases.
Mechanisms:
- Direct trauma (screw/hook/wire)
- Correction maneuver translation injury
- Cord ischemia (prolonged hypotension)
- Hypoxia (low Hb/vascular compromise)

### Positioning‑related Injuries
- Brachial plexus stretch → abduction > 90° or arm extension

- Lateral femoral cutaneous/femoral nerve palsy in obese patients (bolster malplacement)

- Ischemic optic neuropathy → long prone position + hemodilution + diabetes

- Blindness (prevent by protecting eyes & avoiding pressure)

### Thoracic and Lumbar Neurological Deficits
- Anterior approach: ~1 % deficit rate.

- Thoracic critical zone (T4–T9): watershed area → delayed paraparesis.

- Pedicle screw breach: ~0.8 % neural injury.

- Incidence: PLIF > ALIF > TLIF.
Mechanisms by Procedure: | Procedure | At‑risk Structure | Comments |
|————|——————-|———–| | PLIF | Traversing nerve root (L5)
| ↑ neural traction injury (~19 %) | | TLIF | Exiting nerve root (L4)
| Safer due to less retraction | | XLIF/LLIF | Lumbar plexus through psoas
| Risk → shift to OLLIF (oblique corridor) |

Prevention & Management
Intra‑operative: - Maintain MAP ≥ 80 mmHg in myelopathy cases (“sick cord”)
- Neuromonitoring (SSEP + MEP) → stop & reverse maneuver if signals drop > 50 %
- Avoid prolonged traction and hypotension.
- Temporary rod during osteotomy to prevent translation.
Post‑operative: - MAP > 80 mmHg for 24 h.
- Neurological check q 12 h.
- Steroids (methylprednisolone 30 mg/kg bolus → 5.4 mg/kg/h × 23 h if within 8 h).
- Maintain normothermia (> 36.5 °C).
- Re‑image for hematoma → early re‑decompression.

DURAL TEARS / CSF LEAKS
Definition: Inadvertent breach of dura leading to CSF leak — most common spine surger
y complication.

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| Incidence | 1–18 % (lumbar) 0.3–4 % (cerv ical) ↑ in revision cases |
| Common site | Dorsal or dorsolateral aspect du ring posterior approach |
| Etiology | Chronic stenosis (thinned dura), slipped burr, sharp bone edges, pedicle breach, sublaminar wire insertion |

<!-- page 4 -->

Prevention Tips
- Magnification & illumination.
- Adequate exposure and hemostasis.
- Screw insertion before decompression.
- Start central decompression caudal → cephalad (midline V of lamina).
- Lateral decompression cephalad → caudal (along root direction).
- Develop plane between flavum and dura with dissector or cottonoid.
- OPLL/OYL → use floatation technique.

Diagnosis
- Intra‑op: visualization of clear fluid.
- Post‑op: clear fluid in drain, halo sign, pseudomeningocele, MRI fluid collection (T1 low,
T2 high).

Management
Intra‑op:
- Head‑low position, gentle retraction.
- Primary repair with 4‑0 or 5‑0 Prolene under magnification.
- Add fat graft / fibrin glue for reinforcement.
- Watertight closure of fascia & muscle.
Post‑op:
- Avoid negative pressure drain.
- CSF diversion (lumbar subarachnoid drain for 3–5 days).
- Blood patch (20–30 mL) for persistent leak.
- Re‑explore for persistent high‑flow fistula.
Accessible tears: Primary repair ± fibrin sealant.
Inaccessible lateral tears: Muscle‑fat plug or dural patch (artificial or fascia lata).
CSF diversion: Lumbar drain placed proximal to site to reduce pressure and
allow wound healing.

Lumbar Region
- Left common iliac vein = most commonly injured during L4–L5 anterior approach.
- Venous > arterial injuries.
Prevention & Management:
- Prophylactic femoral access for balloon occlusion.
- Initial control = direct pressure/suture/clamp.
- Definitive = primary repair, patch plasty, stent.
- Small laceration → direct suture; large → hemostatic patch or endovascular repair.

PSEUDOARTHROSIS (Failed Fusion)
Definition | Lack of solid bony fusion > 1 yr after index surgery |
Incidence | 5–35 % |
Types (Heggeness & Esse, 1991): 1. Atrophic – no bone formation (biologic failure)
2. Transverse – fusion mass with transverse cleft
3. Shingle – oblique cleft through fusion mass
4. Complex – multiple defects
Clinical: Recurrent pain ± deformity after pain‑free interval; implant loosening.
Investigations: Dynamic X‑rays, CT (most accurate), bone scan/SPECT.
Treatment: - Debridement if infected.
- Antibiotic cement beads ± VAC.
- Revision fusion with graft + stable instrumentation.
- Adjunct: electromagnetic stimulation.

### SURGICAL SITE INFECTION (SSI)

| Category | Definition |
|---|---|
| Superficial | Skin/subcutaneous only |
| Deep | Beneath fascia → discitis, osteomyeliti s, epidural abscess |

Timing: Early (< 1 mo) Late (2–6 mo) Very late (> 6 mo)
Risk Factors: Diabetes, obesity, prolonged surgery, hematoma, revision cases.

<!-- page 6 -->

Prevention: Antibiotic prophylaxis, skin prep, double gloves, vancomycin powder, betadin
e wash, reduce OR traffic.
Diagnosis: ESR/CRP↑, MRI showing T2 hyperintense disc/vertebral body, CT for osteoly
sis, PET for occult infection.
Treatment: Debridement ± implant retention, targeted antibiotics 6–12 weeks, drainage of
collections.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Most common spine‑surgery complication → Dural tear.

- Neurological deficit risk ↑ in deformity, tumor, revision cases.

- Maintain MAP > 80 mmHg in “sick cord” myelopathy.

- C5 palsy = traction on cord post laminectomy.

- Right‑sided ACDF → recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy.

- Dural tear incidence: 1–18 % (lumbar).

- Left common iliac vein = most injured in anterior lumbar approach.

- Pseudoarthrosis: CT = gold standard.

- Infection: MRI T2 bright, ESR/CRP for monitoring.

### TAKE‑HOME SUMMARY
- Respect the cord — neurological injuries are unforgiving.

- Pre‑op planning + neuromonitoring = best protection.

- Handle dura gently; repair immediately if breached.

- Avoid vascular trauma by controlled retraction and clear landmarks.

- Ensure fusion biology and rigid fixation to prevent pseudoarthrosis.

- Meticulous asepsis and antibiotic prophylaxis reduce SSI risk.

- Goal: Safe surgery, stable fusion, and preserved neurological function.
