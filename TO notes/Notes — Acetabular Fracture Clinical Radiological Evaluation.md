Acetabular Fracture – Clinical and Radiological
Evaluation – Dr. Sanjay Arora
Overview
Accurate evaluation of acetabular fractures begins with assessing congruency,
stability, and fracture morphology on X ray and CT scan. Dr. Sanjay Arora
emphasizes early identification of incongruency, dynamic stability testing, and four key
components on axial CT that guide surgical planning.
Faculty Remark: “A congruent hip may tolerate imperfections, but an incongruent
hip never forgives. Always assess congruency first.”

## Clinical Assessment
### Initial Evaluation
- Assess pain, inability to bear weight, deformity.
- Evaluate limb position (often resembles posterior dislocation posture).
- Always test:
  - Neurovascular status.
  - Sciatic nerve (EHL, dorsiflexion, first webspace sensation).
  - Femoral pulse and Narath sign.
- Hip ROM restricted and painful.
### Associated Injuries
- Posterior hip dislocation.
- Femoral head indentation fractures.
- Knee injuries (dashboard trauma).

## Radiological Evaluation – Stepwise Approach
Step 1: Check Congruency
Congruency between acetabular articular surface and femoral head determines long
term outcome.

### Signs of Incongruency
- Increased medial joint space.
- Superior joint space widening.
- Non parallel joint surfaces.

<!-- page 2 -->

- Hinged abduction during movement.
- Loose intra articular fragments.
Important: Increased medial joint space after reduction strongly suggests
incarcerated fragment.

Step 2: Assess Stability
Stability is tested after reduction of dislocation.

### CT Based Posterior Wall Size

| Wall involvement | Stability |
|---|---|
| <20 percent | Stable |
| 20–50 percent | Indeterminate |
| >50 percent | Unstable |

Dynamic Fluoroscopic Stress Test (Under Anaesthesia)
Performed in obturator oblique view: - Hip in flexion. - Adduction. - Internal rotation. -
Apply axial load while observing joint parallelism.
Loss of parallelism = unstable.
Used routinely by Dr. Arora to avoid unnecessary CT in clearly stable hips.
## Understanding the Four Critical Axial CT Questions
Dr. Arora’s structured CT evaluation includes four essential questions:

1. How close is the fracture to the dome?
- Posterior superior dome involvement = highly unstable.
- Often requires trochanteric flip osteotomy for exposure.
- Abductors obstruct visualization without flip.
2. Is the fracture comminuted?
Comminution of posterior wall or column affects fixation choice. - Use spring plates. -
Or Allis T plate for better buttress.

3. Are there intra articular fragments?
Evaluate: - Number - Size - Location
Inferocotyl (infracotyl) fragments may be left if tiny.
Superior dome fragments must be removed even if 3–4 mm.

<!-- page 3 -->

4. Is there marginal impaction?
- Elevate bone.
- Fill cavity with graft (often from GT).
- Fix with mini fragment screws.
Seagull sign indicates dome impaction.
## Important Radiological Signs
### Spur Sign
- Seen in both column fractures.
- Indicates dissociation from axial skeleton.
### Seagull Sign
- Suggests superomedial dome impaction.
### Double Shadow of Posterior Wall
- Suggests large displaced wall fragment.
### Broken Iliopectineal/Ilioischial Lines
- Primary indicators of column involvement.

## Approach Planning Based on Imaging
### Key Considerations
- Where is the major fragment displacement?
- Does the fracture extend to the dome?
- Is the posterior column reducible through an anterior intrapelvic (NTA) approach?
- Need for combined approaches.
Examples from Dr. Arora’s Case Discussion
- Anterior column fixed via NTA.
- Posterior column required separate Kocher Langenbeck approach.
- Final construct required dual plating.

## Percutaneous Screw Fixation – Principles
### Posterior Column Screws
- Antegrade: from iliopubic eminence.
- Retrograde: from ischial tuberosity.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Requires precise knowledge of safe corridors.
### Anterior Column Screws
- Antegrade: from iliac crest.
- Retrograde: from pubic region.
### Technical Tips
- Use inlet, obturator, and iliac views.
- Avoid hip joint penetration.
- Use long guide wires and fluoroscopy.

## Prevention of Complications
- Preserve all posterior wall attachments.
- Maintain cartilage and avoid fragmentation.
- Elevate and graft marginal impaction.
- Achieve perfect anatomic reduction.
- Use buttress plates and screws for stability.
- Remove loose bodies meticulously.

### Exam Pearls
- Increased medial joint space = incarcerated fragment.
- <20 percent posterior wall = stable; >50 percent unstable.
- Dynamic fluoroscopic stress test is gold standard for stability.
- Seagull sign = dome impaction.
- Spur sign = both column fracture.
- Trochanteric flip needed for dome exposure.
- Four CT questions guide surgical planning.
- Hip must be both congruent and stable before avoiding surgery.

Final Faculty Insight: “Congruency first, stability next, CT planning last. Follow this
sequence, and acetabular decisions become clear.”
