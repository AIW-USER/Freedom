ADVANCED AIRWAY ADJUNCTS
### Esophageal–Tracheal Tube (ETT)
- Invasive airway device; acceptable alternative to ET tube.

- Two inflatable balloons → allow ventilation even if tube enters esophagus.

- Enables ventilation via side openings near trachea/vocal cords.
Complications: Esophageal trauma, lacerations, bruising, subcutaneous emphysema.
Sizes:
| Patient Height | Tube Size | |—————-|————| | 4–5.5 ft | 37F | | >5 ft | 41F |
Contraindications: - Responsive patient (gag/cough reflex)
- <16 years of age
- Esophageal disease or caustic ingestion
- Height <4 ft

ENDOTRACHEAL INTUBATION (ET)
Purpose:
- Establish patent airway

- Prevent aspiration of gastric contents

- Facilitate suctioning, PEEP, and long-term ventilation

- Alternative route for drug administration if IV/IO access unavailable

Insertion Steps: 1. Preoxygenate and position in sniffing position (C-spine flexion +

2. Use laryngoscope (curved/straight blade) to visualize vocal cords.
3. Insert tube gently; confirm with capnography (ETCO₂).
4. Inflate cuff, secure tube, and connect BVM. CPR should not be paused longer than 10 seconds during intubation attempts.

SHOCKABLE RHYTHMS
### Ventricular Fibrillation (VF)
- Chaotic, asynchronous electrical activity; no effective cardiac output.

- Amplitude types: Fine (<5 mm), Moderate (5–10 mm), Coarse (>10 mm).

- Clinical: Sudden collapse, no pulse, apnea/agonal gasps.
### Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)
- Regular, rapid wide-complex tachycardia.

- Rate: 150–250 bpm; no P waves, QRS > 0.12 s, wide and bizarre.

- May progress to VF → sudden cardiac arrest.
Torsades de Pointes
- Irregular polymorphic VT (150–250 bpm).

- QRS amplitude varies — hallmark “twisting” pattern.

- Common with prolonged QT or hypomagnesemia.

ACUTE CARDIAC ARREST ALGORITHM
1⃣ Start CPR immediately – 100–120/min, 5–6 cm depth.
2⃣ Attach defibrillator/AED → assess rhythm.
3⃣ Shockable (VF/VT): - Biphasic 120–200 J (preferred) or Monophasic 360 J. - Resume
CPR immediately after each shock.
4⃣ Epinephrine: 1 mg IV/IO every 3–5 min (as early as possible).
5⃣ Amiodarone: 300 mg IV bolus, repeat 150 mg after 3–5 min.
6⃣ Lidocaine (if Amiodarone unavailable): 1–1.5 mg/kg → half dose repeat.
7⃣ Continue until ROSC (Return of Spontaneous Circulation).
ETCO₂ ≥40 mmHg + arterial wave = sign of ROSC.
### POST-CARDIAC ARREST CARE

| Goal | Target |
|---|---|
| Airway | Early advanced airway placement |
| Breathing | 10 breaths/min, SpO₂ 92–98%, PaCO₂ 35–45 mmHg |
| Circulation | SBP > 90 mmHg, MAP > 65 mmHg |
| Vasopressors | Epinephrine (first choice), Dopamine, Phenylephrine, Norepinephrine (last line) |
| Temperature | Maintain 32–36 °C (Targeted Temperature Management) |

MANAGEMENT OF CHOKING
Recognition:
- Inability to speak or breathe, silent cough, cyanosis.

- May lead to collapse or apnea.

<!-- page 5 -->

Management:
1. Encourage coughing if effective.

2. Manual removal if visible (avoid pushing deeper).

3. 5 back blows → 5 abdominal thrusts (alternate).

4. If unconscious → Start BLS protocol. Dr. Shikha: “A strong cough can be more powerful than any maneuver — let them try before you intervene.”

BLEEDING & SHOCK MANAGEMENT
Types:
- External: Open wounds, nasal or facial injuries.

- Internal: Chest, abdominal, pelvic, or long bone fractures.
### On-Field Steps:
- Follow ATLS (D–R–A–B–C).

- Apply manual pressure / tourniquet.

- Start IV fluids (NS/RL).

- Use pelvic binder for pelvic injuries.

- For epistaxis: Pinch nose → apply adrenaline 1:10,000 on cotton swab.
Assessment Parameters: Low BP, weak pulse, clammy skin, altered sensorium.

SPINAL & HEAD INJURY MANAGEMENT
Red Flags: ENT bleed, photophobia, vomiting, loss of consciousness, limb weakness,
or inability to move neck.

### On-Field Protocol:
1. Withdraw player immediately.

2. Assume C-spine injury until proven otherwise.

3. Apply cervical collar (measure chin → suprasternal notch).

4. Maintain neutral head position.

5. Log roll maneuver with ≥4 rescuers.

6. Secure airway and initiate oxygen.

7. Transport urgently for imaging.
NEXUS Criteria (for C-spine imaging):
- Neurologic deficit, Spinal tenderness, Alertness, Intoxication, Distracting injury.
MCQ: NEXUS mnemonic = NSAID (Neuro deficit, Spinal tenderness,
Alertness, Intoxication, Distracting injury).

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Secure airway first — Esophageal-tracheal or ET tube as indicated.

- Capnography is gold standard for confirming tube placement.

- ACLS sequence: CPR → Shock → Drugs → ROSC → Post-arrest care.

<!-- page 8 -->

- Manage bleeding, shock, and anaphylaxis systematically (ABCDE + fluids + drugs).

- Always assume spinal injury in unconscious athletes.

- Medical preparedness = equipment + training + coordination. Final Takeaway: “Every collapse tests your system — not just your skills.”
