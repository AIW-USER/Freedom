OVERVIEW
Topic Focus: On-field management of medical emergencies — emphasizing Basic
Life Support (BLS) and preparation for Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ACLS).
Objective: To prepare every sports physician and support staff to respond promptly and
efficiently to emergencies such as sudden collapse, cardiac arrest, or concussion.
Dr. Shikha’s Quote:
“A well-prepared medical team can turn those precious 30 seconds into a
lifetime saved.”

2. Triage Sort and prioritize patients based on injury severity.

PREPARATION
### Pre-Situation Planning
- Ensure medical knowledge & emergency skills.

- Keep emergency kits and IV setups ready.

- Conduct mock drills & clinical team rehearsals before events.

- Make all staff aware of emergency exits, ambulance routes, AED location, and nearest hospital.
### Scene Safety (During Situation)
- Ensure safety for patient and rescuers.

- Adopt universal precautions (gloves, PPE).

- Organize roles: CPR team, AED handler, stretcher lead, and crowd management. Remember: “Emergency can never be invited — preparedness is the only prevention.”

PRIMARY SURVEY (ABCDE)
### A – Airway & Cervical Spine Control
- Check airway patency (blood, saliva, foreign body).

- Stabilize neck with cervical collar if spinal injury suspected.

- Administer high-flow oxygen (10–15 L/min) after checking SpO₂.
### B – Breathing & Ventilation
- Look, listen, feel for breathing; if absent, initiate assisted ventilation (AMBU bag or mouth-to-mouth).

- Assess symmetry, chest expansion, and tracheal position.
### C – Circulation
- Check carotid pulse (≤10 sec).

- If absent → begin CPR immediately.

- Control external bleeding (apply tourniquet or compression).

- Establish IV access with large-bore cannula; start fluids (NS/RL).
### D – Disability (Neurological Status)
- Use AVPU (Alert, Verbal, Pain, Unresponsive) or GCS scoring.

- Rule out head injury, hypoxia, or hypoglycemia.

| GCS Score | Severity |
|---|---|
| 13–15 | Mild Injury |
| 9–12 | Moderate Injury |
| ≤8 | Severe Brain Injury |

### E – Exposure
- Expose to check hidden injuries; maintain privacy (especially female athletes).

- Check for hypothermia/hyperthermia and signs of trauma.

BASIC LIFE SUPPORT (BLS) FOR A COLLAPSED ATHLETE
### Stepwise Approach
1. Check for Danger: Ensure scene safety.

2. Check Response: Shout and gently tap; no response → call for help.

3. Check Airway: Head tilt–chin lift (or jaw thrust if C-spine injury suspected).

4. Check Breathing:
  - Normal → Recovery position.

  - Absent → Call EMS/AED; start CPR (30:2).

5. Check Circulation: Continue compressions until AED arrives. Dr. Shikha: “Scene safety, response, airway, breathing — these 4 seconds decide survival.”

CHAIN OF SURVIVAL
### Adult Sequence:
1. Activate emergency response.

2. Start early CPR (30:2).

3. Defibrillate early with AED.

4. Begin Advanced Life Support (ALS).

5. Provide post-cardiac arrest care.

### Pediatric Sequence:
- Step 1: Prevention of arrest.

- Step 2: Early CPR, AED, ALS, and recovery.

CPR BASICS
### High-Quality CPR Components:
1. Compression rate: 100–120/min.

2. Compression depth: 5–6 cm (adults).

3. Full chest recoil.

4. Minimize interruptions (<10 sec).

5. Avoid overventilation.
### Adult CPR (Single Rescuer)
- 30 compressions + 2 breaths.

- Hand position: lower half of sternum.

- Continue until AED available or patient revives.
### Rescue Breathing:
- 1 breath every 5–6 seconds (adults) or 3–5 seconds (children).

- Ensure visible chest rise.

- Each breath ≈ 1 second. MCQ Tip: Compression depth in adults = 5–6 cm, rate = 100–120/min.
USE OF AUTOMATED EXTERNAL DEFIBRILLATOR (AED)
Steps:
1. Turn on AED and follow voice prompts.

2. Attach pads – right upper chest & left lateral chest.

3. Clear area during rhythm analysis (“CLEAR!”).

4. Deliver shock if advised.

5. Immediately resume CPR for 2 minutes.

<!-- page 7 -->

6. Reassess rhythm; repeat if required.
Key Principle: Early defibrillation saves lives — success decreases by 10% for every
minute delayed.

### Pediatric AED Use
- Use pediatric pads (1–8 years); if unavailable, use adult pads in antero-posterior position.

### PEDIATRIC BLS DIFFERENCES

| Aspect | Child (>1 yr) | Infant (<1 yr) |
|---|---|---|
| Compression depth | 2 inches | 1.5 inches |
| Hand position | One hand | Two-thumb encircling technique |
| Breath ratio | 30:2 (single rescuer), 15:2 (two rescuers) | Same |
| Priority | Prevention → Early CPR | Emphasize ventilation |

### KEY POINTS FOR GOOD CPR
- Minimize interruptions.

- Ensure adequate chest recoil.

- Maintain oxygen supply.

- Use airway devices and capnography if available.

- Correct reversible causes (5H + 5T).

- Keep adrenaline ready (0.5–1 mL every 3–5 min). Dr. Shikha’s Takeaway: “High-quality CPR with early AED — that’s your real match-winner on the field.”

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Preparation & rehearsal before every event are lifesaving.

- ABCDE approach ensures systematic on-field assessment.

<!-- page 8 -->

- Always identify reversible causes (7H & 5T).

- Early CPR + Defibrillation = Highest survival chance.

- Pediatric emergencies prioritize prevention and ventilation. Final Note: “In every collapsing athlete lies a chance — a chance to revive, only if we act fast.”
