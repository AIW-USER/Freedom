Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – SLAP Tears (Superior Labral Anterior to
Posterior Lesions)
Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### OVERVIEW
- SLAP (Superior Labral Anterior to Posterior) Lesion: A tear of the superior labrum at the attachment of the long head of the biceps (LHB) to the glenoid.
- This lecture builds upon Biceps Tendon Lesions, focusing on superior labral pathologies.
- Common in throwing athletes (pitchers, bowlers, overhead sports).
- Isolated SLAP tears are uncommon; they often coexist with articular-sided supraspinatus tears. Mnemonic: Think of SLAP as the “examiner’s slap” — tricky and challenging for both diagnosis and management.

### ANATOMY RECAP – LONG HEAD OF BICEPS (LHB)
- Short Head: Origin – Coracoid process → Functionally dominant (elbow flexion, supination).
- Long Head: Origin – Superior glenoid region → Pathologically dominant.
- Functional Roles:
  - Flexion at elbow and shoulder.
  - Supination (twice as strong as the supinator muscle).
  - Secondary stabilizer in abduction + external rotation.

### Biceps Pulley System (Medial Restraint)
- Formed by:
  - Subscapularis tendon
  - Coracohumeral ligament (CHL)
  - Superior glenohumeral ligament (SGHL)
- Prevents medial subluxation of LHB in the intertubercular sulcus.

### SLAP LESION – DEFINITION & HISTORY
- SLAP = Superior Labral Tear from Anterior to Posterior.
- Tear occurs at biceps anchor attachment on the superior labrum.

PATHOMECHANICS (Burkhart et al., 2003)
### Sequence of Events – “Disabled Throwing Shoulder”
1. Repetitive throwing → forceful internal rotation → posterior capsule stretch.
2. Leads to posterior capsular fibrosis & contracture.
3. Results in Glenohumeral Internal Rotation Deficit (GIRD) (>25° difference between shoulders).
4. Tight posterior capsule pushes humeral head posterosuperiorly during abduction–external rotation.
5. Causes internal impingement between humeral head & posterosuperior labrum/supraspinatus.
6. Generates:
  - SLAP tear (biceps anchor detachment)
  - Articular-sided supraspinatus tear (PASTA lesion)
Key Association: SLAP + Articular-sided supraspinatus tear = Internal
impingement complex.

### CLINICAL FEATURES
- Age: <40 years, often athletes or throwers.
- Symptoms:
  - Deep-seated shoulder pain.
  - Clicking/popping during overhead activity.
  - Loss of throwing speed and control.
  - Occasional sense of “giving way.”
- No frank dislocation.
- Always evaluate for GIRD and associated cuff pathology.

### IMAGING
- Investigation of Choice: MR Arthrography (contrast MRI).
  - Detects fluid between labrum & glenoid.
  - Sensitivity > MRI plain scan.
- Plain MRI: limited accuracy in differentiating SLAP types.

- Arthroscopic visualization remains gold standard for classification.

DIAGNOSTIC ARTHROSCOPY
### Criteria for SLAP on Arthroscopy:
  - Superior labral excursion > 5 mm.
  - Sublabral granulation or fraying.
  - Peel-back sign: labrum lifts off glenoid in abduction–external rotation (ABER). Peel-back phenomenon confirms Type II SLAP.

<!-- page 5 -->

TREATMENT ALGORITHM
### Type I
- Exhaust conservative options (NSAIDs, physiotherapy, injections).
- Arthroscopic debridement if persistent mechanical symptoms or paralabral cyst.

### Type II
- Low-demand patient: Conservative management.
- High-demand athlete: Arthroscopic SLAP repair (anchor fixation of biceps–labral complex).

### Type III
- Bucket-handle tear: Resection of torn fragment (biceps intact).

### Type IV
- Bucket-handle tear + biceps involvement:
  - If <50% tendon → leave intact.
  - If >50% tendon → Biceps tenodesis (anchor fixation to proximal humerus) or Tenotomy (release, let retract into bicipital groove).

SURGICAL PRINCIPLES – SLAP REPAIR
### Portals Used:
- Posterior portal: Viewing.
- Anterior portal (above coracoid): Instrumentation.
- Anterolateral portal (edge of acromion): Anchor insertion.
- Neviaser (superior) portal: Suture shuttling to superior labrum.

<!-- page 6 -->

Steps:
1. Diagnostic arthroscopy to confirm tear.
2. Debride frayed edges & prepare glenoid bed.
3. Insert anchor posterior to biceps (posterior superior labrum).
4. Shuttle sutures using Neviaser portal.
5. Tie knots securing labrum to glenoid rim (usually 1–2 anchors). Posterior superior labrum is the predominant attachment of LHB – repair focus zone.

### POSTOPERATIVE CONSIDERATIONS
- Immobilization: 3 weeks in sling.
- Passive ROM: Initiate after 3 weeks (avoid ER stress).
- Active strengthening: 6–8 weeks post-op.
- Return to overhead sport: 4–6 months.
- Common complication: Shoulder stiffness (up to 75%). Prioritize compliance with rehab to prevent post-op stiffness.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Biceps pathology mnemonic: “Short head functions, Long head suffers.”
- Commonest SLAP type: Type II.
- Association: Articular-sided supraspinatus tear (PASTA lesion).
- Confirmatory test: Modified Dynamic Labral Shear.
- Investigation of choice: MR Arthrography.
- Peel-back phenomenon: Diagnostic of Type II.
- Tenodesis vs Tenotomy: <40 yrs → tenodesis; >40 yrs → tenotomy.
- Commonest complication post repair: Stiffness.
