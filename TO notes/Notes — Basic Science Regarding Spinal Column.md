### OVERVIEW
- Lecture focuses on the fundamental anatomy, biomechanics, and vascular supply of the spinal column.
- Key emphasis on facet joints, motion segments, spinal cord blood supply, and biomechanics of load transmission.
- Understanding these principles is crucial for interpreting spinal pathologies and surgical decision-making. “The spine is both a protective and a weight-bearing structure — flexibility without compromising stability.” – Dr. C S Vishnu Prasath

FACET JOINTS
### General Characteristics
- Also known as zygapophyseal joints – synovial joints that work in pairs.
- Along with intervertebral discs, they form a three-joint complex that connects and stabilizes adjacent vertebrae.
- Functions:
  - Constrain vertebral motion.
  - Aid in transmission of spinal loads.
  - Maintain segmental stability while permitting controlled motion.
### Motion Segment (3-Joint Complex)
- Components: Intervertebral disc + bilateral facet joints.
- Functions:
  - Stabilization of the spine.
  - Facilitation of articulation between vertebrae.
  - Transmission of loads between vertebral segments.

FACET JOINT PATHOLOGY
### Facet Arthritis (Facet Joint Syndrome)
- Occurs due to increased load transmission to posterior elements with age-related disc degeneration.
- Results in facet cartilage denudation, osteophyte formation, and facet cysts.
- MRI Findings:
  - Facet joint effusion (T2 hyperintensity).
  - Facet cyst extending into the canal → compresses traversing nerve roots → radicular pain.
### Key Points:
- 80% load → anterior elements (vertebral body + disc).
- 20% load → posterior elements (facets).
- In degeneration: Posterior load ↑ → facet arthritis.
Treatment: Flexion exercises → Decompression if persistent symptoms.

### FACET TROPISM
- Definition: Asymmetry in orientation of paired facet joints in the same vertebral level.
- Leads to unequal load distribution → accelerated degeneration and risk of spondylolisthesis.
- Common site: L4–L5.
- Diagnosis: AP X-ray / CT shows asymmetric facet orientation.
- Clinical Test: Diagnostic facet block → pain relief confirms facet-origin pain.

BLOOD SUPPLY OF SPINAL CORD
### Longitudinal Arteries
- 3 main trunks: 1 anterior + 2 posterior spinal arteries.
- Run along the spinal cord → form anastomotic plexuses.
- Supplied by vertebral arteries up to cervical levels.
- Reinforced below by segmental radicular arteries (from intercostal & lumbar arteries).
### Artery of Adamkiewicz (Great Radiculomedullary Artery)
- Largest anterior radiculomedullary artery.
- Arises: Left posterior intercostal artery (T8–L2 in 75% cases).
- Function: Major supply to lower thoracic, lumbar, and sacral cord via anterior spinal artery.
### Clinical Correlation
- Thoracic spine = watershed area → high risk of cord ischemia.
- Avoid extensive periosteal stripping or segmental vessel ligation during surgery.
### Cord Ischemia Findings
- Common in mid-thoracic region.
- Anterior spinal artery injury → motor deficits (supplies anterior 2/3 of cord).
- Preventive Measures:
  - Maintain MAP >80 mmHg.
  - Ensure adequate oxygenation.
  - Use neuromonitoring and, in select cases, CSF drainage.

### LEVER ARM & SPINAL STABILITY
- Instantaneous Axis of Rotation (IAR): Lies anterior to the vertebral body.
- Interspinous ligament, though thin, is crucial due to its long lever arm.
- Anterior ligaments (ALL, PLL) → short lever arms → must be strong.
- Posterior ligament complex → long lever arms → even thin structures resist flexion effectively. MRI Tip: Interspinous ligament edema = posterior tension band failure → spinal instability.

### GRAVITATIONAL FORCES & SPINAL BALANCE
- Axial load: Acts vertically through the vertebral column.
- Center of gravity: ~4 cm anterior to S1.
- Creates anterior bending (compression) countered by posterior tension band (muscles + ligaments).
- Net result: Balanced upright posture for forward gaze. Spine behaves like a crane system:

- Anterior = compression force.
- Posterior = tension band.
- Failure of posterior elements → flexion-distraction injuries.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Motion segment: Disc + bilateral facet joints.
- Lumbar facets: Sagittal → flexion/extension.
- Cervical facets: Planar → rotation/lateral bending.
- Facet tropism: Asymmetry → risk for degeneration/spondylolisthesis.
- Adamkiewicz artery: T8–L2, left posterior intercostal origin.
- Cord ischemia: Common mid-thoracic, anterior artery involvement → paraplegia.
- MAP >80 mmHg & oxygenation essential during surgery.
- Posterior ligament complex crucial for stability (posterior tension band principle).
