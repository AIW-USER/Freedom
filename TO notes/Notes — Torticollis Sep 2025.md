### Overview / Key Concepts
- Torticollis (Latin tortus = twisted, collum = neck): combined TILT of the head with a ROTATORY deformity of the neck, with limited lateral bending; also a cosmetic deformity.
- NOT every torticollis is a sternocleidomastoid (SCM) contracture: about 80% are congenital muscular torticollis (CMT), about 20% non-muscular.
- Two key questions: congenital or acquired? painful or painless?

### Clinical Evaluation

| Clue | Points to |
|---|---|
| Present AT birth | Osseous cause |
| Appears a few weeks after birth (mass then band) | CMT |
| Acute onset | Inflammatory or infective |
| Painful | Trauma, tumour, infection/inflammation, calcified disc |
| Painless | CMT, ocular, osseous |
| Preceding upper respiratory / throat infection | Grisel syndrome |

- Examine tilt and rotation; bend and rotate the neck to both sides and feel for a tight SCM cord (most evident on rotating to the opposite side).

- Late or neglected cases: plagiocephaly and facial asymmetry (flattened side of face and occiput from lying on one side, contralateral frontal protrusion, smaller/higher eye, displaced ear), visual disturbance (diplopia).
- Short neck, low hairline, webbing, scoliosis: think of an osseous cause.
- Associated "packaging" disorders: DDH, plagiocephaly, metatarsus adductus (intrauterine crowding).

### Non-Muscular Causes

| Condition | Key features | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Grisel syndrome | Non-traumatic atlantoaxial (rotatory) subluxation after head/neck infection or inflammation (e.g. group A strep throat infection); inflammatory laxity of the transverse ligament; painful, acute | Antibiotics, anti-inflammatories, rest, collar; if no improvement after about a week: halter traction + muscle relaxants + analgesics; surgery rarely |
| Klippel-Feil syndrome | Fusion of 2 or more cervical vertebrae; short neck, low hairline, restricted movement; Sprengel shoulder | As per associated problems |
| Occipitocervical / craniovertebral anomalies | Present at birth, no muscle spasm, short neck, low hairline, restricted motion; AA subluxation, basilar invagination (McGregor, McRae, Wackenheim lines) | Evaluate stability |
| Ocular torticollis | Painless tilt without SCM contracture, good cervical range, squint/nystagmus; commonest cause: SUPERIOR OBLIQUE weakness (4th nerve palsy) | Eye muscle surgery corrects the tilt |
| Sandifer syndrome (paroxysmal torticollis of infancy) | Under 1 year; brief episodes of torticollis/dystonic posturing after feeding; vomiting, dysphagia, failure to thrive; gastro-oesophageal reflux (often hiatus hernia); EEG normal | Treat the reflux |

### Congenital Muscular Torticollis (CMT, Wry Neck)
- Commonest congenital painless torticollis. Not seen at birth: a painless SCM swelling ("tumour"/haematoma) appears at 4–8 weeks, becomes a fibrous band; residual contracture with tilt and rotation by 6–8 months.
- "Cock robin" posture: head tilted TOWARDS the affected SCM, chin rotated AWAY from it.

<!-- page 3 -->

- SCM anatomy: from the mastoid; sternal (tendinous) and clavicular (muscular) heads; supplied by the spinal accessory nerve.

| Compartment syndrome (packaging) | Intrauterine crowding (breech, primiparity): head twisted in utero, ischaemia and fibrosis of the SCM (most accepted) |

Investigations

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| X-ray | Osseous causes, infection, tumour (children over 4 –5 years) |
| Ultrasound | MOST SENSITIVE test for CMT: homogeneous hyperechoic mass in the SCM |
| CT (dynamic) | Atlantoaxial instability, bony detail |
| MRI brain and cord | Tumours, syrinx, cord abnormalities |
| Others | Ophthalmology evaluation, facial symmetry assessment |

Treatment of CMT
Conservative
- Controlled manual stretching is safe and effective in over 95% of CMT seen before 1 year (Morrison and MacEwen: 232 patients, excellent results under 1 year).
- Good prognostic factors for stretching: age under 1 year, minimal rotational deficit (over 30° rotation possible), no palpable tight band or tumour in the SCM.
- Technique (taught to parents): BEND to the OPPOSITE side (ear towards the opposite shoulder) and ROTATE to the SAME side.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Adjuncts: positioning toys, ROM exercises, Botox + stretching (Botox alone does not help), TOT collar (tubular orthosis for torticollis).
- Prevent plagiocephaly: repositioning with stretching; moulding helmet if needed.

Surgery
- Indications: over 1 year, rotational deficit (under 30° rotation), limited passive rotation, severe SCM contracture with facial asymmetry, gaze deviation over 30°.
- Ideal age: 5–12 years (Shim and Jang). Between 1 and 5 years: small structures, loss of muscle bulk (cosmetic deficit), band hard to define so more recurrence and harder reconstruction.
- Positioning: GA, interscapular pad to extend the neck, ETT and head-neck junction at the table hinge, arm taped down.
- Unipolar release: distal release of the sternal and clavicular heads; poorer results (residual tilt, loss of neck contour).
- Bipolar (Ferkel) release (JBJS 1983): distal: Z-plasty of the sternal head (preserves the neck column) + release of the clavicular head from bone (resect 1–1.5 cm); proximal: release at the mastoid BONY insertion to avoid the spinal accessory nerve; facial and greater auricular nerves also at risk.
- Check correction on table: chin to midline, full lateral flexion, symmetric rotation.
- After surgery: immobilisation/bracing is now largely obsolete; early active and passive- assisted exercises (head traction, suspension, rotation, lateral flexion, lateral translation).
- Endoscopic release: smaller scar, better view of nerves.

- Results: bipolar release about 92% good or excellent vs other releases about 15% good, 77% fair.
- Adults with neglected CMT (Patwardhan et al., Sancheti Institute): bipolar release can still give good results, but outcome cannot be guaranteed (secondary bony changes, fibrosis).

### Exam & Viva Pearls
- 80% muscular, 20% non-muscular.
- At birth = osseous; few weeks after birth = CMT.
- Cock robin: tilt towards, chin away from the tight SCM.
- Ultrasound is the most sensitive test for CMT.
- Stretch under 1 year; operate at 5–12 years with a bipolar (Ferkel) release.

### Take-Home Summary
- Ask congenital or acquired, painful or painless, to separate CMT from osseous, inflammatory, ocular and paroxysmal causes; stretch CMT in infancy and release persistent contractures with a bipolar release in childhood, followed by early physiotherapy.
