Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Lumbar Canal Stenosis
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

### OVERVIEW
- Definition: Narrowing of the lumbar spinal canal leading to compression of the dural sac and nerve roots.
- Common Levels: L4–L5 > L5–S1 > L3–L4.
- Typical Age: 50–60 years.
- Etiology: Degenerative (most common), Congenital (idiopathic, achondroplasia),
Metabolic (Paget’s, Fluorosis).

### ANATOMY & PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
- Normal Canal Dimensions:
- Midsagittal diameter > 13 mm (normal); 10–13 mm = relative stenosis; <10 mm =
absolute stenosis.
- Thecal sac area > 100 mm² (normal); 76–100 mm² = moderate; <76 mm² = severe.
- Lateral recess height > 5 mm (normal); <3 mm = severe narrowing.
- Boundaries of the Lumbar Canal:
- Anterior: Posterior vertebral body & annulus fibrosus.
- Posterior: Lamina & ligamentum flavum.
- Lateral: Facet joints & pedicles.
- Degenerative Cascade:
- Disc dehydration → loss of lordosis → posterior load shift.
- Facet joint arthrosis & osteophytes encroach on canal.
- Ligamentum flavum hypertrophy & buckling add to compression.
- May progress to instability (esp. L4–L5).
- Special Form: Synovial cyst at facet joint – indicates segmental instability.

### CLINICAL FEATURES
- Symptoms:
- Low back pain (65%)
- Neurogenic claudication (80%)
- Radicular pain (10%)
- Cauda equina syndrome (rare)
- Neurogenic Claudication:
- Intermittent leg pain, numbness, paresthesia.
- Worsens with walking/standing/extension.
- Relieved by sitting or flexion (stooped posture).
- Pain + sensory symptoms = hallmark.

### EXAMINATION
- Gait: Forward stoop during walking.
- Motor/Sensory/Reflex: Check for deficits.
- Distal Pulses: Must be palpated to rule out vascular cause.
- Upper Limb Reflexes: Evaluate for cervical/thoracic myelopathy (tandem stenosis).

### IMAGING
- X-ray LS Spine:
- AP: Narrow inter-pedicular distance (congenital).
- Lateral: Disc space collapse, osteophytes, facet hypertrophy.
- Evaluate interlaminar space – narrowing = degeneration.
- Dynamic views (flexion-extension) to assess instability.
- MRI (Whole Spine):
- Gold standard for diagnosis.
- Shows ligamentum flavum thickening, facet arthrosis, triangular canal.
- Evaluate for tandem stenosis (cervical/thoracic).
- Synovial cysts & pseudodisc bulge indicate segmental instability.

MANAGEMENT
1⃣ Conservative (First Line)
- Activity modification & lifestyle adjustment.
- Flexion exercises → relieve nerve root compression.
- Medications: NSAIDs, Pregabalin (for neuropathic pain).
- Physiotherapy: TENS, heat/cold therapy, traction, bracing.
- Epidural steroid injection or selective nerve root block if persistent symptoms.
2⃣ Indications for Surgery
- Progressive neurological deficit.
- Cauda equina syndrome.
- Disabling claudication unresponsive to therapy.
- Radiological instability.

### DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS
- Vascular: Peripheral arterial disease, AAA.
- Musculoskeletal: Hip/knee arthritis, sacroiliac disorders.
- Neurologic: Diabetic/peripheral neuropathy, cervical myelopathy, demyelinating diseases, entrapment neuropathies.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Commonest level: L4–L5.
- Pain relief on flexion is diagnostic.
- Always palpate distal pulses in all leg pain cases.
- Whole spine MRI for tandem stenosis.
- Synovial cyst = instability indicator.
- Never miss: Cauda equina, unstable fracture, cervical/thoracic myelopathy.

TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Degenerative lumbar canal stenosis = mechanical + vascular compromise.
- Pain relieved by sitting/flexion; worsened by walking/extension.
- 40% improve with conservative management.
- MRI is gold standard; look for ligamentum flavum hypertrophy & facet arthrosis.
- Surgery reserved for neuro-deficit or disabling claudication.
