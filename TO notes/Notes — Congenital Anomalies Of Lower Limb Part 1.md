Overview / Key Concepts
This lecture focuses on Congenital Talipes Equino Varus (CTEV) as a core lower limb
congenital deformity, with emphasis on exam oriented definitions, classifications,
severity assessment, terminology, and a structured management algorithm. The
approach is practical, clinic based, and aligned with DNB and MS examination patterns.
CTEV is a three dimensional deformity and must always be visualized in sagittal,
coronal, and axial planes.

Definition and Epidemiology of CTEV
Definition
Congenital Talipes Equino Varus is a congenital deformity of the foot characterized by a
combination of cavus, adduction, varus, and equinus deformities.

Epidemiology
- Incidence: 1 in 1000 live births
- Bilateral involvement: ~50 percent
- Autosomal dominant inheritance described, with incomplete penetrance

Etiopathogenesis of CTEV
### Important Theories (Exam Oriented)
1. Arrest of Embryonic Development Theory
- Arrest occurs at 6–8 weeks of intrauterine life
- Foot at this stage is naturally in equinus, cavus, and adduction
- Failure of normal rotation and remodeling leads to CTEV
2. Fibroblastic Proliferation Theory
- Increased collagen fibers and fibroblasts in:
  - Ligaments
  - Tendons
  - Capsules
- Similar pathology to contracture disorders
3. Germplasm Theory (Most Accepted)
- Defect in cartilage anlage of talus

- Dysmorphic talar neck
- Navicular fails to articulate and subluxates medially
Exam Pearl - Germplasm theory is the most commonly asked theory in OSCEs
### Components of Deformity (CAVE)

| Component | Deformity |
|---|---|
| C | Cavus |
| A | Adduction |
| V | Varus |
| E | Equinus |

The deformity results from abnormal relationship between talus, calcaneus, navicular,
and cuboid.

Deforming Forces and Joint Involvement
### Joints Involved
- Talocalcaneal joint
- Talonavicular joint
- Calcaneocuboid joint
Structures Causing Deformity
Medial Side - Deltoid ligament - Spring ligament (calcaneonavicular) - Talonavicular
capsule - Tibialis posterior tendon - Flexor hallucis longus - Flexor digitorum longus
Posterior Structures - Tendo Achilles - Posterior ankle capsule - Posterior subtalar
capsule
Understanding deforming forces is critical for planning soft tissue release surgeries.

Terminology in Clubfoot (Very High Yield)
### Untreated Clubfoot
- No treatment taken
- Can present at any age
### Neglected Clubfoot
- No treatment taken after 18 months of age
- Defined based on walking age

<!-- page 3 -->

Relapse
- Reappearance of deformity before completion of treatment
Recurrence
- Reappearance of deformity after completion of treatment
Exam Pearl - Timeline for neglected clubfoot is 18 months, not 6 or 9 months
Severity Assessment
Pirani Scoring System (0–10)
Assessed in maximally corrected position
Forefoot - Adduction rigidity - Long flexor contracture
Midfoot - Medial crease - Curved lateral border - Talar head coverage
Hindfoot - Posterior crease - Empty heel - Achilles tightness

Dimeglio Classification (0–20)
Grades four deformities: - Equinus - Varus - Adduction - Rotation
Used more commonly in: - Older children - Neglected cases - Rigid deformities

### Functional Classification

| Score | Type |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | Soft–Soft (Benign) |
| 5–9 | Soft–Stiff |
| 10–14 | Stiff–Soft |
| >15 | Stiff–Stiff (Very Severe) |

Principles of Management
### Universal Rule
- Ponseti casting is first line at any age
Goals
- Gradual correction
- Avoid midfoot break
- Preserve mobility

<!-- page 4 -->

Ponseti Method
### Key Principles
- Serial manipulation and casting
- Long leg casts
- Manipulation duration: 1–3 minutes before each cast
- Cast change every 5–7 days
### Order of Correction
1. Cavus
2. Adduction
3. Varus (corrects with abduction)
4. Equinus
### Important Points
- Never correct all deformities at once
- Never pronate foot

Tendo Achilles Tenotomy
Indications
- Failure to achieve ≥15–20° dorsiflexion
### Technique Highlights
- Percutaneous
- Medial to lateral approach
- Done under tension
- Audible and palpable release felt
### Final Cast
- Applied for 3 weeks
- Foot in maximal dorsiflexion and abduction

### Brace Protocol
- Foot abduction brace
- Bar length: shoulder width + 1 inch
- 23 hours per day initially
- Night and nap time till 4 years
Most common cause of relapse: poor brace compliance

Surgical Management (When Needed)
Indications
- Failure of Ponseti (up to 10–11 casts)
- Rigid or neglected deformities
### Soft Tissue Procedures
- Posteromedial soft tissue release
- Turco incision
- Cincinnati incision
- Carroll’s dual incision
### Structures Released
- 3 tendons (TP, FHL, FDL)
- 3 ligaments (deltoid, spring, talonavicular)
- 3 joint capsules (ankle, subtalar, talonavicular)
- ± Z lengthening of tendo Achilles

Bony Procedures
### Lateral Column Shortening
- Lichtblau procedure
- Evans modification
- Cuboid decancellation
- Calcaneocuboid fusion (Advancement procedure)
### Age Based Approach
- <4 years: casting and soft tissue
- 4–8 years: selective osteotomies
- 10 years: triple arthrodesis

Faculty Quote
“Ponseti casting is not about force. It is about sequence, patience, and respecting foot
biomechanics.” – Dr. Rahul Upadhyay
