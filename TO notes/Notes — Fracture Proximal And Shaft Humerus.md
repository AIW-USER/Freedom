Overview
- Proximal humerus fractures are among the most common osteoporotic fractures in the elderly.
- Shaft humerus fractures are common in both pediatric and adult trauma.
- Understanding anatomy, displacement patterns, blood supply, and fixation principles is essential for successful management.
Dr. Anurag’s Remark:
“Every proximal humerus fracture is a test of respecting both mechanics and biology —
ignore either, and failure follows.”

| Indication | Criteria |
|---|---|

surgery

Protocol: 1. Arm pouch sling or shoulder immobilizer.

3. Isometric – 3 weeks.
4. Strengthening – 6 weeks.
5. Repeat X-rays weekly to detect displacement.

### ORIF with Locking Plate (PHILOS)
- Approach: Deltopectoral (utility approach).
- Plate position: 2–4 mm posterior to bicipital groove, 5–7 mm below GT tip.
- Screw configuration: 4–6 in head, 3–4 in shaft (3x5 rule).
- Bone quality assessment: Combined Average Cortical Thickness >4 mm.
### Preventing Varus Collapse

| Method | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Calcar screws | Support inferomedial quadrant |
| Fibular grafting | Structural support in osteoporotic bone |

Most Common Complication: Screw perforation through humeral head.

Shaft Humerus Fracture
### Anatomy & Features
- Extent: From surgical neck to supracondylar ridge.
- Shape: Cylindrical proximally, conical distally.
- Common Nerve Injury: Radial nerve (3–30%), usually neuropraxia.
Clinical Features
Pain, swelling, deformity, crepitus, abnormal mobility.

Imaging
- X-ray: AP and lateral views.
- Evaluate displacement, comminution, and bone quality.

### Treatment Options

| Type | Methods |
|---|---|
| Non-operative | Hanging cast, Coaptation splint, Functional brace |
| Operative | ORIF (plate), IM nail, External fixator |

### Non-Operative: Functional Brace (Sarmiento Principle)
- Concept: Active muscle contraction within brace provides hydraulic compression.
- Principle: “Continuing limb function encourages union.”
- Criteria: Acceptable shortening ≤3 cm, angulation ≤20°, rotation ≤30°.

Dr. Anurag’s Tip:
“In shaft humerus, the best fixation is the one that restores function and respects the
nerve — not just alignment.”

### Complications – Shaft Humerus

| Complication | Notes |
|---|---|
| Radial nerve palsy | Most common |
| Non-union / Malunion | Especially with poor fixation |
| Infection | Open injuries |
| Shoulder stiffness | Especially post-nailing |
| Implant failure | Due to poor cortical purchase |

### Key Exam Points
- Codman – Four-part anatomy concept.
- Neer’s Classification – Based on displacement.
- Terrible Triad of Shoulder – GT # + Dislocation + Nerve injury.
- Hertel Criteria – Predicts humeral head ischemia.
- Posterior circumflex humeral artery – Major head supply.
- Calcar screws – Prevent varus collapse.
- Functional brace (Sarmiento) – Hydraulic theory.
- Radial nerve palsy (shaft) – Observe unless iatrogenic or open.
Final Faculty Takeaway:
“Fix the humerus not just to unite bone, but to restore movement — stability without
stiffness is the real success.”
