Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Relative
Energy Deficiency Syndrome (RED-S)
Faculty: Dr. Shikha Dhaundiyal

OVERVIEW
Focus: The concept of Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S) — an expanded
model of the Female Athlete Triad describing the multi-systemic consequences of low
energy availability (LEA) on performance and health in both genders.
Dr. Shikha’s Quote: “RED-S is not a women’s issue — it’s an energy
issue.”

DEFINITION
Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S) is a condition of impaired
physiological function caused by energy deficiency relative to energy expenditure,
affecting multiple body systems and athletic performance.

Formula:
Energy Availability (EA) = (Energy Intake – Exercise Energy Expenditure) /
Fat-Free Mass (kg)

| EA Level | Physiological Effect |
|---|---|
| ≥45 kcal/kg FFM/day | Optimal function |
| 30–44 kcal/kg FFM/day | Subclinical adaptation |
| <30 kcal/kg FFM/day | Dysfunctional state → RED-S |

Chronic LEA <30 kcal/kg FFM/day is diagnostic of RED-S.
### ORIGIN & EVOLUTION

| Year | Concept |
|---|---|
| 1992 | Female Athlete Triad (FAT) — Low EA + Menstrual dysfunction + Low BMD |
| 2014 | IOC introduced RED-S → broader systemic impact across males & females |
| 2018 (Update) | Emphasis on performance impairment and psychological health |

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
### 1⃣ Low Energy Availability (LEA)
- Occurs when energy intake < energy required for normal physiological functions after accounting for training load.
- Results in adaptive downregulation of metabolism to conserve energy.
Triggers: - Excessive exercise volume or intensity. - Restrictive diet / disordered eating.
- Prolonged caloric deficit for aesthetic or weight-class sports. - Poor recovery and
inadequate sleep.
“When the body runs on empty, performance pays the price.”

### CLINICAL CLUES

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Performance | Unexplained fatigue, poor recovery, frequent injuries |
| Menstrual | Amenorrhea, irregular cycles (women) |
| Bone | Stress fractures, shin pain, delayed healing |
| Metabolic | Cold intolerance, dizziness, early fatigue |
| Behavioral | Restrictive eating, perfectionism, anxiety around food |

Sports at Risk: Gymnastics, Ballet, Running, Rowing, Figure skating, Cycling, Martial
arts, Bodybuilding.

SCREENING TOOLS
### 1⃣ LEAF-Q (Low Energy Availability in Females Questionnaire)
- Detects risk in female athletes (menstrual, GI, injury history).

- Score ≥8 = High risk.
### 2⃣ RED-S CAT (Clinical Assessment Tool)
- Developed by IOC for both genders.

- Categorizes athletes into Green, Yellow, Red based on severity.

Zone Description Recommendation

Green Healthy, normal EA Full participation
Mild LEA / menstrual Modify training, monitor

Yellow irregularity
Red Amenorrhea, low BMD, No participation until cleared
severe LEA
“Return-to-play is a privilege earned with energy balance.”
MANAGEMENT
1⃣ Multidisciplinary Approach
Team: Sports Physician + Nutritionist + Psychologist + Physiotherapist + Coach.

### 2⃣ Nutritional Intervention

| Focus | Strategy |
|---|---|
| Energy Intake | +300–600 kcal/day initially |
| Macronutrients | Carbs 55–60%, Protein 15–20%, Fat 25–30% |
| Protein Timing | 20–25 g high-quality protein post-exercise |
| Calcium & Vit D | 1500 mg Ca + 1500–2000 IU Vit D per day |

<!-- page 5 -->

| Focus | Strategy |
|---|---|
| Iron / B12 | Supplement if deficient |

Key Principle: Increase intake before reducing training volume.

### 3⃣ Training Modification
- Decrease intensity by 10–20%.

- Avoid back-to-back high-load days.

- Include recovery microcycles & rest days.

- Cross-train with low-impact activities (cycling, swimming).

4⃣ Hormonal Therapy (if Amenorrhea >6 months)

| Treatment | Notes |
|---|---|
| Transdermal Estrogen + Cyclic | Improves BMD, avoids |
| Progesterone | hepatic effects |
| Combined OCPs | Symptomatic use only — may mask LEA |
| Bisphosphonates | Only for severe osteoporosis, post-skeletal maturity |

Males: Avoid early testosterone replacement — restore EA first.

### 5⃣ Psychological Management
- Address body image distortion & performance anxiety.

- Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) preferred.

- Involve family & coaches for supportive environment. “The mind eats before the body performs.”

| Parameter | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Body weight / composition | Monthly |

Goal: Full restoration of menstrual function & energy equilibrium.

RETURN-TO-PLAY CRITERIA
Green Zone (Cleared):
- Normal menstrual function (or testosterone in males).
- EA ≥ 45 kcal/kg FFM/day.
- Stable body weight & normal mood.
- BMD ≥ −1.0 (DEXA).
Yellow Zone (Caution):
- Subclinical LEA or irregular cycles → partial participation only.
Red Zone (Restricted):
- Amenorrhea, severe LEA, low BMD, or eating disorder → no competition.

### PREVENTION STRATEGIES
- Educate coaches & athletes on early warning signs.

- Encourage regular meal timing & adequate fueling around exercise.

- Promote body positivity & discourage weight-based selection.

- Integrate period tracking and performance monitoring as part of athlete screening. “Fuel your body like it’s your equipment — underfueling breaks performance.”

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- RED-S = Low Energy Availability → Multi-system dysfunction + Performance decline.

- Affects both males & females.

<!-- page 7 -->

- Screen early, restore energy, monitor recovery.

- Management = Nutrition first, Psychology next, Hormones last. “Performance thrives on balance — energy is the true endurance.”
