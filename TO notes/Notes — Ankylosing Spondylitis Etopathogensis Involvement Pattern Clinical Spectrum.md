Ankylosing Spondylitis: Etiopathogenesis,
Involvement Pattern & Clinical Spectrum –
Dr. Shikha Dhaundiyal
Overview / Key Concepts
Ankylosing spondylitis is a chronic inflammatory autoimmune disorder that primarily
affects the axial skeleton. It commonly presents with inflammatory back pain, sacroiliitis
and progressive spinal stiffness. Peripheral joints, entheses and extra‑articular organs,
particularly the eyes, may also be involved.
Key characteristics: - Seronegative spondyloarthropathy (rheumatoid factor negative) -
Strong genetic association with HLA‑B27 - Typical onset before 40 years of age - Pain

Etiopathogenesis
improves with exercise and worsens with rest

AS arises from a complex interplay of genetic, immunological, microbial and hormonal
influences.

### Genetic Factors
- 90 to 95 percent of patients with AS are HLA‑B27 positive.
- Only 1 to 2 percent of HLA‑B27 positive individuals develop AS, indicating that additional triggers are required.
- Higher concordance among monozygotic twins (about 63 percent).
- HLA‑B60 increases susceptibility to AS in HLA‑B27 negative patients.
- Population differences in HLA‑B27 subtypes (B2705 in Caucasians, B2704 in Chinese and B2702 in Mediterranean groups).
Structure of HLA‑B27
HLA‑B27 has three unique structural features which predispose to disease: 1.
Glutamine substituted for methionine at position 45. 2. Unpaired cysteine at position 67
that promotes formation of heavy‑chain homodimers. 3. Lysine at position 70 increases
reactivity of cysteine‑67.

### Key Mechanisms
1. Arthritogenic Peptide Hypothesis
HLA‑B27 presents microbial or self‑mimicking peptides to CD8+ T‑cells. This can initiate
an autoimmune response.

<!-- page 2 -->

2. Misfolding Hypothesis
Misfolded HLA‑B27 accumulates in the endoplasmic reticulum and activates: - ER
stress responses - Unfolded protein response - Autophagy This promotes IL‑23 and
IL‑17 mediated inflammation.
3. Homodimer Formation
Loss of β2‑microglobulin allows HLA‑B27 heavy chains to form homodimers. These bind
with high affinity to KIR3DL2 receptors on NK cells and Th17 cells, stimulating IL‑17
production.

ERAP1 and ERAP2
Endoplasmic reticulum aminopeptidases trim peptides for HLA‑B27 loading. - Incorrect
trimming → abnormal peptides → misfolding → homodimers → inflammation. - ERAP1
associated with HLA‑B27 negative AS and HLA‑B40 positive patients. - ERAP2
associated with both B27 positive and negative AS and with inflammatory bowel
disease.

Immunological Pathways
### IL‑23 / IL‑17 Axis
- IL‑23 released from antigen‑presenting cells activates Th17 cells.
- Th17 cells release IL‑17A and IL‑17F.
- IL‑17 stimulates fibroblasts, epithelial cells and macrophages to secrete pro‑inflammatory cytokines.
- IL‑17 also promotes bone resorption through RANKL and paradoxically contributes to syndesmophyte formation.
Microbial Dysbiosis
Common organisms implicated: - Lachnospiraceae - Prevotellaceae species - Klebsiella
pneumoniae - Veillonellaceae - IgA‑coated E. coli - Fungal metabolites such as
beta‑glucans
Gut dysbiosis increases intestinal permeability which allows microbial products to enter
circulation and trigger inflammation.

### Endocrine Influence
- Males: reduced testosterone reserve, elevated LH, altered estradiol:testosterone ratio.

### Pathological Basis
- Females: lower estradiol during menstruation.

Three fundamental pathological processes: 1. Inflammatory reaction with granulation
tissue and bone erosion. 2. Replacement by fibrous tissue. 3. Ossification leading to
joint ankylosis.

Entheseal Inflammation (Enthesitis)
Tendons and ligaments at their bony insertions undergo inflammation, eventually
leading to new bone formation.

### Key Radiological Lesions
- Romanus lesions: inflammatory erosions at vertebral body corners.
- Andersson lesion: discovertebral destruction possibly due to inflammation,

Involvement Pattern
infection or stress.

### Axial Skeleton
- Sacroiliitis is the earliest hallmark.
- Inflammatory back pain, worse at night and improves with activity.
- Progressive fusion of vertebrae leading to restricted mobility and kyphotic deformity.
### Peripheral Joints
- Hip and shoulder arthritis may occur.
- Early hip involvement is associated with poorer prognosis.
- Peripheral arthritis is uncommon but possible.
Enthesitis
Sites include: - Achilles tendon insertion - Plantar fascia - Ischial tuberosity - Greater
trochanter - Costosternal junctions - Tibial tuberosity

### Extra‑articular Involvement
- Uveitis (most common, up to 30 percent)
- Cardiovascular involvement
- Pulmonary fibrosis
- Cauda equina syndrome

### Clinical Spectrum
- Enteric mucosal lesions

Symptoms
- Insidious dull ache in lower lumbar or gluteal region.
- Morning stiffness for hours, improves with exercise.
- Pain becomes bilateral and persistent.
- Nocturnal pain common.
- Neck pain appears late.

### Physical Examination
- Loss of spinal mobility in flexion and extension.
- Reduced chest expansion.
- Tenderness at entheseal sites.
- Progressive kyphosis.
### Laboratory Findings
- ESR and CRP elevated in 50 to 70 percent of active disease.

### Diagnostic Criteria
- Normal inflammatory markers do not exclude AS.

Modified New York Criteria
Clinical - Low back pain ≥3 months, improved by exercise but not rest. - Reduced
lumbar spine mobility in sagittal and frontal planes. - Reduced chest expansion.
Radiological - Bilateral grade 2 to 4 sacroiliitis, or - Unilateral grade 3 to 4 sacroiliitis.
Diagnosis requires one clinical and one radiological criterion.

ASAS Criteria for Axial Spondyloarthritis
Applicable for patients <45 years with >3 months back pain.
Two arms: 1. Imaging arm: sacroiliitis on X‑ray or MRI plus ≥1 spondyloarthritis feature.

### Disease Activity Assessment
2. HLA‑B27 arm: HLA‑B27 positivity plus ≥2 spondyloarthritis features.

BASDAI (Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Index)
Assesses: - Fatigue - Spinal pain - Joint pain and swelling - Enthesitis - Morning
stiffness duration and severity

Red Flags / Complications
Score ≥4 indicates suboptimal disease control.

- Severe kyphotic deformity
- Hip destruction in young patients
- Recurrent uveitis
- Vertebral fractures or pseudoarthrosis
- Aortic regurgitation and conduction defects
- Restrictive pulmonary disease
- Cauda equina syndrome
