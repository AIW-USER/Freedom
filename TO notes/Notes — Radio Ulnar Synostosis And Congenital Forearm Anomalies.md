Radio Ulnar Synostosis & Other Congenital
Anomalies of Forearm – Dr. Shalin Shah
Overview / Key Concepts
Congenital anomalies of the forearm are important pediatric and exam spot diagnoses.
This lecture focuses mainly on Radial Club Hand (RCH) and Congenital Radio Ulnar
Synostosis (RUS), covering their embryology, clinical evaluation, syndromic
associations, classification systems and management principles.
Both conditions are commonly tested in MS and DNB examinations as spot cases, short
cases and viva scenarios.

Embryology and Pathogenesis
### Forearm Development
- Upper limb bud develops around 4th week.
- Radius and ulna arise from a single cartilaginous anlage.
- Differentiation and separation occur from distal to proximal by 7th week.
Failure of differentiation leads to: - Radial Club Hand: pre axial longitudinal deficiency. -
Radio Ulnar Synostosis: persistent proximal fusion.

Radial Club Hand (Radial Longitudinal Deficiency)
Definition
A rare congenital anomaly characterized by radial deviation of the wrist with a
perpendicular relationship between forearm and hand, with variable deficiency of the
radius and radial sided soft tissues.

Epidemiology
- Incidence: ~1 in 100,000 births.
- Bilateral in 50–72 percent.
- Thumb usually deficient.
Exam Pearl - Bilateral cases are usually syndromic. - Unilateral cases are often
idiopathic.

### Etiology and Pathoanatomy
- Pre axial (thumb side) deficiency.
- Not only a bony defect, but a mesenchymal defect involving:
  - Radius
  - Thumb
  - Thenar muscles
  - Radial vessels
  - Radial half of median nerve
  - Radial sided forearm muscles

Clinical Evaluation of Radial Club Hand
History
- Birth history
- Consanguinity
- Antenatal scan findings
- Exposure to teratogens
- Family history
- Associated systemic complaints (cardiac, renal, GI)
### Examination Points
- Unilateral or bilateral involvement
- Presence or absence of elbow flexion (very important)
- Thumb status
- Finger stiffness or associated anomalies
- Grasp and release function
- Degree of radial deviation
- Limb length discrepancy
Contraindication for surgery - Absent active elbow flexion
### Investigations for Radial Club Hand
- CBC (thrombocytopenia, pancytopenia)
- Renal ultrasound
- 2D echocardiography
- Spine examination and X rays
- MRI spine if indicated

### Syndromic Associations of Radial Club Hand

| Syndrome | Key Feature | Diagnostic Clue |
|---|---|---|
| TAR syndrome | Thrombocytopenia, absent radius | Thumb present |
| Fanconi anemia | Pancytopenia, aplastic anemia | Chromosomal breakage test |
| Holt-Oram | Cardiac septal defects | 2D Echo |
| VACTERL | Vertebral, Anal, Cardiac, TEF, Renal, Limb | Multisystem |
| VATER | Vertebral, Anal, TEF, Renal | Multisystem |

Classification of Radial Club Hand
### Bayne and Klug Classification
- Type I: Deficient distal radial epiphysis
- Type II: Deficient distal and proximal radial epiphysis
- Type III: Partial aplasia of radius
- Type IV: Complete absence of radius (most common)
Important Note
Severity of radial deficiency does not correlate with thumb hypoplasia.

Management Principles of Radial Club Hand
Goals
- Correct radial deviation
- Correct volar subluxation of carpus
- Address ulnar bowing
- Improve function
- Manage limb length discrepancy
### Factors Affecting Management
- Age at presentation
- Functional milestones
- Elbow motion
- Thumb status

<!-- page 4 -->

Non Operative Management
### First 6 Months of Life
- Passive stretching by parents
- Serial casting (Riordan technique)
- Observation in children with absent elbow flexion
### Riordan Casting Technique
- Similar principles to Ponseti method
- Lift second metacarpal to correct palmar flexion
- Gradual ulnar deviation with pressure over distal ulna
- Above elbow casts preferred

Operative Management of Radial Club Hand
After 6 Months of Age
### External Fixator Assisted Distraction
- Bilateral uniplanar fixator
- Two metacarpal and two ulnar pins
- Distraction rate: 1 mm per day
- Complications: infection, fracture, pin loosening
### Definitive Procedures
- Tendon transfers (Buck-Gramcko) in milder cases
- Centralization
- Radialization
- Ulnarization (Paley technique)
Centralization
- Described by Sayre (1893)
- Ulna placed into a notch created in carpus
- Overcorrection preferred
- Tendon transfers to rebalance forces
Radialization
- Modified technique to reduce recurrence
- Ulna placed over radial side of carpus
Ulnarization
- Paley technique
- FCU transfer used to augment wrist extension

<!-- page 5 -->

Pollicization
Indications
- Absent or non functional thumb
- Ideal age: up to 3 years (brain plasticity)
### Key Principles
- Index finger converted into thumb
- Shortening, derotation (160 degrees), extension
- Tendon reorientation:
  - EIP → EPL
  - First dorsal interosseous → APB
  - First palmar interosseous → Adductor pollicis

Congenital Radio Ulnar Synostosis
Definition
Congenital fusion of proximal radius and ulna due to failure of differentiation of primitive
mesenchyme.

### Key Feature
- Forearm usually fixed in pronation.

Epidemiology
- Rare condition
- Male to female ratio: 3:2
- Bilateral in 60 percent
- Familial cases in about 20 percent

### Syndromic Associations of RUS
- Poland syndrome
- Apert syndrome
- Arthrogryposis multiplex congenita
- Carpenter syndrome
- Klinefelter syndrome

### Clinical Features of RUS
- Painless condition

<!-- page 6 -->

- Difficulty in specific activities:
  - Eating, washing face (supination)
  - Keyboard and tabletop activities (pronation)
- Elbow flexion usually normal
- Average fixed position: 30 degrees pronation
- Shoulder compensates for loss of rotation

Diagnosis
- Clinical suspicion
- Confirmed on X ray showing proximal bony bridge
- MRI useful for fibrous synostosis

Classification of RUS
### Mital Classification
- Type I: Fusion without radial head dislocation
- Type II: Fusion with radial head dislocation
### Cleary Classification
- Type I: Fibrous synostosis, radial head reduced
- Type II: Osseous synostosis, radial head reduced
- Type III: Long osseous synostosis with posterior dislocation
- Type IV: Short osseous synostosis with anterior mushroom shaped radial head

Management of Radio Ulnar Synostosis
### Non Operative
- Observation if forearm is in functional position
Operative
- Interposition procedures abandoned
- Corrective osteotomy preferred
### Principle of Surgery
- Forearm is repositioned into functional supination
- Range of motion does not return
- Shoulder compensation improves function
### Osteotomy Options
- Through synostosis

<!-- page 7 -->

- Distal to synostosis
- Double level osteotomy (preferred)
### Age Based Fixation
- <5 years: cast
- 5–10 years: elastic nail
- 10 years: plate fixation

Complications
- Compartment syndrome
- Non union
- Elbow stiffness
- Recurrence due to remodeling

Faculty Quote
“In radio ulnar synostosis, surgery does not restore rotation. It restores function by
placing the forearm where the shoulder can compensate best.” – Dr. Shalin Shah

### Exam Pearls
- Radial club hand is a pre axial deficiency.
- Bilateral RCH is commonly syndromic.
- Absence of elbow flexion is a contraindication for surgery.
- Bayne and Klug Type IV is the most common RCH.
- Pollicization is ideally done before 3 years.
- RUS forearm is usually fixed in pronation.
- Supination loss is better compensated than pronation loss.
- Interposition surgery in RUS is obsolete.
- Osteotomy places forearm in functional position, not motion restoration.
