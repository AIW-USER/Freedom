Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Anterior, Posterior & Multidirectional
Shoulder Instability
Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### OVERVIEW
- Shoulder instability = inability to maintain humeral head centered in glenoid fossa and coracoacromial arch throughout motion.
- Instability ≠ Laxity ≠ Dislocation.
  - Laxity: Incomplete translation without pain.
  - Subluxation: Partial translation with symptoms.
  - Dislocation: Complete loss of humeral–glenoid articulation.
Mnemonic: “Born Loose, Worn Loose, Torn Loose” — congenital laxity,
repetitive microtrauma, and acute traumatic lesions.

ANTERIOR SHOULDER INSTABILITY (ASI)
### Epidemiology & Mechanism
- Most common (60–78% of all shoulder dislocations).
- Bimodal: young males (20–40 yrs) and elderly (>70 yrs).
- Mechanism: Abduction + External Rotation + Extension → anterior capsule and labral avulsion.
Pathology
- Bankart Lesion: Detachment of anteroinferior labrum from glenoid margin.
- Bony Bankart: Fracture of anteroinferior glenoid rim.
- Hill–Sachs Lesion: Impaction defect on posterosuperior humeral head. Chronic recurrence → progressive glenoid attrition → bone loss & off-track lesions.
### Clinical Features
- Recurrent episodes with minimal trauma or during overhead movement.
- Positive Apprehension–Relocation Test.
- Loss of external rotation strength; anterior tenderness.
Investigations
- X-rays: AP, Axillary, Stryker’s notch (for Hill–Sachs).
- CT (3D): Quantify glenoid bone loss.
- MRI: Evaluate labral/capsular lesions.

Management
- <15% glenoid loss → Bankart repair.
- 15–25% → evaluate on-/off-track.
- 25% → Latarjet procedure.

POSTERIOR SHOULDER INSTABILITY (PSI)
### Epidemiology & Mechanism
- 2–4% of all dislocations.
- Common in epilepsy, electrocution, seizures, trauma (dashboard injuries).
- Posterior dislocation: humeral head lies posterior to glenoid, inferior to acromion (subacromial type commonest).
### Clinical Features
- Shoulder held in adduction & internal rotation; external rotation limited.
- Prominent coracoid, humeral head palpable posteriorly.
- Apparent normal AP view → posterior dislocation often missed.

### ARTHROSCOPIC REPAIR TECHNIQUE (POSTERIOR)
- Posterior approach (Deltoid split → interval between infraspinatus & teres minor).
- Anchors: Placed at 7, 8, and 9 o’clock (right shoulder).
- Arthroscopic Portals:
  - A: Kim’s posterior portal – 2 cm lateral to posterolateral acromial corner.
  - B: Mid-glenoid anterior portal – viewing.
  - C: Trans-cuff anterosuperior portal – shuttle.
Precautions:

- Avoid posterior circumflex humeral artery (4 cm away) & suprascapular nerve (3 cm away).
- Always probe to rule out Kim’s lesions.

MULTIDIRECTIONAL INSTABILITY (MDI)
### Definition & Key Features
- Involuntary, symptomatic subluxation/dislocation in ≥2 directions (anterior/posterior/inferior).
- Prevalence: 7–10% of instability cases.
- Common in young, hyperlax patients or those with connective tissue disorders (Marfan, Ehlers-Danlos). Essential Lesion: Increased capsular redundancy.
Pathoanatomy

| Component | Changes |
|---|---|
| Capsule | Redundant and patulous |
| Rotator Interval | Lax (gap >1 cm) |
| Labrum | Shallow / dysplastic |
| Dynamic Stabilizers | Weak cuff, poor coordination |
| Proprioception | Reduced |

### Clinical Tests

| Test | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Sulcus Sign | Inferior translation under traction – graded +1 to +3 |
| ER >85° at side | Suggests inferior laxity |
| Gagey’s | >105° abduction → inferior capsule laxity |

### Hyperabduction Test
Beighton Score Generalized hyperlaxity
Sulcus sign disappears in ER → intact rotator interval.

### CLINICAL PRESENTATION
- Patients report vague pain, fatigue, clicking, and sense of looseness.
- “Loose shoulders” rather than frank dislocations.
- Athletes: decreased performance, shoulder fatigue.
- May have neurological symptoms (numbness, tingling) due to traction.

MANAGEMENT OF MDI
Conservative (Mainstay)
- Scapular stabilization, rotator cuff, deltoid, proprioceptive training.
- 6-month trial mandatory before considering surgery.
- Born loose > Worn loose.
- Majority improve with rehab alone.
### Surgical Indications
- Persistent, symptomatic instability after 6 months of physiotherapy.

### EXAM PEARLS
- Posterior dislocation: Vacant glenoid, light bulb, trough signs.
- Reverse Hill–Sachs: Anteromedial head defect.
- Kim’s & Jerk Tests: 97% sensitivity for posterior instability.
- KIM lesion: Incomplete posterior labral tear; probe during arthroscopy.
- MDI: Symptomatic instability ≥2 directions + hyperlaxity.
- Sulcus test: Inferior laxity; disappears in ER if rotator interval intact.
- Treatment: Rehab first; surgery only if failed ≥6 months.
- Born loose > Worn loose.
- Capsular shift/plication: Restores stability by reducing capsule volume.
