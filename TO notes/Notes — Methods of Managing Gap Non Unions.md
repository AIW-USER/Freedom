Concepts of Non-Unions & Methods of Managing Gap
Non-Unions – Dr. Daivik T Shetty

### Key Concepts / Overview
- Non-union is one of the most challenging problems in orthopedic trauma.
- Successful management requires a clear understanding of fracture biology, mechanical environment, and host factors.
- The lecture integrates foundational concepts of fracture healing with modern surgical and biological management strategies.
Dr. Daivik’s Remark:
“Every non-union tells a story — listen to its biology, respect its mechanics, and choose
your plan accordingly.”

### Fracture Healing – Frost (1989)
Stage Description
1. Hematoma Inflammatory stage with growth factor release
2. Granulation tissue Formation of fibrovascular network
3. Callus formation Soft → hard callus through endochondral ossification
4. Modelling Reorientation of trabeculae along stress lines
5. Remodeling Conversion of woven → lamellar bone (Wolff’s law)

### Tissue Differentiation (Based on Mechanical Environment)
- Perren’s Interfragmentary Strain Theory (1975):
  - Type of tissue formed depends on strain tolerance.
  - Tissue fails if strain exceeds yield tolerance.

| Tissue | Yield Strain (%) |
|---|---|
| Granulation | 100 |
| Cartilage | 10 |
| Bone | 2 |

Strain <2% → Primary bone healing
Strain 2–10% → Secondary bone healing
Strain >17% → Fibrous union

### Diabetes Effects
- ↑ Osteoclastogenesis → High RANKL/OPG ratio → Excess resorption.
- ↓ Osteoblast function → Reduced RUNX2, BMPs → Poor bone formation.
### Smoking Effects
- Vasoconstriction & hypoxia.
- ↑ Free radicals → ↓ osteoblast proliferation.
- Impairs angiogenesis → 2.2× higher non-union risk.

Management Principles
Diamond Concept
Three essential components for healing: 1. Osteogenic cells (MSC, osteoblasts) 2.
Osteoconductive scaffold (bone graft / matrix) 3. Osteoinductive signals (BMPs,
growth factors)

Pentagon Concept (Extended)
Adds: 4. Mechanical stability 5. Vascularity / Host optimization

Polytherapy Approach
Simultaneous application of all three Diamond Concept pillars using biologics, grafts,
and stable fixation.

### Bone Defects
Term Definition
Critical Bone Defect Defect that will not heal spontaneously in a
patient’s lifetime (>2.5 cm or >25% cortical loss)
Subcritical <2.5 cm – may heal with graft or fixation alone

Robinson et al. Guidelines
- >25 mm circumferential loss → early bone grafting.
- <25 mm → observation acceptable.

| Type | Description |

| D3 | Segmental/Critical (>2 cm) |

Each subtype (A, B, C) defined by cortical loss percentage and geometry (oblique,
transverse, etc.).

### Operative Management of Gap Non-Unions
1. Decortication (Judet, 1972)
- Subperiosteal cortical chip elevation without muscle detachment.
- Surround defect with cancellous grafts → rich osteogenic environment.
2. Reamer–Irrigator–Aspirator (RIA) Technique
- Harvests autologous bone + MSCs + growth factors (FGF, IGF, TGF) from femoral canal.
- Decreases IM pressure → less fat embolism risk.
- Provides abundant graft material for biological stimulation.

3. Masquelet (Induced Membrane) Technique

| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| 1st | Debridement + antibiotic cement spacer placement |
| 2nd | After 6–8 weeks → remove cement, fill cavity with cancellous grafts |

Indications: Gap non-union, osteomyelitis, infected non-union.

4. Papineau Open Bone Grafting
- Three stages:
  1. Serial debridements → healthy granulation.
  2. Matchstick cancellous grafts placed openly (“gardening” of graft).
  3. Secondary closure / flap coverage.
- Ideal for infected tibial non-unions.

<!-- page 7 -->

5. Phemister Bone Grafting (Modified by Feber)
- Subcortical grafting without disturbing callus.
- Osteoperiosteal flap + cancellous graft → stimulates union.
- Used in fibrous non-union with minimal motion.

6. Harmon Posterolateral Bone Grafting
- Graft placed on interosseous membrane to form synostosis with fibula.
- Used when anterior tibial soft tissue is scarred or unhealthy.

7. Huntington’s Two-Stage Fibular Tibialization
- For infected gap non-union of tibia.
- Stage 1: Transfer fibula to defect maintaining distal/proximal vascularity.
- Stage 2: Allow hypertrophy and union through load-bearing.

8. Ilizarov Compression–Distraction Osteogenesis
- Ideal for infected, atrophic, or segmental defects.
- Stage 1: Radical debridement.
- Stage 2: Frame fixation ± grafting when infection settles.
- Converts biological instability → mechanical & vascular stimulation.

### Decision-Making Algorithm
1. Identify Cause: Biological vs Mechanical vs Infective.
2. Evaluate: Alignment, stability, soft tissue cover, vascularity.
3. Optimize Biology: Debridement, bone grafts, biologics.
4. Optimize Mechanics: Stable fixation (plate/nail/external fixator).
5. Individualize: Consider host condition, defect size, patient demand.

Case Approach Example
27-year-old, polytrauma, open tibial fracture – nonunion at 10 months. * Cause:
Poor fixation + high-energy open injury → poor biology. * Plan: Single-stage revision →
exchange nailing + bone grafting.
Exchange Nailing Advantages: * Reaming → vascular stimulation. * Deposits
autograft locally. * 2 mm larger diameter nail for increased stability. * Allows early
weight-bearing.

### Exam / Viva Pearls
- Perren strain theory – determines tissue formation.
- Weber–Cech classification – viability-based.
- NUSS – integrates biology & mechanics.
- Critical defect: >2.5 cm or >25% cortical loss.
- RUST score ≥11 → united.
- LIPUS: Stimulates ossification, not proliferation.
- Masquelet: Two-stage induced membrane technique.
- Papineau: Open cancellous graft for infected nonunion.
- Huntington: Fibular tibialization.
- Exchange nailing: Single-stage, vascular stimulation.

Final Faculty Takeaway:
“Non-unions are not failures — they’re feedback. Combine biology, stability, and time,
and every gap can bridge itself back to life.”
