Congenital Anomalies of Lower Limb Part 2 –
Dr. Rahul Upadhyay
Overview / Key Concepts
This lecture covers congenital anomalies of the lower limb distal to the knee, which
are commonly tested as MCQs, spotters, and short notes rather than long cases. The
focus is on forefoot anomalies, congenital angular deformities of tibia, congenital vertical
talus, and congenital pseudarthrosis of tibia and fibula.

Polydactyly of Foot
Definition
Presence of one or more supernumerary toes.

Epidemiology
- Incidence: ~2 per 1000 live births
- Most common isolated congenital foot anomaly
- Usually autosomal dominant
- Function usually normal, problem mainly cosmetic or footwear related
Embryology
- Failure of differentiation of Apical Ectodermal Ridge during first trimester
### Venn–Watson Classification
- Preaxial: medial side, usually short block metatarsal
- Postaxial: lateral side, wide Y-shaped or T-shaped metatarsal
- Central duplication: middle rays
### Radiological Patterns
- Y-shaped metatarsal: equal duplication
- T-shaped metatarsal: unequal duplication
- Wide metatarsal head
- Complete ray duplication
Management
- Indication: cosmetic or footwear difficulty
- Procedure: excision of supernumerary toe with capsular reconstruction
- Modified Farmer’s procedure:
  - Used for duplicated hyperplastic second toe

- Removal of extra ray
  - Rotational flap creation
  - Reinsertion of adductor hallucis

Syndactyly of Toes
Definition
Fusion of adjacent toes.

Types
- Simple: soft tissue fusion only
- Complex: bony fusion present
Epidemiology
- Incidence: ~1 in 2000 births
- Autosomal dominant
- Often syndromic
Management
- Mostly cosmetic
- Prefer staged release to prevent skin necrosis
- Osteotomy required in complex syndactyly

Macrodactyly
Definition
Hamartomatous enlargement of one or more toes.

Associations
- Neurofibromatosis
- Congenital lipofibromatosis
- Proteus syndrome
- Maffucci syndrome
Types
- Macrodystrophia lipomatosa: overgrowth of all mesenchymal elements
- Macrodactyly simplex congenita:
  - Phalanges enlarged
  - Metatarsals spared

<!-- page 3 -->

Symptoms
- Footwear difficulty
- Pain
- Cosmetic deformity
### Management Options
- Soft tissue debulking (high recurrence)
- Staged debulking (Kotwal and Paru technique)
- Bony shortening
- Ray excision for severe deformity

Cleft Foot (Lobster Foot)
Definition
Partial adactyly with central cleft of foot.

### Key Features
- Usually first and fifth rays present
- Variable depth of cleft
### Blodgett and Borisch Classification
- Type 1: five metatarsals, normal
- Type 2: five hypoplastic metatarsals
- Type 3: four metatarsals
- Type 4: three metatarsals
- Type 5: two metatarsals
- Type 6: single metatarsal
### Management Principle
- Convert cleft into syndactyly for better cosmesis
### Wood and Shook Technique
- Alternating rectangular skin flaps
- Excision of fibrous tissue
- Closing wedge osteotomies
- Cross K-wire fixation

Congenital Hallux Varus
Definition
Medial deviation of great toe present at birth.

<!-- page 4 -->

### Also Known As
- Atavistic great toe
Pathoanatomy
- Short, thick first metatarsal
- Fibrous medial band
- Rudimentary duplicated great toe
Differentiation
- Hallux varus: deformity in phalanges
- Metatarsus primus varus: deformity in metatarsal
Management
- Modified Farmer’s procedure
- Release of fibrous band
- Creation of synostosis between first and second toe

Metatarsus Adductus
Definition
Adduction of forefoot with normal hindfoot and midfoot.

### Clinical Feature
- In-toeing gait
### Heel Bisector Line
- Normal: passes between 2nd and 3rd toe
- Mild: passes through 3rd toe
- Moderate: passes through 4th toe
- Severe: passes lateral to 4th toe
Types
- Flexible
- Rigid
- Dynamic (tibialis anterior overactivity)
### Management (Campbell Algorithm)
- Mild: observation
- Moderate: stretching and serial casting
- Severe:
  - 2–4 years: trans-metatarsal osteotomy
  - 4 years: multiple metatarsal osteotomies (Berman and Gartland)

Congenital Vertical Talus (CVT)
Definition
Congenital rigid flatfoot with fixed dorsal dislocation of navicular over talus.

### Also Known As
- Rocker bottom foot
Epidemiology
- Rare
- Bilateral in 50 percent
- Associated with arthrogryposis and myelomeningocele
### Clinical Features
- Convex plantar surface
- Palpable talar head plantomedially
- Forefoot dorsiflexed and abducted
- Poor push-off gait
Radiology
- Talus almost vertical
- Talus–first metatarsal axis non-collinear
- Stress views do not reduce deformity
Management
- Preoperative reverse Ponseti casting
- Surgical correction:
  - Open reduction (1–4 years)
  - Extra-articular subtalar fusion (Grice–Green)
  - Triple arthrodesis in older children

Congenital Angular Deformities of Tibia
### Posteromedial Tibial Bowing
- Usually self resolving
- May leave mild limb length discrepancy
- Managed by observation
### Anterolateral Tibial Bowing
- Associated with neurofibromatosis
- High risk of pseudarthrosis

Congenital Pseudarthrosis of Fibula
Types
- Type 1: fibular bowing
- Type 2: fibular pseudarthrosis without ankle deformity
- Type 3: fibular pseudarthrosis with ankle valgus
- Type 4: fibular pseudarthrosis with tibial involvement
Management
- Long term bracing
- Tibiofibular synostosis (Langenskiöld procedure)

Congenital Pseudarthrosis of Tibia
Epidemiology
- Very rare
- Strong association with NF-1
### Boyd Classification
- Type I: anterior bowing with defect
- Type II: hourglass constriction, high risk tibia
- Type III: cystic lesion
- Type IV: sclerotic segment with stress fracture
- Type V: dysplastic fibula
- Type VI: intraosseous neurofibroma
### Key Associations
- High risk tibia: Type II
- Stress fracture: Type IV
### Management Principles
- Bracing before walking age
- Surgical fixation after fracture
- Options:
  - Intramedullary rodding
  - Vascularized fibular graft
  - Ilizarov fixation
### Indications for Amputation
- Multiple failed surgeries
- Shortening >7.5 cm

<!-- page 7 -->

- Non functional limb

Faculty Quote
“Most congenital lower limb anomalies are not long cases. If you understand
classification and principles, you will never miss the MCQs.” – Dr. Rahul Upadhyay

### Exam Pearls
- Polydactyly is the most common isolated foot anomaly.
- Modified Farmer’s procedure is high-yield.
- Metatarsus adductus assessed using heel bisector line.
- CVT is a rigid flatfoot, physiological flatfoot is always flexible.
- High risk tibia is Boyd Type II.
- Stress fracture is seen in Boyd Type IV.
- Posteromedial tibial bowing resolves spontaneously.
- Anterolateral bowing suggests impending pseudarthrosis.
