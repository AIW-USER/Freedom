Metastasis (2025)
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Spinal metastasis refers to secondary malignant deposits in the vertebral column,
most often arising from breast, lung, prostate, kidney, and thyroid cancers.
Dr. Vishnu’s Quote:
“Always examine the breast and thyroid — even in men — before labeling a
spinal lesion as idiopathic.”
Epidemiology: - Most common spinal tumor overall.
- Seen in patients >40 years.
- Thoracic > Lumbar > Cervical regions.
- Multiple vertebral involvement in ~90% of cases.
Pain: Present in 95% of malignant and 76% of benign spinal tumors.

### PATHOPHYSIOLOGY & SPREAD

| Mechanism | Description |
|---|---|
| Batson’s Paravertebral Venous | Major route of spread — valveless |
| Plexus | system allows retrograde flow during Valsalva, enabling seeding to vertebral bodies. |
| Arterial Dissemination | Less common; through segmental/intercostal arteries. |
| Direct Extension | From adjacent structures like ribs, lungs, or pelvis. |

Why Vertebral Body?
Rich vascular marrow → ideal site for tumor emboli lodging.

### AGE-BASED DIFFERENTIALS

| Age Group | Likely Lesions |
|---|---|
| 10–30 yrs | Benign – Osteoid osteoma, Osteoblastoma, ABC, Osteochondroma |
| 30–50 yrs | Chordoma, Chondrosarcoma, Hodgkin’s disease |
| >50 yrs | Metastasis, Multiple Myeloma |

Location Clue: - Posterior Elements → Benign (Osteoid osteoma, Osteoblastoma).
- Vertebral Body → Malignant (Metastasis, Myeloma, Chordoma).

### CLINICAL FEATURES

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Pain | Constant, non-mechanical, nocturnal pain; may precede radiographic findings. |
| Neurological Deficit | Weakness, sensory loss, bowel/bladder involvement. |
| Deformity | Kyphotic collapse or pathological fracture. |
| Systemic Symptoms | Weight loss, anorexia, fatigue. |

Always examine: Breast, thyroid, abdomen, lymph nodes — even in males.

RADIOLOGICAL INVESTIGATIONS
### Plain Radiograph
- Detectable only after 30–40% bone loss.
- May show lytic/blastic/mixed lesions.

<!-- page 3 -->

### CT Scan
- Defines cortical destruction and calcification.

- Helpful for pedicle involvement and surgical planning.
### MRI (Gold Standard)
- Defines extent, cord compression, and soft-tissue infiltration.
- Detects bone marrow infiltration earlier than X-ray or CT.
### Bone Scan (Technetium-99m)
- Detects early metastatic foci.

- Sensitive for multiple lesions.
### CT-Guided Biopsy
- Confirms histopathology and guides therapy.

### TOMITA PROGNOSTIC SCORE

| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Primary Tumor Growth Rate | Slow (1) – Intermediate (2) – Rapid (4) |
| Visceral Metastasis | None (0) – Treatable (2) – Untreatable (4) |

<!-- page 4 -->

| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Bone Metastasis | Solitary (1) – Multiple (2) |

Interpretation: - 2–3 points: Long-term control (wide/marginal excision).
- 4–5 points: Palliative intralesional excision.
- 6–10 points: Non-operative / supportive care.

### WBB CLASSIFICATION (Weinstein–Boriani–Bagnani)
- Divides vertebra into 12 radial zones (like a clock face).

- Defines tumor compartment (A–E):
  - A: Extraosseous, soft tissue
  - B: Intraosseous, superficial
  - C: Intraosseous, deep
  - D: Extraosseous, extradural
  - E: Extraosseous, intradural
Use: Determines whether en bloc resection is feasible.

### NOMS DECISION FRAMEWORK

| Component | Evaluation |
|---|---|
| Neurologic | Epidural cord compression – Grade 0 (none) → Grade 3 (cord engulfed) |
| Oncologic | Tumor radiosensitivity (e.g., lymphoma – sensitive, RCC – resistant) |
| Mechanical | Stability (assess via SINS score) |
| Systemic | Patient’s general condition & tolerance for surgery |

Goal: Integrate neurology, stability, and prognosis → Tailor surgery or palliation.

### DIAGNOSTIC APPROACH
1. MRI Spine (whole) – to assess extent & compression.

2. CT Chest–Abdomen–Pelvis – search for primary lesion.

3. Bone Scan / PET-CT – systemic survey.

<!-- page 5 -->

4. CT-Guided Biopsy – confirm histology.

5. Tumor Markers: PSA, CA-125, TSH, etc.

MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES
### Medical / Non-Surgical

| Modality | Use |
|---|---|
| Steroids | For acute cord compression with incomplete deficit (e.g., Methylprednisolone 1 g IV). |
| Chemotherapy | Tumor-specific (e.g., myeloma, lymphoma, Ewing’s). |
| Radiotherapy | For radiosensitive metastases (e.g., breast, thyroid, prostate). |
| Bisphosphonates / Denosumab | Inhibit osteolysis and control pain. |

### Surgical Indications

Types of Surgery: - Intralesional Curettage – for palliative decompression.
- En Bloc Resection – for isolated metastasis with slow-growing primaries.
- Vertebroplasty / Kyphoplasty – for pain relief in osteolytic lesions.
Surgical Strategy: - Slow-growing (breast, thyroid, prostate) → Aggressive surgery.
- Moderate (renal, uterine) → Marginal excision.
- Rapid (lung, stomach) → Palliative fixation only.

### PROGNOSTIC CONSIDERATIONS

| Factor | Influence |
|---|---|
| Primary tumor biology | Most significant determinant |
| Neurological status | Complete deficit → poor prognosis |
| Life expectancy | Surgery only if >6 weeks expected survival |
| Number of metastases | Solitary → curative, Multiple → palliative |

Good Prognosis: Breast, Thyroid, Prostate.
Poor Prognosis: Lung, GI tract.

### COMPLICATIONS
- Persistent pain / local recurrence.

- Implant failure or infection.

- Neurological deterioration.

- Hemorrhage (esp. sacral or hypervascular metastases).

- Dural tears during decompression.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Spine = most common site of skeletal metastasis.

- Batson’s plexus – key route of spread.

- Thoracic > Lumbar > Cervical.

- Osteoblastic → prostate; osteolytic → breast, kidney, thyroid.

- MRI = best imaging modality.

- Tomita score = prognosis; NOMS = management.

- Steroids only useful in incomplete deficits.

<!-- page 7 -->

- En bloc resection for solitary, slow-growing metastasis.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Always identify primary malignancy before labeling spinal lesion.

- MRI spine and CT-guided biopsy confirm diagnosis.

- Integrate neurology, stability, and systemic status (NOMS).

- Surgery → for pain, instability, or cord compression.

- Radiotherapy & systemic therapy → mainstay for radiosensitive lesions.

- Goal: Pain relief, neurological preservation, and spinal stability.
