### OVERVIEW
- Rotator cuff tears most commonly involve the supraspinatus tendon, which is also the most frequently ruptured tendon in the entire body.
- A strong understanding of cuff anatomy, biomechanics, and clinical testing is essential for accurate diagnosis and management. Quote: “No orthopedic surgeon can afford to be ignorant about the supraspinatus — it’s the commonest tendon rupture in the body.” – Dr. Mukul Mohindra

- Together, these form a “cuff” around the shoulder joint providing dynamic stability.
- Supraspinatus function: Compresses humeral head into glenoid — initiates abduction (0–90°) and synergizes with deltoid.
Without supraspinatus compression, deltoid action elevates the humeral head
→ anterior-superior escape → rotator cuff arthropathy.

### FOOTPRINT & CRITICAL ZONE
- Footprint dimensions:
  - Anterior-posterior (AP): 25 mm
  - Medial-lateral (ML): 15 mm
  - Tendon thickness: ~12 mm

- Critical zone: ~1.5 cm proximal to insertion — area of poor vascularity, commonest site of tear.

### PATHOGENESIS
1. Internal Impingement (Younger Athletes)
- Due to posterior capsular contracture → head moves posterior-superiorly during throwing.
- Impinges articular side of supraspinatus and superior labrum → SLAP + articular-sided tears.

<!-- page 3 -->

2. External Impingement (Elderly)
- Compression of supraspinatus between acromion and humeral head.
- Caused by subacromial bursitis, acromial osteophytes, or hooked acromion.

MANAGEMENT
### Non-Operative (First-Line for All)
- Rest + NSAIDs + physiotherapy.
- Subacromial steroid injection (for external impingement).
- Many patients develop Burkhart’s “Rotator Cable-Crescent” effect:
  - Thickened edges of biceps & infraspinatus act as suspension bridge → function preserved.
- Surgery only after failed conservative trial (4–6 weeks).

### Repair Techniques
1. Trans-Osseous Repair – Threads passed through bone tunnels (classic gold standard).
2. Suture Anchor Repair – Anchors with medial and lateral rows (knotted medial + knotless lateral = double row).
3. Trans-Tendinous Repair – For partial tears; anchors through intact tendon. Each anchor covers ~1.5 cm of tendon; 2 anchors cover entire supraspinatus footprint.

### KNOT TYING IN ARTHROSCOPY
- Use sliding knots (Duncan loop / Weston knot) for arthroscopic fixation.
- Pull single limb to advance knot through cannula and seat securely on bone.

### EXAM PEARLS
- Commonest tendon rupture: Supraspinatus.
- Footprint dimensions: 25×15 mm; thickness 12 mm.
- Critical zone: 1.5 cm proximal (poor vascularity).
- Classification: Ellman (partial), Cofield (size), Burkhart (shape).
- Common causes: Internal (young) / External (elderly) impingement.
- Clinical tests: Neer, Hawkins-Kennedy, Zaslav, O’Brien, Speed, Yergason, Jobe, Lift-off, Beer Hug, Drop sign, Hornblower.
- MRI: Coronal (Patte), Sagittal (Goutallier).
- First-line: Conservative – NSAIDs, steroid injection, physiotherapy.
- Surgery: Trans-tendinous (<50%), Suture anchor (>50%).
- Irreparable: Fatty infiltration >50%, retraction to glenoid, AH interval <5 mm.
- Treatment: <60 yrs – tendon transfer/SCR; >70 yrs – RSA.
