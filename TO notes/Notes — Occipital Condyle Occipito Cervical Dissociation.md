Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Occipital Condyle Fractures &
Occipito-Cervical Dissociation
Faculty: Dr. Mohit Mohindra

### OVERVIEW
- Craniocervical junction (CCJ) injuries include occipital condyle fractures (OCF), transverse atlantal ligament (TAL) injuries, and occipito-cervical dissociation (OCD/AOD).
- Mechanism: High-velocity trauma (RTA, fall, diving).
- Incidence: 10–25% of cervical cord injuries in fatal RTAs involve the occipito-cervical region.
- Spectrum: From stable undisplaced OCF to widely distracted occipito-cervical dissociation. “A stable craniocervical junction is the cornerstone of upper cervical spine integrity.” – Dr. Mohit Mohindra

ANATOMY & BIOMECHANICS
### Bony Anatomy
- Occipital Condyles (OC): Convex structures on anterior rim of foramen magnum.
- Articulate with C1 lateral masses; form atlanto-occipital (C0–C1) joint.
- Movement:
  - 25° flexion/extension (“YES” movement).
  - 5° rotation/lateral bending.
- Ligamentous Support:
  - Anterior atlanto-occipital membrane – continuation of ALL.
  - Posterior atlanto-occipital membrane – continuation of PLL.
  - Cruciate ligament (transverse + vertical bands) – stabilizes dens against atlas arch.
  - Tectorial membrane – continuation of PLL, anchors C2 to occiput.
  - Alar ligaments: Connect dens → occiput; limit rotation.
  - Apical ligament: Dens tip → basion.

### Key Neurovascular Relations
- Vertebral arteries pass around C1 posterior arch.
- Lower cranial nerves (IX–XII) pass near condyles → risk of palsy (Collet–Sicard syndrome).

### CLINICAL FEATURES
- Occipital or upper neck pain, restricted motion.
- Dizziness, vertigo (vertebral artery involvement).
- Dysphagia, hoarseness, tongue deviation, scapular winging (CN IX–XII injury).
- Severe trauma → quadriplegia or autonomic dysfunction. Collet–Sicard Syndrome: IX–XII palsy due to occipital condyle or AOD injury.

### IMAGING
- X-ray: Limited due to overlapping bone; often misses condyle fractures.
- CT (thin-slice): Gold standard for OCF and C0–C2 injuries.
- MRI: Evaluates cord, ligaments, tectorial membrane, alar & cruciate integrity.
- Key Measurement: ADI >3 mm (adult) / >5 mm (child) = TAL disruption.
Rule of Spence:
Lateral mass overhang of C1 on C2 >6.9 mm → TAL injury.

### C1 (ATLAS) FRACTURES
- Incidence: 5–10% of cervical spine injuries.
- Mechanism: Axial compression (fall/diving trauma).
- Classifications: Jefferson, Gehweiler, and Dickman (for TAL lesions).

### OCCIPITAL CONDYLE FRACTURES (OCF)
- Mechanism: Axial compression ± lateral bending.
- >90% unilateral; often detected incidentally on CT for head injury.
- Lower cranial nerve palsy (30%); may cause dysphagia, hoarseness, scapular winging, tongue deviation.

### OCCIPITO-CERVICAL DISSOCIATION (AOD / OCD)
  - Mechanism: Hyperflexion–distraction or axial loading.
  - Common in pediatrics due to flatter condyles & ligamentous laxity.
  - Highly unstable, often fatal – mortality up to 80% due to brainstem / vertebral artery injury.
Pang Criterion (Radiographic Diagnosis)
Basion–dens interval >2 mm → diagnostic of AOD.
### Traynelis Classification

| Type | Displacement | Description |

### Harborview Classification
  - Type I: Distraction injuries.
  - Type II: Translation injuries.
  - Type III: Combined with rotational component.

MANAGEMENT SUMMARY
### General Principles
- Traction: Contraindicated – may worsen neurological deficits.
- Halo/collar: Avoided (can cause distraction).
- Preferred: Occipito-cervical arthrodesis with rigid internal fixation (Occiput → C2).
### Surgical Indications
- AOD / unstable OCF (Anderson III, Tuli IV).
- TAL disruption with instability.
- Irreducible atlas fractures.
- Neurological compromise or progressive deformity. “Never apply traction in AOD – fix what is unstable, preserve the brainstem.” – Dr. Mohit Mohindra

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- YES joint: Atlanto-occipital (C0–C1). NO joint: Atlanto-axial (C1–C2).
- Rule of Spence: >6.9 mm → TAL rupture.
- ADI: >3 mm (adult), >5 mm (child) → instability.
- Anderson–Montesano III / Tuli IV: unstable → surgery.
- Pang Criterion: Basion–dens >2 mm → AOD.
- Avoid traction or halo in AOD.
- Fixation: Occiput to C2 with rigid construct.
