Overview / Key Concepts
Correlation and regression are statistical tools used to study the relationship between
two variables. Correlation quantifies the strength and direction of association, while
regression explains how a change in one variable predicts change in another.
This lecture focuses on: - Concept and interpretation of correlation - Pearson and
Spearman correlation coefficients - Limitations of correlation analysis - Concept of
simple linear regression - Interpretation of regression coefficients, intercept, and R
square

Concept of Correlation
Correlation identifies whether a relationship exists between two variables without
establishing cause and effect.
- Describes degree and direction of association
- Does not imply causality
- Both variables are quantitative
Examples include: - Father’s height and son’s height - Hours of study and exam score -
Age and bone density

Scatter Diagram
Scatter plot is the first step in correlation analysis.
- X-axis represents independent variable
- Y-axis represents dependent variable
- Each point proves one observation
- Points are not joined
Scatter plots help visualize: - Direction of relationship - Approximate linearity

### Patterns of Correlation
- Positive correlation
- Negative correlation
- Perfectly positive correlation
- Perfectly negative correlation
- No linear correlation
Important distinction: - r = 0 means no linear relationship, not absence of relationship

Correlation Coefficients
### Karl Pearson Correlation Coefficient (r)
- Measures strength and direction of linear relationship
- Used for quantitative variables
Assumptions - Linear relationship between X and Y - Both variables are normally
distributed

Interpretation of r
- Sign indicates direction
  - Positive sign indicates direct relationship
  - Negative sign indicates inverse relationship
- Magnitude indicates strength
Value of r | Interpretation |
0 | No linear relationship |
0–0.25 | Weak correlation |
0.25–0.75 | Moderate correlation |
0.75–1 | Strong correlation |

### Spearman Rank Correlation Coefficient (rs)
- Non-parametric measure of correlation
- Uses ranks instead of actual values
- Applied when Pearson assumptions are not satisfied
- Interpretation same as Pearson r

### Limitations of Correlation
- Correlation does not imply causation
- Association may be due to a common underlying factor
- Spurious correlations may occur
Faculty Quote “A strong correlation only tells you two variables move together, not
that one causes the other.”

Concept of Simple Linear Regression
Regression explains how changes in one variable affect another.

<!-- page 3 -->

- Predicts outcome variable (Y)
- Uses one predictor variable (X)
Regression Equation
Y = a + bX
Where: - a = Y-intercept - b = regression coefficient (slope)

### Line of Regression
- Known as line of best fit
- Obtained by principle of least squares
- Minimizes sum of squared residuals
Residual = Observed value – Predicted value

Components of Simple Linear Regression
### Regression Coefficient (b)
- Also called slope
- Indicates average change in Y for one unit change in X
- Can be positive or negative
Y-Intercept (a)
- Value of Y when X = 0
- Represents baseline outcome

### R Square (Coefficient of Determination)
- Represents proportion of variation in Y explained by X
- R² ranges from 0 to 1
Example: - R² = 0.85 means 85 percent variation in Y is explained by X

### Assumptions of Simple Linear Regression
- Linearity between X and Y
- Independence of observations
- Homoscedasticity (constant variance of residuals)
- Normal distribution of residuals

Correlation vs Regression
Correlation | Regression |
Tells strength and direction | Predicts change in outcome |
No dependent or independent variable | Requires dependent and independent variables
|
No causality inference | Helps establish cause-effect with domain knowledge |
Visualized using scatter plot | Based on least squares method |

### Exam Pearls
- Correlation quantifies association, not causation
- r ranges from −1 to +1
- r = 0 indicates no linear relationship
- Pearson correlation requires normal distribution
- Use Spearman correlation for ranked or non-normal data
- Regression predicts Y from X
- Regression coefficient represents average change in outcome
- R² indicates goodness of fit
- Always interpret regression with clinical reasoning
