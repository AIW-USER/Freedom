### Overview / Key Concepts
- The ulnar nerve is the "musician's nerve" – it controls the intrinsic muscles and fine motor movements of the hand, so its signs, deformities and transfers are more numerous than for the radial or median nerve.
- Clinical signs are used to (1) diagnose ulnar palsy, (2) separate it from median palsy, (3) separate high from low ulnar palsy, and (4) detect a combined injury.
- Recovery after repair: radial nerve best, then median, ulnar the least and slowest (many small, specific intrinsic muscles to re-innervate).
- Management: repair/graft → nerve transfer → static or dynamic claw correction and tendon transfers.

Anatomy
- Root value: C8, T1 (sometimes C7); from the medial cord (lower trunk).
- Arm: enters between the axillary artery (lateral) and vein (medial); runs medial to the brachial artery; at mid-arm pierces the medial intermuscular septum into the posterior compartment.
- Elbow – cubital tunnel: behind the medial epicondyle, between it and the olecranon (palpable, rolls under the finger); gives an articular branch to the elbow. Common compression site → release (neurolysis) plus anterior transposition to prevent repeated compression.
- Forearm: pierces the two heads of FCU; branches – muscular (FCU, FDP to ring and little), palmar cutaneous (medial palm), dorsal cutaneous (dorsum of medial 1½ fingers).
- Wrist – Guyon's canal: superficial to the flexor retinaculum, medial to the ulnar artery; borders: pisiform (ulnar), hook of hamate (radial), palmar carpal ligament (roof), flexor retinaculum (floor). Divides into superficial and deep branches.
- Hand – deep (motor) branch: hypothenar muscles (FDM, ADM, ODM), medial two lumbricals, adductor pollicis, deep head of FPB, all interossei (3 PAD, 4 DAB). Palmaris brevis is the exception – superficial branch.
- Sensory: ulnar 1½ fingers, palmar and dorsal.
Remember: adductor pollicis is a thenar-region muscle but is ulnar supplied – the key to
Froment, Bunnell and Jeanne signs.
- Examine the WHOLE length of the nerve, proximal to distal (clinically and on NCS): there can be multiple compression sites – e.g. one patient had cubital tunnel + Guyon's canal compression + a schwannoma at the brachial plexus; all three had to be treated.

Aetiology
- Trauma
- Inflammation
- Leprosy (less common now, still seen in remote areas)
- Occupational
- Cubital tunnel syndrome
- Guyon's canal compression
- Nerve tumours (neuroma, schwannoma, external compressing tumours)

### Intrinsic vs Extrinsic – the Basis of the Signs
- Intrinsic muscles (origin and insertion in the hand): thenar, hypothenar, interossei, lumbricals – IP movements, finger adduction/abduction.
- Extrinsic muscles (origin in the forearm): long flexors and long extensors.
- Lumbricals: arise from the radial side of FDP (a flexor) and insert into the extensor expansion (an extensor) → MP flexion with IP extension. This "intrinsic-plus" movement is what is lost in ulnar palsy.
- Rule for every sign: the deformity is produced by the muscle that still works and overacts for the paralysed one.

### Nerve Transfers
- Motor: anterior interosseous nerve (AIN, branch to pronator quadratus) → deep motor branch of the ulnar nerve at forearm level – restores intrinsic function (hypothenar, interossei, lumbricals) in one transfer.
- Topography: ulnar nerve fascicles are sensory–motor–sensory from medial to lateral (motor in the centre).
- Sensory: 3rd web-space fascicle of the median nerve end-to-end to the main sensory part of the ulnar nerve; dorsal cutaneous branch end-to-side to the sensory component of the median nerve.
- Release of Guyon's canal / carpal tunnel or cubital tunnel decompression may be needed as well.

### Tendon Transfers and Claw Correction
- Main goal: correct the claw and restore intrinsic function; then finer functions – thumb adduction (key pinch), index abduction, thumb MP stability.
- Principles: supple joints, no contracture, synergistic, strong and dispensable donor, simple route with a pulley, straight line of pull, correct insertion and angle, immobilisation in a functional position.
- Static vs dynamic: Bouvier manoeuvre decides – supple fingers → dynamic transfer; static procedures only correct MP hyperextension (no independent intrinsic action).

### Exam & Viva Pearls
- Ulnar claw = ring and little; claw of all four fingers = ulnar + median.
- Ulnar paradox: high lesion → less claw (FDP also paralysed).
- Froment: FPL substitutes for adductor pollicis; Jeanne: thumb MP hyperextension; Wartenberg: EDM keeps little finger abducted.
- Pollock sign (no DIP flexion of ring/little) = high ulnar palsy.
- Bouvier manoeuvre tells you whether a dynamic transfer is possible.
- Stiles–Bunnell (Littler) only in low ulnar palsy; high palsy → middle finger FDS or extensor- driven (Brand) transfers.
- EE4T = Extensor tendon, Extensor route, 4 Tails (ECRB); EF4T = Extensor tendon, Flexor route (carpal tunnel), 4 Tails (ECRL); PF4T = palmaris longus (Fritschi).
- Palande = direct interossei activation (5 slips).
- Intrinsic plus = safe splinting position.

### Take-Home Summary
- Understand lumbrical/interossei action → the deformities and signs follow logically.
- Localise (high vs low, isolated vs combined) with the signs; check the whole nerve length.
- Repair early; AIN-to-deep-motor-branch transfer is an option; recovery is slowest of the three nerves.
- Claw correction: static (capsulodesis, tenodesis, lasso) if needed, dynamic (Stiles–Bunnell, Brand EE4T/EF4T, Palande) when joints are supple; then key pinch and index abduction transfers.
