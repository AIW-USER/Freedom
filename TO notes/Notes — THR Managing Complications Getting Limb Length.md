Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – THR Complications & Limb Length
Correction
Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

Overview
This lecture combines THR complications, dislocation mechanisms, infection
management, and limb length correction techniques, as well as hip templating
principles. The focus is on practical surgical pearls and exam-oriented facts relevant for
DNB/FNB orthopedic exams.

### Mortality & Importance of Complication Management
- Primary THR: ~1% 90-day mortality.
- Revision THR: 2.5–3× higher mortality.
- Emphasis: A patient walks in; ensure he walks out. Managing complications is as important as performing surgery correctly.

MAJOR COMPLICATIONS IN THR
1⃣ Intraoperative Bleeding
High-risk zones:
- Transverse acetabular ligament: Inferior edge → risk of obturator vessels.
- Gluteus maximus insertion: Posterior femur → risk of profunda femoris artery branches.
- Medial wall penetration: Risk of iliac vessels injury.
Prevention:
- Identify anatomy precisely; avoid deep reaming/over-medialization.
- If bleeding occurs → Cauterize or ligate. Use a drain only if major vessel injured and keep clamped for 6–8 hrs to allow tamponade effect.

### 2⃣ Thromboembolism (DVT / PE)
- Incidence: ~70% develop DVT; ~2% progress to fatal PE.
- Risk factors:
  - Sedentary lifestyle
  - H/o DVT, stroke, or cardiac disease

<!-- page 2 -->

- Fracture cases (neck/trochanteric)
  - Hypercoagulable states / OCP use

Prophylaxis:
- Start immediately post-op; continue 4–6 weeks.
- Options:
  - LMWH (Clexane / Fondaparinux): 3 weeks
  - Aspirin 75 mg daily × 6 weeks (as per NICE UK)
- Stop LMWH before removing epidural catheter/drain (avoid spinal bleed).
Most sensitive test for DVT: Venography (MCQ point)

### 3⃣ Neurological Injury
- Commonest nerve: Sciatic nerve (esp. with posterior approach)
- Highest risk:
  - Revision THR
  - Hip dysplasia
  - Excessive limb lengthening >2.5–4 cm
- Cause: Stretch during correction or from dislocation.
Cemented vs. Cementless:
- Higher nerve injury risk in cementless fixation (possibly due to snug-fit and over-lengthening).
Exploration:
- Not routinely required; indicated only in revision cases (scarred bed).

### 4⃣ Dislocation After THR
- Incidence: ~3% overall, most within first 3 months.
- Risk Factors:
  - Component malposition
  - Posterior approach
  - Small head size
  - Weak abductors / inadequate soft-tissue repair
  - Contractures (flexion/adduction)
  - Neuromuscular disorders
  - Revision or AVN cases

Relative Risk (vs OA):

<!-- page 3 -->

- AVN → ×2; DDH → ×3; Fracture → ×4; Nonunion → ×5; Revision → ×11.
### Lewinnek’s Safe Zone (1978)
- Cup inclination: 40° ± 10°
- Cup anteversion: 15° ± 10°
- Formula: Anteversion = sin⁻¹ (CD/AB) Note: Even if within safe zone, dislocations may occur due to patient and soft tissue factors.
Wera et al. Classification (2012)

| Type | Cause | Management |
|---|---|---|
| I | Acetabular malposition | Cup revision |
| II | Femoral malposition | Stem revision |
| III | Abductor deficiency | Constrained liner |
| IV | Impingement | Remove source / increase offset |
| V | Late wear | Head + liner exchange |
| VI | Unknown cause | Trochanteric advancement |

Key Rule: Identify cause → correct at revision.
### Implant Factors
- Elevated-rim liners: ↓ dislocation (2.2% vs 3.8%) but ↑ impingement/wear.
- Dual mobility cups:
  - ↑ stability, ↑ jump distance.
  - Disadvantage → PE wear & intraprosthetic dislocation.

### 5⃣ Infection (THR Periprosthetic)
- Incidence: 1–2% (Campbell; previously 6.8% per Charnley).
- Risk factors: Diabetes, obesity, RA, coagulopathy, anemia, prior hip surgery, prolonged op-time.
### Prophylaxis (AAOS Guidelines)
- Antibiotics: Cefazolin / Cefuroxime / Clindamycin.
- Timing: Within 60 min pre-incision, 10 min before tourniquet.
- Post-op: Continue 24–48 hrs (Dr. Mukul extends 7–10 days in Indian setup).
Pathogenesis
- Direct contamination (early infection)
- Hematogenous spread (late) – common after dental procedures / UTI
- Reactivation of latent TB infection

<!-- page 4 -->

Diagnosis
- Acute infection: <1 month (Campbell)
- Chronic infection: >1 month
Investigations:
- ESR >30 mm/hr, CRP >10 mg/L → suspicion.
- Best early marker: Interleukin-6 (IL-6)
- Confirmatory test: Hip aspiration (after stopping antibiotics ≥2 wks)
- WBC >4200/mm³ and >85% PMN → infection (Schinsky criteria)
- New marker: Alpha-defensin 1 – Sensitivity 100%, Specificity 95%.
### Consensus (2013): Infection =
- ≥2 positive cultures with identical organism, or
- Sinus tract, or
- ≥3 minor criteria (ESR/CRP↑, PMN%, histology+, single culture)

- IV antibiotics: 6 weeks → reimplant at 3 months if ESR/CRP normal & aspiration negative.
If recurrent infection: Resection arthroplasty (Girdlestone) preferred.

### THR in Tuberculous Hip
- Active TB: No longer contraindication.
- Mycobacterium TB: No biofilm formation; Rifampicin can penetrate intracellularly.
### Updated Approach:
- Minimum 3 weeks ATT pre-op (preferably 2–3 months).
- Continue ATT for 9–12 months total.
- If sinus present → 2-stage THR.
Cemented vs. Cementless:
- Both work; uncemented preferred if bone stock good.
- ATT not added to cement (poor elution & toxicity risk).

Limb Length Discrepancy (LLD)
Tolerable limits:
- <1 cm → unnoticed.
- 1–2.5 cm → may cause dissatisfaction.
- 2.5 cm → symptomatic.
Lengthening >2.5–4 cm → risk of nerve injury.
Biomechanical note: Both excessive shortening and lengthening can increase OA risk
in contralateral hip (altered load distribution).

Getting Limb Length Right – Templating
### Principles of Hip Templating
1. Obtain bilateral pelvis X-ray with 15° internal rotation.
2. Identify ischial tuberosity line and top of lesser trochanter (LT).
3. Draw reference lines:
  - Inter-ischial line
  - LT line → compare both sides for shortening.
4. Mark hip center of rotation on normal side → replicate on diseased side.

<!-- page 6 -->

5. Cup placement: Inferior edge on tear drop line; medial edge not beyond vertical tear drop line.
  - Inclination angle: ~40°
6. Femoral templating:
  - Mark distances from GT & LT to head center.
  - Match neck cut level and stem offset.
7. Maintain femoral and acetabular centers of rotation at same height → restores limb length.
### Interoperative Checks:
- Ranawat technique (greater trochanter reference)
- Shuck test: Half of femoral head should remain in acetabulum when pulled.
- Drop kick test: For soft-tissue tension balance. Digital templating now simplifies measurements – faster and more reproducible.

### Key Exam Pearls
- Mortality: 1% (Primary) vs 2.5% (Revision).
- Safe Zone (Lewinnek): Inclination 40°±10°, Anteversion 15°±10°.
- DVT prophylaxis: 4–6 weeks (Aspirin 75 mg / LMWH).
- Best test for DVT: Venography.
- Commonest nerve injury: Sciatic (posterior approach).
- Early infection management: Debridement + component retention.
- Spacer type: Prostalac (Antibiotic-loaded acrylic cement).
- TB hip: 3–12 months ATT; uncemented preferred.
- LLD tolerance: ≤2.5 cm acceptable.
- Shuck test: Half-head excursion → adequate tension.
