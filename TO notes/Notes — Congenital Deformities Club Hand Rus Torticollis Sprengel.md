Dr. Sanjay Arora
Overview / Key Concepts
This lecture covers four high-yield congenital deformities frequently asked in exams as
short cases and theory questions: - Congenital Talipes Equino Varus (Club Foot) -
Congenital Radio Ulnar Synostosis - Congenital Muscular Torticollis - Sprengel
Shoulder
The emphasis is on etiology, pathoanatomy, classification systems, examination points,
and management principles.

Congenital Talipes Equino Varus (CTEV)
Definition
A congenital deformity characterized by: - Equinus at ankle joint - Varus at subtalar joint
- Forefoot adduction and supination - Cavus at midfoot

Epidemiology
- Incidence: 1 per 1000 live births
- Male:female = 2.5:1
- Bilateral in 50 percent
- Sibling risk: 2–5 percent
- Parent and sibling affected: up to 25 percent
Etiology
Idiopathic (most common) - Extrinsic: intrauterine compression, oligohydramnios -
Intrinsic: arrested development at 5th week, abnormal tendon attachments,
neuromuscular defect, vascular anomaly, hereditary AD with incomplete penetrance
Secondary - Myelomeningocele - Spina bifida - Arthrogryposis - Poliomyelitis

Pathoanatomy
- Talus: medially directed, short neck, broad superior surface
- Calcaneus: small, medially displaced, supinated
- Navicular: medially displaced, may articulate with medial malleolus
- All posteromedial and plantar soft tissues contracted

### Clinical Features
- Small stout foot, small heel
- Deep medial and posterior creases
- Calf atrophy
- Convex lateral border
- Dorsolateral bony prominence
- Internal tibial torsion
Radiology (older children)
- Talocalcaneal angle reduced (<20°)
- Talus–1st metatarsal angle negative
- Tibio-talar angle negative
- Talocalcaneal index <40
### Scoring Systems
- Pirani score (0–6)
- Dimeglio score (0–20)
Management
Ponseti Method (Gold standard) - Long leg casts, weekly change - Correct cavus first
- Pure abduction with counter pressure on talar neck - Never pronate - Percutaneous
Tendo Achilles tenotomy - Denis Browne splint for maintenance
Surgical Indications - Resistant, recurrent, neglected deformity
Surgical Options - Postero-medial soft tissue release (Turco) - Cincinnati incision -
Tendon transfers - Osteotomies - Triple arthrodesis (>10–12 years)

Congenital Radio Ulnar Synostosis
Definition
Congenital fusion of proximal radius and ulna due to failure of segmentation of common
perichondrium.

Epidemiology
- Boys > girls (3:2)
- Bilateral in 80 percent
- Diagnosis often delayed (mean age 6 years)
### Clinical Features
- Painless limitation of forearm rotation
- Elbow flexion preserved

<!-- page 3 -->

- Forearm fixed in ~30° pronation
- Wrist hypermobility
Classification
Cleary and Omer - Type I: fibrous synostosis - Type II: osseous synostosis, radial head
reduced - Type III: osseous synostosis with posterior dislocation - Type IV: osseous
synostosis with anterior dislocation

Management
- Observation if functional
- Forearm derotation osteotomy (preferred)
- Goal is functional positioning, not motion restoration

Congenital Muscular Torticollis
Definition
Unilateral shortening of sternocleidomastoid muscle causing head tilt toward affected
side and chin rotation to opposite side.

Features
- Palpable SCM mass in infancy
- More common on right side
- Associated with DDH, clubfoot, plagiocephaly
Classification (Cheng)
- Sternomastoid tumor group
- Muscular torticollis group
- Postural torticollis group
Management
Non operative - Stretching exercises - Cervical orthosis - Botulinum toxin (adjunct)
Operative Indications - Resistant cases >12–18 months - Facial asymmetry,
movement restriction >30°
Surgical Techniques - Unipolar release - Bipolar release

### Postoperative Care
- Cervical collar
- Stretching and strengthening exercises

Sprengel Shoulder
Definition
Congenital elevation of scapula due to failure of caudal migration during
embryogenesis.

Epidemiology
- Most common congenital shoulder girdle anomaly
- Equal sex distribution
- Left side more common
Pathophysiology
- Arrest between 9th–12th weeks
- Muscle hypoplasia (trapezius, rhomboids, serratus anterior)
- Omovertebral bar present in ~30 percent
### Cavendish Classification
- Grade I: very mild
- Grade II: mild
- Grade III: moderate
- Grade IV: severe (near occiput)
### Associated Anomalies
- Klippel–Feil syndrome
- Scoliosis
- Rib anomalies
- Torticollis
Management
- Observation for mild cases
- Surgery between 3–6 years
### Surgical Procedures
- Woodward procedure (preferred)
- Excision of omovertebral bar
- Partial scapulectomy
Complications
- Brachial plexus injury (radial nerve most common)
- Keloid formation
- Shoulder stiffness

### Exam Pearls
- CTEV is the most common congenital foot deformity.
- Ponseti method is first line at any age if foot is supple.
- Poor brace compliance is the most common cause of relapse.
- Radio ulnar synostosis surgery restores function, not rotation.
- Forearm derotation osteotomy is preferred over synostosis excision.
- SCM release before 6–8 years improves facial remodeling.
- Sprengel shoulder most commonly treated by Woodward procedure.
- Radial nerve is most commonly affected during Sprengel surgery.
