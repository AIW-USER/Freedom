Overview of Musculoskeletal Rheumatology –
Dr. Mukul Mohindra
Overview / Key Concepts
Musculoskeletal rheumatology covers autoimmune disorders affecting spine and
peripheral joints. These disorders are broadly classified into seropositive and
seronegative spondyloarthropathies depending on whether identifiable autoantibodies
are present. Proper evaluation requires recognizing joint involvement patterns, extra
articular features, and appropriate serology to establish diagnosis.

Autoimmunity and Classification
### Seropositive Spondyloarthropathy
- Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is the prototype.
- Positive autoantibodies: Rheumatoid factor and Anti CCP.
- Autoantibodies attack citrullinated proteins in synovium.
Seronegative Spondyloarthropathy
Includes: - Ankylosing spondylitis - Psoriatic arthritis - Reactive arthritis - Enteropathic
arthritis
Characteristics: - No specific serum autoantibodies. - Strong association with HLA B27.
- Evidence of autoimmunity through defective HLA antigen expression.

### HLA Concept Simplified
- HLA molecules are present on all cells, not just leukocytes.
- HLA proteins help immune system recognize self versus non self.
- Defective HLA (for example HLA B27, DR1, DR4) triggers autoimmunity.

Pathophysiology of Rheumatoid Arthritis
Key initiating event: Citrullination of synovial HLA peptides leading to loss of immune
tolerance.

### Cellular Interactions
1. Antigen presenting cells detect abnormal HLA peptides.
2. Activate T helper cells via cytokines, especially IL 6.
3. T cells stimulate:

<!-- page 2 -->

- B cells through CD20 receptors → formation of autoantibodies and immune complexes.
  - Macrophages → release TNF alpha and IL 1.
4. Influx of neutrophils into synovium.
5. Final pathway: Janus kinase signalling, leading to inflammation and joint destruction.
### Serum Markers
- Rheumatoid factor, anti CCP.
- Acute phase reactants: ESR, CRP.

Joint Involvement Patterns
Rheumatoid Arthritis
Commonly involved joints (bilateral): - MCP, PIP - Wrist - Elbow - Knee - Ankle - MTP
Less common: - Hip - TMJ - Subtalar - Atlanto axial joint (C1 C2 instability)
Not involved: Lumbar spine and DIP joints.

### Osteoarthritis of Hand
- Nodes: Heberden (DIP), Bouchard (PIP).
- Common joint: 1st CMC.
- Radiograph: osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis, cysts (geodes).
### Psoriatic Arthritis
- Classically involves DIP joints.
- Features:
  - Sausage digits
  - Telescoping digits
  - Pencil in cup deformity
### Differentiating OA vs RA Knee
- OA: asymmetric joint space reduction, sclerosis.
- RA: symmetric joint space narrowing, erosions.

Deformities of the Hand
### Swan Neck Deformity
- PIP hyperextension, DIP flexion.
- Cause: lax volar plate at PIP, tight dorsal structures.
- DIP deformity is compensatory.

<!-- page 3 -->

- Treatment: volar plate advancement, Fowler tenotomy if required.
### Boutonniere Deformity
- PIP flexion, DIP hyperextension.
- Cause:
  - Central slip rupture.
  - Triangular ligament laxity.
  - Volar subluxation of lateral bands.
- Treatment: early central slip reconstruction; in chronic RA with arthritic joint, PIP arthrodesis.
### Detecting Early Boutonniere: Elson Test
- PIP flexed 90 degrees over table.
- Patient attempts PIP extension.
- DIP rigidity indicates central slip injury.
- Injury site: Zone 3.

Clinical Scenarios and Diagnostic Pearls
### OA vs RA Hand Stiffness
- Morning stiffness is not a reliable discriminator.
- Pattern of joint involvement is more specific than stiffness duration.
- DIP involvement rules out RA in most cases.
### Extra Articular Features
- RA: dry eye, scleritis, pericarditis, lung nodules, anemia, vasculitis.
- AS: acute uveitis.
- Reactive arthritis: urethritis, conjunctivitis.

Imaging in Spondyloarthropathies
### Ankylosing Spondylitis
- Earliest involvement: sacroiliac joints.
- Spinal radiographic features:
  - Syndesmophytes
  - Bamboo spine
  - Dagger sign (interspinous ligament ossification)
- MRI lesions:
  - Romanus lesion: shiny corner sign
  - Anderson lesion: spondylodiscitis

<!-- page 4 -->

### Differentiating AS vs DISH
- AS: marginal syndesmophytes, SI joint involvement, elevated ESR, HLA B27 positive.
- DISH: flowing calcifications, no SI involvement, normal inflammatory markers.
### Sacroiliac Joint Differential
- Osteitis condensans ilii: triangular iliac sclerosis, preserved joint space.

Medical Management Overview
NSAIDs
- First line in ankylosing spondylitis.
- Symptomatic relief only.
Corticosteroids
- Useful for flares.
- Short term courses only (less than 3 months).
### Conventional DMARDs
- Methotrexate, sulfasalazine, hydroxychloroquine, leflunomide.
- MTX: anti proliferative, cornerstone of RA therapy.
- Limited benefit in axial spondyloarthritis.
Biologic Therapy
Derived from recombinant DNA technology.
Classes: 1. TNF alpha inhibitors: infliximab, etanercept, adalimumab, golimumab. 2. IL
6 inhibitor: tocilizumab. 3. IL 1 inhibitor: anakinra. 4. CD20 inhibitor: rituximab.

### JAK Inhibitors
- Tofacitinib (oral), baricitinib, upadacitinib.
Biosimilars
Used widely due to cost effectiveness.

Treatment Pathway in Rheumatoid Arthritis
Based on Disease Activity (DAS 28)
### Low Activity
- Start DMARD monotherapy.

<!-- page 5 -->

### Moderate to High Activity
- Start DMARD combinations plus short steroids.
- If persistent activity beyond 3 months → start biologic (TNF inhibitor or non TNF agent).
- JAK inhibitor acceptable alternative.
### Pregnancy Considerations
- Safe: hydroxychloroquine, sulfasalazine.
- Avoid: methotrexate, leflunomide, biologics, JAK inhibitors.
- RA often improves during pregnancy.
Monitoring
- Baseline CBC, LFT, KFT.
- Repeat every 2 to 4 weeks initially, then every 3 months.
- TB screening before biologics.
- Viral markers: HIV, HBV, HCV.

Management of Synovitis / Monoarthritis
Approach
1. Blood markers: ESR, CRP, RF, anti CCP, HLA B27, ENA profile.
2. MRI if diagnosis unclear.
3. Joint aspiration: cell count, gram stain, AFB culture, TB PCR.
4. Synovial biopsy only if non conclusive.

### Ankylosing Spondylitis: Clinical Evaluation
- Occiput to wall test (cervical flexion).
- Chest expansion at nipple line.
- Modified Schober test: normal excursion more than 5 to 10 cm.
- SI joint tests: FABER, Gaenslen, sacral thrust.
### AS Management
- NSAIDs first line.
- DMARDs have limited role.
- Biologic therapy often started early.
- TNF inhibitors highly effective.
- For surgery, biologics must be held until completion of half life.

Faculty Quote
“Pattern recognition is the backbone of rheumatologic diagnosis. Joint involvement tells
you more than most laboratory tests ever will.”

### Exam Pearls
- MCP is the most common joint involved in RA.
- DIP involvement suggests OA or psoriatic arthritis, not RA.
- Pencil in cup deformity, sausage digits and telescoping are hallmarks of psoriatic arthritis.
- Romanus and Anderson lesions are MRI signs of ankylosing spondylitis.
- Osteitis condensans ilii shows triangular iliac sclerosis with preserved SI joint space.
- Methotrexate is the anchor DMARD in RA, but ineffective in axial SpA.
- Biologicals must be avoided during pregnancy.
- In Boutonniere deformity, Zone 3 injury is classical and Elson test is diagnostic.
- Distinguishing AS from DISH: SI involvement and marginal syndesmophytes favor AS.
