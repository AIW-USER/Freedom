Important Fracture Dislocations of Hand – Dr. Amit
Mittal
Overview
Fracture dislocations of the hand, especially around the PIP and MCP joints, demand
precise anatomical understanding and early, appropriate management to prevent long
term stiffness and deformity. Stability assessment, joint congruity, and articular surface
involvement guide treatment.
Faculty Remark: “At least two sides of the ligament box must fail for a PIP joint to
dislocate. Understand the anatomy, and the injuries will make sense.”

Anatomy Essentials
### PIP Joint – Ligament Box Concept
- Three dimensional ligament box consisting of:
  - Proper collateral ligament – primary stabilizer, inserts on volar one third of middle phalanx.
  - Accessory collateral ligament – inserts on volar plate.
  - Volar plate.
- Two sides must be disrupted for PIP dislocation.
- Proximal phalanx head has slight condylar asymmetry, creating ~9 degrees of supination during motion.
### Collateral Ligament Failure Patterns
- PIP Joint: Collateral ligament fails proximally.
- Volar plate avulses distally.
### Secondary Stabilizers
- In extension, volar plate becomes taut and resists dorsal displacement.

### Clinical Assessment & Stability Grading (PIP)
- Grade I: Pain but no laxity.
- Grade II: Laxity with firm endpoint and stable arc.
- Grade III: No firm endpoint, gross instability.
### V Sign (Dorsal Subluxation Indicator)
- Dorsal widening seen on lateral X ray.
- Indicates incomplete reduction.

## PIP Joint Dorsal Dislocations – Types
1. Type I: Hyperextension deformity, joint surfaces still touching.
2. Type II: Bayonet position.
3. Type III: Fracture dislocation (fragment visible on X ray).

## Simple vs Complex PIP Dislocations
### Simple Dorsal Dislocation
- Collateral ligament intact or minimally injured.
- Concentric reduction possible.
- Manage with dorsal block splint.
### Complex Dorsal Dislocation
- Volar plate interposed.
- Soft tissue “tendon noose” around proximal phalanx prevents reduction.
- Requires open reduction.

## Fracture Dislocations of PIP Joint
### Stability Depends on Articular Surface Involvement
- <40% volar articular surface involvement → usually stable.
- >40% involvement or comminution → unstable.
- Mechanism: Proper collateral ligament detaches with volar fragment when articular loss exceeds 40 percent.

## Management Options (PIP Fracture Dislocations)
1. Dorsal Block Splint (Stable Injuries)
Indications: - No articular incongruity. - Reduction stable in ≤30 degrees flexion. -
Collateral ligaments intact.
Protocol: - Start at 30 degrees flexion. - Increase extension by 10 degrees each week.
Avoid: - In short, swollen fingers → poor compliance. Choose extension block
pinning instead.

<!-- page 3 -->

2. Extension Block Pinning
Indication: - PIP dislocation with short, swollen finger where splint cannot be applied.
- Technique: K wire inserted obliquely between central slip and lateral bands to allow
early motion.

3. Dynamic Traction (Suzuki Frame)
Indications: - Pilon fractures. - Multiple fragments involving 30 to 50 percent articular
surface. - Joint must be reducible. - Early mobilization required.
Key Point: - Distance between proximal and distal hooks should be ≤2.5 cm to avoid
over distraction.

4. ORIF
Indication: - Single large volar fragment that can be anatomically fixed.

5. Hemihamate Arthroplasty
Indication: - Large volar lip defect involving the middle phalanx. - Chronic cases or
failed primary management. - Provides stable articular reconstruction.
Key Points: - Graft harvested from dorsal distal hamate, centered over 4th and 5th
CMC joints. - Take half of dorsal volar thickness and half radial ulnar width. - Correct
graft orientation restores volar buttress.

6. Volar Plate Arthroplasty
Indications: - Volar rim defects where grafting not ideal.
Technique Highlights: - Create transverse trough on middle phalanx base. - Mobilize
volar plate 4–6 mm distally. - Release accessory collateral ligaments for mobility. -
Central exit hole is mandatory to avoid tethering of lateral bands. - Flex DIP during
suture passage.

<!-- page 4 -->

7. Cerclage Wiring
Indication: - Severe pilon fractures with central impaction. - Holds multiple fragments
when ORIF is not possible.

Pilon Fracture (PIP)
Features
- Axial compression injury.
- Dorsal and volar fragments separate.
- Central cartilage depression.
- Widening of middle phalanx base.
Management
- Dynamic traction or arthroplasty depending on fragment size and reducibility.

MCP Joint Dorsal Dislocation
### Anatomy & Mechanism
- Collateral ligaments more taut in flexion.
- MCP volar plate attached to proximal phalanx, not metacarpal.
- Volar plate displaced dorsally during dislocation.
- Tendon “noose” around metacarpal neck prevents reduction.

Simple vs Complex MCP Dislocation
Simple
- Hyperextension deformity.
- Reducible by flexing wrist to relax flexor tendons.
Complex
- Proximal phalanx not markedly hyperextended.
- Sesamoid within joint indicates entrapped volar plate.
- Requires open reduction.

### UCL Injury – Stener Lesion (Thumb MCP)
- Adductor aponeurosis interposed between:
  - Distally avulsed UCL.

- Proximal phalanx insertion.
- Cannot heal without surgery.
Management
- Acute partial tears → splint.
- Complete tears or Stener lesion → suture anchor repair.
Chronic (>6 weeks)
- PL tendon graft reconstruction – most reliable.
- Adductor advancement is dynamic option.

### Thumb CMC Dislocation
- Usually dorsal.
- Acute cases → closed reduction + percutaneous pinning.
- Chronic instability → FCR slip reconstruction of volar oblique ligament.

Base of Thumb Fractures
### Bennett Fracture
- Single volar ulnar fragment.
- Anterior oblique ligament holds fragment.
- Metacarpal displaces proximally and dorsally.
Treatment: - Percutaneous pinning from metacarpal into trapezium. - Bennett fragment
need not be directly pinned if <2 mm step off post reduction.

### Rolando Fracture (Comminuted)
- Y or T shaped intra articular fracture.
- Requires traction, external fixation or mini T plate.
- Bone grafting often needed.

### Seymour Fracture (Pediatric)
- Open physeal fracture of distal phalanx.
- Nail plate often avulsed proximally.
- Requires:
  - Washout.
  - Matrix repair.
  - Nail plate repositioning.

<!-- page 6 -->

### Exam Pearls
- PIP dislocation: two sides of ligament box must fail.
- Proper collateral ligament is primary stabilizer.
- Articular involvement >40% → unstable fracture dislocation.
- V sign = incomplete reduction.
- Hemihamate: graft centered over 4th & 5th CMC joints.
- Suzuki frame: hook distance ≤2.5 cm.
- MCP complex dislocation → sesamoid in joint.
- Stener lesion → always surgical.
- Bennett = single fragment; Rolando = comminuted.

Final Faculty Insight: “Hand fracture dislocations demand precision. Small details
in anatomy decide whether a joint stays stable or collapses over time.”
