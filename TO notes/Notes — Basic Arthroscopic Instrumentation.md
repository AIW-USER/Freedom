Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Basic Arthroscopic Instrumentation
Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### OVERVIEW
- Understanding arthroscopic instrumentation is fundamental before learning therapeutic arthroscopy.
- This lecture covers instruments, optics, visualization systems, fluid management, energy devices, sutures, anchors, and sterilization protocols.
- Father of Sports Medicine: Galen.
- First successful arthroscopy: Kenji Takagi (1918) – for TB knee using cystoscope.
- Therapeutic arthroscopy popularized by: Masaki Watanabe – introduced triangulation technique.
- Brought to the West by: Robert Jackson (Canada, 1965 Tokyo Olympics).
- International Arthroscopy Association founded: 1974, with Watanabe as President and Jackson as founding member. Quote: “Every good arthroscopist is first a good instrument handler.” – Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### BASIC SETUP & OPERATING ROOM LAYOUT
- Monitor: ≥20-inch, at eye level of surgeon (usually 26-inch HD screen).
- Tower components:
  - Monitor, camera control unit, light source, shaver control, pump system.
- Saline irrigation bottles: 3–5 L placed at height → builds distension pressure.
  - Knee: 30 mmHg
  - Shoulder: 40–50 mmHg
  - Raising bottle 1 ft increases pressure by ~20 mmHg.

ARTHROSCOPE (TELESCOPE)
### Parts of Arthroscope
- Eyepiece → Connects to camera coupler.
- Light post → Fiber-optic light entry.
- Telescope (barrel) → Main body.
- Objective lens system → At distal tip (thin lenses + spacers). Handle carefully — pressure or impact disturbs lens alignment → blurred image.

| Movement | Description | Importance |
|---|---|---|
| Pistoning | In–out | Depth adjustment |
| Angulation | Up/down/side | Orientation change |
| Rotation | Twisting along axis | Most important – alters field of view |

### Angle of Inclination
- 0° → Straight view (spine/endoscopy).
- 30° → Standard for knee/shoulder.
- 70° → For hip/peripheral visualization. Higher inclination → wider peripheral view but central blind spot.
### Field of View (FOV)
- Apparent FOV: Depends on diameter.
- Actual FOV: Depends on diameter + angle of inclination.

LIGHT SOURCE & CAMERA SYSTEM
### Light Source
- Power: 300–350 W.
- Types: Tungsten < Halogen < Xenon < LED (best).
  - LED = cooler temperature, prevents chondral damage.
### Fiber Optic Cable
- Length: 300 cm (~3 m); Diameter: 4.8 mm.
- Structure: Core (fibers) → Cladding → Coating (insulation).
### Camera System
- Inventors: McGinty and Johnson.
- Now: 3-chip HD cameras for high resolution.

TYPES OF INSTRUMENTATION
### 1⃣ Mechanical Instruments
- Probe: Extension of surgeon’s finger; used to palpate, pull, measure (tip = 4 mm; shaft = 12 cm).
- Punch (Basket Forceps): Cuts tissue.
  - Types: Straight / Side-cutting / Up-biting.
- Grasper: Holds tissue or foreign body.
- Retriever: Pulls sutures (suture retriever).
- Switching Stick (Singer Rod): For portal shuttle (diameter 4 mm).
- Cannulas: Maintain portals; come as wash cannula (rigid) or passport cannula (flexible → allows jig passage).

### 3⃣ Motorized Instruments
- Shaver/Burr System: Control unit → tubing → handpiece → tip blade.
  - Common diameters: 3.5, 4.5, 5.5 mm.
  - Length: 120 mm (knee), 180 mm (hip).
  - RPM: Shaver – 1,000–3,000; Burr – 8,000–9,000.
  - Types of blades:
    - Standard (smooth barrel)
    - Aggressive (teeth on barrel)
  - Full-radius resector: Combines side and open-ended shaver; most used.

KNOTS IN ARTHROSCOPY
Non-sliding (Conventional)
- Square knot + half-hitch (×5) → Best security but cannot pass through cannula.
### Sliding Knots
- Used in arthroscopy to advance through cannula (one limb pulled).

| Type | Example | Property |
|---|---|---|
| Non-locking | Duncan Loop | Easy to tie; may loosen under back pressure |
| Locking – | Nicky’s Knot | Secure; complex |

Proximal
Locking – SMC (Samson Most popular, balanced
Middle Medical Center) Knot security

Definitions:
- Knot Security: Resistance to slippage under tension.
- Loop Security: Ability to resist tissue back pressure.
- Failure: Knot displacement >3 mm. All sliding knots must be backed by 3 reverse half-hitches on alternate loops for security.

### COMPLICATIONS
- Most common complication of arthroscopy: Hemarthrosis (especially after synovectomy or lateral release).
