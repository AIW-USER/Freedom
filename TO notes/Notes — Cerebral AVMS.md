Arteriovenous Malformations (AVMs)
Faculty: Dr. Himanshu Champaneri

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Cerebral Arteriovenous Malformations (AVMs) are tangles of dysplastic vessels
where arterial blood flows directly into veins without a capillary bed, leading to
high-pressure shunting and risk of hemorrhage, seizures, and neurological
deficits.
Dr. Himanshu’s Quote:
“AVM surgery is not about removing a mass — it’s about navigating through
chaos without losing the map.”
Key Features: - High-flow lesions (arterial → venous shunt) - No capillary bed →
direct arterial–venous connection - Red draining veins (due to oxygenated blood) -
Congenital origin, but can enlarge and increase flow with age

### PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
- Nidus: Central tangle of dysplastic arteries and veins.

- Feeder Arteries: Supply the nidus (often multiple).

- Draining Veins: Carry high-pressure oxygenated blood → appear red.
- Absence of capillary bed → No oxygen exchange → risk of rupture.
Hemodynamics:
- Normal arterial pressure: ~120 mmHg → capillary: ~0 mmHg → venous: 5–15 mmHg.
- In AVMs → venous side under arterial pressure → venous dilation & rupture risk.

- Posterior fossa (15%) – Brainstem and cerebellum AVMs.

higher hemorrhage risk.

### EPIDEMIOLOGY & ASSOCIATIONS
- Prevalence: ~0.14% (undetected AVMs).

- Age at diagnosis: Mean 30–35 years.

- Gender: Slight male predominance.

- Syndromic Association: Osler–Weber–Rendu Syndrome (Hereditary Hemorrhagic Telangiectasia).
Syndrome Features: - Multiple telangiectasias on lips, skin, mucosa.
- Recurrent epistaxis, GI bleeding.
- 15–20% have cerebral AVMs.

### SEIZURE PATHOGENESIS
- Gliotic, hyperexcitable cortex around nidus.

- More likely in temporal lobe or superficial cortical AVMs.

- Larger nidus (>3 cm) → higher risk.

- Post-hemorrhagic scarring → chronic epilepsy.

ASSOCIATED ANEURYSMS
% of AVMs with

| Location | Aneurysm | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Feeder artery | 75% | High-flow stress on arterial wall |
| Intranidal | 15% | Within AVM core |
| Draining vein | 10% | Rare, due to venous hypertension |

Priority of Management: - If both aneurysm + AVM present → treat aneurysm first,
as rupture risk is catastrophic.

### IMAGING & DIAGNOSIS

| Modality | Findings / Utility |
|---|---|
| CT / MRI | Detects hemorrhage, edema, mass effect |
| MRI (T2) | “Popcorn” or Medusa head appearance due to flow voids |
| CT Angiography | Defines vessel morphology; useful in acute bleed |
| MR Angiography | Noninvasive, shows nidus and feeders (≥1 mm) |
| Digital Subtraction | Gold standard – shows nidus, feeders, and |
| Angiography (DSA) | early venous drainage in arterial phase |

Characteristic Sign: Draining veins visible in arterial phase of DSA.

GRADING SYSTEMS
### Spetzler–Martin Grading System (SN Grade)

| Parameter | Criteria | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Size | <3 cm / 3–6 cm / >6 cm | 1/2/3 |
| Eloquence of location | Non-eloquent / Eloquent | 0/1 |
| Venous drainage | Superficial / Deep | 0/1 |

Grades: 1–5 (higher = higher risk).
Grade 6 = Inoperable.
Management Groups:
- Low grade (I–II): Surgical excision.
- Moderate (III): Multimodality (surgery + embolization/radiosurgery).
- High (IV–V): Conservative / selective intervention.

Lawton–Young Grading System (Modified):
Adds age and rupture status to Spetzler–Martin scale.

MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES
### 1⃣ Surgery (Microsurgical Resection)

| Indication | Features |
|---|---|
| Low-grade (I–II) AVMs | Superficial, non-eloquent, accessible |
| Ruptured AVMs | Done with hematoma evacuation |
| Advantages | Immediate cure, 100% removal possible |
| Disadvantages | Risk of bleeding, deficits if eloquent area |

Technique: - Identify feeder → clip → remove nidus → close draining vein last.

2⃣ Stereotactic Radiosurgery (SRS – Gamma Knife / CyberKnife)
Indication | Lesion <3 cm, deep-seated, or in eloquent area |
Advantages | Non-invasive, outpatient, helps shrink lesion (80% obliteration in 2–3 yrs) |
Disadvantages | Latency period (1–3 yrs), 70–80% success, radiation necrosis risk |

<!-- page 6 -->

Mechanism: Focused high-dose radiation → endothelial damage → vessel thrombosis
→ nidus closure.

3⃣ Endovascular Embolization
Indication | Adjunct before surgery/SRS or palliative for inoperable AVMs |
Material | Onyx / N-butyl cyanoacrylate (NBCA) glue |
Advantages | Reduces flow, facilitates later surgery |
Disadvantages | Incomplete occlusion, recurrence, multiple sessions needed |
Goal: Obliterate feeders and reduce shunt flow without ischemic injury.

### COMPLICATIONS
- Normal perfusion pressure breakthrough (NPPB) – hyperemia after resection.

- Rebleeding / rupture during or after treatment.

- Seizures (peri- or post-op).

- Radiation necrosis or cyst formation (SRS).

- Residual nidus → recurrence.

### FOLLOW-UP

| Treatment | Imaging Follow-up |
|---|---|
| Surgery | Post-op DSA → at 1 & 5 years |
| SRS | MRI every 6 months × 3 yrs → confirm by DSA after obliteration |
| Embolization | MRI + angiography annually |

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- AVM = most common vascular malformation.

- Classical MRI: “Popcorn” / “Medusa head” appearance.

- Most common artery: MCA; most common lobe: Parietal.

- Posterior fossa AVMs: 15%.

<!-- page 7 -->

- Gold standard diagnosis: DSA.

- Spetzler–Martin Grade 6: Inoperable.

- Treatment of choice (low-grade): Surgical excision.

- CyberKnife / Gamma Knife: For small, deep AVMs.

- Endovascular embolization: For high-grade or palliative cases.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- AVMs = congenital high-flow shunts between artery & vein.

- Hemorrhage is the most common presentation.

- Risk factors for bleed: Deep drainage, prior bleed, large nidus, female sex.

- Spetzler–Martin grade guides management strategy.

- Surgery cures low-grade AVMs; SRS & embolization used for high-grade or deep lesions.

- Goal: Prevent rebleed, control seizures, preserve neurological function.
