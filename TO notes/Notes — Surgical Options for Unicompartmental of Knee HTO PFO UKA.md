Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Surgical Options for Unicompartmental
Osteoarthritis of the Knee (HTO · PFO · UKA)
Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

OVERVIEW
This lecture discusses the joint-preserving and partial replacement options for
isolated medial compartment osteoarthritis of the knee—namely High Tibial
Osteotomy (HTO), Proximal Fibular Osteotomy (PFO), and Unicompartmental
Knee Arthroplasty (UKA). These procedures aim to unload the medial compartment
and delay or avoid total knee replacement, depending on age, activity, and deformity
severity.
Quote: “Correct the mechanics before replacing the joint.” – Dr. Mukul
Mohindra

HIGH TIBIAL OSTEOTOMY (HTO)
### Biomechanical Goal
- Unload the medial compartment by correcting varus malalignment.
- Transfer load laterally → relieve pain and slow OA progression.
Introduced by: Jackson & Waugh (1961)
Popularized by: Coventry (1965)

### Ideal Candidate
- Age < 60 years
- Active lifestyle, BMI < 30 ( < 90 kg )
- Ahlbäck Grade I–II OA
- Intact ACL & Lateral Compartment
- Flexion > 100°
Contraindications
- Age > 60 years / Sedentary
- Advanced OA / bone loss >15×15 mm
- Concave medial plateau (“Teeter-Totter” effect – Kettlekamp)
- Lateral subluxation >10 mm
- Tibial slope >10° (uncorrected)
- Symptomatic PFJ OA
- Flexion <90° or contracture >10°
- RA / Nicotine use / Obesity

### Radiographic Planning
- Goal: Weight-bearing line at 62.5% of tibial width (Dugdale).
- Scanogram: Hip–knee–ankle alignment.
- Wedge calculation: (Base (mm) = Tibial width × 0.02 × Angle correction (°)) ≈ 1 mm wedge ≈ 1° correction for 57 mm tibia.
Tibial slope: Maintain <10°; anterior gap ≈ ½ posterior gap.
Patella Baja common post-op → stabilizing effect.

<!-- page 3 -->

### Surgical Highlights
- Incision: 5 cm vertical; between tibial tuberosity & posteromedial margin.
- Expose: MCL & detach pes tendons.
- Osteotomy: 3.5–4 cm below joint line → aim toward fibular head → preserve 7–10 mm hinge laterally.
- Fixation: Tomofix / Puddu plate; Graft: Iliac crest (≥10 mm opening).
- Rehab: Early ROM + isometrics → WB at 6 wks → full WB at 3 mo.

PROXIMAL FIBULAR OSTEOTOMY (PFO)
### Concept & Rationale
- Based on non-uniform settlement theory:
  - Medial tibia = cancellous → collapses early.
  - Lateral fibula = rigid cortical support → varus progression.
- Removing fibular support → allows even tibial plateau loading → pain relief.
Introduced: Xia et al. (2014, China)

### Surgical Steps
- 1–2 cm fibular segment removed 7–8 cm below fibular head.
- Approach: Posterior (between peroneus & soleus).
- Key precaution: Keep incision posterior to coronal plane → avoid common peroneal nerve.

### Advantages vs HTO

| PFO | HTO |
|---|---|
| Simple, quick, inexpensive | Technically demanding |
| No implant / graft | Needs plate & graft |
| Early recovery | Delayed WB |
| Useful in elderly / high-risk | Ideal for young, active |

Limitations: Variable outcomes, possible CPN injury, mild correction only (5–10°).

UNICOMPARTMENTAL KNEE ARTHROPLASTY (UKA)
Concept
- Replaces only the medial or lateral compartment, preserving cruciates & native kinematics.
- Provides near-normal gait and proprioception.
### HTO vs UKA:
- HTO: Younger, heavier, varus >10°.
- UKA: Older, lower demand, varus <5°.

Indications
- Isolated unicompartmental OA.
- Varus <5°, MCL intact, ACL functional.
- Weight <80 kg ideal.
- No lateral or patellofemoral degeneration.
Contraindications
- Fixed varus >10°, MCL contracture.
- ACL deficiency (posterior subluxation).
- Inflammatory arthritis / RA.
- BMI >30.

### Implant Design Features
- Femoral: Lug + fin fixation; posterior condyle wide; anterior taper to avoid patellar impingement.
- Tibial: Metal-backed polyethylene ≥6 mm thick; 75–80% coverage.
  - Mobile bearing: Less wear, higher skill requirement.
  - Fixed bearing: Simplified, less dislocation risk.

### Surgical Pearls
- No overcorrection or ligament release.
- Use gap balancing technique – femoral jig on tibial spacer.
- Spacer should move with light two-finger resistance.
- Cut perpendicular to tibial surface.
Tibial Cut Example: Bone loss (2 mm) + cartilage (3 mm) = 5 mm.
Implant thickness (8 mm) → resect 3 mm bone.

Complications

| Stage | Complications |
|---|---|
| Early (Intra-op) | Tibial plateau fracture |
| Mid/Late | OA progression, PE wear, subsidence, loosening PE dislocation (mobile bearing) PF OA from overcorrection |

### Clinical Pearls
- Medial Opening Wedge HTO = gold standard for joint preservation.
- PFO = simple, cost-effective option for mild varus OA.
- UKA = best for elderly with isolated unicompartmental disease.
- Preserve alignment, ACL, & proprioception whenever possible.
