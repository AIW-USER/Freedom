Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Injuries of Occipito-Cervical Region
Faculty: Dr. C S Vishnu Prasath

### OVERVIEW
- The occipito-cervical (OC) junction is a highly mobile yet inherently unstable segment.
- Responsible for 30–40% of neck flexion-extension (C0–C1) and ~50% of rotation (C1–C2).
- Stability depends primarily on ligamentous structures, not bone.
- Common pathologies: Dislocations, condylar fractures, Jefferson (C1) fractures, odontoid fractures, Hangman’s fractures. “Maximum motion means minimum stability – and that’s what makes the occipito-cervical junction treacherous in trauma.” – Dr. C S Vishnu Prasath

### ANATOMY & LIGAMENTOUS STRUCTURES

| Ligament | Attachment | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Alar ligament | Tip of odontoid → occipital condyles | Restrains rotation & lateral bending |
| Transverse ligament | Lateral mass of C1 → around dens | Holds dens against anterior arch of atlas |
| Tectorial membrane | Continuation of PLL to foramen magnum | Reinforces posterior stability |

Remember the two key stabilizers:
- Alar ligament → C0–C1 stability
- Transverse ligament → C1–C2 stability

### OCCIPITO-CERVICAL DISLOCATION (OCD)
- Mechanism: High-velocity trauma (RTAs, pedestrian impact).
- Mortality: Very high; most patients die on scene.
- Seen in 5–12% of traffic fatalities.

Diagnosis
  - X-ray / CT: Non-congruent C0–C1 articulation, widened OC gap.
  - Power’s Ratio = AB / CD
  - A = basion → posterior arch of C1
  - C = opisthion → anterior arch of C1
  - Normal < 1.0; >1 suggests anterior dislocation.
Management
  - Type I–III (stable survivors):
  - Definitive treatment: Occipito-cervical fusion (OCF) with plate, occipital bolts, and C2/C3 screws.
  - Goal: Restore alignment & prevent late instability.
  - Majority: Fatal before hospital arrival.

ATLAS (C1) FRACTURE – JEFFERSON FRACTURE
Mechanism: Axial loading → “hoop stress” → burst of C1 ring.
  - Accounts for 3–13% of cervical fractures.
  - Often associated with odontoid or Hangman’s fracture (40–50%).

Diagnosis
- Open-mouth (odontoid) view / CT: Displacement of lateral masses of C1 from odontoid.

Treatment
- <8 mm: Rigid collar / Halo vest.
- >8 mm: C1–C2 fusion or occipito-cervical fixation.
- Posterior C1–C2 fixation eliminates rotation (~50% of neck motion lost). Newer techniques include anterior C1 ring fixation to preserve rotation.

### Surgical Options
  - C2 pars screw fixation (direct pars repair).
  - Anterior C2–C3 plating (ACDF) – preferred by many orthopedic surgeons.
  - Posterior C1–C3 fusion – for neglected / irreducible cases. Key rule: Do not apply traction in Type IIA (risk of cord stretch injury).

### OCCIPITO-CERVICAL FIXATION PRINCIPLES
  - Construct: Occipital plate + cervical screws (C2 pedicle / pars / lateral mass).
  - Anchorage points:
  - C1: Lateral mass screw.
  - C2: Pedicle, pars, or translaminar screw.
  - Alternative: If vertebral artery high-riding → translaminar screw.
  - Bone grafting: Essential for fusion. Goal: Stable fixation while preserving as much motion as possible.

### ASSOCIATED CONDITIONS
- Hypoglossal nerve palsy: Dysarthria, tongue deviation (Type III condyle fracture).
- Wallenberg’s syndrome: Ipsilateral facial sensory loss, contralateral body pain-temp loss, ataxia.
- Post-traumatic torticollis: From C1–C2 rotatory subluxation.
- C1–C2 TB / infection: May mimic instability – requires fusion + ATT.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- C0–C1: Flexion-extension; C1–C2: Rotation.
- Alar & transverse ligaments = key stabilizers.
- Power’s ratio >1 → anterior OC dislocation.
- Type III condyle fracture → unstable, needs halo.
- Jefferson’s = C1 burst; Odontoid II = non-union prone.
- >8 mm lateral mass gap = transverse ligament rupture.
- Odontoid II (anterior screw) preserves motion.
- Hangman’s IIa → NO traction.
- C1 lateral mass screw = anchor point; C2 pars screw = standard.
- Translaminar C2 screw → when artery high-riding.
- OC fusion reserved for irreducible / unstable cases.
