Fracture Neck Femur in Children & SCFE –
Dr. Rahul Upadhyay
Overview / Key Concepts
Fracture neck of femur in children and Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis (SCFE) are
pediatric hip emergencies with a high risk of avascular necrosis, deformity, and
early osteoarthritis.
While fracture neck femur represents an acute vascular insult, SCFE is a chronic
physeal shear disorder of adolescence. Early diagnosis, correct classification, and
appropriate fixation determine long-term hip survival.

Part A – Fracture Neck of Femur in Children
Epidemiology
- Rare injury (<1% of pediatric fractures)
- High-energy trauma common cause
- AVN risk higher than adults

### Anatomy and Blood Supply
- Primary blood supply: Medial femoral circumflex artery
- Retinacular vessels easily disrupted
- Tamponade from intracapsular hematoma worsens ischemia
Any delay in reduction increases AVN risk
### Delbet Classification

| Type | Location | AVN Risk |
|---|---|---|
| I | Transepiphyseal | Very high (≈100%) |
| II | Transcervical | High |
| III | Cervicotrochanteric | Moderate |
| IV | Intertrochanteric | Low |

### Management Principles
- Emergency surgery
- Gentle reduction under anesthesia

- Capsular decompression recommended
- Stable internal fixation
### Fixation Options
- Cannulated cancellous screws
- Smooth pins in very young children
- Avoid multiple passes across physis

Complications
- Avascular necrosis
- Coxa vara
- Non-union
- Premature physeal closure
- Limb length discrepancy

Part B – Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis (SCFE)
Definition
SCFE is a condition where metaphysis displaces anterosuperiorly while the
epiphysis remains in the acetabulum.
“It is the metaphysis that moves, not the epiphysis.”

Epidemiology
- Age: 10–16 years
- Boys > girls (2:1)
- Left side more common
- Bilateral involvement: 25–40%

Pathophysiology
- Weak hypertrophic zone of physis
- Thinning of perichondrial ring
- Increased shear forces due to:
  - Obesity
  - Vertical neck–shaft angle
  - Femoral retroversion

### Risk Factors
- Obesity (most common)
- Hypothyroidism (most common endocrine cause)
- Renal osteodystrophy
- Growth hormone disorders
- Prior radiotherapy
SCFE in children <10 years or thin children warrants endocrine workup
### Clinical Features
- Groin, thigh, or knee pain (referred pain)
- Limp or antalgic gait
- Externally rotated limb
- Loss of hip internal rotation
- Drehmann sign: Obligatory external rotation during hip flexion

Radiological Evaluation
### Mandatory Views
- AP pelvis
- Frog-leg lateral view
### Key Radiological Signs
- Klein’s line (Trethowan sign) does not intersect epiphysis
- Blanch sign of Steel (chronic slip)
- Widened and irregular physis
- Broken Shenton line

Southwick Slip Angle (Most Important)
Measurement
- Draw line along epiphysis
- Draw perpendicular to epiphysis
- Measure angle with femoral shaft
- Slip angle = affected side − normal side
Normal Values (for bilateral slips)
- AP view: 145°
- Frog-leg view: 10°

<!-- page 4 -->

### Severity Grading

| Grade | Slip Angle |
|---|---|
| Mild | <30° |
| Moderate | 30–60° |
| Severe | >60° |

Stability Classification (Most Prognostic)
### Loder Classification

| Type | Weight Bearing | AVN Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Stable | Able to bear weight | <10% |
| Unstable | Unable to bear weight | Up to 40% |

Stability predicts AVN risk better than slip angle
Management of SCFE
### General Principles
- Treat as orthopedic emergency
- No forceful reduction
- Prevent further slip

### In-situ Fixation (Gold Standard)
- Indicated for:
  - Stable slips
  - Mild to moderate slips
  - Pre-slip stage
### Technical Points
- Single partially threaded screw sufficient
- At least 5 threads across physis
- Screw tip ≥5 mm from subchondral bone
- Use approach–withdrawal technique

Reduction
- Gentle positioning only
- Forceful reduction increases AVN risk

<!-- page 5 -->

### Open Procedures (Selected Cases)
- Modified Dunn procedure
- Surgical hip dislocation
- Indicated for unstable or severe slips

Contralateral Hip Prophylactic Fixation
### Absolute Indications
- Endocrinopathies
- Renal osteodystrophy
### Relative Indications
- Age <10 years
- Obese adolescent
- Open triradiate cartilage
- High posterior physeal slope (>14°)

### Complications of SCFE
- Avascular necrosis
- Chondrolysis
- Slip progression
- Femoroacetabular impingement
- Early osteoarthritis
- Limb length discrepancy

Faculty Quote
“The most important prognostic factor in SCFE is not the slip angle, but whether the
child can bear weight.” – Dr. Rahul Upadhyay

### Exam Pearls
- SCFE is metaphyseal displacement, not epiphyseal
- Knee pain in adolescents may indicate hip pathology
- AP pelvis + frog-leg lateral are gold standard views
- Loder classification best predicts AVN
- In-situ single screw fixation is sufficient
- Forceful reduction markedly increases AVN risk
- Always assess need for contralateral hip fixation
