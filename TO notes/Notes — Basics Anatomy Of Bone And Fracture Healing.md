Basics Anatomy of Bone & Fracture Healing – Dr. Daivik T Shetty

### Key Concepts / Overview
- Bone is a composite and dynamic form of specialized connective tissue that is anisotropic.
- It performs five key functions – Posture, Protection, Propulsion, Proprioception, and Production.
- Strong in compression, weak in tension, weakest in shear.
- Understanding bone biology and healing mechanisms is vital for managing fractures and optimizing fixation strategies.
Dr. Daivik’s Quote:
“Fracture healing is like nature’s magic — our job as orthopedicians is only to align it;
the body takes care of the rest.”

Periosteum
- Two layers:
  - Outer fibrous layer – vascular and protective.
  - Inner cambium layer – osteogenic; key source of progenitor cells during fracture healing.
- Sharpey’s fibers anchor the periosteum to bone.
- Absent over articular surfaces and sesamoid bones (e.g., patella).

### Bone Cell Biology

| Cell Type | Origin | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Osteoblast | Mesenchymal stem cell | Matrix synthesis (Type I collagen, ALP, Osteocalcin) |
| Osteocyte | Trapped osteoblast (90% of bone cells) | Maintains bone, regulates Ca/PO₄; stimulated by calcitonin |
| Bone lining cell | Inactive osteoblast | Reactivates during remodeling |
| Osteoclast | Monocyte/macro phage lineage | Bone resorption via RANK–RANKL–OPG system |

### Osteoblast Differentiation Pathway
- RUNX2, BMP, β-catenin/WNT, Core binding protein, PDGF, IGF – promote differentiation.
- Cathepsin K – not involved (osteoclastic enzyme).
Toxic to osteoblasts: Hydrogen peroxide, Betadine, Bacitracin.

Fracture Healing
### Perren’s Strain Theory

| Strain (%) | Tissue Type / Healing |
|---|---|
| <2% | Primary (direct) bone healing |
| 2–10% | Secondary (callus) healing |
| >17% | Fibrous union |
| ~100% | Granulation tissue |

Mnemonic: “Less strain, more bone; more strain, more fibrous.”
Types of Bone Healing
### Primary (Direct) Healing
- Seen with absolute stability (e.g., compression plating).
- No callus formation. Healing by cutting cones (osteoclast + osteoblast unit).
- Gap healing – small rigid gap; transverse woven bone replaced by lamellar bone.
- Contact healing – direct osteonal bridging.
### Secondary (Indirect) Healing
- Seen with relative stability (e.g., IM nailing, cast, bridge plating).
- Callus formation present.
- Sequence: Hematoma → Soft callus → Hard callus → Remodeling.
- Seen in locked plates (strain 2–10%).

| Comparison | Primary Healing | Secondary Healing |
|---|---|---|
| Stability | Absolute | Relative |
| Callus | Absent | Present |
| Strain | <2% | 2–10% |
| Mechanism | Cutting cones | Endochondral ossification |

### Cancellous Bone Healing
- Minimal callus; heals via creeping substitution (direct osteoblastic activity).
- If gap exists → endochondral callus formation.

### Stages of Fracture Healing
Stage Events
1. Reactive (Inflammatory) Hematoma formation, platelet aggregation → PDGF & cytokines → chemotaxis, angiogenesis, osteoinduction
2. Reparative Formation of soft → hard callus via intramembranous (periosteal) and endochondral ossification
3. Remodeling Woven → lamellar bone (Wolff’s Law: bone adapts to stress lines)

Wolff’s Law: Bone remodels along stress lines – osteoclastic activity on tension side,
osteoblastic on compression side.

### Exam / Viva Pearls
- Law of ossification – Fibula is the exception.
- Most abundant non-collagenous protein: Osteocalcin.
- Weakest physis zone: Hypertrophic zone.
- Porosity: Cortical 5–10%, Cancellous 75–95%.
- Cortical remodeling: via Cutting cones.
- Creeping substitution: Mechanism of cancellous bone healing.
- Primary healing: Compression plate; Secondary: Locking plate.
- High strain in small gap; low strain in large gap.
