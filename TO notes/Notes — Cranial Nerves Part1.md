OVERVIEW
Cranial Nerves: 12 pairs of nerves that arise directly from the brain (not the spinal cord)
and supply structures of the head, neck, and special sense organs.

I. OLFACTORY NERVE (CN I)
Type: Purely Sensory (Smell)
Components:
- Olfactory Epithelium
- Olfactory Bulb
- Olfactory Tract
- Olfactory Striae
- Olfactory Cortex

### Olfactory Epithelium

| Cell Type | Function |
|---|---|
| Olfactory receptor cells | First-order bipolar sensory neurons |
| Supporting cells | Structural and metabolic support |
| Basal cells | Regenerative progenitor cells |

- Located in upper nasal cavity, beneath the cribriform plate.

- Receptor axons pierce cribriform plate → form olfactory filaments (12–16 bundles) → synapse in olfactory bulb.
### Olfactory Pathway
1. Olfactory epithelium → bipolar receptor neurons (1st order)

2. Olfactory bulb → mitral cells (2nd order)

3. Olfactory tract divides into:
  - Lateral striae → Primary olfactory cortex (uncus)

  - Medial striae → via anterior commissure to opposite side

  - Intermediate striae → olfactory tubercle

|---|---|---|
| Primary olfactory | Medial temporal lobe (uncus) | Smell perception |

Unique Features: - Only two-order neuron system.
- Does not relay through thalamus (exception among sensory systems).
- Linked with limbic system → emotional & memory association with smell.
Mnemonic: “Smell skips the thalamus.”
II. OPTIC NERVE (CN II)
Type: Sensory – Vision

### Components of the Visual System
- Retina → Optic Nerve → Optic Chiasma → Optic Tract → Lateral Geniculate Body → Optic Radiations → Visual Cortex (Area 17)
### Retina Layers
- Photoreceptors: Rods (brightness) and Cones (color).

- First-order neurons: Bipolar cells.

- Second-order neurons: Ganglion cells (axons form optic nerve).

- Optic Disc: Site where optic nerve exits; no photoreceptors (blind spot).

### Optic Pathway

| Structure | Description |
|---|---|
| Optic nerve | Formed by ganglion cell axons |
| Optic chiasma | Nasal fibers cross; temporal fibers remain ipsilateral |
| Optic tract | Carries contralateral visual field |
| Lateral Geniculate Body (LGB) | 3rd order neurons; part of thalamus |
| Optic radiations | Axons to visual cortex via temporal & parietal lobes |

### Important Terms

| Term | Description |
|---|---|
| Meyer’s Loop | Inferior fibers looping around temporal horn; lesion → pie in the sky defect |
| Von Willebrand’s | Inferior nasal fibers crossing into opposite |
| Knee | optic nerve; lesion → junctional scotoma |
| Macular Vision | Sharpest vision; represented at occipital pole |

### Light Reflex Pathway
- Retina → Optic nerve → Optic tract → Superior colliculus → Edinger-Westphal nucleus → Oculomotor nerve → Ciliary ganglion → Sphincter pupillae.

- Consensual response: Both pupils constrict when one eye is stimulated.
### Accommodation Reflex Pathway
- Retina → Visual cortex → Frontal eye field → Edinger-Westphal nucleus → Ciliary muscle → Lens thickening & pupillary constriction.
### Unique Features
- Not a true peripheral nerve – an outgrowth of forebrain.

- Covered by meninges, no neurilemma, myelinated by oligodendrocytes. Clinical tip: Optic nerve lesion → monocular blindness; Chiasmal lesion → bitemporal hemianopia.

III. OCULOMOTOR NERVE (CN III)
Type: Motor (GSE + GVE)

Nuclei
- Oculomotor nucleus – Motor to extraocular muscles.

- Edinger-Westphal nucleus – Parasympathetic to ciliary ganglion.

### LR6 SO4 Rule
- Lateral Rectus (LR) → CN VI (Abducens)

- Superior Oblique (SO) → CN IV (Trochlear)

- All others → CN III
### Lesion Effects (3rd Nerve Palsy)

| Finding | Cause |
|---|---|
| Eye down & out | Unopposed action of LR & SO |
| Ptosis | Paralysis of LPS |
| Mydriasis | Loss of parasympathetic to sphincter pupillae |
| Loss of accommodation | Ciliary muscle paralysis |

### Clinical Correlation
- Commonly compressed between PCA and SCA (aneurysm/herniation).

- Pupil-involving 3rd nerve palsy → surgical emergency.

IV. TROCHLEAR NERVE (CN IV)
Type: Motor – supplies Superior Oblique
- Only cranial nerve emerging dorsally from brainstem.

- Nucleus in midbrain (at inferior colliculus level).

- Fibers cross to opposite side before emerging.

- Enters orbit via superior orbital fissure (outside annulus of Zinn).
Action: Intorsion and depression in adduction.
Lesion: Eye elevated & extorted → vertical diplopia (worse on looking down stairs).

VI. ABDUCENS NERVE (CN VI)
Type: Motor – supplies Lateral Rectus
- Nucleus in pons (beneath facial colliculus).

- Fibers pass through petroclival ligament of Gruber → Cavernous sinus → Superior orbital fissure.
Action: Abduction of eyeball.
Lesion: Medial squint (inability to abduct eye).

### CONJUGATE GAZE & MLF CONNECTION
- Medial Longitudinal Fasciculus (MLF): Connects III (Oculomotor) and VI (Abducens) nuclei for coordinated eye movement.

- Enables conjugate gaze → both eyes move simultaneously in same direction. Example: Looking right → right lateral rectus (CN VI) + left medial rectus (CN III) activated together.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- CN I – Smell; only sensory pathway not relaying through thalamus.

- CN II – Vision; fibers cross at chiasma; Meyer’s loop = pie in the sky.

- CN III – Eye movements + Pupil constriction; lesion → eye down & out.

- CN IV – Superior oblique; only dorsal emerging nerve.

- CN VI – Lateral rectus; lesion → medial squint.

- MLF connects CN III & VI for conjugate gaze. Dr. Himanshu’s Closing Note: “Once you understand how the eyes move together, you understand the orchestra of the brainstem.”
