Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Early Onset
Scoliosis
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Early Onset Scoliosis (EOS) refers to any spinal deformity occurring before 10 years
of age.
Earlier, deformity before 5 years was termed EOS, but treatment principles for <5 and
<10 years are similar — hence the expanded definition.
Why Important:
At this young age, thoracic cage and lung growth are still ongoing. Early deformity →
restricted thoracic volume → restrictive lung disease.
Dr. Vishnu’s Quote:
“Adolescent scoliosis changes appearance — early onset scoliosis changes
physiology.”
### Etiological Types: 1. Idiopathic
2. Congenital
3. Neuromuscular
4. Syndromic

### IDIOPATHIC SCOLIOSIS – SUBTYPES

| Type | Age of Onset |
|---|---|
| Infantile | 0–3 years |
| Juvenile | 4–9 years |
| Adolescent | 10–20 years |

Infantile and Juvenile idiopathic scoliosis fall under Early Onset Scoliosis.
Key Concern:
- EOS → deformity of coronal, sagittal, and rotational planes.
- Rib deformity + rotation → reduced thoracic volume → impaired lung expansion.

### PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
- EOS is three-dimensional: lateral curve + rotation + rib deformity.

- Rib crowding → thoracic volume loss → restrictive pulmonary disease.

- Early deformity (<5 yrs) → decreased alveolar growth → irreversible cardiorespiratory limitation.
### Thoracic & Lung Development

| Age | % Thoracic Growth | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Birth | 6% | Small thoracic cavity |
| 5 years | 30% | Rapid expansion phase |
| 10 years | 50% | “Golden period” for thoracic growth |

10–Maturity Volume doubles

Alveolar Growth:
- 10× increase in alveoli from birth to 4 years.
- Deformity before 5 years → irreversible restrictive pattern.

ETIOLOGICAL TYPES
### 1⃣ Idiopathic EOS
- No vertebral or neurological abnormality.

- Classified by age: infantile / juvenile.

- Assessed by Mehta’s Rib–Vertebral Angle Difference (RVAD) & Rib Phase.
### 2⃣ Congenital EOS
- Defect in formation or segmentation.

- Common types: hemivertebra, wedge, bar, block vertebra.

- Worst Prognosis: Unilateral bar with contralateral hemivertebra.

- Associated anomalies: fused ribs → thoracic volume depletion.
### 3⃣ Neuromuscular EOS
- Due to muscle imbalance (e.g., CP, SMA).

<!-- page 3 -->

- Rapid progression → early intervention needed.
### 4⃣ Syndromic EOS
- Seen in NF1, Marfan, Arthrogryposis multiplex congenita.

- Dystrophic curves: sharp, angular, thin pedicles → fixation difficulty.

### CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE
- EOS affects spinal alignment + thoracic growth + lung maturity.

- Severe deformity → disabling dyspnea, recurrent infections, cardiorespiratory failure.

- Hence early identification and timely intervention are key.

RADIOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT
### 1⃣ Cobb’s Angle
- <20° → Observation

- 25° → Intervention threshold
### 2⃣ Rib Phase (Mehta’s)

| Phase | Description | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Convex rib not overlapping vertebral body | Stable / Observe |
| Phase 2 | Convex rib overlaps vertebral body | Progressive – Intervene |

### 3⃣ Rib–Vertebral Angle Difference (RVAD)
- Measure angles of convex and concave ribs.

- RVAD > 20° → High risk of progression.
### 4⃣ Risser’s Grading (Iliac Apophysis)

| Grade | Description | Growth Stage |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | None visible | Immature |
| 1 | 25% ossification | Early growth |
| 2 | 50% | Rapid growth |
| 3 | 75% | Slowing |

| Grade | Description | Growth Stage |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 100% | Nearly mature |
| 5 | Fusion complete | Mature |

MANAGEMENT PROTOCOL
### Observation (Cobb <20°, RVAD <20°)
- Radiographic follow-up every 6 months.

- Intervene if:
  - Progression ≥10°/6 months OR ≥20°/year.

  - Transition to Phase 2 rib head.
### Casting (25°–40° Curves)
- Mehta’s EDF (Elongation–Derotation–Flexion) Cast.

- Applied under GA with traction.

- Reapplied every 6 months.

- Corrects deformity via rib pressure, not spinal pressure.

### CONGENITAL SCOLIOSIS – PROGNOSIS

| Type | Progression Potential |
|---|---|
| Fully Segmented Hemivertebra | Highest |
| Semi-segmented | Moderate |
| Incarcerated / Non-segmented | Minimal |
| Unilateral Bar + Opposite Hemi | Worst Combination |

Associated Thoracic Anomalies:
- Fused ribs → volume depletion deformities (Type 1–3B).
- Type 3A & 3B → worst outcomes.

### SURGICAL OPTIONS SUMMARY

| Indication | Procedure |
|---|---|
| Progressive deformity <40° | Casting ± Bracing |
| >40° flexible deformity | Growth rods / tethering / guided growth |
| Congenital anomaly with bar + hemi | Hemi-vertebrectomy / convex epiphysiodesis |
| Rib fusion deformities | Thoracic expansion (VEPTR) |

### VEPTR (Vertical Expandable Prosthetic Titanium Rib)
- Used for thoracic volume depletion with rib fusion.

- Allows lung and chest cavity expansion.

- Device lengthened periodically.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- EOS = scoliosis onset <10 yrs.

- Early deformity → restrictive lung disease.

- Golden period for lung growth: birth–8 years.

- RVAD >20° or Phase 2 rib → intervene.

<!-- page 6 -->

- Casting = first-line correction method for 25°–40° curves.

- Fusionless surgery = mainstay for >40° deformities.

- Avoid early fusion → prevents thoracic growth.

- VEPTR indicated for fused ribs with thoracic insufficiency.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- EOS = scoliosis <10 yrs; affects both spine and lung growth.

- Identify early using RVAD & rib phase (Mehta’s).

- Observe mild curves; cast 25–40°; operate >40° (fusionless).

- Congenital scoliosis = segmental/formation defects; worst = unilateral bar + contralateral hemi.

- Thoracic expansion devices (VEPTR) for volume depletion deformities.

- Aim: Preserve spinal growth, maintain thoracic volume, and prevent respiratory compromise.
