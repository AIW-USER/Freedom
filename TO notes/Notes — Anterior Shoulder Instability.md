Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Anterior Shoulder Instability
Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### OVERVIEW
- Anterior shoulder instability = inability of the humeral head to stay centered in the glenoid fossa throughout motion.
- Instability ≠ Laxity ≠ Dislocation.
  - Laxity: Increased translation, no symptoms.
  - Instability: Laxity + symptoms.
  - Dislocation: Complete loss of articulation.
- Most common type of shoulder instability due to the anatomy — large head, shallow socket, and soft-tissue dependence. Mnemonic: “Born loose, Torn loose, Worn loose.” Congenital laxity, acute traumatic tear, or repetitive microtrauma.

STABILIZERS OF THE SHOULDER
### Static Stabilizers
- Glenoid labrum – deepens socket, increases concavity.
- Glenohumeral ligaments (SGHL, MGHL, IGHL).
- Negative intra-articular pressure (~–42 cm H₂O).
### Dynamic Stabilizers
- Rotator cuff, long head of biceps, scapular muscles.
Functional Role by Abduction Angle
Primary

| Position | Ligament | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 0° | SGHL | Prevents inferior translation |
| 0–45° | MGHL | Resists anterior translation |
| >45° (ABER position) | IGHL | Main stabilizer; acts as a hammock |

IGHL complex (anterior band) = key restraint in anterior instability.

### EPIDEMIOLOGY & RISK FACTORS
- Common in young males, contact athletes, and <30 years.
- Recurrence high in:

- <30 years age
  - Contact/overhead sports
  - Generalized hyperlaxity (positive Beighton)
Recurrence after first dislocation = up to 80% in young athletes.

### CLINICAL FEATURES
- History of throwing or punching movement → sudden pain/dislocation.
- Sense of shoulder “slipping out.”
- Apprehension in abduction + external rotation.
- May report “unstable painful shoulder” (Boyd): pain without frank dislocation but labral tear present.

IMAGING IN ANTERIOR INSTABILITY
### Stepwise Approach
  1. X-ray → baseline; detect fractures or bone loss.
  2. MRI → labral and capsular lesions.
  3. CT (3D) → quantify glenoid bone loss (if suspected).

MRI INTERPRETATION
### Axial Sections (Identify by Coracoid)
- Coracoid = 12–2 o’clock.
- Bankart Lesion: 3–6 o’clock (anteroinferior labrum detachment).
### Variants of Labral Tears

| Lesion | Description |
|---|---|
| Soft Tissue Bankart | Labral detachment only |
| Bony Bankart | Anteroinferior glenoid rim fracture |
| GLAD (Glenolabral | Labral tear + cartilage defect |

### Articular Disruption)

| Perthes Lesion | Medial periosteum stripped but intact |
|---|---|
| ALPSA Lesion | Labrum displaced medially with intact periosteum |
| HAGL Lesion | Humeral avulsion of IGHL |
| Buford Complex | Normal variant – absent anterosuperior labrum (1–3 o’clock) + thick MGHL |

Mnemonic: Bankart = Labrum; HAGL = Humerus.
### MRI Signs
- Hill–Sachs: Posterior-superior humeral head flattening.
- J-sign: Indicates HAGL (IGHL pulled from humerus).

BANKART REPAIR – PRINCIPLES & TECHNIQUE
Goals
- Reattach labrum to glenoid.
- Retension IGHL.
- Create bumper effect along glenoid rim.
### Patient Positioning

| Position | Description |
|---|---|
| Lateral decubitus | 45° abduction + 15–20° forward flexion; |
| (preferred) | 20–30° posterior tilt |
| Beach chair | Alternative but risk of cerebral hypoperfusion |

Portals

| Portal | Location | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Posterior | 2 fingers down & medial to acromion | Viewing |
| Anterior | Between coracoid & anterior acromion edge | Working – anchors/lasso |
| Anterosuperi | Just lateral to acromion edge | Shuttle thread |

or

### Key Steps
1. Debridement & Mobilization of labrum.
2. Anchor placement on glenoid face (4–5 o’clock) → bumper creation.
3. Labral reattachment + superior shift of capsule.
4. 2–3 anchors sufficient (4, 5, 3 o’clock positions).
5. Ensure anchor below coracoid (labrum functional only up to 2 o’clock). Concept of Balanced Repair (Snyder): Add posterior capsular plication if humeral head not centered.

### COMPLICATIONS & PEARLS
- Over-tightening → loss of external rotation.
- Missed HAGL → persistent instability.
- Incorrect portal → poor visualization.

<!-- page 6 -->

- Recurrent instability due to unrecognized bone loss. Exam Tip: Arthroscopic Bankart = gold standard for anterior instability without bone loss.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Apprehension + Relocation + Release = Diagnostic trio.
- Hill–Sachs: Posterior-superior humeral head indentation.
- Bony Bankart: Glenoid rim fracture.
- Buford Complex: Normal variant, not pathology.
- Perthes vs ALPSA: Medial displacement differentiates ALPSA.
- HAGL: Avulsion of IGHL; J-sign on MRI.
- Anchors: 4–5–3 o’clock positions.
- Posterior plication (Balanced repair): To centralize humeral head.
- Capsular plication: Reduces capsular volume.
- Bankart = Labrum reattached, capsule tightened.
