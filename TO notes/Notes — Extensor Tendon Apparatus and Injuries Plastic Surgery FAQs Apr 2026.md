### Overview / Key Concepts
- Part 1: anatomy of the extensor apparatus (compartments, sagittal bands, juncturae, central slip, lateral bands, retinacular and triangular ligaments), related syndromes, zones of injury, mallet finger, swan neck and boutonniere, repair and rehabilitation.
- Part 2: plastic and hand surgery FAQs from recent exams (MCQs with answers).
- Extensor tendons are flat distally (different repair techniques from flexors) and retract less when the juncturae and sagittal bands are intact.

### Extensor Compartments at the Wrist

| Compartment | Tendons |
|---|---|
| 1 | Abductor pollicis longus (APL), extensor pollicis brevis (EPB) |
| 2 | Extensor carpi radialis longus and brevis (ECRL, ECRB) |
| 3 | Extensor pollicis longus (EPL): turns around Lister tubercle towards the thumb |
| 4 | Extensor digitorum communis (EDC) + extensor indicis (EIP) |
| 5 | Extensor digiti minimi (EDM) |
| 6 | Extensor carpi ulnaris (ECU) |

- EDC gives slips to all four fingers; EIP and EDM are additional independent extensors and lie on the ULNAR side of the EDC slip to the same finger.
- MCQ favourites: the tendon that crosses Lister tubercle = EPL; sixth compartment = ECU; first compartment = APL and EPB.

### The Extensor Apparatus
- Juncturae tendinum: oblique bands between EDC slips on the hand dorsum; if intact proximal to a laceration they stop the tendon retracting, so it is found at the wound.
- Sagittal bands: ring-like bands from the volar plate and deep transverse metacarpal ligament around the MP joint to the extensor tendon; keep it centralised, prevent lateral slippage and bowstringing, limit excursion. If torn, the tendon subluxates (and may retract).

<!-- page 2 -->

- Extensor hood (expansion): over the proximal phalanx; receives the interossei and lumbricals from the volar side, giving MP flexion with IP extension.
- Central slip: inserts on the base of the middle phalanx (PIP extension).
- Lateral bands: formed with the intrinsic tendons, join over the middle phalanx as the terminal tendon on the base of the distal phalanx (DIP extension).
- Vincula (longa and brevia) supply blood from the volar side.

Elson test (zone III, central slip)
- Flex the PIP 90° over the edge of a table and ask the patient to extend the middle phalanx against resistance.
- Central slip intact: the DIP stays floppy. Central slip torn: extension force goes through the lateral bands and the DIP becomes rigid (extends).

### Mallet Finger
- Disruption of the terminal extensor tendon at or distal to the DIP: the distal phalanx rests in about 45° flexion with no active extension. X-ray for a bony avulsion.
- Conservative: splint the DIP ONLY (not the PIP) in slight hyperextension continuously for 6 weeks, never removing it (if it must come off, keep the DIP extended), then night splinting for 2 more weeks.
- Surgery: large bony fragment, joint subluxation or arthritis, chronic injuries, open injuries: K-wire or pull-out button fixation.
- Poor compliance (removing the splint) is the commonest cause of failure.

### Repair and Rehabilitation
- Distal (flat) tendons: horizontal mattress (most used) or figure-of-8; proximal (rounder) tendons: modified Bunnell or modified Kessler.
- 4-0 non-absorbable suture (nylon or Prolene); freshen clean tendon ends; bury the knots; check the tension with the tenodesis effect (not lax, not tight).
- Splint position (zones V–VIII): thumb in full extension for EPL repairs; fingers with IP joints straight, MP neutral, wrist in mild extension (flexors are stronger and must be counteracted).
- Early active short-arc motion (SAM): after zone III central slip repair: larger final arc of motion, shorter therapy, better DIP motion than static immobilisation.
- Relative motion (yoke) splint: repaired finger's MP held in slight extension relative to its neighbours; for zone IV–VII repairs: early active motion, fewer adhesions, better compliance, earlier return to work.
- Complications: rupture (too early or forced movement), adhesions, lax or over-tight repairs needing revision. Principles and complications otherwise match flexor tendon repair.

Ten Commandments of plastic surgery (Gillies)
- Make a plan; have a style; honour what is normal and return it to normal position; do not throw away a living thing (spare-part surgery); do not bear false witness against the defect; treat the primary defect before the secondary (rob Peter to pay Paul, carefully); always provide a lifeboat (plan B); do not do today what can be put off until tomorrow (e.g. wait in electrical burns); do not have a routine; do not covet thy neighbour's plastic unit.
- Earlier principles of Paré (1564): remove what is superfluous, restore displaced parts, separate what is joined, join what is separated, supply the defects of nature.

Littler neurovascular island flap
- Pulp skin from the ulnar side of the middle finger or radial side of the ring finger (about 2.5 x 1 cm) on its digital neurovascular bundle, dissected to the palm and tunnelled to the thumb.
- Gives excellent sensation for slicing or bike-chain injuries of the thumb pulp; donor closed primarily (graft distally); commonest complication venous congestion from a tight tunnel; the patient must relearn localisation (cortical reorientation).

Replantation
- Order of repair in a digit: bone fixation (K-wires), extensor tendon, flexor tendon, arteries, nerves, veins, then skin closure.
- At the wrist with a long ischaemia time (approaching 6 hours), restore arterial flow first (more muscle, reperfusion risk); always repair arteries before veins.
- Transporting the amputated part: wrap in saline-moistened gauze, seal in a plastic bag, place the bag in ice-saline slurry; never in direct contact with ice, cold water or a freezer.

- Cold ischaemia gives better results than warm (more metabolism).

### Exam & Viva Pearls
- First compartment APL + EPB; EPL around Lister tubercle; ECU in the sixth; EIP and EDM ulnar to EDC.
- Sagittal bands centralise the tendon at the MP joint; juncturae limit retraction.
- TRL lax = swan neck; central slip + triangular ligament failure = boutonniere.
- Elson test for the central slip.
- Mallet: DIP-only splint in slight hyperextension, 6 + 2 weeks; Doyle classification.
- Repair: mattress or figure-of-8 distally, 4-0 non-absorbable; SAM after zone III, relative motion splint for IV–VII.
- Trigger release injuring the proximal A2: bowstringing.
- Replant order: bone, extensor, flexor, artery, nerve, vein, skin.

### Take-Home Summary
- Understand the extensor apparatus as linked systems (ORL, TRL, triangular ligament) to explain mallet, swan neck and boutonniere deformities.
- Repair by zone, check tension, and mobilise early with protective splints.
