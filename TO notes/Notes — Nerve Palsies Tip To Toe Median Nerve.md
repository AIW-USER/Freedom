Nerve Palsies Tip to Toe – Median Nerve –
Dr. Priyanka Sharma
Overview / Key Concepts
The median nerve, also known as the labourer’s nerve, is responsible for most power
grip and coarse movements of the hand. Median nerve palsy presents with
characteristic deformities, sensory loss, and functional deficits that vary depending on
the level of injury.
This lecture integrates: - Applied anatomy - Clinical signs and tests - Compression
syndromes - Nerve repair principles - Motor and sensory nerve transfers - Tendon
transfer strategies for high and low median nerve palsy

Anatomy of the Median Nerve
### Root Value
- Contributions from C5 to T1
- Formed by:
  - Lateral cord (C5–C7)
  - Medial cord (C8–T1)

### Course in the Arm
- Closely related to brachial artery
- Initially lateral to artery, then crosses anteriorly to lie medial
- At cubital fossa:
  - Medial to brachial artery
  - Deep to bicipital aponeurosis
  - Anterior to brachialis
Median nerve gives no motor branches in the arm
### Course in the Forearm
- Passes between humeral and ulnar heads of pronator teres
- Travels beneath fibrous arch of flexor digitorum superficialis (FDS)
- Lies between FDS and FDP
- Becomes superficial ~5 cm proximal to wrist
- At wrist, lies between FCR and palmaris longus

Branches
### At Cubital Fossa
- Pronator teres
- Flexor carpi radialis
- Flexor digitorum superficialis
### Anterior Interosseous Nerve (AIN)
- Arises ~4 cm below elbow
- Supplies:
  - Flexor pollicis longus (FPL)
  - Radial half of FDP (index and middle)
  - Pronator quadratus
### Palmar Cutaneous Branch
- Arises proximal to flexor retinaculum
- Does not pass through carpal tunnel
- Sensation to thenar eminence and central palm

### Course in the Hand
- Passes through carpal tunnel
- Gives:
  - Recurrent motor branch to thenar muscles
  - Motor supply to first two lumbricals
  - Sensory supply to radial palm and radial 3½ fingers

Carpal Tunnel and Kaplan’s Cardinal Line
### Carpal Tunnel Contents
- 9 flexor tendons (4 FDS, 4 FDP, 1 FPL)
- 1 nerve (median nerve)
### Kaplan’s Cardinal Line
- Drawn from thumb in radial abduction to ulnar border of palm
- Important landmark for:
  - Motor branch of median nerve
  - Palmar cutaneous branch
  - Superficial and deep palmar arches
  - Safe carpal tunnel release

### Motor Supply (Proximal to Distal)
- Pronator teres
- Flexor carpi radialis
- Palmaris longus
- Flexor digitorum superficialis
- Pronator quadratus
- Flexor pollicis longus
- FDP (index and middle)
- Thenar muscles:
  - Abductor pollicis brevis (most important)
  - Opponens pollicis
  - Flexor pollicis brevis (superficial head)

Clinical Signs of Median Nerve Palsy
### Benedict Hand
- Inability to flex thumb, index, and middle finger
- Ring and little finger flex due to intact ulnar nerve
- Seen in high median nerve palsy
### Pointing Index Sign
- Index finger remains extended when making fist
### O Sign (AIN Palsy)
- Inability to form a round O
- Due to loss of FPL and FDP (index)
### Ape Thumb Deformity
- Thumb adducted, laterally rotated
- Loss of opposition
- Flattened thenar eminence
- Seen in low median nerve palsy

### Important Tests
- Pen test: Tests abductor pollicis brevis
- Phalen’s test: Carpal tunnel compression
- Reverse Phalen’s test

Sites of Compression
### Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (Most Common)
- Early morning numbness in thumb and index
- Sensory symptoms precede motor weakness
- Positive Phalen’s and reverse Phalen’s tests
### Pronator Syndrome
- Compression between pronator teres or FDS arch
- Pain over forearm
- No early morning symptoms
- No Tinel’s at wrist
### Anterior Interosseous Syndrome
- Forearm pain
- Weak pinch
- Positive O sign
Initial management: 6–8 weeks observation, splints, activity modification

Nerve Repair Principles
Non-Operative
- Neuropraxia
- Axonotmesis
- Observation with EMG, NCS, advancing Tinel’s
### Operative Timing

| Type | Timing |
|---|---|
| Primary | At injury or within 1 week |
| Delayed primary | 2 weeks to 1 month |
| Secondary | >1 month |

### Surgical Techniques
- Epineural repair (gold standard)
- Fascicular repair (no proven superiority)
- Nerve grafting if defect >2.5 cm
  - Sural nerve
  - Saphenous nerve
  - Lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve

Motor and Sensory Nerve Transfers
### Motor Transfers
- Brachialis branch → Median nerve
- ECRB branch → AIN
### Sensory Transfers
- Fascicles from dorsal ulnar nerve to restore palmar sensation

Median Nerve Palsy Levels
### High Median Nerve Palsy
- Loss of:
  - PT, FCR, FDS
  - FPL, FDP (index and middle)
  - Pronator quadratus
  - Thenar muscles
### Low Median Nerve Palsy
- Loss of:
  - APB, FPB, opponens pollicis
  - Radial two lumbricals
- Sensory loss over median nerve distribution

### Tendon Transfers – General Principles
- Good skin cover
- Adequate sensation
- Full passive ROM
- Expendable donor
- Synergistic muscle
- Donor power ≥4/5
- Straight line of pull
- One tendon, one function

Tendon Transfers for High Median Nerve Palsy
### Thumb IP Flexion
- Brachioradialis → FPL

- Tendon freed proximally
- Pulvertaft weave
- Tension set with elbow at 45° flexion
### Index and Middle Finger Flexion
- Side-to-side tagging of FDP
### Power Finger Flexion
- ECRL → FDP (index and middle)
- Wrist-based tension adjustment using tenodesis

Tendon Transfers for Low Median Nerve Palsy
(Opponensplasty)
### Common Techniques
- Burkhalter: EIP → APB
- Bunnell: FDS (ring) → APB
- Camitz: Palmaris longus → APB
- Miller: ECU → APB (with division at origin)
- Huber: Abductor digiti minimi muscle transfer (preferred in children)

Faculty Quote
“If you have to test only one muscle for the median nerve in the hand, test the abductor
pollicis brevis.” – Dr. Priyanka Sharma

### Exam Pearls
- Median nerve is the labourer’s nerve
- APB is the key muscle to test
- Palmar cutaneous branch does not pass through carpal tunnel
- O sign is specific for AIN palsy
- Benedict hand indicates high median nerve injury
- Ape thumb deformity indicates low median nerve palsy
- Epineural repair gives outcomes comparable to fascicular repair
- Burkhalter and Bunnell are the most commonly asked opponensplasties
