Fracture)
Faculty: Dr. Mohit Mohindra

### OVERVIEW
- C2 (Axis) – A strong vertebra characterized by the dens (odontoid process) projecting upward from its body, serving as a pivot for C1 (atlas) rotation.
- Axis fractures account for ~20% of all cervical spine fractures.
- Neurological injury is uncommon (<10%) due to canal widening during fracture.
- Three main types:
  1. Odontoid fractures
  2. Hangman’s fractures
  3. Miscellaneous C2 body fractures
“The odontoid is the pivot of life — fracture it, and you compromise stability.” –
Dr. Mohit Mohindra

### ANATOMY & OSSIFICATION
- Odontoid (Dens): Mean length ≈ 15 mm.
  - Anterior surface: Facet for anterior arch of atlas.
  - Posterior surface: Groove for transverse ligament.
- Ossification centers: 5 primary + 2 secondary.
  - 2 neural arches → appear at 7–8 weeks.
  - 1 body center → 4–5 months.
  - 2 dens centers → 6–7 months.
  - Tip appears at 3–6 years; fuses with body by ~12 years.
- Only the circumference of intervening cartilage between dens and body ossifies.

Common Causes:

<!-- page 2 -->

- Young: High-energy trauma (RTA, sports, fall from height).
  - Elderly: Low-energy ground-level falls.
  - Pathological: Metastatic deposits weakening C2.
Vascular watershed: Between apex (ICA branches) & base (vertebral artery branches)
→ poor healing in Type II odontoid fractures.

### CLINICAL PRESENTATION
  - Posterior upper cervical or occipital pain (greater occipital nerve).
  - Neck stiffness and restricted motion.
  - Paraspinal spasm and tenderness.
  - Dysphagia (large retropharyngeal hematoma).
  - Chronic nonunion → Myelopathy.
  - Paresthesias and exaggerated reflexes.

HANGMAN’S FRACTURE (Traumatic Spondylolisthesis of

- Definition: Bilateral fracture through C2 pars interarticularis → C2–C3 subluxation.
- Mechanism: Hyperextension + axial loading (e.g., RTA, diving).
- Neuro deficit rare (canal widens).
### Levine & Edwards Classification (Modified Effendi)

| Type | Features | Management |
|---|---|---|
| I | <3 mm displacement, <11° angulation | Collar 8–12 wks |
| II | >3 mm displacement, >11° | Traction → Halo vest / Surgery |
| IIA | Oblique line, severe angulation | Halo (no traction) |
| III | Bilateral C2–C3 facet dislocation | Surgery |
| IV–V | Lamina/facet disruption | Surgery |

### Francis Grading
- Grade I: <3.5 mm displacement, <11° – conservative.
- Grade II–V: Increasing angulation/displacement → surgical stabilization.

### KEY EXAM PEARLS
- Steele’s Rule of Thirds → Dens, space, cord = ⅓ each.
- Type II = unstable, high nonunion risk (watershed zone).
- ADI >5 mm → instability.
- Anterior screw – acute + intact ligament.
- Posterior fixation – comminuted / chronic.
- Hangman’s: Hyperextension + axial load.
- Type I–IIA → Halo; Type III–V → Surgery.
