### Stages of RA
1. Triggering Stage
- Trigger sites include lung, oral cavity and gut.
- Environmental factors and microbes (Porphyromonas gingivalis, Aggregatibacter, EBV) promote citrullination of proteins.
- Citrullination mediated by PAD enzyme converts arginine to citrulline.
- Neoantigens activate MHC class II T cells which stimulate B cells to produce ACPA.
- Represents early loss of immune tolerance.
2. Maturation Stage
- Occurs in lymphoid tissue and bone marrow.
- Epitope spreading leads to increasing ACPA titers.
- ACPA induces inflammation, pain and early bone loss.
- RA specific autoantigens: GNS and filamin A.
3. Targeting Stage
- Synovial compartment densely infiltrated by macrophages, dendritic cells, T and B cells.
- Endothelial proliferation contributes to angiogenesis.
- Bone resorption at bare areas where synovium inserts into periosteum.
- Subchondral bone destruction due to reduced osteoblast and increased osteoclast activity.
- Progressive degeneration of articular cartilage.
4. Fulminant Stage
- Hyperplastic synovium with uncontrolled fibroblast-like synoviocyte proliferation.
- Loss of contact inhibition, resistance to apoptosis and overexpression of heat shock proteins.

### Cartilage Damage
- Accelerated joint destruction and systemic involvement.

- Caused by invasive hyperplastic synovium.

- Key mediators: MMPs, ADAMTS, cathepsins.
- IL 1 and IL 17 accelerate cartilage apoptosis.

### Bone Erosion
- Leads to joint space narrowing.

Mechanisms
- Inflammatory theory: TNF alpha, IL 6, IL 1 beta and RANKL promote osteoclast formation.
- Autoimmunity theory: ACPA binds osteoclast precursors, stimulating osteoclastogenesis.

### Management Principles
- Results in localized, periarticular and systemic bone loss.

Goals
- Pain relief.
- Prevention of joint destruction and disability.
- Patient education.
- Physical therapy and occupational therapy.
- Start early aggressive treatment.

### Pharmacologic Management
- Surgery (limited indications)

NSAIDs
- Reduce pain and swelling.
- No effect on disease progression.
- GI toxicity common.
Corticosteroids
- Used for flares and extra-articular disease.
- Bridge therapy for 6 to 8 weeks.
- Maintenance dose less than or equal to 10 mg prednisolone.
- May be used in pregnancy.

Disease Modifying Anti Rheumatic Drugs
Conventional DMARDs
Methotrexate
- Weekly 7.5 to 25 mg oral, subcutaneous or IM.
- Folate antagonist reducing inflammatory mediators.
- Adverse effects: hepatotoxicity, marrow suppression, pulmonary fibrosis.
Leflunomide
- Loading 100 mg for 3 days then 10 to 20 mg daily.
- Inhibits pyrimidine synthesis.
- Adverse effects: diarrhea, alopecia, neuropathy, hypertension.
- Avoid in pregnancy.
Sulfasalazine
- 2 g daily.
- Anti inflammatory and antimicrobial.
- Adverse effects: GI distress, rash, marrow suppression, infertility.
Hydroxychloroquine
- 200 mg twice daily for 3 months then daily.
- Modulates T cell activity and intracellular signaling.

### Immunosuppressive Therapies
- Retinopathy risk with long term use.

- Azathioprine: marrow suppression, infection risk.
- Cyclosporine A: nephrotoxic, causes hypertension.

### Biologic DMARDs
- Cyclophosphamide: marrow suppression, gonadal toxicity.

Anti TNF Agents
Infliximab
- 3 mg per kg IV at 0, 2, 6 weeks then every 8 weeks.
- Must be combined with methotrexate.
- Infusion reactions, TB reactivation.
Etanercept
- 25 mg subcutaneous twice weekly.
- Injection site reactions.
Adalimumab
- 40 mg subcutaneous every 2 weeks.
- Fully human monoclonal antibody.

Tofacitinib

Baricitinib

### When to Start DMARDs
- Start if active disease persists after 3 months of NSAIDs.
- Many clinicians start DMARDs from Day 1.
### DMARD Selection
- Methotrexate is first choice due to rapid effect and ease of dosing.
- Combination therapy preferred when single drug inadequate.
### Limitations of Conventional DMARDs
- Slow onset and partial remission.
- Lose effectiveness over time.
- Toxicity requires monitoring.
### Biologic Response Modifiers
- Target TNF, IL 1, IL 6, CD20, CTLA4 pathways.
- Used when conventional DMARDs fail.
### Duration of Therapy
- Maintain therapy long term once remission achieved.
- Relapse common if drugs stopped.

### Surgical Management
- Flare needs dose escalation and short steroid course.

- Synovectomy rarely recommended.
- Indicated for persistent wrist synovitis with tendon rupture risk.
- Arthroplasty useful in advanced joint destruction.

- Nerve decompression procedures when indicated.
