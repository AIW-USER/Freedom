Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Arnold Chiari
Malformations & Syrinx (2025)
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Arnold–Chiari Malformation (ACM) refers to herniation of the cerebellar tonsils
through the foramen magnum, leading to compression of the medulla and spinal
cord, and frequently associated with hydrocephalus or syringomyelia.
Dr. Vishnu’s Quote:
“Whenever you see a syrinx, always look for a Chiari malformation — because
Chiari is the most common cause.”
Key Associated Defects:
- Hydrocephalus
- Syringomyelia / Syringobulbia
- Spinal dysraphism
- Encephalocele
Mechanism:
Reduced posterior fossa volume → herniation of cerebellar structures through foramen
magnum → compression & CSF flow obstruction.

### CLASSIFICATION

| Type | Description | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| Chiari 0 | Syringomyelia without hindbrain herniation | Minimal or no tonsillar descent |
| Chiari I | Cerebellar tonsils descend >5 mm below foramen magnum | Commonest form; seen in adolescents/adults |
| Chiari 1.5 | Progression of Type I | Increased tonsillar descent with mild brainstem involvement |
| Chiari II | Herniation of brainstem + cerebellum + vermis | Associated with myelomeningocele & hydrocephalus |

<!-- page 2 -->

| Type | Description | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| Chiari III | Herniation of hindbrain through high cervical/occipital encephalocele | Rare; diagnosed in utero |
| Chiari IV | Cerebellar hypoplasia/agenesis | Lethal; obsolete category |
| Chiari V | Cerebellar agenesis + occipital lobe herniation | Most severe, non-viable |

### ETIOLOGY
- Primary: Congenital hypoplasia of posterior fossa (underdeveloped occipital bone).

- Secondary: Premature suture closure, calvarial dysplasia, or syndromic/genetic causes (chromosome 1, 22 mutations).
Chiari I: Reduced posterior fossa → cerebellum displaced through foramen magnum.
Chiari II: Open neural tube defect → altered CSF flow → collapsed 4th ventricle →
hindbrain herniation.

### EPIDEMIOLOGY

| Type | Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Chiari I | 0.5–3.5% of general population | Slight female predominance (1.3:1) |
| Chiari II | 0.44/1000 births | Decreasing incidence with maternal folate therapy |
| Chiari III | 1–4.5% of all Chiari malformations | Usually non-viable |

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Two Mechanisms of Neurological Dysfunction: 1. Direct compression of medulla
and spinal cord at foramen magnum.
2. CSF flow obstruction → Syringomyelia or Syringobulbia formation.

<!-- page 3 -->

Syrinx formation mechanism:
CSF outflow blocked → fluid redirected into spinal canal → expansion of central canal
→ cord compression.

RADIOLOGICAL FEATURES
### MRI Findings (Diagnostic Tool of Choice)
- Cerebellar tonsillar descent >5 mm below foramen magnum (McRae line).

- Small posterior fossa volume.

- Syrinx (T2 hyperintense cavity in spinal cord).

- CSF flow study (CINE-MRI) – confirms obstruction at foramen magnum.
### Fetal Ultrasound Signs (Chiari II)

| Sign | Description |
|---|---|
| Lemon Sign | Flattening of frontal bones → ‘lemon’ shape skull |
| Banana Sign | Curved cerebellum with obliteration of cisterna magna |

CLINICAL FEATURES
### Chiari I – Commonest Type

| System | Symptoms |
|---|---|
| Headache | Suboccipital, worsened by Valsalva (cough, strain) |
| Cerebellar signs | Gait ataxia, dysmetria, nystagmus |
| Myelopathy | Weakness, clumsiness, dissociated sensory loss |
| Cranial nerves IX–XII | Dysphagia, hoarseness, sleep apnea |
| Ocular/vestibular | Diplopia, vertigo, tinnitus |

Left-sided scoliosis with absent reflexes should prompt MRI to rule out
Chiari malformation.
### Chiari II
- Detected in utero or at birth.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Associated with myelomeningocele and hydrocephalus.

- Causes respiratory distress, feeding difficulty, and brainstem signs.

### DIFFERENTIATION BETWEEN TYPE I & TYPE II

| Feature | Chiari I | Chiari II |
|---|---|---|
| Age | Adolescence/adult | Neonatal/in utero |
| Primary cause | Small posterior fossa | Neural tube defect |
| Hydrocephalus | Rare | Common |
| Associated anomaly | Syringomyelia | Myelomeningocele |
| Prognosis | Excellent | Poor |

SYRINGOMYELIA (ASSOCIATED CONDITION)
Definition:
A fluid-filled cavity (syrinx) within the spinal cord parenchyma or central canal, most
often secondary to Chiari I.
Etiology: - Secondary to CSF flow obstruction (Chiari I, basilar invagination).
- Post-traumatic, post-infective, or neoplastic causes (tumors, arachnoiditis).

### Pathophysiology (Hydrodynamic Theory)
- Hindbrain herniation → pressure difference across foramen magnum → CSF pulses into spinal canal (“water-hammer” effect) → syrinx expansion.
### Clinical Features

| System | Findings |
|---|---|
| Sensory | Dissociated sensory loss – loss of pain/temp with preserved touch & vibration (cape-like distribution) |
| Motor | LMN hand weakness, claw hand; UMN lower limb spasticity |
| Autonomic | Horner’s syndrome (cervical syrinx) |
| Skeletal | Progressive scoliosis due to paraspinal muscle atrophy |

<!-- page 5 -->

### EVALUATION

| Investigation | Purpose |
|---|---|
| MRI Spine & Brain | Syrinx extent, tonsillar herniation, posterior fossa size |
| CINE MRI (CSF Flow Study) | Detects flow obstruction at foramen magnum |
| CT / X-ray | Bony anomalies (basilar invagination, assimilation) |
| Myelography | Alternative when MRI contraindicated |

MANAGEMENT
Non-Operative
- Observation if asymptomatic.

- Analgesics, muscle relaxants for headache/neck pain.

- Avoid straining and trauma.
### Surgical Indications
- Symptomatic Chiari I with confirmed tonsillar herniation (>5 mm).

- Progressive neurological deficit or syringomyelia.

- Hydrocephalus or significant brainstem compression.

### Surgical Techniques

| Procedure | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Posterior Fossa Decompression | Suboccipital craniectomy + C1 (±C2) laminectomy to enlarge foramen magnum |
| Duroplasty | Opening dura and grafting (autograft/artificial) to expand space |
| Tonsillar Reduction (Cauterization) | Shrinks non-functional herniated tonsils |
| Arachnoid Lysis | Removes adhesions restricting CSF flow |

<!-- page 6 -->

| Procedure | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Shunt Procedures | Syringosubarachnoid / Syringopleural / Syringoperitoneal for persistent syrinx |

Note: Early surgery (<2 years from symptom onset) → better outcomes.

### COMPLICATIONS
- CSF leak / Pseudomeningocele

- Meningitis / Wound infection

- Brainstem dysfunction

- Vertebral artery injury

- Shunt blockage / failure

### PROGNOSIS

| Type | Prognosis |
|---|---|
| Chiari I | Excellent if no preexisting neurological deficit |
| Chiari II | Poor; 3% neonatal, 15% 3-year mortality |
| Chiari III–V | Dismal, often fatal |

- Neurological deficits pre-surgery → poorer recovery.

- Scoliosis may stabilize after foramen magnum decompression.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Cut-off for Chiari I: >5 mm cerebellar tonsillar descent (McRae line).

- Most common symptom: Suboccipital headache, worsened by Valsalva.

- Common association: Syringomyelia.

- Hydrodynamic theory: Explains syrinx propagation.

<!-- page 7 -->

- Chiari II: Always with myelomeningocele.

- Syrinx sensory loss: Cape-like pattern, dissociated sensory loss.

- Dr. Vishnu’s Tip: “Don’t touch asymptomatic tonsillar descent — observe unless symptoms progress.”

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Arnold–Chiari = congenital hindbrain herniation causing CSF obstruction.

- Always evaluate for syrinx and hydrocephalus.

- MRI + CINE MRI = gold standard for diagnosis.

- Posterior fossa decompression is the mainstay for symptomatic Chiari I.

- Syrinx often regresses after successful decompression.

- Prognosis excellent for Type I; poor for higher grades.
