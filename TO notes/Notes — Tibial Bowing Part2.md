Tibial Bowing Part 2 – Dr. Shalin Shah
Overview / Key Concepts
This lecture builds on Tibial Bowing Part 1 and focuses on intramedial tibial bowing,
which is classically associated with fibular hemimelia and tibial hemimelia. These
conditions represent longitudinal limb deficiencies and require a life plan rather than
isolated deformity correction.
Key learning objectives include: - Embryological basis of hemimelias - Systematic
identification of fibular and tibial hemimelia - Understanding multiple classification
systems - Principles of reconstruction, limb lengthening, and amputation
decision-making

### Embryology of Limb Development
- Limb bud development occurs between 4–8 weeks of gestation.
- Apical ectodermal ridge regulates longitudinal growth.
- Tissue apoptosis shapes digits.
- Ossification centers appear in all long bones by 12 weeks.
### Vascular Theory
- Exogenous vascular or mechanical insult to limb bud
- Absent anterior tibial artery commonly associated with fibular hemimelia
- Results in postaxial or preaxial longitudinal deficiencies

Intramedial Tibial Bowing
Mechanism
- Seen when fibula is absent or deficient
- Loss of lateral tether
- Tibia drifts medially and anteriorly
### Diagnostic Rule

| Apex Direction | Likely Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Intramedial (anterior + medial) | Fibular hemimelia |

Fibular Hemimelia
Definition
Partial or complete absence of fibula, associated with limb shortening and complex foot
and ankle deformities.

Epidemiology
- Most common long bone deficiency
- Incidence: ~7.4 per 20 million live births
- Bilateral in 9–52 percent
- Usually sporadic, no fixed inheritance pattern

### Clinical Features of Fibular Hemimelia
- Intramedial tibial bowing
- Limb length discrepancy (LLD)
- Short foot
- Missing rays (lateral rays commonly absent)
- Equinus or equinovalgus foot
- Ankle valgus
- Ball-and-socket ankle joint
- Tarsal coalition
- Genu valgum
- Skin dimpling at apex of deformity
Short limb with clubfoot-like deformity is an indication for X-ray
Classification Systems for Fibular Hemimelia
### Coventry and Johnson (Basic)
- Type 1: Short fibula
- Type 2: Absent fibula
- Type 3: Bilateral involvement

### Achterman and Kalamchi (Resident Level)
- Type IA: Mild fibular shortening
- Type IB: Partial absence
- Type II: Near complete or complete absence

<!-- page 3 -->

Stanitski Classification (Comprehensive)
Considers: - Fibular length - Ankle stability (normal, valgus, ball-and-socket) - Presence
of tarsal coalition - Number of foot rays

Birch Classification (Prognostic)
Based on percentage limb length discrepancy and foot rays:

| LLD | Clinical Implication |
|---|---|
| <6% | Minor reconstruction |
| 6–10% | Limited lengthening |
| 11–30% | Multiple lengthenings |
| >30% | Consider amputation |

### Paley Classification (Surgical Planning)
- Type 1: Stable ankle
- Type 2: Dynamic valgus ankle
- Type 3: Fixed equinovalgus ankle
- Type 4: Equinovarus ankle
Each type has specific reconstructive strategies.

Principles of Management in Fibular Hemimelia
### Common Problems to Address
- Limb length discrepancy
- Foot and ankle deformity
- Coronal plane deformity (genu valgum)
- Joint instability (ankle, knee)
### Life Plan Concept
- Estimate final LLD at maturity
- Decide between reconstruction versus amputation
- Shared decision-making with family

Limb Length Discrepancy Prediction
Methods
- Moseley straight-line graph

<!-- page 4 -->

- Green–Anderson charts
- Multiplier method (Paley)
Example: - 4-year-old boy with 4 cm LLD - Multiplier ≈ 2 - Expected LLD at maturity ≈ 8
cm

Surgical Options in Fibular Hemimelia
### Accessory Procedures
- Guided growth for genu valgum
- Equinus correction
- Orthotic support
### Lengthening Techniques
- External fixator
- Lengthening over nail
- Hexapod fixator
- Magnetic intramedullary nails
Always assess ankle and knee stability before lengthening.
Special Procedures
SHORDT Procedure
SHortening Osteotomy Realignment Distal Tibia - Indicated for dynamic valgus ankle -
Corrects ankle alignment by tibial shortening

SUPERankle Procedure
Systematic Utilitarian Procedure for Extremity Reconstruction - For fixed equinovalgus
ankle - Involves: - Soft tissue release - Excision of fibular anlage - Tibial osteotomy and
realignment - Ankle stabilization

### Indications for Amputation in Fibular Hemimelia
- LLD >30 percent
- Non reconstructable foot (≤2 rays)
- Severe deformity requiring multiple high-risk surgeries
### Common Levels
- Syme’s amputation
- Boyd’s amputation

<!-- page 5 -->

Avoid amputation if significant upper limb deficiency is present.
Tibial Hemimelia
Definition
Partial or complete absence of tibia with intact fibula.

Key Differences from Fibular Hemimelia

| Feature | Tibial Hemimelia | Fibular Hemimelia |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | Very rare | Most common |
| Foot | Equinovarus, duplication | Missing rays |
| Inheritance | Autosomal dominant | Sporadic |

### Clinical Evaluation in Tibial Hemimelia
- Assess quadriceps and knee extension
- Examine ankle alignment and stability
- Evaluate limb length discrepancy
- Look for toe duplication
Presence of knee extensor mechanism is critical for reconstruction.
### Jones Classification (Commonly Used)
- Type I: Complete absence of tibia
- Type II: Proximal tibia present, distal absent
- Type III: Distal tibia present, proximal absent
- Type IV: Tibiofibular diastasis

### Weber Classification (Comprehensive)
- Type A: Tibial anlage present
- Type B: Tibial anlage absent
Subtypes describe: - Proximal diastasis - Distal diastasis - Focal aplasia - Bifocal
aplasia - Complete absence
Includes rare entities such as double fibula.

<!-- page 6 -->

### Surgical Principles in Tibial Hemimelia
- Primary goal: obtain stable, weight-bearing limb
- Align ankle first, lengthening later
- Reconstruction preferred when extensor mechanism present
- Amputation considered when knee extension absent

Weber’s Patellar Arthroplasty
Indication
- Complete or proximal tibial aplasia with absent knee joint support
Principle
- Patella used as articular surface
- Patella fused to proximal fibular epiphysis
- Creates functional knee articulation

### Key Examination Triggers for Radiographs
- Clubfoot with short limb
- Rigid deformity unresponsive to casting
- Multiple rays or duplicated toes
- Suspected hemimelia

Faculty Quote
“Hemimelias are not isolated deformities. They are life-long conditions that demand a
plan, not a procedure.” – Dr. Shalin Shah

### Exam Pearls
- Intramedial tibial bowing suggests fibular hemimelia
- Fibular hemimelia is the most common long bone deficiency
- Absent anterior tibial artery supports vascular theory
- Birch classification guides prognosis and counseling
- Paley classification guides surgical planning
- Multiplier method predicts final LLD accurately
- Tibial hemimelia has autosomal dominant inheritance
- Knee extensor mechanism decides reconstruction vs amputation
