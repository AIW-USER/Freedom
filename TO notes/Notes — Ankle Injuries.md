Ankle Injuries – Dr. Rahul Upadhyay
Overview
Ankle fractures are the most common intraarticular fractures of a weight bearing
joint. Malreduction leads to abnormal contact pressures and early arthritis. Correct
understanding of anatomy, mechanism, radiology, and fixation principles is crucial
for optimal outcomes.
Faculty Remark: “Every ankle fracture is a ring injury. Restore the ring, and the
ankle works.”

Anatomy Essentials
### Joint Type
- Modified hinge joint.
- Formed by tibia, fibula, talus.
- Movements:
  - Sagittal: dorsiflexion, plantarflexion.
  - Coronal + axial: coupled rotations.
### Ligamentous Anatomy
- Lateral collateral ligament complex: ATFL, CFL, PTFL.
- Medial (deltoid) ligament: superficial + deep layers.
- Syndesmosis components: AITFL, PITFL, transverse ligament, interosseous ligament.
### Key Concept: Syndesmosis
- Maintains ankle mortise stability.
- Disruption leads to talar shift and degenerative changes.

### Classification Systems
1. Lauge Hansen Classification (Mechanism based)
Foot position + direction of force.
- Supination adduction.
- Supination external rotation.
- Pronation abduction.
- Pronation external rotation.

<!-- page 2 -->

2. Danis Weber Classification (Level of fibular fracture)
- Type A: infrasyndesmotic.
- Type B: transsyndesmotic.
- Type C: suprasyndesmotic.
3. AO Classification
Type A/B/C correspond to Weber.

Why classifications matter
- Identify instability.
- Guide surgical approach.
- Predict soft tissue injury.

Mechanisms of Injury
### Supination Adduction (SAD)
- Stage 1: transverse fibular avulsion.
- Stage 2: vertical medial malleolus fracture.
### Supination External Rotation (SER)
- Most common pattern.
- Four stages involving AITFL → fibula → PITFL → medial structures.
### Pronation Abduction (PAB)
- Transverse fibula + medial injury.
### Pronation External Rotation (PER)
- High fibular fracture (Maisonneuve).
- Syndesmotic disruption.
- Medial malleolus/deltoid injury.

Radiology Essentials
Ottawa Ankle Rules
Obtain X rays if:
- Pain in malleolar zone + any of the following:
  - Tenderness posterior edge/tip of malleoli.
  - Inability to bear weight immediately and in ER.
### Standard Views
- AP.

- Mortise.
- Oblique.
- Full length tibia fibula for suspected Maisonneuve fracture.
### Syndesmotic Imaging Criteria (Pages 26–27)
- Talocrural angle: 83° ± 4°.
- Medial clear space ≤4 mm.
- Talar tilt ≤2 mm.

CT Scan – When to Use
Indications:
- Comminuted fractures.
- Posterior malleolar fractures.
- Suspected syndesmotic injuries.
- Preoperative planning.

Treatment Concepts
Goals:
- Anatomical reduction.
- Structural stability.
- Early mobilization.
Avoid prolonged immobilization since it causes:
- Capsular adhesions.
- Cartilage degeneration.
- Myostatic contracture.
- Bone atrophy.

Timing of Surgery (Page 37)
Urgent situations
- Dislocations.
- Open fractures.
Contraindications
- Deep dirty wounds after 12–24 hours.

<!-- page 4 -->

Ideal timing
- Before significant swelling.
- Delay if blisters along incision line.

### Preoperative Planning
- Careful evaluation with X-rays and CT.
- Templating for complex fractures.
- Decide patient positioning and incision.

### Sequence of Fixation
1. Reduce and provisionally fix fibula.
2. Address medial malleolus/deltoid if needed.
3. Definitive fibula fixation.
4. Assess syndesmosis (Cotton test).

Management by Weber Types
### Type A (Infrasyndesmotic)
- Stable → cast + non weight bearing.
- Unstable → ORIF with plate or TBW.
- Medial malleolus → screws or antiglide plates.
### Type B (Transsyndesmotic)
- Oblique fibular fracture.
- Fibula displaced posteriorly and rotated externally.
- Reduce using internal rotation.
### Type C (Suprasyndesmotic)
- High fibular fracture.
- Syndesmosis almost always injured.
- Fix fibula first to restore length.

Syndesmotic Fixation
Indications:
- PER injuries.
- Suprasyndesmotic fractures.
- Medial joint space widening >2 mm after fibula fixation.

<!-- page 5 -->

Assessment:
- Cotton’s test: lateral pull on fibula; look for medial clear space widening.
Fixation options:
- 3.5 mm or 4.5 mm cortical screw.
- Tightrope/ suture button.

### Exam Pearls
- SER pattern is most common.
- Maisonneuve injury → always examine full length tibia fibula.
- Medial clear space >4 mm indicates deltoid injury.
- Syndesmotic injury best assessed intraoperatively with Cotton test.
- Restore fibular length first.
- Joint congruency determines long term outcome.

Final Faculty Insight: “Ankle fractures fail not because of hardware, but because
fibular length or rotation was not restored.”
