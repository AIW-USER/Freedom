Multiple Myeloma – Dr. Mukul Mohindra
Overview
Multiple Myeloma is the most common primary malignant tumor of bone, distinct
from metastasis which remains the commonest overall. It represents a multisystem
plasma cell dyscrasia with skeletal, renal, hematologic, and immunologic involvement.
Faculty Remark: “Alkaline phosphatase is normal in myeloma. That single clue
often clinches the diagnosis.” turn35file1

## Plasma Cell Dyscrasias Spectrum
### MGUS (Monoclonal Gammopathy of Undetermined Significance)
- Serum monoclonal protein <30 g/L.

- Plasma cells <10 percent.

- No CRAB features.

- Asymptomatic.
### Smoldering Myeloma
- M protein >30 g/L or plasma cells 10 to 60 percent.

- No CRAB features.

- Surveillance only.
### Multiple Myeloma
- Plasma cells >60 percent or 10 to 60 percent with ≥1 CRAB feature. turn35file0
### Solitary Plasmacytoma
- One biopsy proven osseous lesion.

- Bone marrow <60 percent plasma cells.

- No systemic involvement.

## Pathophysiology
### Cytokine Driven Bone Destruction
- Myeloma cell → RANKL↑ → osteoclast activation.

- Osteoblast suppression → no ALP elevation.

- Net effect: lytic lesions, hypercalcemia, pathological fractures. turn35file1
### Renal Failure: Mechanisms
- Light chain (Bence Jones protein) nephropathy.

- Tubular obstruction → glomerular damage → proteinuria.
### Marrow Infiltration
- Pancytopenia → fatigue, infections.

## Genetics
Common abnormalities:
- Hyperdiploidy, 13q14 deletions, t(11,14), t(4,14), t(14,16).
- N-RAS, K-RAS, B-RAF mutations. turn35file0

## Clinical Features
### Common Features
- Bone pain.

- Fatigue, generalized weakness.

- Renal insufficiency.

- Anemia.

- Hypercalcemia. turn35file0
### Uncommon Features
- Hyperviscosity syndrome.

- Hepatosplenomegaly.

<!-- page 3 -->

- Spinal cord compression (plasmacytoma).

- Pleural effusion.
### CRAB Criteria
- C: Calcium >2.75 mmol/L.

- R: Renal failure (Cr >1.96 mg/dL).

- A: Anemia (Hb <10 g/dL).

- B: Bone lesions.

## Investigations
### Basic Tests
- Hemogram: anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, ESR↑.

- Peripheral Smear: rouleaux formation, plasma cells (if >2,000/mm³ → plasma cell leukemia). turn35file0
### Bone Marrow Examination
- Monoclonal plasma cells >10 percent.

- Oval cells with eccentric nucleus, perinuclear halo, clock-face chromatin.

- Mott cells, Russell bodies (immunoglobulin inclusions).
### SPEP / UPEP
- Monoclonal M spike in gamma region. turn35file0
- Reversed A:G ratio.

- Urine: Bence Jones protein (light chains).
### Other Markers
- Beta 2 microglobulin ↑ (prognostic).

- LDH ↑.

- Calcium ↑, uric acid ↑.

## Radiology
### Classical Findings
  - Multiple punched out lytic lesions (skull) – hallmark. turn35file1
  - Skull patterns differentiation:
  - Salt and pepper → Hyperparathyroidism.

  - Cotton wool → Paget disease.

  - Punched out without beveling → Multiple myeloma.

  - Double beveled → EG / LCH.

Spine
  - Vertebra plana may occur (also in plasma cytoma).
### Whole Body Evaluation
  - Skeletal survey preferred.

  - Bone scan less useful (cold lesions).

  - PET-CT helpful in selected cases.

## Management Overview
### MGUS & Smoldering Myeloma
  - Observation only.

  - Bisphosphonates for osteoporosis (optional).

<!-- page 5 -->

- No chemotherapy unless progression.
### Solitary Plasmacytoma
- Radiotherapy is primary treatment. turn35file0
- Stabilize impending fractures (Mirel >8).

- Kyphoplasty for vertebral lesions.

## Active Multiple Myeloma – Medical Management
Bisphosphonates
- Zoledronic acid IV every 4 weeks × 2 years.

- Avoid in renal failure.
Denosumab (Xgeva)
- 120 mg SC at 0, 1, 2 weeks then every 4 weeks. turn35file1
- Safe in renal failure.
Chemotherapy
- Melphalan + Prednisolone/Dexamethasone (standard).

- Other drugs: Cyclophosphamide, Doxorubicin, Etoposide, Bendamustine.
Immunomodulators
- Thalidomide, Lenalidomide, Pomalidomide.
### Proteasome Inhibitors
- Bortezomib, Carfilzomib, Ixazomib.
### BCMA Targeted Therapy
1. Belantamab mafodotin (antibody drug conjugate).

2. Bispecific T Cell Engagers (Teclistamab) – attaches CD3 on T cell to BCMA on myeloma cell. turn35file1
### Monoclonal Antibodies
- Daratumumab, Elotuzumab, Isatuximab.

- Daratumumab + Pomalidomide + Dexamethasone effective in refractory cases.

<!-- page 6 -->

### CAR-T Cell Therapy
- Idecabtagene, Ciltacabtagene.

- Patient-derived T cells engineered to express BCMA receptors.
### Stem Cell Transplant
- Autologous transplant improves survival (not curative).

- Best in <65 years without comorbidities.

## Orthopaedic Management
### Stabilization Principles
- IM nails preferred for long bone lesions.

- Span entire bone length.

- Periarticular lesions → megaprosthesis or arthroplasty.
Spine
- Kyphoplasty for vertebral compression.
### Adjuvant Radiation
- Begin 3 weeks after surgery (myeloma is radiosensitive). turn35file1
Adjuncts
- Denosumab or bisphosphonates for bone strengthening.

- Treat hypercalcemia aggressively.

### Exam Pearls
- Most common primary malignant bone tumor = Multiple Myeloma.

- ALP normal despite severe bone destruction.

- Bence Jones protein = early renal clue.

- Punched out skull lesions without sclerotic rim = MM.

<!-- page 7 -->

- Smoldering myeloma → surveillance only.

- Solitary plasmacytoma → radiotherapy.

- Denosumab preferred in renal failure.

- IM nailing spans entire bone for pathological fractures.

confuse you, whether in OPD, ward, or exam.”
