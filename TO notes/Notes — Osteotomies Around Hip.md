Osteotomies Around Hip – Dr. Sanjay Arora
Overview / Key Concepts
Osteotomies around the hip are powerful reconstructive and salvage procedures aimed
at restoring biomechanical alignment, improving load transmission, relieving pain, and
preserving joint function. These procedures may involve the proximal femur, the
pelvis, or both, and must be chosen based on pathology, age, joint congruency, and
available range of motion.
The key principle is that osteotomy corrects mechanics, not pathology, and success
depends on correct indication, precise planning, and appropriate execution.

Definition of Osteotomy
An osteotomy is a surgical procedure in which a bone is cut to obtain corrective
biomechanical alignment of the extremity, achieving equitable load transmission, with or
without removal of bone, and sometimes with addition of bone graft.

### Historical Perspective
- 1826: First femoral osteotomy by John Rhea
- 1835: First subtrochanteric osteotomy for CDH (Sourvier)
- 1854: Subcutaneous femoral osteotomy (Langenbeck)
- 1918–19: Bifurcation operation described by Von Baeyer and Lorenz
- 1935: Pauwels described intertrochanteric abduction osteotomy
- 1936: McMurray described displacement osteotomy
- 1955: Chiari pelvic osteotomy

Subcapital Osteotomies
Indications
- Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis (SCFE)
Types
- Fish osteotomy
- Dunn osteotomy
- Modified Dunn osteotomy
### Modified Dunn Osteotomy
- Femoral neck osteotomy at level of slip
- Performed via safe surgical dislocation
- Allows anatomical realignment of epiphysis
- Preserves femoral head vascularity

Head and Neck Osteotomies
Indications
- Healed SCFE
- Healed Perthes disease
- Femoroacetabular impingement due to head–neck deformity
Procedures
- Head–neck osteochondroplasty
- Femoral head reduction osteotomy
### Key Principle
- Used when simple osteochondroplasty cannot restore sphericity

### Femoral Head Reduction Osteotomy
- Indicated for severely deformed, aspherical heads
- Removes non functional head segment
- Remaining viable head is reconstructed to spherical shape
- Fixed using screws

Neck Osteotomies
### Closing Wedge Osteotomy (Base of Neck)
- Described by Kramer
- Osteotomy from lesser to greater trochanter
- Posterior cortex kept intact
- Corrects deformity away from physis
- Lower risk of avascular necrosis

Transtrochanteric Osteotomies for AVN
Types
- Transtrochanteric rotational osteotomy (TRO)
- Transtrochanteric curved varus osteotomy (TCVO)
Indications
- AVN with viable femoral head segment
### Viable Bone Criteria

| Osteotomy | Required Arc of Viable Bone |
|---|---|
| TRO | >120° on mid sagittal MRI |
| TCVO | >150° on mid coronal MRI |

Trochanteric Osteotomies
### Relative Neck Lengthening
- Indicated for trochanteric overgrowth
- Improves abductor lever arm
- Reduces extra-articular impingement
### True Neck Lengthening
- Used in post septic hip sequelae
- Involves shaft lateralization and graft interposition

Intertrochanteric Osteotomies
### Key Features
- Most commonly used level
- Iliopsoas attachment stabilizes proximal fragment

<!-- page 4 -->

### Very Good Indications
- Trochanteric fracture malunion
- Neck nonunion with viable head
- Congenital coxa vara
- Small localized AVN
- Healed arthrodesis malposition
### Good Indications
- Coxa valga with retained abduction
- Chronic SCFE (25–50°)
- Excessive femoral anteversion
- Perthes disease

Varus and Valgus Osteotomies
### CORA Location
- Varus deformity: Center of femoral head
- Valgus deformity: Base of greater trochanter
Prerequisites
- Varus osteotomy: Adequate hip abduction
- Valgus osteotomy: Adequate hip adduction

### Technique Principles (Intertrochanteric)
- Start with transverse osteotomy above lesser trochanter
- Allows rotation and translation
- Angular correction achieved later
- Fixation:
  - Varus: 90° blade plate
  - Valgus: Double angle plate

Subtrochanteric Osteotomy (Pelvic Support Osteotomy)
Purpose
- Restores hip stability
- Reduces Trendelenburg gait
- Preserves mobility
Indications
- Post septic hip sequelae

<!-- page 5 -->

- Neglected or failed DDH
- Girdlestone hip
- Femoral neck pseudoarthrosis

Pelvic Support Osteotomy Planning
### Level of Osteotomy
- Determined by maximum adduction X-ray
- Osteotomy performed at contact point with pelvis
### Valgus Angle Calculation
- Maximum adduction possible
- Add adduction contracture
- Add overcorrection of 15–30°
Excess valgus leads to knee valgus and pelvic obliquity
Classification Based on Displacement
### Transpositional Osteotomy
- Axes of fragments remain parallel
- Examples: McMurray, Pauwels, Putti
### Angulation Osteotomy
- Axes form an angle
- Examples: Extension osteotomy, pelvic support osteotomy

### Classification Based on Indication
- Nonunion neck femur
- Osteoarthritis hip
- Unstable intertrochanteric fractures
- DDH
- Coxa vara
- Perthes disease
- SCFE
- AVN
- Paralytic hip disorders
- Femoroacetabular impingement

Pelvic Osteotomies
Indications
- Correct acetabular deficiency
- Improve femoral head coverage
- Reduce contact stresses
- Stimulate acetabular growth

Classification of Pelvic Osteotomies
Redirectional
- Complete cuts
- Reorient acetabulum
- Examples: Salter, Double innominate, Triple innominate, PAO
### Shape Changing (Acetabuloplasty)
- Incomplete cuts
- Volume reducing
- Examples: Pemberton, Dega, San Diego
Augmentation (Salvage)
- Increase acetabular volume
- Examples: Shelf procedures, Chiari

### Salter Innominate Osteotomy
- Indicated in DDH after 2 years
- Provides anterolateral coverage
- Corrects AI by 10–15°
- Requires open triradiate cartilage
Complications
- Loss of correction
- Triradiate cartilage closure
- Redislocation
- AVN

### Double and Triple Innominate Osteotomy
- Used in older children
- Greater correction possible
- Triple osteotomy allows maximum mobility

<!-- page 7 -->

- Steel osteotomy has limited mobility due to ligament constraints

### Periacetabular Osteotomy (PAO)
- Adolescents and adults
- Preserves posterior column
- Technically demanding
- Powerful correction

Acetabuloplasties
Pemberton
- Hinges on triradiate cartilage
- Improves anterior and lateral coverage
Dega
- Ideal for neuromuscular hips
- Provides posterior coverage
- Age range: 2–12 years
### San Diego
- Most versatile
- Anterior and posterior coverage

### Augmentation Procedures (Shelf)
- Staheli slotted acetabular augmentation
- Wilson shelf
- Wainwright shelf
- Japanese tectoplasty
Indication
- Deficient acetabulum not correctable by redirectional osteotomy

Chiari Osteotomy
Indications
- Uncontainable femoral head
- Symptomatic subluxation
- Center edge angle <10°

Contraindications
- Advanced osteoarthritis
- Labral tear
- Bilateral disease

Faculty Quote
“Osteotomy around the hip is not about cutting bone. It is about restoring biomechanics
at the right place, for the right indication.” – Dr. Sanjay Arora

### Exam Pearls
- Osteotomy corrects mechanics, not pathology
- Intertrochanteric level is the most versatile
- Adequate ROM is mandatory before varus or valgus osteotomy
- Pelvic support osteotomy is a salvage procedure
- Salter osteotomy provides anterior coverage only
- Dega is preferred in neuromuscular hips
- PAO is preferred in adolescents with dysplasia
- Excess valgus causes knee valgus and pelvic obliquity
