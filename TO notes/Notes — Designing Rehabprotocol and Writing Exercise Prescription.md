STRUCTURE OF A REHAB PROGRAM
A structured rehabilitation program progresses through defined phases:

| Phase | Focus | Key Exercises |
|---|---|---|
| Early Phase | Muscle activation, mobility, pain control | Isometrics, ROM (passive/assisted), closed-chain exercises |
| Late Phase | Strength, proprioception, endurance | Isotonic / Isokinetic, balance training, open-chain strengthening |

### PRINCIPLES OF EXERCISE DESIGN
1. Safety before specificity – start with closed-chain, then progress to open-chain.
2. Progressive overload – increase intensity and complexity gradually.
3. Integration of balance, strength, and endurance – not isolated development.
4. Goal-based sequencing – functional recovery before sports performance.

BALANCE & PROPRIOCEPTION
### Key Definitions:
- Balance: Ability to maintain posture.
- Proprioception: Sense of joint position in 3D space.
- Kinesia: Sense of shape or movement of an object.
### Receptor Mechanism:
- Mechanoreceptors in joints (e.g., ACL femoral insertion) detect motion & position.
### Training Methods:

| Type | Tools / Methods |
|---|---|
| Upper Limb | Ball rolling, wall slides, balance with eyes closed |
| Lower Limb | Wobble board, trampoline, Dura disc, single-leg stance |

Combine proprioception with strength training to prevent re-injury.
### HYDROTHERAPY
- Utilizes buoyancy and hydrostatic pressure to offload joints.
- Ideal for early mobility in lower limb rehab.
- Allows ROM & strengthening in partial weight-bearing.

### BIOFEEDBACK SYSTEM
- Integrated with isokinetic machines.
- Provides real-time performance data → adjusts exercise intensity accordingly.
- Enhances motivation and safety during rehab.

### Aerobic (Cardiovascular) Exercises
- HRmax = 220 – Age
- Target HR: 55–70% of HRmax → moderate intensity.
- Fuel Utilization:
  - <1 min → Creatine phosphate
  - 1–20 min → Glucose (Glycolysis)
  - 20 min → Fats (Krebs cycle) → Aerobic endurance.
### Resistance (Strength) Exercises
- Use Repetition Maximum (RM):
  - 50–60% RM → Beginner (low intensity)
  - 60–75% RM → Intermediate
  - 75% RM → Advanced
“Two days rest a week is part of the prescription — not a break.”
SUMMARY LADDER
Rehabilitation Progression: 1. Isometric activation → 2. ROM recovery → 3.
Isotonic / Isokinetic strengthening → 4. Balance & proprioception → 5. Flexibility
(Static / PNF) → 6. Cardiovascular conditioning → 7. Sports-specific retraining →
8. Return to sport (≥85% strength) “Structure transforms motion into medicine.”
