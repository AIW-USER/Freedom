Basic Sciences – Dr. Daivik T Shetty

### Key Concepts / Overview
- Basic Sciences form the foundation of orthopedic understanding — including bone biology, cartilage structure, and cellular physiology.
- Common misconception: Students often treat it superficially, but deep understanding is crucial for DNB/FNB entrance and fellowship exams.
- Dr. Daivik’s Remark: “What gets you in trouble isn’t what you don’t know — it’s what you know for sure that just isn’t so.”

### Histology of Bone

| Type | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Lamellar | Organized, stress-oriented (Wolff’s Law), hypocellular | Normal cortical & cancellous bone |
| Woven | Disorganized, random, hypercellular, increased turnover | Fracture callus, infection, malignancy, stress reaction |

80% of skeletal mass is cortical bone – slow turnover, organized in osteons, high
resistance to torsion and bending.

Microscopic Anatomy
### Cortical Bone (Compact)
- Dense, organized, and highly resistant to torsional stress.
- Functional unit: Osteon (Haversian System).

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| Volkmann’s Canal | Transverse vessels connecting osteons |
| Interstitial Lamellae | Layers between osteons |
| Cement Lines | Weak points – common fracture planes |
| Canaliculi | Channels connecting osteocytes for nutrient exchange |

### Cancellous (Trabecular) Bone
- Less dense, highly vascular, high turnover rate.

- Heals faster – major site of bone remodeling.

### Cellular Biology of Bone

| Cell Type | Origin | Function | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Osteoblast | Undifferent iated mesenchy mal cell | Forms bone, synthesizes Type I collagen, ALP activity | PTH & Vit D receptors; inhibited by glucocorticoids |
| Osteocyte | Trapped osteoblast (90% of bone cells) | Maintains bone homeostasis | Stimulated by calcitonin, inhibited by PTH |
| Osteoclast | Monocyte Bone lineage | resorption | Multinucleated, TRAP enzyme, Calcitonin receptor |
| Osteoprogenito r Cell | Endosteu m& periosteu m | Precursor for osteoblasts | Activated during fracture healing |

### Osteoblast Receptors
- PTH → Stimulates osteoclast activity via secondary messenger.
- 1,25(OH)₂ Vitamin D → Stimulates osteocalcin & matrix synthesis.
- Glucocorticoids → Inhibit DNA and collagen synthesis.
- Prostaglandins → Activate adenylate cyclase → resorption.
- Estrogen → Anabolic, prevents resorption.
Dr. Daivik’s Tip:
“Remember — PTH acts on osteoblasts first, not osteoclasts.”

### Bone Matrix

| Component | Composition | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Organic (40%) | Collagen (Type I – 90%), proteoglycans, non-collagenous proteins | Tensile strength |
| Inorganic (60%) | Calcium hydroxyapatite [Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂] | Compressive strength |

### Important Matrix Proteins

| Protein | Source | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Osteocalcin | Osteoblasts | Most abundant non-collagenous protein; marker of bone turnover |
| Osteonectin | Platelets, osteoblasts | Promotes mineralization |
| Osteopontin | Bone lining cells | Cell adhesion and binding |

Osteocalcin – Inhibited by PTH, stimulated by Vitamin D.

### Bone Remodeling
- Regulated by local & systemic hormones (PTH, Vit D, estrogen).
- Follows Wolff’s Law – bone adapts to mechanical stress.
### Cortical Remodeling
- Via cutting cones (osteoclastic tunnels followed by osteoblastic bone formation).
- Coupled resorption and formation ensure strength.

### Articular (Hyaline) Cartilage

| Property | Definition |
|---|---|
| Anisotropic | Varies with direction of stress |
| Biphasic | Acts as solid + fluid composite |
| Viscoelastic | Deforms under load, springs back on release |

Composition

| Component | % | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Water | 74% | Load bearing & lubrication |
| Collagen | 15% | Tensile stiffness |
| Proteoglycans | 10% | Compressive strength |
| Chondrocytes | 1% | Matrix synthesis |

Coefficient of friction: 0.003 – smoother than lubricated metal surfaces.

### Collagen in Cartilage

| Type | Location / Function |
|---|---|
| Type II | 90–95% of cartilage – main structural framework |
| Type IX | Cross-links Type II fibrils |
| Type XI | Regulates fibril diameter |
| Type X | Found near calcified cartilage & hypertrophic zone |

Exam Tip:
Calcified cartilage → Type X collagen.

### Proteoglycans & Aggrecan
- 95% chondroitin sulfate + 5% keratan sulfate.
- Carry negative charges, attract cations (Na⁺, Ca²⁺) → increase osmotic pressure → compressive strength.
- Linked to hyaluronan backbone → forms large aggregates that provide elasticity.

### Types of Ossification

| Type | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Endochondr al | Bone replaces cartilage model | Long bones, fracture callus |
| Intramembra nous | Direct ossification from mesenchyme | Flat bones, clavicle, distraction osteogenesis |
| Appositional | Bone width increase via periosteum | Periosteal bone enlargement |

MCQ Tip:
Endochondral ossification → not seen in distraction osteogenesis.

### Physis & Growth Plate Zones

| Zone | Function | Pathology |

Physeal fracture: Zone of provisional calcification.

Fracture Healing
Stages
1. Inflammation (Hematoma) → cytokines & progenitor recruitment.
2. Soft Callus (2 weeks) → fibrocartilage bridge.
3. Hard Callus → endochondral ossification.
4. Remodeling → woven → lamellar bone (Wolff’s law).

| Healing Type | Stability | Callus | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary (Direct) | Absolute | Absent | Cutting cones |
| Secondary | Relative | Present | Endochondral |

(Indirect) ossification

Callus formation ∝ movement; inversely ∝ stability.

as ECM calcifies.

### Growth Factors in Bone Healing

| Factor | Action |
|---|---|
| BMP | Osteoinductive; differentiates mesenchymal → osteoblasts |
| TGF-β | Induces Type II collagen & PG synthesis in callus |
| IGF-II | Promotes Type I collagen & cartilage matrix formation |
| PDGF | Chemotactic; recruits inflammatory cells to fracture site |

Concept: BMP = osteoinductive; calcium salts = osteoconductive.

### Bone Metabolism

| Element / Hormone | Function |
|---|---|
| Calcium | 99% in bone; absorbed in duodenum (Vit D–dependent) |

| Vitamin D | Formed in skin → 25-OH (liver) → 1,25-OH (kidney); increases Ca & PO₄ absorption |

### Exam / Viva Pearls
- Osteon = functional unit of bone.
- Osteocalcin – marker of bone turnover.
- Type I collagen – bone; Type II – cartilage; Type X – calcified cartilage.

<!-- page 8 -->

- Endochondral ossification – fracture callus, physis.
- Nutrient artery – high pressure, supplies 2/3 cortex.
- BMP – osteoinductive; Ca phosphate – osteoconductive.
- Fracture vascularity peak – 2 weeks post-injury.
- Wolff’s Law – bone adapts to mechanical stress.
