Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Scoliosis Part
3: Treatment of Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis
(2025)
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Adolescent Idiopathic Scoliosis (AIS) is a three-dimensional spinal deformity
occurring between 10–20 years of age without any identifiable cause.
Dr. Vishnu’s Quote:
“In scoliosis, timing is everything — treat the spine according to its growth, not
just its curve.”
Subtypes of Idiopathic Scoliosis:

Type Age Range

Infantile 0–3 years

Juvenile 4–9 years

Adolescent 10–20 years (most common)

Key Points: - AIS = 80% of all scoliosis cases.
- Female predominance (6:1).
- Commonest curve: Right thoracic (Lenke Type 1).
Natural History: - Rarely causes neurological deficit.
- Main issues → cosmetic deformity, back pain, and restrictive lung disease if >90°.
- Curves >90° → cardiopulmonary dysfunction.

|---|---|
| Before skeletal | >25° will progress. |

maturity
After skeletal maturity Thoracic >50° and Lumbar >40° progress at

Factors Affecting Progression: 1. Curve magnitude (>25° before maturity).
2. Skeletal maturity – assessed by Risser grading, triradiate cartilage, and greater trochanter fusion.
3. Curve pattern – thoracic > lumbar; double > single.

### Skeletal Maturity Assessment

| Parameter | Description |
|---|---|
| Risser’s Sign | Iliac apophysis ossification; Grades 0–5. |
| Triradiate Cartilage | Open → immature; Closed → near puberty. |
| Greater Trochanter Fusion | Indicates completion of growth. |

Risser Grading:

Grade Coverage Maturity

0 None Immature

1 25% Early growth

2 50% Active growth

3 75% Slowing

4 100% Nearly mature

5 Fused Mature

### MANAGEMENT OVERVIEW

| Cobb’s Angle | Maturity | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| <25° | Immature | Observation |
| 25°–45° | Immature & flexible | Bracing |
| >45° | Mature or progressive | Surgery |

Goals: - Prevent progression.
- Achieve a balanced spine.
- Preserve as much motion as possible.

OBSERVATION
Indications: - Cobb’s angle <25°.
- No progression on serial radiographs.
Follow-up: - Every 6–12 months.
- Every 6 months during growth spurt (Risser 0–2).
Radiographs: Standing whole-spine AP & lateral X-rays each visit.

BRACING
Indications: - Cobb’s 25°–45° in skeletally immature (Risser 0–2).
- Flexible curves only.
Goal: Prevent progression, not correct deformity.

Contraindications: - Skeletally mature (Risser 4–5).
- Curve >45° or <25°.
- Obesity.
- Thoracic lordosis.
Tip: Brace success depends on compliance, flexibility, and early detection.
SURGICAL MANAGEMENT
Indications: 1. Curve magnitude >45° (progressive).
2. Curve-related pain.
3. Cosmetic deformity.
4. Failure of brace.

### NEUROMONITORING

| Type | Monitored Pathway | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| SSEP | Sensory | Continuous; ↓ amplitude >50% → warning |
| MEP | Motor | Intermittent; ↓ amplitude → impending cord injury |

If Neurological Event Occurs: - Check technical issues.
- Correct hypotension or anemia.
- Reduce correction.
- Perform Stagnara’s wake-up test.
- Remove implant if instability persists.

SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS
Crankshaft Phenomenon
Occurs in immature spines when only posterior fusion is done — anterior column
continues to grow, causing rotation.
Prevention: - Avoid posterior-only fusion in <10 yrs.
- Use combined anterior + posterior fusion or growth-friendly procedures.

### Growth Rod Technique (Early Onset Cases)
- Dual rods anchored at proximal and distal ends.

- Distracted every 6 months.

- Preserves spinal and thoracic growth until skeletal maturity.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- AIS = scoliosis between 10–20 years.

- Cobb’s >45° → surgical indication.

- Bracing: 25°–45°, Risser 0–2, flexible.

- Posterior fusion = gold standard.

- Crankshaft → continued anterior growth after posterior fusion.

- Always use neuromonitoring (SSEP/MEP).

- Thoracic >50° or Lumbar >40° → progressive after maturity.

<!-- page 7 -->

- Flat-back = loss of lumbar lordosis after Harrington rods.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- AIS = most common scoliosis (10–20 yrs).

- Assess growth potential using Risser, triradiate cartilage, and trochanter fusion.

- Observe <25°, brace 25–45°, operate >45°.

- Posterior release + rod techniques = mainstay.

- Prevent crankshaft by avoiding early posterior fusion.

- Goal: Balanced, stable, and cosmetically acceptable spine at maturity.
