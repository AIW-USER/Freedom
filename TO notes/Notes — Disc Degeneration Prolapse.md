Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Disc
Degeneration & Disc Prolapse
Faculty: Dr. C.S. Vishnu Prasath

OVERVIEW
Definition:
Degenerative changes in the intervertebral disc lead to loss of hydration, height, and
biomechanical function. Herniation of the nucleus pulposus through the annulus fibrosus
results in disc prolapse, compressing adjacent nerve roots.
Common Sites: Lumbar (L4–L5, L5–S1) and Cervical regions (high mobility zones).
Age: Peak incidence 30–50 years.

ANATOMY & BIOMECHANICS
### Structure of Intervertebral Disc

| Component | Composition | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Annulus Fibrosus | Collagen fibers, fibroblasts | Resists tension, expands circumferentially |
| Nucleus Pulposus | Proteoglycans (GAGs), chondrocytes | Hydrophilic; absorbs axial load and converts it into radial force |

Nutrition:
- Avascular in adults.
- Nutrients diffuse through vertebral endplates.
- Degeneration begins with endplate sclerosis → impaired diffusion → disc desiccation.

DISC DEGENERATION
Pathophysiology
1. Endplate sclerosis → reduced nutrient diffusion.

2. Loss of hydration → nucleus hardens.

<!-- page 2 -->

3. Decreased elasticity → abnormal load transfer to annulus.

  4. Annular fissuring → potential for prolapse.

### Pfirrmann’s MRI Grading of Disc Degeneration

| Grade | MRI Appearance | Description |
|---|---|---|
| I | Homogeneous white nucleus | Normal disc |
| II | Non-uniform white, few dark spots | Early degeneration |
| III | Grey nucleus, nucleus–annulus distinction present | Moderate degeneration |
| IV | Uniform dark, indistinct nucleus–annulus boundary | Advanced degeneration |
| V | Collapsed disc space | Severe degeneration |

DISC PROLAPSE
Definition:
Displacement of nucleus pulposus through annulus fibrosus → nerve root compression
→ radiculopathy.

### Types (Morphological Classification)

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Protrusion | Nucleus bulges but annulus intact |
| Extrusion | Nucleus breaches annulus but remains continuous with parent disc |
| Sequestration | Free fragment separated from parent disc |

### Based on Location (Axial MRI)

| Type | Region Involved |
|---|---|
| Central | Thecal sac compression |
| Paracentral | Most common; compresses traversing root |
| Foraminal | Compresses exiting root |
| Extraforaminal | Lateral to foramen; compresses exiting root |

CLINICAL FEATURES
Symptoms
- Low back pain ± radiating leg pain (sciatica).

- Pain exacerbated by bending, lifting, coughing, sneezing.

- Relieved by lying supine with hip and knee flexion.

- May have paresthesia, numbness, or weakness in root distribution.

- Severe cases → bladder/bowel dysfunction (Cauda Equina Syndrome).

INVESTIGATIONS
### X-ray (Standing Lateral)
- Initial imaging modality.

- Rules out instability, spondylolysis/spondylolisthesis.
### MRI
- Investigation of choice.

- Indicates disc herniation, level, direction (central/paracentral/foraminal), and migration (superior/inferior).
Indications for MRI: - Persistent symptoms >6 weeks.
- Neurological deficit (e.g., foot drop).
- Cauda equina symptoms.
- Suspicion of infection or malignancy.

MANAGEMENT
1⃣ Conservative (First-line for majority)
- Rest (2–7 days).

<!-- page 5 -->

- NSAIDs and Pregabalin for neuropathic pain.

- Gentle back exercises after pain subsides.

- Physiotherapy: pelvic traction, IFT, ultrasound massage (optional).

- Nerve root block under CT/fluoroscopy for refractory radiculopathy.
  - Local anesthetic + steroid at affected root.

  - Temporary relief by reducing inflammation.
### 2⃣ Surgical Indications
- Failure of conservative therapy.

- Progressive neurological deficit.

- Cauda equina syndrome.

CAUDA EQUINA SYNDROME (Hard-Eichen Syndrome)
Features: - Severe low back pain.
- Bilateral sciatica.
- Saddle anesthesia.
- Bladder, bowel, sexual dysfunction.
Cause: Large central disc prolapse at lower lumbar levels.
Management: Urgent wide laminectomy and decompression.

<!-- page 6 -->

### COMPLICATIONS
- Persistent back pain.

- Recurrent disc prolapse (~5%).

- Surgical site infection.

- Postoperative instability.

### VIVA & EXAM PEARLS
- Most common level: L4–L5 > L5–S1.

- Pain relief with flexion (hip and knee) → diagnostic clue.

- Radicular pain: constant; Claudication pain: on walking.

- Foot drop: L5 root; Toe walking difficulty: S1 root.

- MRI correlation: MRI finding must match clinical symptoms before surgery.

- Pfirrmann’s Grading – essential viva question.

- Never miss: Cauda equina syndrome.

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Disc degeneration begins with endplate sclerosis → loss of hydration → annular tear → prolapse.

- Majority improve with conservative care within 6 weeks.

- MRI only when persistent symptoms or neuro-deficit.

- Surgery reserved for failure of conservative therapy or cauda equina.

- Micro-lumbar discectomy remains the gold standard for lumbar disc herniation.
