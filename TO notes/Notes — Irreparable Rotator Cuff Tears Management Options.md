Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Irreparable Rotator Cuff Tears: Management
Options (SCR & Tendon Transfers)
Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### OVERVIEW
- Irreparable Rotator Cuff Tears (RCT) are defined as tears >5 cm, involving ≥2 tendons, with retraction to the glenoid and fatty infiltration >50% (Goutallier Grade III+).
- Characterized by static superior migration of the humeral head, acromiohumeral interval <5 mm, and loss of cuff function. “When the rotator cuff fails, our task is to recreate a stable fulcrum for the humeral head — by restoring biomechanics, not necessarily anatomy.” – Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### BIOMECHANICAL PRINCIPLES
- Stable fulcrum: Core requirement for functional shoulder abduction.
- Compression effect: Rotator cuff compresses humeral head into glenoid.
- Deltoid acts as prime mover, but needs the cuff to stabilize head inferiorly.
- In massive cuff tears, this fulcrum is lost → humeral head migrates superiorly. Goal of surgery: Restore balanced force couples (anterior–posterior and superior–inferior stability).

### DIAGNOSTIC CONSIDERATIONS
- Posterior–superior tears → involve supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor (most common).
- Anterior–superior tears → involve subscapularis, less frequent.
### Clinical Clues:
- Loss of active elevation.
- Positive drop arm and hornblower sign.
- Lag signs for subscapularis and external rotators.

SUPERIOR CAPSULAR RECONSTRUCTION (SCR)
Concept:
- Provides a static constraint preventing superior humeral migration, recreating a stable fulcrum.
- Initially described by Mihata et al., 2013 using Tensor Fascia Lata (TFL) autograft.
### Graft Options:

| Graft | Source | Comments |
|---|---|---|
| Autograft | Tensor Fascia Lata | Strong but donor site morbidity |
| Allograft | Dermal / Fascia | Avoids morbidity but costly |
| Synthetic grafts | Various materials | Easier handling, long-term unknown |

### Technique Summary:
1. Arthroscopic debridement of cuff remnants.
2. Fix graft medially to superior glenoid, laterally to greater tuberosity.
3. Tension adjusted to restore acromiohumeral interval (~8 mm).
4. Fix with suture anchors medially and laterally. Biomechanical Role: Recreates superior restraint → deltoid regains effective lever arm.
Problems:
- Donor site morbidity (TFL).
- High cost of allograft.
- Technically demanding; long learning curve.

VARIANTS – LHBT-BASED RECONSTRUCTIONS
### 1⃣ Biceps Augmentation / Anterior Cable Reconstruction
- LHB tenotomy distally → transposed posteriorly to supraspinatus footprint.
- Maintains glenoid attachment.
- Restores anterior cable tension → stabilizes humeral head.
2⃣ Biceps Rerouting (Kim et al.)
- LHBT kept intact proximally; freed from bicipital groove.
- New groove created posteriorly at cuff footprint; tendon fixed with cinch anchors.
Advantages:

- All-inside, simple, no donor morbidity.
- LHBT acts as internal splint & depressor of humeral head.
- Provides biological tenodesis effect. Decision: Depends on biceps tendon quality.

### SUBACROMIAL SPACER BALLOON (InSpace – STRYKER)
- Biodegradable saline-filled balloon inserted between humerus & acromion.
- Maintains subacromial distance, reducing friction and pain.
### Ideal Candidates:
- Low-demand, comorbid patients with irreparable tear.
- Pain-dominant presentation; preserved motion.
- Not suitable for pseudoparalysis or glenohumeral arthritis.
### Key Points:
- Sized intraoperatively; prefer larger size if borderline.
- Avoid overinflation → deltoid tension.
- Avoid underinflation → risk of balloon escape. Temporary mechanical spacer aiding rehabilitation and pain relief (resorbs over ~1 year).

### TENDON TRANSFER PRINCIPLES
- Indicated in younger, active patients with irreparable tears.
- Goals: Restore balanced force couples, humeral head depression, and specific lost motion.
### Basic Principles (Gerber):
1. Donor must be expendable.
2. Similar excursion and tension as recipient.
3. Matching line of pull.
4. Substitute at least one function of lost muscle.

### PECTORALIS MAJOR TRANSFER (Anterosuperior Tears)
- Approach: Deltopectoral.
- Technique: Sternal head detached and transferred beneath conjoined tendon → fixed to lesser tuberosity.
- Mechanics: Restores internal rotation; compensates for subscapularis. Caution: Line of pull differs from subscapularis → may cause anterior humeral translation.
Innervation: Medial & lateral pectoral nerves (~12 cm from insertion).
Blood supply: Pectoral branch of thoracoacromial artery.

### LATISSIMUS DORSI TRANSFER (Posterosuperior Tears)
- Origin: Posterior chest wall (T7–L5, iliac crest, ribs 10–12).
- Insertion: Intertubercular groove.
- Transfer: Tendon released, passed between deltoid & cuff → fixed to greater tuberosity.
- Purpose: Restores external rotation and humeral head depression.
Nerve: Thoracodorsal nerve (C6–C8).
Artery: Thoracodorsal artery.
Originally described by Gerber (1988); reconstitutes posterior force couple.

### LOWER TRAPEZIUS TRANSFER (Alternative to LD)
- Origin: Spinous processes C7–T12.
- Insertion: Medial spine of scapula & acromion.
- Line of pull closely matches infraspinatus.
- Restores external rotation & humeral head depression.
Advantages over LD:
- More anatomic line of pull.
- Synergistic firing pattern.
- Better restoration of rotation and overhead function.
Caution: Spinal accessory nerve lies 2 cm medial to scapular border.

### EXAM & VIVA PEARLS
- Irreparable cuff: >5 cm, >2 tendons, fatty infiltration >50%, AH interval <5 mm.
- Posterior-superior tears are more common.
- SCR recreates superior constraint; Mihata 2013.
- TFL autograft: Gold standard for SCR.
- LHBT rerouting (Kim): All-inside, biological SCR substitute.
- LD Transfer: Gerber 1988; restores external rotation.
- Lower Trapezius Transfer: Elhassan – anatomical, now preferred.
- Pectoralis Major Transfer: For anterosuperior tears.
- Subacromial Spacer Balloon: Low-demand, pain-dominant patients.
- Rehab: 0–4 wks immobilization; full ROM by 3 months.
