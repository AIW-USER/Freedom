Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Meniscal Injuries: Resection & Repair
Techniques
Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### OVERVIEW
- Orthopedics has shifted focus from meniscal resection to preservation.
- Meniscus provides rotational stability, shock absorption, and load distribution.
- Meniscectomy increases risk of early osteoarthritis; hence, current approach is meniscal preservation and repair. Quote: “Preserve the meniscus whenever possible – resect only what cannot be saved.” – Dr. Mukul Mohindra

ANATOMY & FUNCTION
### Meniscus Anatomy
- Medial meniscus: C-shaped, less mobile, more fixed to MCL.
- Lateral meniscus: Circular, more mobile, bears more weight (~70%).
- Thickness: 12–16 mm.
- Attachments:
  - Anterior/Posterior horns: To intercondylar area of tibia.
  - Body: Attached to tibia via coronary (meniscotibial) ligament.
  - Lateral meniscus additionally stabilized by meniscofemoral ligaments (Ligaments of Humphrey & Wrisberg).
### Blood Supply
- Outer 2–4 mm supplied by genicular branches (inferior geniculate artery).
- Zones:
  - Red-red zone: Vascular.
  - Red-white zone: Partial vascular.
  - White-white zone: Avascular.

Function
- Converts tibial surface from flat to concave for rotational stability.
- Shock absorption via redistribution of synovial fluid.
- Provides nutrition to articular cartilage and distributes load.

### BIOMECHANICS & COMPOSITION
- Composition: Type I collagen (predominant) + Type III collagen + elastin.
- Fiber Orientation:
  - Circumferential fibers resist hoop stress.
  - Radial fibers resist compressive stress.
  - Interwoven fibers resist shear.

MENISCAL TEARS
### Common Tear Patterns

| Tear Type | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Longitudinal (Vertical) | Along meniscal length | Commonest in medial meniscus |
| Radial | Perpendicular to circumference | Common in lateral meniscus |
| Horizontal | Splits upper/lower lamina | Seen in degenerative cases |
| Bucket Handle | Displaced inner fragment | Advanced longitudinal tear |
| Parrot Beak | Flap-like | Variant of radial tear |
| Flap Tear | Partial thickness tear | Common degenerative |
| Ramp Lesion | Tear of posterior meniscocapsular junction | Seen with ACL injury |

Medial meniscus tears are 5–6× more common due to relative fixation.
### O’Donoghue’s Triad
- Classical (old): ACL + MCL + Medial meniscus.
- Current understanding: ACL + MCL + Lateral meniscus.
- Chronic ACL deficiency: Medial meniscus tear predominates.

### MRI SIGNS

| MRI Sign | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Double PCL Sign | Bucket-handle tear of medial meniscus |
| Fragment in Notch Sign | Displaced meniscal fragment |
| Double Delta Sign | Flipped meniscus |
| Ghost Sign | Disappearing posterior horn (extruded meniscus) |
| Absent Bow-Tie Sign | Meniscal detachment or loss |
| Tram Track Appearance | Mucoid degeneration of PCL |

Prerequisite for Double PCL Sign: Intact ACL & PCL.

SURGICAL MANAGEMENT
Meniscectomy (Resection)
- Indications: Complex, degenerative, or avascular zone (white-white) tears.
- Principles:
  - Excise only unstable fragment.
  - Preserve 2–4 mm peripheral rim.

- Hybrid Repair: Combination of techniques (used for bucket-handle tears).
- Suture Material: UHMW Polyethylene (FiberWire / FiberTape).
- Preferred Configurations: Vertical or Horizontal Mattress.
### Repair Zones
- Red-red: Always repair.
- Red-white: Repair with augmentation (PRP/fibrin clot).
- White-white: Resection (avascular, poor healing).
### Augmentation Methods:
- Synovial abrasion or RASP device.
- Vascular access channels (meniscal refenestration).
- PRP or fibrin clot injection.
- Combined ACL reconstruction provides natural biological stimulation.

### REHABILITATION PROTOCOL

| Phase | Duration | Key Points |
|---|---|---|
| Phase I (0–6 wks) | Brace, no weight-bearing | Isometrics only |
| Phase II (6–12 wks) | Partial weight-bearing, ROM 0–90° | Avoid hamstring activation |
| Phase III (3–6 mo) | Gradual full WB, quadriceps strengthening | Closed-chain exercises |
| Phase IV (>6 mo) | Proprioception & sport-specific drills | Full return when pain-free |

MENISCAL ALLOGRAFT TRANSPLANTATION (MAT)
Indications:
- Symptomatic post-meniscectomy young patient.
- No advanced arthritis / malalignment.
- Intact ligaments.
Techniques:
- Bone Plug Technique: Cylindrical bone blocks fixed in tibial tunnels.
- Bone Bridge Technique: Single keyhole-shaped bone bridge → slot fixation (preferred).
### Graft Preservation:

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| Fresh | Best viability, 1-week shelf life |
| Fresh-Frozen | Commonest, –80°C, good balance |
| Lyophilized | Dehydrated, long storage but weak biomechanics |
| Cryopreserved | Glycerol-based, best cell viability, costly |

### EXAM PEARLS
- Lateral meniscus bears more load (70%).
- Medial meniscus tears are more common.
- Meniscofemoral ligaments: Humphrey (ant), Wrisberg (post).
- Thessaly test – Most specific.
- Red-red zone = repair, white-white zone = resect.
- Double PCL sign = Bucket-handle tear.
- Ramp lesion = Posterior meniscocapsular tear.
- MAT indicated in symptomatic post-meniscectomy knees.

Summary: Meniscus preservation is the current gold standard. Understanding tear
pattern, vascular zone, and repair options determines success in modern arthroscopic
practice.
