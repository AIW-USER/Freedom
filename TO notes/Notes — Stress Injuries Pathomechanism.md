Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Stress Injuries:
Basic Pathomechanism & Mechanism
Faculty: Dr. Shikha Dhaundiyal
Department: Sports & Exercise Medicine

OVERVIEW
Definition: Stress injuries are overuse injuries caused by repetitive submaximal forces
exceeding the adaptive capacity of bone.
Common Sites: Tibia, Metatarsals, Fibula (weight-bearing bones).
Other Sites: Femur, Pelvis, Ribs, Scapula, Ulna (in specific sports).
Dr. Shikha’s Quote: “Microtrauma becomes a fracture only when
adaptation fails.”

### PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
- Intermittent mechanical loading stimulates bone remodeling (Wolff’s Law).

- Balance between damage and repair maintains bone integrity.

- When repetitive strain exceeds the repair capacity → microdamage accumulation → stress reaction → stress fracture → complete fracture.
Continuum:
Repetitive submaximal force → Bone microdamage → Remodeling → Imbalance →
Stress reaction → Stress fracture.

### RISK FACTORS

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Biomechanical | Limb length discrepancy, Pes planus/cavus, External rotation deficit of hip |
| Training | Rapid increase in intensity, duration, or frequency (FIT principle) |
| Muscular | Fatigue, Weakness, Poor shock absorption |
| Playing Surface | Hard or inconsistent terrain, downhill slopes |
| Footwear | Worn-out shoes, improper insoles, anti-pronation or motion-control shoes |
| Physiological | Low Vitamin D/Calcium, Energy deficiency, Female Athlete Triad (RED-S) |

“The wrong surface and bad shoes are silent contributors to every stress
injury.”

HISTORY & CLINICAL FEATURES
Pain Pattern: - Mild, diffuse pain after specific activity; later becomes focal.
- Initially resolves with rest; later persists at rest or night.
- Aggravated by repetitive loading; no relief with warm-up.
Special Considerations (Females):
- Take detailed menstrual history (LMP, regularity, duration, flow, contraceptive use,
discharge).
- Assess nutrition, BMI, weight changes, and energy intake (Female Athlete Triad).

CLINICAL EXAMINATION
Observation:
- Gait, limb alignment, pelvic tilt, flat foot, valgus/varus deformities.
Palpation:

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Diffuse tenderness | Stress reaction |
| Focal tenderness | Stress fracture |
| Warmth/Redness | Inflammation or acute phase |

BIOMECHANICAL ASSESSMENT
### Tibiofemoral Alignment (Q Angle):
- Definition: Angle between ASIS–patella and patella–tibial tubercle.

- Normal: 14° (Males), 17° (Females).

- ↑ Q-angle: Predisposes to patellar maltracking, stress injuries, and ACL injuries.

<!-- page 4 -->

### Foot Posture Index (FPI):

| FPI Score | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| −5 to −12 | Highly supinated |
| −1 to −4 | Supinated |
| 0 to +5 +6 to +9 | Neutral Pronated |
| > +10 | Highly pronated |

Normal FPI ≈ 2.4 (slightly pronated foot).

### Sport-Specific Technique Errors

| Sport | Technique Error | Likely Injury |
|---|---|---|
| Tennis | Excessive backhand wrist action | Extensor tendinopathy |
| Swimming | Low elbow recovery | Rotator cuff tendinopathy |
| Cycling | High saddle or wrong handlebar | Lumbar/SI pain |
| Weightlifting | Bar too forward / Wide grip | Lumbar or pectoralis injury |
| Pole Vault | Late plantarflexion | Ankle impingement |
| Running | Anterior pelvic tilt / Poor control | Hamstring / ITB syndrome |
| Cricket | Mixed action | Pars interarticularis fracture |
| Ballet | Poor turnout | Hip & medial knee pain |

INVESTIGATIONS
### Blood Tests:
- Serum calcium, Vitamin D, PTH, CBC, Thyroid profile.

Imaging:

| Modality | Key Features |
|---|---|
| X-ray | Seen after 2–8 weeks; low sensitivity (12–56%), high specificity (88–96%) |
| Scintigraphy | Early detection (24–36 hrs); ↑ tracer uptake but nonspecific |
| CT Scan | Best for cortical fracture line |
| MRI | Gold standard; detects early marrow edema (sensitivity 68–99%) |
| Ultrasound | Detects periosteal thickening, edema, callus; Doppler for hypervascularity |

MANAGEMENT
### 1⃣ Stress Fractures Requiring Specific Treatment

| Site | Management |
|---|---|
| Femoral Neck | Bed rest (1 wk), gradual WB; if displaced → surgery |
| Anterior Tibial Cortex | NWB 6–8 wks or IM screw fixation |
| Medial Malleolus | Cast 6 wks or surgical fixation |
| Talus | NWB cast 6 wks or fragment excision |
| Navicular | NWB cast 6–8 wks or surgery |
| 2nd Metatarsal Base | NWB 2 wks → Partial WB 2 wks |
| 5th Metatarsal Base | Cast 6 wks or percutaneous screw |
| Sesamoid | NWB 4 wks |

### 2⃣ Initial Management Principles
- Activity Modification: Ensure pain-free gait; use pneumatic brace or crutches if needed.

- Footwear Correction: Rearfoot → cushioned; mid/forefoot → stiff insoles.

- Pain Control: Short-term NSAIDs only.

- Risk Factor Correction: Training errors, nutrition, BMI, Vitamin D, menstrual health.

- Muscle Strengthening: Hip–knee–ankle chain, intrinsic & extrinsic foot muscles.

- Physical Conditioning: Swimming, cycling, deep-water running, anti-gravity treadmill (AGT) when pain-free.

### 3⃣ Accelerated Tissue Healing Modalities

| Modality | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| LIPUS | Promotes early pain relief and bone repair |
| ECSWT | Stimulates growth factors; avoid if pain >5/10 |
| TECAR Therapy | Deep heat → faster recovery and pain reduction |

### RETURN-TO-RUNNING PROTOCOL

| Stage | Activity | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Pain-free walking ≥5 days | – |
| 1 | Walk + Jog (50% pace) | Alternate days |
| 2 | Gradually ↑ speed (60–100%) | With rest days |
| 3 | Run on consecutive days | 2 days run → 1 rest |
| 4 | Return to full activity | Pain-free, stable gait |

AGT Protocol:
- Start after 1 week of pain-free walking.
- 3×5 min jogs @ 50–70% BW, 1 min rest each.
- ↑ Speed & body weight by 5–10% every 2 weeks → resume ground running at 90%
BW.
“Return should be earned, not rushed — pain-free strides mean proper
healing.”

### TAKE-HOME SUMMARY
- Stress injuries = imbalance between bone loading & remodeling.

- MRI = gold standard for early detection.

- Low-risk vs High-risk dictates management urgency.

- Preventive focus: Training load, biomechanics, nutrition, menstrual health.

- Rehab focus: Strengthen kinetic chain + correct footwear + gradual load return. “A runner’s best armor is adaptation — build, recover, repeat.”
