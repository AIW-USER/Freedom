Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Recurrent Dislocation Patella (RDP)
Faculty: Dr. Mukul Mohindra

OVERVIEW
Recurrent Dislocation of Patella (RDP) is a common cause of anterior knee pain and
instability, especially in adolescents and young adults. It results from a combination of
soft tissue laxity, bony malalignment, and muscular imbalance. Understanding
predisposing factors and choosing the correct realignment procedure are essential.
Quote: “Each recurrent dislocation tells you two things — the MPFL is torn,
and the anatomy is wrong. Fix both.” – Dr. Mukul Mohindra

### CLASSIFICATION / TYPES OF DISLOCATION

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Recurrent | Repeated episodes after initial traumatic event |
| Habitual | Dislocation with every flexion, relocation in extension |
| Congenital | Present since birth, usually due to severe bony dysplasia |
| Acute | First-time dislocation with hemarthrosis |

Traumatic
Recurrent dislocation is the most common form seen in clinical practice.

ANATOMICAL BASIS
### Static Stabilizers:
- Medial Patellofemoral Ligament (MPFL) – major restraint to lateral translation (60–70%)
- Medial retinaculum
- Patellomeniscal & patellotibial ligaments
### Dynamic Stabilizers:
- Vastus Medialis Obliquus (VMO) – counters lateral pull
- Quadriceps mechanism (especially in terminal extension)
### Bony Stabilizer:
- Trochlear Groove – acts as a track; shallowness predisposes to dislocation. MPFL lies in Layer II of medial knee anatomy (between Layers I & III – Warren & Marshall).

PREDISPOSING FACTORS
### Soft-Tissue Related
- MPFL rupture (post-trauma)
- VMO atrophy
- Tight IT band / lateral retinaculum
### Bony Factors
- Trochlear dysplasia (most important)
- Patella alta (high-riding patella)
- Increased TT–TG distance (>20 mm)
- High Q angle (>20° in females)
- Femoral anteversion / External tibial torsion
- Genu valgum

- Merchant view: assesses patellar subluxation and trochlear depth
- Lateral view: evaluates patellar height

### CT Scan:
- TT–TG (Tibial Tubercle–Trochlear Groove) distance: Normal <15 mm; Pathologic >20 mm.
- Trochlear depth and lateral inclination – assess trochlear dysplasia.
### MRI:
- Best for MPFL integrity, cartilage damage, and associated osteochondral fragments.

### DEJOUR CLASSIFICATION OF TROCHLEAR DYSPLASIA

| Type | Radiological Feature |
|---|---|
| A | Shallow trochlea, convex floor |
| B | Flat trochlea with supratrochlear spur |
| C | Asymmetric facets, lateral convexity |
| D | Cliff pattern with deep groove + spur |

Key takeaway: The higher the Dejour grade, the more the risk of recurrence.

### CLINICAL EVALUATION
- Tenderness over MPFL course
- Apprehension test: lateral pressure → apprehension in 20–30° flexion
- Patellar glide test: excessive lateral translation (>3 quadrants)
- Patellar tilt test: inability to lift lateral border off femur
- “J” sign: lateral jump of patella during terminal extension

MANAGEMENT APPROACH
### A. First-time (Acute) Dislocation
- Closed reduction if unreduced
- Immobilization: Knee in extension for 3 weeks
- Rehabilitation: Quadriceps and VMO strengthening
- MRI to rule out osteochondral fracture or MPFL tear If Merchant view shows subluxation or osteochondral fragment → consider arthroscopy + repair.

<!-- page 4 -->

B. Recurrent Dislocation
Surgery is the mainstay once conservative measures fail.
1. Proximal Realignment Procedures
- MPFL Reconstruction: cornerstone of treatment.
- VMO advancement
- Lateral retinacular release
2. Distal Realignment Procedures
- Tibial Tubercle Osteotomy (Elmslie–Trillat / Fulkerson):
  - Medial shift: corrects TT–TG >20 mm.
  - Distal shift: corrects patella alta.
  - Anteromedialization (Fulkerson): improves patellar tracking & decreases contact pressure.
3. Trochleoplasty
- Sulcus deepening or Facet elevation for severe trochlear dysplasia (Dejour C/D).
Question Example: 17-year-old female, TT–TG = 19 mm, shallow trochlea →
Treatment?
Answer: Trochleoplasty + Proximal realignment.

MPFL RECONSTRUCTION – KEY CONCEPTS
### Graft Options:
- Ipsilateral Gracilis (most preferred)
- Superficial Quadriceps (Konkani et al.)
### Femoral Attachment Point:
- Between adductor tubercle & medial femoral epicondyle.
- Too proximal → tight in flexion.
- Too distal → tight in extension.
Fixation:
- Fix graft in 30–45° knee flexion.
- Confirm by checking lateral patellar translation (should tighten only on lateral translation). Layer Location: MPFL lies in Layer II – between Medial Retinaculum and Joint Capsule.

### SURGICAL PEARLS
- Always perform diagnostic arthroscopy first.

<!-- page 5 -->

- Address concomitant osteochondral lesions.
- Avoid overtightening the graft → leads to medial overload.
- Combine with soft tissue fixation to VMO if required.
- Roux–Goldthwaithe / Fulkerson modification as adjuncts for distal realignment. Caution: Excessive tightening may cause increased medial facet pressure.

### COMMON COMPLICATIONS

| Complication | Cause / Comment |
|---|---|
| Overtight MPFL graft | Medial facet overload |
| Graft loosening | Improper fixation / tunnel placement |
| Residual instability | Missed trochlear dysplasia or high TT–TG |
| Stiffness | Prolonged immobilization |
| Fracture / Hardware irritation | Tibial tubercle osteotomy |

### EXAM PEARLS & TAKEAWAYS
- MPFL lies in Layer II of knee anatomy.
- Insall–Salvati ratio = best for patellar height.
- TT–TG >20 mm → distal realignment indicated.
- Dejour Type D dysplasia → Trochleoplasty.
- Superficial Quadriceps technique → Konkani et al.
- MPFL fixed in 30–45° flexion.
- Always perform arthroscopy before reconstruction.
- Miserable malalignment triad: ↑ femoral anteversion + genu valgum + external tibial torsion.
