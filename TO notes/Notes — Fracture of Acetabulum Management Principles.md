Fracture of Acetabulum – Management Principles –
Dr. Sanjay Arora
Overview
Management of acetabular fractures demands a clear understanding of fracture
morphology, congruency, stability, surgical approaches, and meticulous reduction
techniques. The aim is to restore joint congruency, preserve the sciatic nerve, protect
vascular structures, and achieve stable fixation allowing early mobilization.
Faculty Remark: “Your approach depends on the fracture. Your reduction depends
on your exposure. And the outcome depends on getting both right.”

## Core Principles
### Initial Priorities
- Hemodynamic stabilization.
- Evaluate for associated injuries.
- Assess congruency and stability.
- Decide surgical approach based on fracture pattern.
### Key Determinants
1. Is the hip congruent?
2. Is the hip stable?
3. What is the fracture pattern?
If incongruent or unstable → surgery is mandatory.

## Surgical Approaches – Overview
Dr. Arora focuses on two major approaches: - Ilioinguinal Approach (anterior) -
Anterior Intrapelvic / Modified Stoppa Approach - Kocher Langenbeck Approach
(posterior)
Approach selection depends entirely on fracture pattern, displacement direction, and
location of major fragments.

## Ilioinguinal Approach
Indications
- Anterior wall fractures.
- Anterior column fractures.
- Associated both column fractures.
- Anterior column with posterior hemitransverse.
- Transverse fractures with anterior displacement.
- T type fractures where anterior column is dominant.
### Not Suitable For
- Typical posterior wall fractures.
- Posterior column injuries.
- Posterior wall + posterior column.
- Transverse fractures displaced posteriorly.

Positioning
- Supine.
- Foley catheter mandatory.
- Lumbosacral bump to drop pelvis.
- Hip flexion to relax iliopsoas.
- Prep widely to chest and buttock.

### Three Surgical Windows
1. Lateral Window
- Exposure of iliac crest, anterior ilium.
- Useful for anterior column plating.
2. Middle Window
- Most critical window.
- Structures encountered:
  - Inguinal canal contents.
  - Ilioinguinal nerve.
  - Lateral femoral cutaneous nerve.
  - Femoral vessels.
- Iliopectineal fascia must be identified and incised for access.
3. Medial Window / Anterior Intrapelvic (Stoppa) Window
- Access to:

<!-- page 3 -->

- Symphysis.
  - Superior pubic ramus.
  - Quadrilateral plate.
  - Posterior column (indirect).
- Requires careful bladder retraction.
- Corona mortis must be identified and ligated.
Important: Incising iliopectineal fascia opens the entire anterior pelvis for reduction.

## Anterior Intrapelvic (Modified Stoppa) Approach
Indications
- Anterior column fractures.
- Quadrilateral plate fractures.
- Both column fractures.

### Key Steps
- T type fractures.

- Midline rectus split along linea alba.
- Protect bladder using wet mop and malleable retractor.
- Identify and ligate corona mortis.
- Incise iliopectineal fascia until SI joint.
- Retract neurovascular bundle to expose medial wall and quadrilateral plate.

## Fracture Exposure and Reduction
### Exposure Principles
- Fracture hematoma is left untouched until full exposure.

### Reduction Tools
- Allows bleeding control and clear visualization.

- Shanz pin in iliac pillar for joystick manipulation.
- Ball spike pusher.
- Multiple clamps: Farabeuf, Matta, Parareva.
- Under contoured plates for indirect reduction.
Tip: Always reduce the column first, then the wall.

## Fixation Principles
Plating Options
### Common Surfaces
- Pelvic brim (superior ramus).
- Intrapelvic quadrilateral surface.
- Combined suprapectineal and infrapectineal plates.
### Screw Principles
- Independent screws possible but must avoid plate path.
- Divergent screw placement increases stability.
- Buttress plate essential for comminuted wall.
### Wound Care
- Drains placed in Space of Retzius and internal iliac fossa.
- Layered closure prevents herniation.

## Kocher Langenbeck Approach (Posterior)
Indications
- Posterior wall fractures.
- Posterior column fractures.
- Transverse + posterior wall.

Positioning
- Certain T type fractures.

- Lateral position.

### Key Steps
- Hip and knee flexed to relax sciatic nerve.

- Split fascia lata.
- Release gluteus maximus tendon.
- Tag and detach short external rotators.
- Expose sciatic notch.

### Reduction Techniques
- Identify posterior wall and column.

- Clamps applied across posterior wall.
- Shanz pins for joystick reduction.
- Use of under contoured plate for reduction.

<!-- page 5 -->

Fixation
- Buttress plating of posterior wall.
- Lag screws for large fragments.
- Consider trochanteric flip for dome exposure.

## Case Based Insight (From Lecture)
- Both column fracture pattern.
- Reduction sequence:
  1. Reduce anterior column to intact ilium.
  2. Plate pelvic brim.
  3. Reduce posterior column.
  4. Add posterior column screws through pelvic brim.
  5. Intrapelvic buttress plate for added stability.

### Exam Pearls
- Approach depends strictly on fracture pattern.
- Ilioinguinal has three windows; middle window has major neurovascular structures.
- Corona mortis must always be ligated.
- Iliopectineal fascia is the key barrier that must be opened.
- Stoppa approach offers excellent access to quadrilateral plate.
- Posterior wall fractures → Kocher Langenbeck.
- Under contoured plates help achieve reduction.
- Divergent screws improve stability.
- Sciatic nerve at risk during posterior approach.

Final Faculty Insight: “Choose your approach wisely. A wrong approach ruins a
perfect plan. Pelvic and acetabular surgery is all about exposure, protection, and
precision.”
