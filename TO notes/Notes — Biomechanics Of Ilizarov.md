Biomechanics of Ilizarov – Dr. Daivik T Shetty
Overview
The Ilizarov apparatus functions on the principle of stability with micromotion,
allowing controlled axial load transmission that promotes union, deformity correction,
and distraction osteogenesis. Understanding the biomechanical behavior of rings, wires,
and tension is essential for optimal outcomes.
Faculty Remark: “Ilizarov is not rigid, it is controlled instability. The moment you
understand that, the entire system becomes logical.”

## Core Biomechanical Principles
### Stability With Micromotion
- Ilizarov provides controlled axial micromotion, essential for bone healing.
- Not designed for rigid fixation. Excessive rigidity leads to stress shielding and delayed union.
- Optimal micromotion: <1 mm under physiological loading.
### Types of Micromotion and Their Effect

| Type of Micromotion | Result |
|---|---|
| Angular | Horse-hoof nonunion |
| Rotatory | Hypertrophy |
| Distraction/Side-to-side | Atrophic nonunion |
| Dynamic compressive | Ideal for union |

micromotion

## Why Ilizarov Is Superior to Other Fixators
### Uniplanar Fixators
- Stable only in plane of fixator.
- Unstable at 90 degrees to plane.
- Cantilever effect causes shear, bending, and reduced axial motion.
### Delta Frames
- Very stiff.
- Cancel instability of individual fixators but eliminate axial micromotion.
- Known as “nonunion machines”.

<!-- page 2 -->

Orthofix
- Axially unstable by design.
- Stable in bending and rotation, but still not ideal.
### Ilizarov (Circular Fixator)
- Beam bending rather than cantilever bending.
- Pins supported at both ends.
- 16 times less bending under axial load.
- Superior bending, torsional, and shear stability.

## Non Linear Stiffness – Key Concept
- Ilizarov exhibits low stiffness under physiological load and high stiffness under increased load.
- Due to tensioned thin wires acting like beams and cables.
- Provides micromotion when needed, stiffness when overloaded.

## Bicycle Spoke Configuration
- Arrangement of wires resists angular and rotational micromotion.
- Allows surgeon-controlled axial compression and distraction.

## Wire Tensioning
- Wires must be tensioned using dynamometer/tensioner.
- Recommended: 50 percent of wire’s yield load.
### Safe Tensioning Limits

| Wire Diameter | Max Safe Load |
|---|---|
| 1.5 mm | ~212 kg |
| 1.8 mm | ~305 kg |

### Distraction Histogenesis
- Promotes cell division and bone formation.

- Controlled tension → new bone and soft tissue formation.
- Always follows the vector of applied force.
- Works in all age groups if healing potential persists.
Principles
- Mechanical induction of new bone.
- Neovascularization.
- Activation of osteoprogenitor cells.
- Intramembranous ossification.

## Stages of Ilizarov Treatment
1. Fixator application.
2. Corticotomy.
3. Latency period.
4. Distraction phase.
5. Consolidation.
6. Frame dynamization.
7. Frame removal.

## Corticotomy – Key Concepts
- Low energy, subperiosteal osteotomy.
- Preserves periosteum and medullary blood supply.
- Maintains local biology with 330 percent increased blood flow.
### Technique Essentials
- 0.5–1 cm incision.
- Use narrow osteotome (0.5 cm).
- Fan-shaped cutting, avoid medullary penetration.
- Manual osteoclasis for the final cortex.
- Confirm completion with tactile feedback and radiology.
### Ideal Corticotomy
- Long oblique cut.
- Metaphyseal region.
- No comminution.
- <2 mm initial gap.

<!-- page 4 -->

## Corticotomy Levels – Factors
1. Anatomical
- Avoid nutrient artery zone.
- Metadiaphyseal junction preferred.
2. Biomechanical
- Minimum 6–7 cm from joint to preserve motion.
3. Physiological
- Avoid scars, sclerosis, porosis, previous infected areas.

## Monofocal vs Bifocal Corticotomy
Monofocal
- Lengthening up to 5 cm.
- Bone transport up to 5–7 cm.
- Nonunion biology stimulation.
- Gradual deformity correction.
Bifocal
- Lengthening 10–12 cm.
- Bone transport 10–16 cm.
- Dual level: lengthening + deformity correction.
- Useful in metabolic bone disorders.

## Distraction Phase – Histology
### Latent Period
- Average 5–7 days.
- Hematoma formation and stabilization.
- Children: 3–5 days. Adults: 7–10 days.
### Rate and Rhythm
- Standard: 1 mm/day (0.25 mm × 4 times).
- Children: up to 1.5 mm/day.
- Elderly/malnourished: 0.5–0.75 mm/day.

<!-- page 5 -->

Week-wise Regenerate Formation
### Week 1 – Fibrous Interzone (FIZ)
- Parallel collagen bundles.
- Avascular central zone.
- Acts like a pseudo growth plate.
### Week 2 – Primary Mineralization Front (PMF)
- Osteoblast clusters appear.
- Intramembranous ossification begins.
### Week 3 – Microcolumn Formation (MCF)
- Increasing length with columnar bone formation.
- Continued sinusoidal vascularity.
### End of Distraction
- FIZ ossifies.
- A single zone of MCF bridges the gap.

## Consolidation Phase
- Microcolumns unite and remodel.
- Bone resumes weight bearing.
Immobilization Rule
Immobilization time = 2 × Distraction period.

## Dynamization
- Done 4–6 weeks before frame removal.
- Loosening rod nuts increases frame elasticity.
- Promotes cortical contact and bone strengthening.

## Frame Removal
### Radiological Criteria
- At least three cortices ossified.
- Recanalization present.
### Clinical Criteria
- Painless full weight bearing.

<!-- page 6 -->

## Healing Index
- Months from operation to full weight bearing per cm of new bone formed.

## Regenerate Types

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| Normotrophic | Normal regenerate by day 20 |
| Hypertrophic | Exuberant regenerate |
| Hypotrophic | Poor regenerate after day 30 (scarring, poor vascularity) |

## Indications and Contraindications
Indications
- Nonunions.
- Limb lengthening.
- Deformity correction.
- Bone defects.
- Open fractures.
- Congenital deformities.
- Joint contracture.
Contraindications
- Excessive wire diameter.
- Too many wires causing operative trauma.

### Exam Pearls
- Ilizarov works via stability with micromotion, not rigidity.
- Optimal micromotion <1 mm.
- Non-linear stiffness is key advantage.
- Latent period: average 5–7 days.
- Distraction: 1 mm/day.
- Immobilization = 2 × distraction period.
- Dynamization: 4–6 weeks before removal.
- Ideal corticotomy: metaphyseal, long oblique, biologically friendly.
