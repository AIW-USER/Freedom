Overview / Key Concepts
Part 2 focuses on congenital hand anomalies related to dysplasia, deformation and
failure of formation. These include overgrowth conditions such as macrodactyly,
constriction band syndrome, longitudinal deficiencies, cleft hand, thumb hypoplasia and
principles of complex reconstructions including pollicization.
The emphasis is on understanding growth patterns, timing of surgery and selecting

Dysplasia – Overgrowth Anomalies
function over cosmesis.

Macrodactyly
- Rare condition, incidence around 0.2 per 1000 births.
- Not hereditary.
- Usually unilateral.
- Commonly associated syndromes:
  - Proteus syndrome
  - Ollier disease
  - Maffucci syndrome
- Most commonly associated with hypertrophy along nerve distribution.
- Median nerve hypertrophy is common in hand involvement.
Pathophysiology
- Overgrowth of tissues supplied by affected nerve.
- Involves fat, skin, bone and neurovascular structures.
### Flatt’s Classification
1. Gigantism with lipofibromatosis
2. Gigantism with neurofibromatosis
3. Gigantism with digital hyperostosis
4. Gigantism with hemihypertrophy
### Functional Classification
- Static: overgrowth present at birth, minimal progression.
- Dynamic: progressive enlargement with growth.
### Management Principles
- Epiphysiodesis: most common, performed once digit reaches adult size of same sex parent.

<!-- page 2 -->

- Debulking: staged soft tissue reduction protecting neurovascular bundles.
- Corrective osteotomy: closing wedge for deviation.
- Thumb involvement: MP arthrodesis if instability present.

### Deformation – Constriction Band Syndrome
- Ray amputation: last resort when digit is non functional.

### Key Features
- Also called Amniotic Band Syndrome or Streeter’s dysplasia.
- Circumferential constriction of limb or digits.
- Proximal limb well developed.
- Distal neurovascular compromise possible.
Management
- Early release is mandatory.
- Multiple Z plasties around circumference.

### Madelung Deformity
- Prevents distal ischemia and auto amputation.

Pathoanatomy
- Dyschondrosis of radial growth plate.
- Secondary overgrowth of distal ulna.
- Radial deviation of hand.
- Bayonet or dinner fork appearance.
- Wrist stiffness common.
### Indications for Surgery
- Wrist pain
- Reduced range of motion
- Functional limitation
- Significant cosmetic deformity
### Surgical Options
- Radial corrective osteotomy.
- Distal ulnar shortening osteotomy.
- Release of Vickers ligament.
### DRUJ Arthroplasty
- Highly controversial.
- Reserved for painful instability.

Congenital Trigger Thumb
### Key Features
- Very common condition.
- Flexion deformity at IP joint.
- Caused by tenosynovitis of FPL.
- Constriction at A1 pulley.
- Presence of Notta’s node proximal to pulley.
Management
- Release of A1 pulley under local anesthesia.

### Failure of Hand Plate Formation
- Excellent prognosis.

### Transverse Arrest
- Can occur at humerus, forearm, carpal or metacarpal level.
### Brachydactyly Spectrum
- Brachymesophalangy
- Brachymetacarpia
- Symbrachydactyly (atypical cleft hand)
### Management Options
- Web space deepening (phalangization).
- Free phalangeal transfer from toe.
- Distraction lengthening with or without bone graft.

Phocomelia
- Vascularized toe transfer.

Features
- Failure of axis formation and differentiation.
- Intercalated deficiency.
- Long bones absent, distal appendages present.
- Seal limb appearance.
Associations
- Thalidomide exposure.
- Roberts syndrome.
Management
- Orthotics and prosthetic support.
- Functional training.

Radial Longitudinal Deficiency
### Also Known As
- Radial aplasia
- Radial hypoplasia
- Radial club hand
Features
- Preaxial border deficiency.
- Hand deviated radially, flexed and pronated.
- Often associated with thumb hypoplasia.
### Bayne and Klug Classification
- Type I: short distal radius
- Type II: hypoplastic radius
- Type III: partial absence
- Type IV: complete absence
Associations
- Fanconi anemia
- TAR syndrome
- Holt-Oram syndrome
Management
- Early splintage.
- Tendon balancing.
- Distraction lengthening.
- Centralization (stability).
- Radialization (mobility).
Contraindication

### Ulnar Longitudinal Deficiency
- Stiff elbow (treat elbow first).

- Rarest longitudinal deficiency.

### Central Ray Deficiency – Cleft Hand
- Managed with splinting, distraction or one bone forearm.

Features
- Missing central (third) ray.
- Functionally good, cosmetically poor.
- Male predominance.
- Autosomal dominant in about 70 percent.

<!-- page 5 -->

- Related to AER abnormality.
Typical vs Atypical Cleft
Atypical

| Feature | Typical | (Symbrachydactyly) |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | V shaped | U shaped |
| Laterality | Bilateral | Usually unilateral |
| Genetics | Autosomal dominant | Sporadic |
| Associated syndromes | Common | Rare |

Management
- Excision of transverse bones.
- Cleft narrowing.
- Web space creation.

### Thumb Hypoplasia
- Shifting index finger to central position.

### Blauth and Manske Classification
- Type I: mild hypoplasia
- Type II: web narrowing, thenar hypoplasia, UCL deficiency
- Type IIIA: stable CMC joint
- Type IIIB: unstable CMC joint
- Type IV: floating thumb
- Type V: complete aplasia
Management
- Web space Z plasty.
- Huber opponensplasty.
- FDS ring transfer to thumb.

Pollicization
- Pollicization (12–18 months).

### Core Concept
- Index finger converted into thumb.
- Relies on brain plasticity.
Principles
- Transposition of index finger on neurovascular pedicle.
- Shortening and rotation of ray.
- Creation of wide first web space.
- Tendon rebalancing:

<!-- page 6 -->

- EIP → EPL
  - EDC → APL
  - First dorsal interosseous → APB
  - First palmar interosseous → Adductor pollicis
### Key Joint Conversion
- Index MP joint becomes thumb CMC joint.
### Postoperative Care
- Above elbow splint for 4–5 weeks.
- K wire removal at 4–6 weeks.

### Faculty Quote
- Early functional training using large object grasp and pinch.

- Macrodactyly is non hereditary and commonly nerve related.
- Epiphysiodesis is done when digit reaches adult size.
- Constriction band syndrome requires early Z plasty release.
- Madelung deformity involves dyschondrosis of radial physis.
- Notta’s node is pathognomonic of congenital trigger thumb.
- Radial club hand is contraindicated for surgery if elbow is stiff.
- Centralization gives stability, radialization gives mobility.
- Pollicization is ideally performed at 12–18 months.
- Index MP joint becomes CMC joint of thumb in pollicization.
