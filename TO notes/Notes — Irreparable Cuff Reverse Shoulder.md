Target Ortho Quick Revision Notes – Irreparable Rotator Cuff & Concepts of
Reverse Shoulder Arthroplasty (RSA)
Faculty: Dr. Anurag Tiwari

OVERVIEW
This session covers the approach to irreparable rotator cuff tears, including tendon
transfer options and reverse shoulder arthroplasty (RSA) concepts. The choice
depends primarily on age, activity level, joint condition, and rotator cuff integrity.
Quote: “When the cuff fails to stabilize, the deltoid must be empowered to take
charge — that’s the principle of Reverse Shoulder Arthroplasty.” – Dr. Anurag
Tiwari

### MASSIVE ROTATOR CUFF TEAR – DEFINITION
- Massive tear: Involvement of two or more tendons of the rotator cuff.
  - Supraspinatus + Infraspinatus (± Teres Minor)
  - Supraspinatus + Subscapularis
  - All four tendons (global tear)
- Size-based definition: Tear gap > 5 cm in any dimension.

CRITERIA FOR IRREPARABILITY
### Clinical Findings:
- Profound weakness or absent active external rotation
- Positive External Rotation Lag Sign
- Superior migration of humeral head on X-ray
- Acromiohumeral distance (AHD) < 7 mm

<!-- page 2 -->

### Imaging Findings:
- MRI: Fatty degeneration ≥ Grade 2 (Goutallier classification)
- Retraction > 3 cm or tendon gap > 5 cm Key Point: Irreparability is both anatomical (retraction, fatty infiltration) and functional (absent external rotation).

### MANAGEMENT OPTIONS

| Approach | Typical Patient | Key Indication |
|---|---|---|
| Tendon Transfer | Young, active, minimal arthritis | Joint preservation & motion restoration |
| Reverse Shoulder | Elderly, low-demand, | Pain relief & function |
| Arthroplasty (RSA) | cuff tear arthropathy | restoration |

### TENDON TRANSFER OPTIONS
1. Latissimus Dorsi (LD) Transfer – For Posterosuperior Tears
- Indications:
  - Young, active patient with minimal/no arthritis.
  - Posterosuperior irreparable cuff tear.
  - Subscapularis intact (prerequisite).
- Contraindications:
  - Subscapularis tear (positive Lift-off test)
  - Pseudoparalysis
  - Deltoid dysfunction
  - Shoulder stiffness
- Technique Summary:
  - Detach LD from humeral shaft → reroute to greater tuberosity (posterior-superior).
  - Provides improved external rotation & abduction.
- Clinical Tip: Combine LD transfer with RSA if Teres Minor deficient → restores external rotation.
2. Pectoralis Major (PM) Transfer – For Anterosuperior Tears
- Indications: Irreparable subscapularis tear (anterior-superior cuff loss).
- Technique: Transfer sternocostal head of PM → reroute under clavicular head → attach to lesser tuberosity.
- Function: Recreates internal rotation & adduction, mimicking subscapularis. Summary:
- Posterior Superior Tear → Latissimus Dorsi

- Anterior Superior Tear → Pectoralis Major

REVERSE SHOULDER ARTHROPLASTY (RSA)
### Concept & Indications
- Designed for cases with deficient or irreparable rotator cuff ± arthritis.
- Typical Indications:
  - Cuff tear arthropathy
  - Irreparable cuff tear with arthritis
  - Proximal humerus fracture with tuberosity non-union
  - Revision TSA / Hemiarthroplasty failure
  - Rheumatoid arthritis with cuff involvement

Prerequisites:
- Functional Deltoid muscle
- Adequate bone stock (humerus & glenoid)
Contraindications:
- Active infection
- Deltoid paralysis
- Poor bone stock
- Neurological disorder

### BIOMECHANICAL PRINCIPLES OF RSA

| Mechanism | Biomechanical Effect |
|---|---|
| Medialization of COR | Increases deltoid lever arm (power × efficiency) |
| Distalization of COR | Increases deltoid tension and moment arm |
| 10 mm medial shift | ↑ deltoid moment by ~20% |
| 10 mm distal shift | ↑ deltoid efficacy by ~30% |

Core Concept: Medialization + Distalization of Center of Rotation (COR)
converts deltoid into a prime abductor.

### SURGICAL STEPS (Summary)
- Approach: Deltopectoral.
- Exposure: Retract Deltoid & Pectoralis → identify Subscapularis → incise capsule → dislocate joint.
- Humeral Preparation:
  - Osteotomy at 30° retroversion & 45° inclination.

<!-- page 4 -->

- Cemented / uncemented stem with concave poly liner.
- Glenoid Preparation:
  - Central metaglene baseplate with inferior tilt (10–15°).
  - Attach glenosphere → creates convex glenoid.
- Final Components: 4 parts – Humeral stem, Poly liner, Metaglene, Glenosphere. Inferior tilt = Prevents scapular notching & increases implant longevity.

### Scapular Notching (Details)
- Nerot–Sirveaux Classification:
  - Grade 1 → Small notch (scapular neck only)
  - Grade 2 → Erosion up to inferior screw
  - Grade 3 → Erosion crossing inferior screw
  - Grade 4 → Involves baseplate loosening
### Risk Factors:
- Medialized COR
- Superior glenoid placement / tilt
Prevention:
- Inferior tilt (10–15°)
- Inferior placement of baseplate
- Larger glenosphere (↑ lateral offset) Mnemonic: “Lateralize & Inferiorize” to minimize scapular notching.

GLENOHUMERAL ARTHRODESIS (Final Bailout Procedure)
Used after failed RSA or TSA when reconstruction not possible.
### Functional Position:
- 20° Abduction
- 30° Forward Flexion
- 40° Internal Rotation Goal: Allows self-feeding and hand-to-mouth activity comfortably.

### EXAM PEARLS & TAKEAWAYS
- Irreparable cuff tear: ≥2 tendons, retraction >3 cm, AHD <7 mm.
- Posterosuperior tear → Latissimus Dorsi transfer.
- Anterosuperior tear → Pectoralis Major transfer.
- RSA: Indicated in elderly, low-demand, cuff tear arthropathy.
- Biomechanics: Medialization + Distalization ↑ deltoid torque.
- Scapular notching: 40–90% cases; prevented by inferior tilt/placement.
- Final salvage: Glenohumeral arthrodesis (20–30–40 rule).
