### Revision Notes/Trauma/Radial Head Fractures.md — under "### Longitudinal instability (Essex-Lopresti) — radial head as buttress, excision contraindicated"
**Primary stabiliser against proximal radial migration (TO)** — **radial head**; secondary = **IOM central band** + TFCC/DRUJ · ~**75–80%** of wrist axial load crosses the radiocarpal joint → IOM transfers it to the ulna

**IOM (TO)** — **5 bands**; **central band** most important, runs at **~20–25°** (angle used for reconstruction, PL or gracilis graft — rarely done)

**Recognise early (TO)** — radial head + forearm tenderness, DRUJ tenderness/instability → **X-ray elbow AND wrist**; **ultrasound** can show IOM tear · missed → painful loss of pronation/supination

**Pull test (TO)** — radial head unfixable → excise → clamp pulls radial shaft proximally under IITV: ulnar variance **↑ ≥3 mm → radial head replacement** · **<3 mm → excision alone acceptable**

**DRUJ after head fixation/replacement (TO)** — unstable → **above-elbow slab in supination**, **DRUJ pinning**, or **TFCC repair**

**Neglected Essex-Lopresti (TO)** — salvage **single-bone forearm** (proximal ulna to distal radius, DCP) → pain relief, no rotation · position **slightly more pronated than mid-prone**

### Revision Notes/Trauma/Clavicle Fractures - Midshaft.md — under "### Anatomy"
**Unique clavicle facts (TO)** — **only long bone lying horizontally** · **first bone to ossify, last to complete** · **only long bone ossifying by intramembranous ossification**

**Why middle third (TO)** — junction of medial convex + lateral concave curves · thinnest cross-section · subcutaneous · **nutrient artery entry** weakens cortex · mechanism: **direct fall on shoulder (commonest)** > FOOSH

**Trapezius (TO)** — elevates lateral end/acromion · result: medial fragment up, distal fragment down + medial + anteriorly rotated

### Revision Notes/Trauma/Clavicle Fractures - Midshaft.md — under "### Operative vs nonoperative – indications"
**TO operative indications** — **displacement/shortening >2 cm** · comminution · **floating shoulder** · open / skin threatened (spike) · neurovascular injury · **bilateral** fractures · nonop: **sling preferred over figure-of-8**, no reduction attempt, early pendulums

### Revision Notes/Trauma/Clavicle Fractures - Midshaft.md — under "### Plating technique"
**Approach (TO)** — **superior (anterosuperior) = workhorse** (risk to NV structures below) · **anteroinferior = safer** but demanding · protect the **three supraclavicular nerve branches** · **TEN = cosmetic but rotationally less stable** · lateral fragment: lateral locking plate or **hook plate under posterior acromion** · CC screw augments construct

### Revision Notes/Trauma/Scapula Fractures.md — under "### Floating shoulder"
| Floating-shoulder history (TO) | |
|---|---|
| **1799 Vogt** | Ipsilateral clavicle + scapula fracture → "unstable shoulder" |
| **1975 Ganz & Noesberger** | Unstable shoulder: scapular neck + clavicle fracture / AC dislocation + CC + CA ligament rupture |
| **Herscovici (1980s–90s)** | Coined **"floating shoulder"** = ipsilateral midshaft clavicle + scapular neck |
| **Goss 1993** | **Superior shoulder suspensory complex (SSSC)** |
| **Goss 2006** | Floating shoulder = **double disruption of SSSC** |

**SSSC (TO)** — osseoligamentous ring: **glenoid, coracoid, CC ligament, distal clavicle, AC joint, acromion** (+ CA ligament later) · **superior strut = middle third + distal clavicle** · **inferior strut = lateral scapular body–medial glenoid neck junction** · **pelvic-ring analogy**: one break → look for another

**Fragments (TO)** — distal = **glenoid + coracoid** · proximal = acromion, spine, body · double disruption → drooping shoulder, malunion/nonunion, impingement, weakness; high plexus + axillary vessel association

**Imaging (TO)** — X-ray inadequate → **CT with 3D subtraction essential** (Bartoníček 2018)

**Glenopolar angle (TO)** — normal **30–45°** · **<20° → surgery** (Romero) · **glenoid inclination angle** (AP, glenoid vs medial border perpendiculars) = caudal tilt

**TO operative thresholds** — GPA **<20°** · step-off **>4–5 mm** or **>20%** glenoid · lateral border offset **>20 mm** · angulation **>45°** · **100% translation** · nonop if **<5 mm**, GPA **>30°**

**Sequence (TO)** — **fix clavicle first** (ligamentotaxis realigns neck) → reassess → fix scapula only if GPA <20°, lateralisation, persistent displacement

**Strong bone for fixation (TO)** — **glenoid neck · lateral scapular border · base of spine · coracoid** · 3.5 / 2.7 mm recon or LC-DCP; locking for comminuted/osteoporotic

| Literature (TO) | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Edwards (JBJS 2000) | Nonop if displacement **<5 mm** |
| Van Noort | Nonop if glenoid well aligned (no caudal displacement) |
| Goss | Fix both; nonop → **drooping shoulder** |
| Ada & Miller | Cuff dysfunction if both displaced |
| Romero / Romeo | Poor outcome if **GPA <20°** or displacement **>1 cm** |

### Revision Notes/Trauma/Proximal Humerus Fractures.md — under "### Anatomy"
**Head geometry (TO)** — articular arc **160°** · radius of curvature **~25 mm** (23–29) · **head–tuberosity distance (HTD) 8–10 mm** · head height **18 mm** (¾ of radius) · NSA **135°** (valgus tolerated, **varus poorly tolerated**) · retroversion **20° (10–40°)** · bicipital groove **~7 mm anterior to medullary axis** = retroversion landmark

**Mechanisms (TO)** — glenoid compression of head · bending at surgical neck · cuff tension → tuberosity avulsion · bimodal: young males high-energy, older females osteoporotic · **3rd commonest non-vertebral osteoporotic fracture**

### Revision Notes/Trauma/Proximal Humerus Fractures.md — under "### Humeral head vascularity & ischaemia predictors (Hertel criteria)"
**Campochiaro 2015 (TO)** — Hertel criteria **less predictive than quality of reduction** for AVN · anatomical neck fracture **90–100%** AVN

### Revision Notes/Trauma/Proximal Humerus Fractures.md — under "### Classification"
**Codman (TO)** — 4 parts: head · GT · LT · shaft (proximal to pec major insertion) · Neer displacement = **>1 cm or >45°** · AO/OTA **11-A extra-articular unifocal · 11-B extra-articular bifocal · 11-C intra-articular**

### Revision Notes/Trauma/Proximal Humerus Fractures.md — under "### Nonoperative management & natural history"
**PROFHER (JAMA 2015) (TO)** — no long-term difference surgery vs sling in selected displaced fractures

|---|---|

| 3–9 wk | Active-assisted elevation after radiographic healing |
| >9 wk | Active + strengthening |

### Revision Notes/Trauma/Proximal Humerus Fractures.md — under "### Greater tuberosity fractures — displacement thresholds & fixation"
**GT / LT fixation by size (TO)** — GT >5 mm: fragment **>2.5 cm → screw**, **<2.5 cm → suture anchor** · LT: <2.5 cm → suture/anchor, >2.5 cm → ORIF

### Revision Notes/Trauma/Proximal Humerus Fractures.md — under "### CRPP"
**Modified Resch (TO)** — **2 lateral-to-medial + 1 anterior-to-posterior** K-wires · contraindicated: calcar deficiency, LT fracture, metaphyseal comminution

### Revision Notes/Trauma/Proximal Humerus Fractures.md — under "### Locked plating — screw penetration, varus collapse & calcar support"
**PHILOS position (TO)** — **5–8 mm below GT tip**, **2–4 mm posterior to bicipital groove** · **4–6 head screws incl. 2 inferomedial calcar** · ≥3 shaft screws · **Gavaskar 2016: ≥7 locking head screws** · 10 suture holes for cuff · fill defects with calcium phosphate cement · **torus concept** = head on a "three-legged stool"

**Bone quality (TO)** — **CACT <4 mm = poor bone** → ORIF failure likely · **deltoid tuberosity index** correlates with BMD

**Beach chair (TO)** — BP cuff on dependent leg misreads cerebral perfusion

### Revision Notes/Trauma/Proximal Humerus Fractures.md — under "### Intramedullary nailing"
**Nail (TO)** — 2-part surgical neck / 3-part GT · **8–12 mm** · 3 cm anterolateral incision, split cuff along fibres · straight nails enter via cuff split · **MultiLoc** (2nd generation) · complication: **cuff dysfunction + shoulder pain**
